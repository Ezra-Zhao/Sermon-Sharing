## Content

The procedure of salvation. What is the sequence of the experience of being saved? God's saving has steps, and our being saved also has a sequence. As God bestows mercy step by step, so we receive grace step by step. Using our own method in place of God's method cannot save us; using our own procedure in place of God's procedure is also in vain. What, after all, is the sequence of our being saved? [A stretch of the original text at this point is illegible garbled characters and is omitted as unreadable in the source.]

1. We are God's children: "[that we] should be called children of God... we are children of God" (1 John 3:1-2, NKJV). 2. We are new creations: "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation" (2 Cor. 5:17, NKJV). 3. We are called saints: "...to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints" (1 Cor. 1:2, NKJV). The first step of salvation realized in us is that it puts us in the position of [being in Christ]. Because we have God's life, we [are] God's children; although we are always [not yet fully like Him], yet [we are His children]. And because God has put us in Christ, accomplishing a new creation, we [are] new creations. Even if we do not look like it, yet we [are]! The title of saints is the same: although we are not holy enough, our position is that we have been sanctified in Christ! God's order is first that we have the position, and only afterward the experience.

1. Conduct like the Lord: "[that] as He was raised from the dead... even so we also should walk in newness of life" (Rom. 6:4, NKJV). 2. Completely like the Father: "Therefore you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect" (Matt. 5:48, NKJV). 3. Character like God: "...the new man which was created according to God, [in true righteousness and holiness]" (Eph. 4:24, NKJV). [Original text partially illegible.] The first step is to be brought to the point of [being]; the second step is only then to be brought to [resembling]. [Being] is immediate; [resembling] is gradual. [Being] concerns life; [resembling] concerns living. [Being] solves the inward for us; [resembling] solves the outward for us. Without the inward renewal of the essence of life, there will never be the outward expression of the character of living. If there is, it is either pretense or hypocrisy.

1. Work out your own salvation: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" (Phil. 2:12, NKJV). 2. Show forth the word of life: "holding fast [holding forth] the word of life" (Phil. 2:16, NKJV) (3 John). 3. Christ magnified: "that Christ will be magnified in my body" (Phil. 1:20, NKJV). In salvation, we have already said that apart from faith there is nothing we need to do. But for a person who has already been saved, to [work out / manifest] the salvation he has obtained — that is, to show forth the word of life — is possible, and it ought to be done. This is not forced pretense; it is a natural outflow. It is not obtaining by [working]; it is [manifesting] what has already been obtained. It is not the [cause] of the work, but its [effect/function]; it is not striving to [produce], but bringing into play.

Homework questions: What is the procedure of our being saved? Standing in the position of salvation, what then are we? If we [are] but do not [resemble], what is the difference? In salvation, what does God want us to be like? [Original text partially illegible.] What does it mean to [work out / manifest salvation]? Can anyone at all work out salvation?
