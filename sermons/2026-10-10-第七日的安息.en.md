## (00:00 - 08:00) Opening · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Speaker:
Peace be with you, brothers and sisters! Yesterday we looked at Genesis 1:4-25—God divided the light from the darkness, established the firmament and the dry land, caused all living things to reproduce according to their kind, and finally God looked at everything He had made, and it was "very good."

What happened after "very good"? What did God do next?

Today we look at Genesis 2:1-3. There are only three verses, but these three verses may be the most underestimated in the entire Bible. Please turn to Genesis chapter 2, and I will read:

"Thus the heavens and the earth, and all the host of them, were finished. And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done. [Pause 3 seconds]

Brothers and sisters, for six days, God created light, created the heavens, created the land and the sea, created the sun, moon, and stars, created the fish, birds, and beasts, and created man. On the seventh day, God did one thing—**He rested**.

Today we will look at this in three parts:

First, why God rested—the rest of "it is finished," not the rest of "being tired";
Second, the seventh day set apart as holy—the institution of the Sabbath and the lesson of the manna;
Third, entering His rest—from the rest of creation to the rest of salvation.

Let us first bow our heads in prayer:

In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen!"

## (08:00 - 25:00) Part 1: Why God Rested—"It Is Finished," Not "Tired"

### 1.1 What Does "Rest" Mean in the Original Language?

Verse 2: "And He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done."

Both "ended/ceased" and "rested" come from the same Hebrew word: **Shavath** (shavat)—to cease, desist, or knock off work. This word does not mean "collapsed from exhaustion"; it means "finished the job"! It is like a craftsman laying down his hammer, clapping his hands, and saying: It is finished, time to pack up!

Verse 1: "Thus the heavens and the earth, and all the host of them, were finished."—The Hebrew word for "finished" is **Kalah** (kalah)—to complete, to bring to an end, with nothing lacking.

Brothers and sisters, the order is crucial: **first "finished," then "rested."** God's rest is established upon the foundation of "it is finished." It is not collapsing from exhaustion halfway through; it is completing everything and stopping with complete satisfaction.

English comparison:
- KJV: "and he **rested** on the seventh day from all his work which he had made."
- NIV: "so on the seventh day he **rested** from all his work."

### 1.2 Did God Get Tired?

Someone might ask: Does God also need to rest? Does God get tired?

The Bible gives a very straightforward answer. Isaiah 40:28 says: "**The Creator of the ends of the earth, Neither faints nor is weary.**" God is not tired! He spoke and it came to be; He created the universe with a single word. What would tire Him out?

Then why did He rest? Exodus 31:17 gives the answer: "**For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, and on the seventh day He rested and was refreshed.**"

The Hebrew word for "refreshed" is **Naphash** (nafash)—to breathe freely, to be refreshed, to recover breath.

English comparison:
- KJV: "on the seventh day he rested, and **was refreshed**."
- NIV: "on the seventh day he rested and **was refreshed**."

Brothers and sisters, "was refreshed"—God "took a breath"! Not panting from exhaustion, but taking a deep breath of satisfaction. It is just like after you finish preparing a lavish New Year's Eve feast, watching the whole family gather around eating heartily; you stand at the kitchen doorway, take a deep breath, and say in your heart: It was all worth it!

God's rest is not recharging; it is a **celebration**! It is not "I cannot go on"; it is "It is very good"!

Spoken pulpit explanation: When a master craftsman finishes a work, he steps back three paces, examines his creation, and nods. What God did on the seventh day was to step back three paces, examine His universe, and say: "Very good." Then, He sat down and enjoyed His "very good." Brothers and sisters, **even God stopped to enjoy it—what right do you have not to stop?**

### 1.3 Golden Scripture Passages and Original Word Analysis

**"Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth!" (Ps. 46:10)**

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "be still" is **Raphah** (raphah)—to let go, release, be quiet. This word means to let go, loosen one's grip, cease striving—in modern terms, it means releasing the tight grip of your hands.

Spoken pulpit explanation: God says, "Let go!" The tighter you grip, the harder it is for God's hand to step in. If you refuse to let go of the steering wheel, how can God drive for you? If you clutch onto your bills and refuse to let go, how can God pay them for you? If you grip your children's future and refuse to let go, how can God lead them for you? **Raphah—let go, be still, and you will know that He is God!**

---

**"For thus says the Lord GOD, the Holy One of Israel: 'In returning and rest you shall be saved; In quietness and confidence shall be your strength.' But you would not," (Isa. 30:15)**

Spoken pulpit explanation: In this verse, God gives you the formula: Salvation = returning and rest; strength = quietness and confidence. You have no strength, not because you are not trying hard enough, but because you refuse to rest! The last sentence pierces the deepest: "But you would not." God set rest on the table, but you deliberately refuse to eat, and then blame God for not giving you strength.

---

**"It is vain for you to rise up early, To sit up late, To eat the bread of sorrows; For so He gives His beloved sleep." (Ps. 127:2)**

Spoken pulpit explanation: "To rise up early, to sit up late"—this is not diligence, this is anxiety! Rising early and sleeping late to work desperately, eating the "bread of sorrows"—the more you eat the bread of toil, the more exhausted you become. But to "His beloved," God "gives sleep"—**sleep is also God's grace!** Your insomnia may not be an issue with your bed or an issue with melatonin; it is an issue of surrender. If you bring the daytime burdens to bed, how can you sleep?

---

**"I will both lie down in peace, and sleep; For You alone, O LORD, make me dwell in safety." (Ps. 4:8)**

Spoken pulpit explanation: When David wrote this psalm, he was on the run as a refugee! His son Absalom was hunting him down, and he was sleeping in the open wilderness. Yet he said, "I will both lie down in peace, and sleep." **Peace is not in the circumstances; it is in "You alone, O LORD."** With God, even the wilderness is a place of rest; without God, even a mansion is a battlefield.

### 1.4 Biblical Example: Elijah Under the Broom Tree

In 1 Kings 19, the prophet Elijah had just won a great victory on Mount Carmel—fire burned the altar, and one man against eight hundred and fifty false prophets won an overwhelming victory!

Right after that, Jezebel sent a single message: "By this time tomorrow, I will take your life!" Elijah was terrified, fleeing for his life through the night. He ran into the wilderness, slumped down under a broom tree, and prayed to die: "It is enough! Now, LORD, take my life!"

Brothers and sisters, even a great prophet can break down! Falling into depression right after a victory, longing for death right after glory. Human beings are just that fragile.

What did God do? God did not preach a sermon first, He did not rebuke him first, He did not say, "Why do you have so little faith?" God sent an angel, who touched him and said one sentence:

"**Arise and eat!**" (1 Kgs. 19:5)

Elijah looked, and there by his head was a jar of water and a cake baked on coals. He ate and drank, and **lay down again**. The angel of the Lord came back a second time and touched him: "Arise and eat, because the journey is too great for you." So he arose, and ate and drank; and he went in the strength of that food forty days and forty nights as far as the mountain of God.

[Pause 2 seconds]

Brothers and sisters, look at God's sequence of healing: **first sleep, then eat; eat again and sleep, sleep again and eat. Only when he had slept enough and eaten his fill did God speak to him.**

God knows that an exhausted person cannot take in a sermon. A person burned dry cannot receive a vision. God does not rush you; He lets you sleep first!

How many people today are exhausted to the point of wishing they were dead, yet are still forcing themselves through? Today, through Elijah, God says to you: **Child, take a sleep first; eat a good meal first. The sky will not collapse; I am holding it up.**

### 🔥 Ignition Point 1: Stop! God Rested—Do You Dare to Rest Too?

[Slow down, emphasize phrase by phrase]

Brothers and sisters, Genesis 2:2 says that God "rested from all His work which He had done."

God rested—not because He was tired, but because it was finished!
Today, God wants to say one sentence to you—

**Stop!**

It is not telling you to give up; it is telling you to rest.
It is not telling you to check out; it is telling you to entrust.

[Parallel build-up, increase volume]

You cannot stop because you do not believe—
You do not believe that if the sky collapses, God is holding it up;
You do not believe that when you are asleep, God is still awake;
You do not believe that if you earn a little less, God can still provide for you!

[Prophetic direct address]

Child, listen to me:
**God created the heavens and the earth in six days, and on the seventh day He rested—**
**What makes you think that if you rest for one day, the sky will collapse?**
**You think you are the load-bearing pillar, but in truth you are a child in God's arms!**

[Pause 3 seconds]

**Right today, not tomorrow!**
**Today, you must learn to rest in the arms of God!**

[Pause 3 seconds]

### 1.5 Pastoral Reference

Warren Wiersbe notes in his Genesis commentary (general sense of teaching): God's rest is the crown of creation, not its full stop. The six days of work were "good," but the seventh day of rest gave the "good" its enjoyment—God stopped not because the work had ended, but because joy had begun. If one does not know how to rest, one will never taste the sweetness of "very good."

Pastor Jieren Li looks at it from a theological angle (general sense of teaching): God's rest is a declaration of "completion." Hebrews says that God "rested from all His works"; this "rest" is God's satisfaction with His own work. Today, the foundation of a believer's rest is not "I have finished," but "He has accomplished it"—on the cross Jesus said, "It is finished," and that is the ultimate foundation of our rest.

[Pulpit a cappella singing]

"It is well with my soul, it is well."

— Chorus of *It Is Well with My Soul*

## (25:00 - 42:00) Part Two: The Seventh Day Set Apart—The Institution of the Sabbath

### 2.1 What Does "Sanctified It" Mean in the Original Text?

Verse 3: "Then God blessed the seventh day and **sanctified it**."

The Hebrew word for "sanctified it" is **Qadash** (קָדַשׁ)—to set apart, to consecrate. This word means taking something out of the realm of the "common" and stamping it with the seal of "belonging to God."

Brothers and sisters, take note: God blessed many things—He blessed the fish and birds to "be fruitful and multiply" (Gen. 1:22), and He blessed mankind to "be fruitful and multiply" (Gen. 1:28). But there are only two things God **sanctified**: the seventh day, and His people!

The Hebrew word for "blessed" is **Barak** (בָּרַךְ). God blessed the seventh day—**time itself can be blessed!** It is not only gold and silver, houses and cars that can be blessed; a day of time can also be blessed.

Pulpit oral exposition: Out of seven days, God pulled out one day and stamped it separately: "This day is Mine." Brothers and sisters, in your time, is there a day stamped with God's seal? Or are all seven days "mine"—my work, my anxiety, my phone?

English comparison:
- KJV: "And God **blessed** the seventh day, and **sanctified** it."
- NIV: "Then God **blessed** the seventh day and **made it holy**."

### 2.2 The Fourth of the Ten Commandments: The Longest Commandment

Exodus 20:8-11:

"**Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days you shall labor and do all your work, but the seventh day is the Sabbath of the Lord your God.** In it you shall do no work: you, nor your son, nor your daughter, nor your male servant, nor your female servant, nor your cattle, nor your stranger who is within your gates. For in six days the Lord made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and all that is in them, and rested the seventh day. Therefore the Lord blessed the Sabbath day and hallowed it."

Brothers and sisters, have you ever counted? Among the Ten Commandments, "You shall not murder" is four words in Chinese; "You shall not commit adultery" is four words; "You shall not steal" is four words—**only for "Remember the Sabbath day" did God use four entire verses to explain!** The longest commandment in the Ten Commandments is not about murder or arson; it is about "taking a day off"!

Why was God so elaborate? Because He understands human nature so well—**the hardest commandment for humans to keep is not "You shall not murder," but "Stop!"**

Deuteronomy 5:15 gives another reason for the Sabbath: "**And remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt**, and the Lord your God brought you out from there by a mighty hand and by an outstretched arm; **therefore the Lord your God commanded you to keep the Sabbath day.**"

[Pause 2 seconds]

Brothers and sisters, this reason is so powerful! In Egypt, Pharaoh would not let you rest—"You are idle!" (Ex. 5:8). The quota of bricks could not be reduced by one, and you had to gather your own straw. Slaves have no Sabbath; slaves only have endless work.

But God says: You are no longer slaves! I have brought you out. **Therefore, I command you to rest!**

Pulpit oral exposition: The Sabbath is a declaration that "we are slaves no more"! Who is your "Pharaoh" today? Is it your boss? Your bills? Your own ambition? That voice telling you, "The moment you stop, you should feel guilty"? God says to you today: **You are no longer a slave; you are My child. Child, rest—this is a command, and it is also a gift!**

### 2.3 Golden Scripture Passages and Word Studies

**"The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath." (Mark 2:27)**

Pulpit oral exposition: With this statement, Jesus seized the Sabbath back from the hands of the Pharisees! The Pharisees had turned the Sabbath into thirty-nine categories of forbidden labor, limits on how many steps one could walk, and even defined plucking heads of grain as "harvesting." They turned a gift into shackles, and transformed the Sabbath into a "day of suffering."

Jesus said: You have it completely backwards! **The Sabbath was made as a gift, not a burden; it was made for you, not to bind you.** Brothers and sisters, if your "Sabbath" is more exhausting than going to work—rushing around, social obligations, catching up on homework, scrolling on your phone late into the night—that is not a Sabbath; that is a seventh workday!

---

**"Of how much more value then is a man than a sheep? Therefore it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath." (Matt. 12:12)**

Word study: The background of this statement was Jesus healing a man with a withered hand on the Sabbath. The Pharisees were looking for an accusation against Him. Jesus asked in return: "What man is there among you who has one sheep, and if it falls into a pit on the Sabbath, will not lay hold of it and lift it out?"

Pulpit oral exposition: The Sabbath is not "doing nothing"; it is "not doing a slave's work," but doing "the work of a son"! What is the work of a son? Worshiping God, spending time with family, doing good, bringing healing to people—**the Sabbath is a day for life to be refreshed and nourished, not a day for rules to be satisfied.**

---

**"Better a handful with quietness than both hands full, with toil and grasping for the wind." (Eccl. 4:6)**

Pulpit oral exposition: This is God's mathematics! Human mathematics says: one handful < two handfuls, the more the better. God's mathematics says: **one handful + quietness > two handfuls + grasping for the wind**! If you grasp full with both hands, what you are grasping is the wind—when the wind blows, nothing is left. If you hold with one hand while keeping the other hand open, with quietness in your heart—that is true gain.

Brothers and sisters, what are you grasping today with both hands? Houses? Stocks? Your children's grades? The tighter you grasp, the more it is like grasping the wind. Release one hand; leave one hand open for God, for rest, and for what truly matters.

---

**"When you lie down, you will not be afraid; yes, you will lie down and your sleep will be sweet." (Prov. 3:24)**

Spoken pulpit elaboration: The context preceding this verse speaks of "wisdom." Those who have wisdom will not be afraid when they lie down, and their sleep will be sweet. **Sleeping soundly is evidence of wisdom!** If you cannot sleep, it might not just be because you drank too much coffee; it may be a lack of wisdom—failing to entrust things to God is simply unwise.

### 2.4 Biblical Example: The Lesson of the Manna

In Exodus 16, while the Israelites were in the wilderness, God rained down manna every morning. The rule was very clear:

"Let no one leave any of it till morning." Some did not listen and kept it until morning, and the result was that "**it bred worms and stank**."

Yet the sixth day was different: on the sixth day they were to gather **twice as much**, because "**Tomorrow is a Sabbath rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD**" (Ex. 16:23). On the seventh day, God did not send manna. Some still went out on the seventh day to gather—and "**they found none**" (Ex. 16:27).

"See! For the LORD has given you the Sabbath; therefore He gives you on the sixth day bread for two days. **Let every man remain in his place; let no man go out of his place on the seventh day.**" (Ex. 16:29)

[Pause for 2 seconds]

Brothers and sisters, look: manna was a free lunch provided by God, but God used manna to teach a lesson called "The Faith Lesson of the Sabbath":

Lesson One: **Daily dependence**—gather only for each day; do not hoard. What was hoarded began to rot.
Lesson Two: **Daring to rest for a day**—God gave a double portion on the sixth day, telling you: if you rest for one day, I will sustain you for two!
Lesson Three: **Do not go out searching on the seventh day**—those who went out to look found nothing. If God does not send it down, searching is entirely useless.

Spoken pulpit elaboration: How many people today are still "going out to look" on the seventh day? Still answering work emails on Sunday, still balancing next week's accounts on Sunday, still agonizing over tomorrow's affairs on Sunday. God says: My child, **on the seventh day I send no manna; even if you go out, you will find none. Better to stay in your place and rest!**

The double portion on the sixth day is the assurance God gives you so that you "dare to rest." **Do you dare to believe: you rest for one day, and God provides for all seven?**

### 2.5 Pastoral Reference

According to the consistent teaching of Pastor Tong Liu at River of Life Christian Church (general teaching summary): Rest is God's most underestimated gift to mankind. Many equate "busyness" with godliness and view an "inability to stop" as faithfulness. But what God looks at is this: if you are so busy that you have no time to draw near to Him, that is not faithfulness; it is putting the cart before the horse. Slowing down is not laziness; it is returning sovereignty to God—only when you slow down can God catch up with you; only when you become quiet can you hear His still, small voice.

[A cappella singing from the pulpit]

"God hath not promised skies always blue, flower-strewn pathways all our lives through."

— *What God Hath Promised*, Stanza 1

## (42:00 - 55:00) Part Three: Entering His Rest—From Creation to Salvation

### 3.1 What Does "I Will Give You Rest" Mean in the Original Language?

Matthew 11:28-30 records Jesus' personal invitation:

"**Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For My yoke is easy and My burden is light.**"

The Greek word for "give rest" is **Anapauo**—to cause to rest, to refresh, to recover strength. The Greek for "labor" is **Kopiao**—to toil to the point of utter exhaustion; "heavy laden" is **Phortizo**—stooped low, crushed beneath a heavy load.

English comparisons:
- KJV: "Come unto me, **all ye that labour and are heavy laden**, and **I will give you rest**."
- NIV: "Come to me, **all you who are weary and burdened**, and **I will give you rest**."

Brothers and sisters, notice what Jesus did not say. He did not say, "I will take away all your burdens." He said, "I will give you rest." **The burden may still be there, but the one carrying the burden has changed!**

Before, you were bearing it all by yourself; now Jesus carries it together with you—"Take My yoke upon you." A yoke is put on two together! Oxen yokes are always double yokes. Jesus says: Take off your yoke, and put on My yoke—**for My yoke is easy and My burden is light**, because on the main side, I am the One pulling!

Spoken pulpit elaboration: Today you are pulling with a single yoke, carrying the dead weight all alone. Jesus says: Switch to My double yoke; I will be yoked with you. You provide 10% of the strength; I will provide 90%. All you need to do is be "gentle and lowly in heart"—gentleness means not stubbornly resisting; lowliness means admitting, "I can carry this no longer." **Admitting you cannot carry it is the first step toward finding rest!**

### 3.2 There Remains a "Sabbath Rest" Kept for You

Hebrews 4:9-10:

"**There remains therefore a rest for the people of God. For he who has entered His rest has himself also ceased from his works as God did from His.**"

The phrase "Sabbath rest" in Greek is **Sabbatismos**—a word that appears only this once in the entire New Testament! It is a word uniquely coined by the author, meaning "a Sabbath-style rest."

Brothers and sisters, in Genesis 2, God rested from His work of **creation**; in Hebrews 4, you must rest from your **own** works. What are "your own works"?

- The work of trying to earn your own salvation—cease from it; Jesus has already accomplished it;
- The work of trying to earn your own self-worth—cease from it; God has already stamped your value as "Tov";
- The work of trying to earn your own security—cease from it; numbers in a bank account cannot grant you peace;
- The work of trying to change your family members by your own strength—cease from it; you can only love them, but God is the One who changes them.

[Pause for 2 seconds]

"As God did from His"—God finished His work in six days and rested on the seventh; you cannot finish your work in an entire lifetime, yet you can rest starting today. **Because Jesus said on the cross, "It is finished!" (Jn. 19:30)—He has already spoken your "It is finished" on your behalf!**

### 3.3 Golden Scripture Passages and Word Studies

**"He makes me to lie down in green pastures; He leads me beside the still waters." (Ps. 23:2)**

Word study: The Hebrew word for "makes me to lie down" is **Rabat**—like a flock lying down to chew the cud and rest. This word depicts the action of the shepherd gently pressing the sheep down to lie down!

Spoken pulpit elaboration: Notice "**He makes me**"—it is not "I lie down on my own," but "He makes me lie down"! When you refuse to lie down, He presses you down. Sometimes God leads you into "forced rest": an illness, a lost job, a shattered plan—do not be so quick to grumble; that may be the Shepherd making you lie down. **Lie down; green pastures are far more comfortable than the wilderness, and still waters are far more tranquil than the battlefield.**

---

**"Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Phil. 4:6-7)**

Spoken pulpit elaboration: "The peace of God, which surpasses all understanding"—your circumstances haven't changed, the bills are still there, the sickness is still there, but your heart has been transformed! This is rest: **the surroundings did not shift, but the heart was renewed**. Peace does not mean the absence of storms; it means being able to sleep in the midst of the storm—just as Jesus slept in the stern of the boat (Mk. 4:38), with the tempest raging while He slept peacefully. The disciples cried out, "Teacher, do You not care that we are perishing?" Jesus arose and said, "Peace, be still!"—**the storm inside your heart today also needs to hear this command: Peace, be still!**

---

**"And He said, 'My Presence will go with you, and I will give you rest.'" (Ex. 33:14)**

Spoken pulpit elaboration: These words were spoken by God to Moses. Moses had to lead two million people into Canaan, facing an unknown path ahead. God gave him no map, no strategic manual; He gave only this one promise: "My Presence will go with you."

Brothers and sisters, **rest is not a physical place; it is a Presence**. With His presence, even the wilderness is a place of rest; without His presence, even a luxury mansion becomes a battlefield. No matter how daunting the place you must go today, as long as He goes with you, you will find rest.

---

**"casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you." (1 Pet. 5:7)**

**"Cast your burden on the LORD, and He shall sustain you; He shall never permit the righteous to be moved." (Ps. 55:22)**

Pulpit Oral Elaboration: "Casting"—just like unloading cargo! The truck arrives at the terminal, unloads the boxes one by one, and drives away empty. What you drove into God's presence today is a fully loaded truck: anxieties, fears, bills, children's grades, parents' health... **Unload them box by box, and do not load them back in!** Many people finish praying and then hoist the burdens back onto their shoulders piece by piece—that is not called surrender; that is called window shopping. Once you unload it, do not carry it back!

### 3.4 Biblical Example: Martha and Mary

In Luke 10:38-42, Jesus came to the home of Martha and Mary. Mary **"sat at Jesus' feet and heard His word,"** while Martha "was distracted with much serving" and came up complaining, "Lord, do You not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Therefore tell her to help me."

And Jesus answered and said to her, **"Martha, Martha, you are worried and troubled about many things. But one thing is needed, and Mary has chosen that good part, which will not be taken away from her."**

[Pause for 2 seconds]

Brothers and sisters, Martha did nothing wrong! Serving the Lord, setting the table, pouring water—these are all good things. But where did she go wrong? **She was busy for the "Lord," yet she never sat with the "Lord."** She was busy with the things of the Lord, yet she missed the Lord Himself.

"Worried and troubled"—the Greek word is **Merimnao**, the very same word used in Matthew 6 for "do not worry"! Martha's problem was not being busy; it was an anxious busyness: the busier she got, the more agitated she became; the more agitated she became, the busier she got, and then she blamed others for not helping.

Pulpit Oral Elaboration: How many "Marthas" are sitting among us today? Busy for the church, busy for the family, busy for work—busy with "good things," yet "worried and troubled" in heart. Today, Jesus also calls your name twice: "Martha, Martha!"—what is your name? He calls you twice as well. **Only one thing is needed: to sit at His feet.** When was the last time you sat quietly at Jesus' feet?

### 3.5 Pastoral Reference

According to Pastor Erjun Zhuo's consistent teaching (general teaching summary): Surrender is not a slogan; it is a daily discipline. Many people have "surrender" on their lips while keeping the burden firmly strapped to their shoulders. Genuine surrender carries three marks: being able to sleep, being able to smile, and being able to wait. If you cannot sleep, it shows you haven't surrendered yet; if you cannot smile, it shows you are still carrying the weight; if you cannot wait, it shows you are still wrestling with God for the steering wheel.

Pastor Bill Johnson of Bethel Church often teaches (general teaching summary): The kingdom of God operates out of rest. A person who fights from rest and a person who fights for rest are completely different. The former says, "It is finished"; the latter says, "Hurry up." ## (55:00 - 60:00) Application for Six Groups · Weekly Practice · Summary Response Prayer

Preacher:
Brothers and sisters, Genesis 2:1-3, three verses, and God did only one thing: He rested. Now, let us apply this lesson of "rest" to the lives of six groups of people:

**The First Group: The Unstoppable Workaholics.** Ecclesiastes 4:6 says, "Better a handful with quietness than both hands full, with travail and grasping for the wind." Both of your hands are full, but what you are grasping is wind. When God commands you to rest, He is not shortchanging you; He is saving you. This week, shut down one "non-essential" busy task and keep one hand open for God.

**The Second Group: Full-Time Mothers and Long-Term Caregivers.** You are on call 24 hours a day, with no clocking out and no weekends. Listen to me: Even God rested on the seventh day; what makes you think you cannot rest? When Elijah was exhausted to the point of begging to die, God first had him sleep and eat. You also need your time "under the broom tree." This week, carve out half a day for yourself to sleep, eat, and think about nothing—this is ordained by God, not stolen by you.

**The Third Group: Those with Anxiety, Insomnia, and Restless Hearts.** Psalm 4:8 says, "I will both lie down in peace, and sleep; for You alone, O Lord, make me dwell in safety." Starting tonight, do some "unloading" before bed: name three worries out loud, hand them over to God, and then say, "Lord, I am resting." Your sleeplessness is not a problem with the bed; it is a problem of surrender.

**The Fourth Group: Students and Young People.** What you fear most is "slowing down"—fearing you will fall behind, fearing you will be outdone. But Mary "has chosen that good part, which will not be taken away from her"—grades can be taken away, job offers can be taken away, but the blessing of sitting at Jesus' feet can never be taken away by anyone. Only when you slow down can you go far; only those who have rested will have strength when they run.

**The Fifth Group: Entrepreneurs, Business Owners, and Those Anxious About Tomorrow.** Matthew 6:34 says, "Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things. Sufficient for the day is its own trouble." The lesson of manna: God gave a double portion on the sixth day; when you rest for one day, He feeds you for seven days. God cares more about your company and your cash flow than you do. Your responsibility is faithfulness; His responsibility is the outcome.

**The Sixth Group: The Elderly.** Your gray hair is God's "season of rest" for you. Moses "was one hundred and twenty years old when he died. His eyes were not dim nor his natural vigor diminished" (Deut. 34:7)—those who rest do not wither away. The second half of your life is not "useless"; it is "finished"—just like God on the seventh day, sit down, enjoy His "very good," and pray blessings over the next generation.

💡 **Weekly Spiritual Action Practice (Weekly Practice)**

**Practice One: The "True Sabbath" Experiment.** Choose one day this week (or at least half a day) to silence your phone, avoid talking about work, and refrain from scrolling short videos. Go to church, spend time with family, read Scripture, and pray, or simply daydream, sleep, and take a walk. Experience time that is "set apart," and see whether your heart feels different.

**Practice Two: Three Bedtime "Unloading" Sentences.** Every night before bed, lie down and say three things: "Lord, I hand these three things over to You. Lord, I am resting. Lord, see You tomorrow." Then go to sleep, and do not think about them anymore. After a week, count how many minutes it takes you to fall asleep.
