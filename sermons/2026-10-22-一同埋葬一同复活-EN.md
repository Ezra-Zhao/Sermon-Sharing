## Summary (about 100 words)

Baptism is not a procedure for joining the church; it is a sign of being buried with Christ and raised with Him. Paul says that those who are baptized into Christ were baptized into His death and buried with Him, so that we should walk in newness of life. The most important lesson after baptism is to reckon ourselves, day by day, dead to sin and alive to God — to reckon ourselves as new, and to live out that newness.

## (00:00 - 03:00) Opening · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Brothers and sisters, friends and family, today is a joyful day. Some will confess before everyone that Jesus is Lord, and some will go down into the water to be baptized into Christ. This water is not ordinary water, and this step is not a mere formality. For two thousand years the church has welcomed newcomers in this way: a person believes, a person is baptized, and from then on that person has a new standing, a new family, and a new way of life.

Let us first reverently read Romans chapter 6, the passage the church reads most often, and most importantly, when it speaks of baptism:

"Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life." (Rom. 6:3-4, KJV)

"Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord." (Rom. 6:11, KJV)

Let us pray: Lord, thank You that today there are again people willing to confess You openly and follow You. Through Your word, let our brothers and sisters who are being baptized understand the meaning of the water, and let those of us who were baptized years ago revisit the grace of that day. May every step today be according to Your will. In the name of the Lord Jesus, Amen.

Let me first explain today's order of service clearly, so that everyone knows what to expect: the brothers and sisters being baptized have already given their testimonies. This sermon that follows is preached before the baptism is administered — we first listen and understand what baptism means, and then we watch with our own eyes as they go down into the water. After the sermon, the candidates will publicly answer the questions of confession, and then enter the water in turn, and the one baptizing will immerse them in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, immersing the whole body into the water, and then they will come up from the water. Friends and family, please watch carefully: that going down and that rising up is a living picture of today's Scripture passage.

## (03:00 - 10:00) Part One: Baptized into His Death — The Water Is a Tomb, and Also a Sign (6:3-5)

Paul begins by asking: Know ye not? He means: this is something you should already know — baptism is not being baptized into an organization or a ritual; it is being baptized into a Person — Christ Jesus; and it is being baptized into His death.

Baptism, in the original baptizō[1], means to immerse, to plunge into water. When the early church baptized, the whole person was immersed in the water. Why in this way? Because the action itself preaches a sermon: going down into the water is burial; coming up is resurrection. Paul says we were buried with Him through baptism into death. Buried together, in the original synthaptō[2], means to be buried with Him, buried together with Him. The old self, the old way of life, the old account — today, in the water, they receive a public settlement.

Brothers and sisters who are being baptized, listen carefully: baptism does not mean that from now on you will never sin again or never be weak again; baptism means that your old identity has been closed as a case. The old me — who was an enemy of God, bound by sin, and living in darkness — through faith and through baptism, has been crucified with Christ, has died with Him, and has been buried with Him. The moment the water goes over your head is a witness before everyone: this person's past, of every kind, is reckoned to the account of the old self; from now on, he belongs to Christ.

That is also why baptism must not be taken carelessly, and must not be treated as a way of giving an account to one's family or obtaining a charm for peace. If the water were only a ritual for peace, Paul would not have used such weighty words as death and burial. He used them precisely so that we would take this seriously: before you go down into the water today, ask yourself in your heart — Do I believe that Jesus is the Son of God, who died for my sins and rose again? Am I willing to leave my old way of life in the water and follow Him from now on? The questions of confession in a moment ask exactly this. Your nod is not given for the pastor to see; it is a witness made to the Lord and to everyone present.

And after being buried together, there must necessarily be being raised together. Paul says: if we have been united with Him in the likeness of His death, we shall also be united with Him in the likeness of His resurrection. Going down into the water is not the end; coming out of the water is the beginning of a new story.

The truth of being buried together also carries a very practical reminder: once buried, do not dig the old self up again. Among us there are people who have believed in the Lord for many years, yet who always love to turn over old accounts — turning over their own old accounts in inferiority, and turning over others' old accounts in judgment. Here Paul states the matter decisively: the old self has been crucified and buried together with Christ. The church, when it looks at a baptized person, must look at them as a new person; and the baptized person, when looking at himself, must also look at himself as a new person. This is not pretending that the past never happened. Past wounds, past debts, and past consequences — what must be acknowledged should be acknowledged, what must be made up should be made up, and apologies owed to others should, after rising up, be made one by one. But the column of identity has already changed: no longer regarding oneself as a hopeless sinner, but as a child of God and a disciple of Christ. In a moment, when the water goes over your head, say a formal farewell in your heart to the old self: the person who lived by lying, the person bound by addiction, the person whose heart was filled with bitterness — today they are buried here, and will not be brought out of the water. After you come out, if old habits come to pull at you again, remind yourself: I have already been buried once; I do not need to go back and live in the tomb.

## (10:00 - 17:00) Part Two: The Pattern of New Life — Those Who Rise from the Water Walk Differently (6:4b - 6:8)

Paul states the purpose of baptism very concretely: it is so that we should walk in newness of life, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father.

Newness of life, in the original kainotēs zōēs[3], means a life of a new kind, an entirely new quality of life. Kainotēs is not an old thing patched up and refurbished; it is new in texture — a newly created person, a new person's way of living. Christ rose from the dead, and His life overcame death; we rise from the water to live out precisely these days that carry the scent of resurrection: "in every step we take" does not refer to the two hours on Sunday, but to ordinary days — walking, speaking, working, and treating others — all having a new pattern.

What does the new pattern include? In this chapter Paul makes it very clear. First, no longer being a slave to sin. He says our old self was crucified with Him, so that the body of sin might be done away with, and we should no longer serve sin. Before baptism, whatever sin said, we did, with no control over ourselves; after baptism, when sin knocks at the door again, we have the standing and the ability to say no — not because I have suddenly become strong, but because my Master has changed. Second, believing that we live together with Him. If we died with Christ, we believe we shall also live with Him. This hope is not only for the future; it is also for today: every morning when you get up, you can remind yourself — I am a person united with the risen Lord, and He is living this day together with me.

Before the baptism, I want to remind you of an honest word: when you rise from the water, you will not immediately become a person with a different temper and different habits. Old habits will still come back to pull at you, old friends will still come to invite you, and temptations will be no fewer. The pattern of new life means the direction is set and the identity is set, and then it grows day by day. Just as on the day a baby is born, its life is complete, yet walking and speaking must be learned slowly. The church is the home that accompanies you as you learn to walk. We will all accompany you. When you fall, do not hide; get up and come home.

The pattern of new life also has a mark that cannot be hidden: treating other people differently. Before, you cared only for yourself; after believing in the Lord, you begin to learn to care for others: your family first receives your good temper, your colleagues first receive your honesty, and new friends at church first receive your greeting. The resurrection life has a fragrance; it cannot be hidden. Some brothers and sisters also worry: I believe, but my family does not yet believe; when I am baptized and go home, will life become harder? There may be difficulties, but please remember: your lesson is not to go home and lecture your family with grand doctrines, but to live out the new pattern: get up a little earlier, do a little more housework, argue back a little less, and show a little more consideration. When family members look at a person, they do not first look at how many Bible verses you have memorized; they first look at whether you as a person have changed. When they ask you why you are different from before, then slowly tell them about today and about the Lord Jesus. Such a witness carries more weight than ten sermons, and it is precisely why we invite friends and family to attend today: let them first see, and then hear.

## (17:00 - 21:00) Part Three: Reckon Yourself as New — The Daily Lesson after Baptism (6:9-11)

At the end of this passage, Paul gives the baptized a verb to use every day: reckon.

He says: "Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord." To reckon oneself, in the original logizomai[4], means to count as, to regard as, to reckon according to the facts. It is an accounting word, like an accountant settling an account entry by entry: not by feeling, but by regarding as true the facts already recorded in the account. What are the facts? Christ died, rose again, and dies no more; death no longer rules over Him. In baptism I have been united with Him, so toward sin, my account is already settled; toward God, I am alive, and a new account has already been opened.

Why reckon every day? Because feelings often run contrary to the facts. After baptism there will be a day when you feel you are utterly terrible, not at all like a Christian; temptation will also whisper in your ear: Look at you, you are still the same as before; going down into the water was nothing more than taking a bath. At that time, do not argue with your feelings; settle the account: I have been baptized into Christ — this is a fact; the old self has been crucified and buried together with Him — this is a fact; I am dead to sin and alive to God — this is a fact personally recorded by the Lord. Stand according to the facts, and do not drift according to moods — this is a lesson from which a baptized person will benefit for a lifetime.

Reckoning will also be put into practice in hands and feet. In the next passage Paul says: do not present your members to sin as instruments of unrighteousness, but present them to God as instruments of righteousness. In other words: what the eyes look at, what the hands do, where the feet go, what the mouth says — all begin to be governed by the new Master. This is not a miserable law; this is the natural way of living of a new identity: I am new, therefore I live that way; it is not that I live that way, and only then am I worthy to be new. The order must never be reversed.

Reckoning yourself as new must also be reckoned onto a very specific battlefield: your private life. A person finds it easy to be devout in front of others; the difficulty is when no one sees: what you look at on your phone, what you think about late at night, whether you keep a bottom line when making a decision alone. The reckoning of logizomai must be reckoned into these places. Every time you stand by the facts in a hidden place and refuse the greeting of the old self, your new life becomes one degree more solid; every time you indulge yourself, you also need not despair: return before the cross, confess your sin, settle the account anew, and rise again. In the history of the church, those who truly went far were not those who never fell, but those who, after falling, knew to return to settle the account, return to confess, and return to offer themselves anew. You who are being baptized, engrave this sentence in your heart today: I am dead to sin, and alive to God. These words are enough for you to use for a lifetime.

## (21:00 - 24:00) Application: Each Person Present Receives One Word

To those being baptized. In a moment you will go down into the water, and I leave you three sentences. First, remember today firmly: write down the date, the people who accompanied you, and the words you spoke to the Lord in your heart; in days of weakness ahead, this day is your anchor. Second, from tomorrow, live a regular spiritual life: begin reading the Bible from the Gospels, begin prayer from the words of your heart, return to church on Sundays, be sure to join a small group, and do not be a loner. Third, when you fall, do not hide. Baptism is not a graduation certificate; it is a birth certificate. What child learns to walk without falling? When you fall, admit your fault, get up, and keep walking. The church is your home, and we are all here waiting for you and accompanying you.

To the brothers and sisters accompanying those being baptized. Among you, some brought them to church, and some studied the Bible with them. You share in today's joy, and your companionship will be even more needed in the days ahead. Please be their spiritual family in their first year: more greetings, less judgment; more invitations, less doing things in their place. When you see them absent, make a phone call; when you see them in difficulty, pray first, then reach out a hand. Whether a newcomer walks steadily or not depends in large part on the companionship of older believers.

To friends and family observing. Thank you for coming today. You may not yet believe, or you may still be thinking it over. I will not compel you; I only ask you to watch the next scene carefully: a person publicly confesses that he needs a Savior, publicly leaves his old life in the water, and publicly rises from the water — this is not superstition; this is the witness of a person's life being turned around. If in your heart you also long for a new beginning, after the service you are welcome to talk with us, or to begin by reading the Gospel of Mark. The Lord Jesus said that whoever believes and is baptized will be saved; this door is open for you today as well. There is no need to hurry, but please do not close it.

After baptism, there are also two gifts the church will entrust to you; please treasure them. The first is a spiritual home. From today, the way you are addressed in the church has changed: we call you brother, we call you sister — this is not politeness; this is a real relationship: sharing hardship together, sharing joy together, reminding each other of faults, and walking the road together. Do not be a guest who only appears on Sundays; be a member of this family, and slowly find the place where you can serve, even if you begin with setting out chairs and welcoming newcomers. The second is the Lord's Table. In the future, when the church observes the Lord's Supper, you will come to the Lord's Table in your identity as a baptized disciple, remembering His death, examining your own heart, and waiting together with all the saints for His coming again. The Lord's Supper will remind you again and again of today's reckoning: whose person am I, and to whom am I living? Baptism happens once; the Lord's Supper is observed often; the lesson in the water must be revisited again and again at the Table throughout a lifetime. May these two gifts accompany you on the long road, so that your new life has roots, companions, and food, and will not be blown away in the wind.

## (24:00 - 25:00) Conclusion · Prayer of Response

Everyone, to be baptized into Christ is to be baptized into His death, to be buried with Him, and to be united with Him in the likeness of His resurrection, walking in newness of life in every step we take. From today, our brothers and sisters who are baptized must reckon themselves, day by day, dead to sin and alive to God. We who are present are witnesses, and we are also family.

Let us pray together: Lord Jesus, You died for us and rose for us, and Your grace has come to this sanctuary again today. We thank You for our brothers and sisters who are being baptized. Through the burial in the water and the rising up, let the pattern of new life become a reality in them. Guard the steps of their whole lives: lift them up when they fall, lead them back when they are lost, and rejoice with them when they are joyful. We also pray for the friends and family observing; may You personally work in their hearts. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.

After the prayer, please let those being baptized come forward and publicly answer the questions of confession; we will then administer baptism by immersion.

## Endnotes

[1] baptizō: Greek βαπτίζω (G907), to immerse, to plunge in, to baptize; Rom. 6:3, "were baptized into Jesus Christ"; the meaning is according to BDAG. This manuscript, according to the early church's form of baptism by full immersion, explains the symbolic meaning of going down into the water as burial and coming up as resurrection, and notes this here expressly.

[2] synthaptō: Greek συνθάπτω (G4916), to be buried with, to be buried together; Rom. 6:4, "we are buried with him"; the meaning is according to BDAG. This word appears in the New Testament only here and in Col. 2:12; this manuscript accordingly emphasizes the depth of the baptized person's union with Christ.

[3] kainotēs zōēs: Greek καινότης ζωῆς, kainotēs (καινότης, G2538, new in quality, an entirely new state) plus zōēs (ζωῆς, from zōē, G2222, life), that is, life of a new kind; Rom. 6:4, "we also should walk in newness of life"; the meaning is according to BDAG. The distinction in this manuscript between being new and refurbishing the old is a homiletical exposition based on the meaning of the kainos word family.

[4] logizomai: Greek λογίζομαι (G3049), to count as, to regard as, to reckon according to an account; Rom. 6:11, "reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed... but alive"; this is the word rendered "reckon yourselves" here; the meaning is according to BDAG. The teaching in this manuscript of settling the account by the facts and not by feelings is a homiletical exposition based on the accounting usage of this word.

## Devotional Extract

The day of baptism is easy to remember; the days after baptism are the hard part. Paul leaves the baptized with one verb: reckon. Reckon yourself dead to sin, and alive to God. Feelings can deceive you: some days you feel far from God, some days an old habit returns and you wonder whether you have really changed, and in private, when no one sees, the old self knocks again. At such times, do not draw conclusions from feelings; settle the account by the facts — Christ died and rose again, and death no longer rules over Him; you have been united with Him; the old account is closed and a new one opened; the old self has been buried with Him, and you need not go back to live in the tomb. Today, find a quiet moment and work through that account carefully: what are three evidences that you now belong to Christ, which hand, which word, which step will you offer to God today as an instrument of righteousness, and what old matter must be confessed to God and made right with others? Reckon it clearly, and then live and act by it. The new identity comes first, the new walk follows; when you fall, return, confess, and reckon again; come back to this home, the church, and do not hide away alone. God will walk with you as you grow, day by day, into the likeness of His Son, until your every step carries the fragrance of resurrection life.
