# Nothing New Under the Sun: Raising Children

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：养育子女».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of raising children

## Summary

Children are a heritage from the LORD, yet raising them is the hardest to get right in measure: control too much and you provoke your children to wrath; indulge them and you lose the boundaries. Through four bad examples and one good example in the Bible — Laban, Eli, Rebekah, David, and Timothy's grandmother Lois and mother Eunice — this chapter sees clearly the root of improper discipline: keep a balance between love and boundaries, and bring children up in the training of the Lord.

## I. Children Are a Heritage from the LORD

The key Scripture of this chapter is only one sentence, yet it speaks clearly of both the parents' responsibility and the boundaries:

> "And you, fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but bring them up in the training and admonition of the Lord." (Eph. 6:4)

"Bring them up" in Greek is **ektrephō (ἐκτρέφω)** (original text ἐκτρέφω, transliterated ektrephō; Strong G1625; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to nourish, to bring up to maturity"). Bringing up is first feeding — raising a child from childhood to adulthood; but Paul immediately limits the direction: in the training and admonition of the Lord. The child in the parents' hands first has an identity:

> "Behold, children are a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb is a reward. Like arrows in the hand of a warrior, so are the children of one's youth." (Ps. 127:3-4)

Children are a heritage, a reward, arrows — placed by God in the parents' hands, to be shot toward the place He has appointed. Parents are stewards, not owners. When Paul wrote to the church in Thessalonica, he used two images of parents: on one side gentleness, on the other side exhortation:

> "But we were gentle among you, just as a nursing mother cherishes her own children." (1 Thess. 2:7)

> "As you know how we exhorted, and comforted, and charged every one of you, as a father does his own children, that you would walk worthy of God who calls you into His own kingdom and glory." (1 Thess. 2:11-12)

A mother's gentleness and a father's exhortation are two sides of the same upbringing. The four bad examples below have each lost one side, or lost both sides.

## II. Four Bad Examples

**The first type, the authoritarian type: Laban.** Rachel's father Laban is the representative of the authoritarian father (Gen. 29:1–31:55). He used his own authority to control the decisions and actions of his family and relatives — his children's marriages, work, staying or leaving, all had to pass through him. The problem with authoritarian parents is not that they have no boundaries, but that the boundaries serve only to consolidate their own control. The apostle's reminder speaks precisely to such fathers:

> "Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged." (Col. 3:21)

**The second type, the indulgent type: Eli.** Eli was a good man, the spiritual leader of Israel, holding both the offices of judge and priest (1 Sam. chapters 2–4). Yet he was an incapable father who doted on and indulged his children. He neither restrained his sons' rebellious behavior nor showed them the image of God. In order to maintain an atmosphere of harmony, he gave up his own authority and raised his children into people with no sense of responsibility, who despised God and God's law. The words of Proverbs are unsparing:

> "He who spares his rod hates his son, but he who loves him disciplines him promptly." (Prov. 13:24)

Indulgence is not love. Proverbs says that parents who cannot bear to discipline — that is hating him.

**The third type, the dependent type: Rebekah.** Rebekah doted greatly on her younger son Jacob, trying to play the role of God in his life (Gen. 27:2-17, 41-45). She felt she not only had to protect her younger son, but also had to make all important decisions for him. Because she did not trust God, Rebekah planned and manipulated Jacob to deceive his father in order to obtain God's blessing. This web of love between a very controlling mother and a passive son ultimately plunged this family into hatred and division. The prophet's words speak precisely to this kind of relying on man and manipulating people:

> "Thus says the LORD: 'Cursed is the man who trusts in man and makes flesh his strength, whose heart departs from the LORD!'" (Jer. 17:5)

When parents make decisions in God's place, that is not trusting God — even if the motive is love for the child.

**The fourth type, the distant type: David.** King David achieved great success on the battlefield, but unfortunately was very incompetent in his family (2 Sam. chapters 13–15; 1 Kings 1:5-6). In fact, he did not take up responsibility for his many children. Evidently, David spent his energy and time on "handling official business." In the Bible one can see his children had obvious provocative and criminal behavior, yet one cannot see him making any rules for it. When Paul speaks of the qualifications of church leaders, he looks first at his home:

> "One who rules his own house well, having his children in submission with all reverence." (1 Tim. 3:4)

However successful the career, if the children at home are disciplined by no one, that success is missing a corner.

## III. One Good Example: Developmental Parents

Opposite the four bad examples are developmental parents — Timothy's grandmother Lois and mother Eunice (2 Tim. 1:5-7; 3:14-15).

Timothy was raised to adulthood by a godly mother and grandmother. Lois and Eunice were Timothy's godly examples; their faith inspired Timothy's love and cultivated his character of self-discipline. When Timothy was still a child, they taught him with the Bible, and ultimately led him to faith in the Lord Jesus Christ. Because Timothy became a church leader when he was young, he was deeply loved by the apostle Paul. Paul remembered this inheritance of faith in this way:

> "When I call to remembrance the genuine faith that is in you, which dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice, and I am persuaded is in you also." (2 Tim. 1:5)

And he reminded him not to lose what he had learned from childhood:

> "And that from childhood you have known the Holy Scriptures, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus." (2 Tim. 3:15)

What developmental parents do, put simply, is not complicated: first have genuine faith themselves, then teach their children with the Bible, and shape their children by example. Not controlling, not indulging, not replacing God, and not absent — but developing what God has placed inside the child. Luke's record of the Lord Jesus' own growth uses a sentence that can serve as the goal of all upbringing:

> "And the Child grew and became strong in spirit, filled with wisdom; and the grace of God was upon Him." (Luke 2:40)

## IV. The Root Cause of Improper Discipline

The four bad examples, traced to the root, are two wrong understandings.

**Permissive parents** think like this: "What my child needs is love. Setting all kinds of rules for my child will produce bitterness in his heart, and then I will lose my child's love." Eli took this road.

**Overbearing parents** think like this: "What my child needs is strict limits and norms. Allowing them independence will make them self-centered and rebellious, and then I will lose control of my child." Laban-style control has its root precisely here.

The right understanding, as the original manuscript writes it very steadily, is: "Even if our relationship will have some tension, I will consistently keep a balance between love and boundaries, and through my way of life teach my child the attributes of God."

Love and boundaries are not a choice between two. Love without boundaries is indulgence; boundaries without love is overbearingness. The parents' own way of life is the child's first lesson in knowing God. The promise of Proverbs is placed here:

> "Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it." (Prov. 22:6)

"Train up" in Hebrew is **ḥānak (חָנַךְ)** (original text חָנַךְ, transliterated ḥānak; Strong H2596; according to the *BDB Lexicon*, "to train, to teach, to make one submit to the right way"). Training is a process of training, with a direction, from beginning to end — causing him to walk in the way he should go.

## V. Steps to Resolution: Memorization and Repeated Reading

The steps to resolution given in the original manuscript are very plain: store two passages of Scripture in the heart.

The one to memorize is the key Scripture of this chapter (Eph. 6:4): "And you, fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but bring them up in the training and admonition of the Lord."

The one to read repeatedly is 1 Thessalonians chapter 2, verses 7 to 12 — the passage where Paul treats believers as a father and as a mother. Among them, verse 8 fully expresses the heart of upbringing:

> "So, affectionately longing for you, we were well pleased to impart to you not only the gospel of God, but also our own lives, because you had become dear to us." (1 Thess. 2:8)

Parents toward their children should be exactly like this: give the gospel, and also give their own lives; have boundaries, and also have love that does not count the cost. Add to this Psalm 127:3-4 — children are a heritage, a reward, arrows in the hand of a warrior — every time parents read it, they remember anew: the child is God's, and they themselves are stewards.

## Conclusion

Laban's control, Eli's indulgence, Rebekah's manipulation, David's absence — none of these four roads leads through. The road of Lois and Eunice looks the slowest: teaching with the Bible from childhood, using faith as an example, raising year after year. The result is the genuine faith in Timothy's heart. There is no shortcut in raising children; there is only the balance between love and boundaries, and the faith the parents live out first.

## Prayer of Response

Heavenly Father, children are a heritage given by You; we are only stewards in Your hand. Forgive us: when we should have disciplined, we indulged; when we should have let go, we controlled; when we were busy with our own affairs, we were absent. Help us, like Lois and Eunice, first have genuine faith, then teach our children with Your word, stand firm between love and boundaries, not provoke our children to wrath, and bring them up in Your training and admonition. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.
