## Content

What is the order of God's saving? God's way of salvation is not only a merciful bestowing of grace; there is also a thorough replacement. It is not only construction; there is also demolition! God must on the one hand remove the old, and on the other hand build the new. On the one hand He removes Adam; on the other hand He builds Christ. His principle in working is that new cloth is not put on an old garment, and new wine is not put into old wineskins. Not only so, God also pays attention to order. The God who carries out salvation has steps in salvation! Let us now learn to understand the procedure God has arranged, so that we may know that He does not allow the order to be confused, nor does He permit the primary and the secondary to be reversed.

First cut off, then grafted in (Ephesians). "You were cut off from the olive tree which is by nature wild" (Rom. 11:24, NKJV). [Original text partially illegible.] That is, it refers to breaking with Adam; [and grafting] refers to union with Christ. Our relationship with Adam is innate and passive; our relationship with Christ is acquired — and it becomes active! This is a rather solemn fact! For you cannot refuse the corruption you inherited from Adam; but if you are unwilling to obtain union with Christ, that is your responsibility today — how can you evade it?

First renewed, then transformed (James). "and be renewed in the spirit of your mind" (Eph. 4:23, NKJV); "we... are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord" (2 Cor. 3:18, NKJV). We must be renewed by God before we can be transformed by God! There is something very strange: the good can become bad, but the bad cannot become good. [Original text partially illegible.] [God] first gives us His own life, and then [transforms us into] His image. Without first being renewed, we can never be transformed! For salvation does not merely change character; salvation renews the essence.

First born, then made (1 John). "Everyone who... has been born of God, because he is born of God" (cf. 1 John 3:9; 5:1, NKJV) (1 Peter). "For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus" (Eph. 2:10, NKJV). God's begetting us is the inward work; God's making us is the outward work. [One] is rebirth; [the other] is new creation. Because of [the former, we become] His children; because of [the latter, we become] His children [in manifestation]; the former is receiving Christ, the latter is putting on Christ. God's work is from the inside out. Without Christ's life, merely learning Christ's character is impossible — and very dangerous.

First saved, then taught (1 John). "But when the kindness and the love of God our Savior toward man appeared, ... He saved us" (Titus 3:4-5, NKJV). "you have heard Him and have been taught by Him" (Eph. 4:21, NKJV). The work of saving comes first; the work of teaching comes after. This is God's order! Without [being saved, teaching] is of no use; having [been saved], one still needs [to be led/taught]! Through [salvation] God gives us life; through [teaching] God instructs our living. Without [the former], we have no experience of grace; without [the latter], we have no knowledge of salvation. We must first obtain, and we must also afterward understand (2 John).

Homework questions: State the four sequences, first-and-after, in God's saving us. What is the principle of God's work? To what do the construction and demolition in God's way of salvation refer? How do our relationship with Adam and our relationship with Christ differ? What is the difference between being renewed and being transformed? What is the difference between God's begetting us again and His making us anew? What is the difference between the Lord saving us and the Lord teaching us?
