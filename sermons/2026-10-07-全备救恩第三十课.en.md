## Content

The security of salvation. Can one still perish after being saved? [Original text partially illegible.] [Salvation and] perdition are two relative terms, and also two opposite facts. To be saved is not to perish; to perish is not to be saved. According to the Bible, once saved, one will not perish; one who perishes has never been saved. The Bible never has a single meaning that a saved person will still perish. When the Bible speaks of the "son of perdition," it means a person who has never been saved by faith. The words in the Bible about perdition all refer to false believers. A person who truly belongs to the Lord — please hear the Lord say: "My sheep... shall never perish" (John 10:27-28, NKJV).

The Lord's choosing is firm and unchanging (2 Peter). "You did not choose Me, but I chose you" (John 15:16, NKJV) (2 Thessalonians). The story of salvation is not that we sought the Lord, but that the Lord sought us; not that we loved God, but that God first loved us; not that we chose Him, but that He chose us. Think of it: since our Lord loves us, can He not love us to the end? Since He chose us, can He still let us perish? Could He have chosen wrongly?

The Lord's promises are all "Yes" (2 Corinthians). "And everyone who comes to Me I will by no means cast out. ... All that the Father gives Me will come to Me" (John 6:37, NKJV). This is the Lord's own promise! Everyone who comes to Him, He will by no means cast out. The Lord's power is our guarantee; the Lord's faithfulness is our shield. He says He will by no means cast out — you will never find a reason for Him to cast out!

The Lord's redemption is eternally complete (Hebrews). "He, having become perfect, became the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him" (Heb. 5:9, NKJV). As Christ's salvation is eternal salvation, so our being saved is also eternal salvation. He Himself became for us the source of eternal salvation, or, the basis of eternal salvation. "The source of eternal salvation" can be better translated as "the cause of eternal salvation"; "the reason for eternal salvation" can be better understood as "the ground of eternal salvation"; "the cause of eternal salvation" — what doubt do you still have?

The Lord's new covenant is an everlasting covenant (Hebrews). "For this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many for the remission of sins" (Matt. 26:28, NKJV). The Lord made a new covenant with us. He says: "I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins... I will remember no more" (Heb. 8:12, NKJV). And this covenant was made with His own blood. Even if we break the covenant, He is still faithful, for He cannot deny Himself.

The Lord's intercession has already been heard (John). "Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them" (Heb. 7:25, NKJV). Our Lord is the ever-living Lord, so His salvation is also eternal salvation. As He is willing to love us to the end, so He is able to save us to the uttermost. For He not only gives us preservation on earth; He also gives us intercession in heaven. Think: what guarantee could be more secure than these?

Homework questions: Can a saved person still perish? What evidence is there that a saved person will not perish? Will the all-knowing Lord make a mistake in His choosing? Will the Lord cast out a person who comes to Him? What does "the author of eternal salvation" mean? Since the Lord made the new covenant with His blood, will He break faith? What is meant by saving to the uttermost?
