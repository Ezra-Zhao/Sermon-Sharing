# Nothing New Under the Sun: Dependence

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：依赖».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of dependence

## Summary

Everyone needs love and security, and so it is very easy to place our reliance in the wrong place — on people: relying on a spouse, on friends, on children, on a boss. This chapter distinguishes two kinds of misplaced dependence — the dependent type, who treats people as the source of love and peace; and the codependent type, who proves his own worth by meeting all of other people's needs. There is only one way out: trust in the LORD alone, each bearing his own load, and also bearing one another's burdens.

## I. Depending on People Is Misplaced Dependence

First, answer the most fundamental question: What is wrong with depending on others?

Many people rely on others in life. This is a misplaced dependence, because God wants us to trust Him completely. Paul exhorts believers:

> "And that you also aspire to lead a quiet life, to mind your own business, and to work with your own hands, as we commanded you, that you may walk properly toward those who are outside, and that you may lack nothing." (1 Thess. 4:11-12)

"Lacking nothing" does not come from clinging to a certain person; it comes from minding one's own business, working with one's own hands, and trusting in peace. Human need itself is not sin; need makes us understand that only by relying on the LORD can we obtain love and peace. If we hand our need over to people, people become our god; if we bring our need before God, need becomes our path to draw near to Him.

## II. Marriage: The Difference Between Codependence and a Healthy Marriage

Codependence is most common in marriage. A codependent marriage looks like this: one party, in order to make the spouse dependent on the relationship, tries by every means to meet all of the other party's needs; one party conceals his own attitude and indulges the other party's bad behavior. On the surface it is giving; in reality it is control — I satisfy you, so you cannot leave me.

A healthy and harmonious marriage is the opposite: husband and wife encourage each other to rely on God and value each other's strengths. A spouse is a fellow traveler, not anyone's god. The first of the Ten Commandments holds just as true in marriage:

> "You shall have no other gods before Me." (Exod. 20:3)

Treating a spouse as a god to rely on is a burden to the spouse and bondage to oneself; putting God back in God's place, husband and wife can instead love each other with ease.

## III. Friendship: Replacing Christ with a Friend, or Strengthening One Another in the Lord

If two friends rely on each other, is their friendship codependent? It depends on what kind of reliance it is.

A codependent friendship has several marks: one party is in a weak position, the other in a strong position; one party may become jealous of the other party's relationships with others; one party may replace Christ with the friend. Such a friendship looks intimate, but in reality the two control each other.

A healthy and harmonious friendship is: both parties relate as equals; both seek and cherish other friendships; and they strengthen each other in the Lord. There is an example in the Bible — Jonathan to David:

> "Then Jonathan, Saul's son, arose and went to David in the woods and strengthened his hand in God." (1 Sam. 23:16)

Notice what Jonathan did: he did not make David rely on himself, but made David rely on God. A true friend brings a person before God, rather than tying a person to himself.

## IV. Children: Guiding Them No Longer to Be Overly Dependent

How can we guide children no longer to be overly dependent on their parents? The original manuscript gives five steps:

First, teach children to pray about all kinds of decisions and to rely on God's will. Second, train them earlier, and allow children to make their own choices. Third, praise children for wise decisions. Fourth, allow children to learn lessons from wrong decisions. Fifth, encourage children to make choices about future matters.

Behind these five steps is this Scripture:

> "Train up a child in the way he should go, and when he is old he will not depart from it." (Prov. 22:6)

The goal of training is that the child can eventually rely on God himself. If parents never let go of anything, what the child relies on when he grows up will not be God, but his parents — that is not love; it is prolonging dependence.

## V. The Workplace: Do Not Become Your Boss's Steward

How can we avoid codependent relationships at work? The reminders in the original manuscript are very specific: Do not try to become your boss's steward; do not become a person who wants to meddle in everything; do not be controlled by manipulation and fear; do not let working late become a habit; do not be afraid to say "no."

Codependence in the workplace often wears the clothing of "loyalty" and "competence": using endless giving to exchange for an irreplaceable position. The antidote in Proverbs is:

> "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths." (Prov. 3:5-6)

To acknowledge Him includes acknowledging Him at work — my worth, security, and future are in His hands, not in whether I can make my boss unable to do without me.

## VI. Root Cause: Two Wrong Understandings

The root cause of this lesson, in the original manuscript, falls into two types.

**The dependent type.** The wrong understanding is: "I need to rely on a person with a strong personality; he can give me love and a sense of security." The right understanding is: Although God often shows His love through other people, He does not want me to rely on people; my need makes me understand that only by relying on the LORD can I obtain love and peace.

> "When he calls upon Me, I will answer him; I will be with him in trouble; I will deliver him and honor him." (Ps. 91:15)

**The codependent type.** The wrong understanding is: "Bearing responsibility for other people's needs lets me experience true personal worth." The right understanding is: If I try to meet all of other people's needs, then I am taking God's place and assuming His role; my self-worth cannot be realized by pleasing people, but is realized by being pleasing to God.

> "Therefore, whether we are present or absent, we make it our aim to be well pleasing to Him." (2 Cor. 5:9)

One type treats people as god to rely on; the other treats oneself as god to be used. Both types err in the same place: God's position has been moved.

## VII. Steps to Resolution: Leave Codependence and Rely on Christ Alone

The first step is to memorize Jeremiah 17:5-7, and let these words first correct our intuition:

> "Thus says the LORD: 'Cursed is the man who trusts in man and makes flesh his strength, whose heart departs from the LORD. For he shall be like a shrub in the desert, and shall not see when good comes, but shall inhabit the parched places in the wilderness, in a salt land which is not inhabited. Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, and whose hope is the LORD.'" (Jer. 17:5-7)

The key Scripture of this chapter is precisely verse 7. "Trust" in Hebrew is **bāṭaḥ (בָּטַח)** (original text בָּטַח, transliterated bāṭaḥ; Strong H982; according to the *BDB Lexicon*, "to trust, to rely on, to entrust oneself in security"). With the same word, the prophet contrasts two kinds of life: the one who trusts in man dwells in a salt land; the one who trusts in the LORD is blessed.

The second step is to read repeatedly Galatians 6:1-5, distinguishing "burdens" from "load":

> "Brethren, if a man is overtaken in any trespass, you who are spiritual restore such a one in a spirit of gentleness, considering yourself lest you also be tempted. Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ. For if anyone thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, he deceives himself. But let each one examine his own work, and then he will have rejoicing in himself alone, and not in another. For each one shall bear his own load." (Gal. 6:1-5)

Here two words must be distinguished. "Burdens" is **baros (βάρος)** (original text βάρος, transliterated baros; Strong G922; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "a heavy weight, a burden that presses one down so he can hardly breathe") — the heavy load of one who has fallen; brothers and sisters are to bear it for one another, restoring him with a gentle heart. "Load" is **phortion (φορτίον)** (original text φορτίον, transliterated phortion; Strong G5413; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "each one's proper load, responsibility") — each one's proper responsibility, which he must bear himself; no one can bear it in his place. Snatching another's load to carry it oneself is codependence; pushing one's own burden onto another to carry is dependence. The picture of a healthy community is: burdens borne for one another, loads borne by each one himself.

## Conclusion

This lesson on dependence is, in the end, a matter of order: God sitting in God's place, and man standing in man's place. Marriage, friendship, parent-child relations, and the workplace — all four relationships must return to this order. "Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, and whose hope is the LORD" — this is not a slogan; it is an account the prophet has calculated for us, using the two outcomes of desert and blessing.

## Prayer of Response

O LORD, we confess that we often trust in the arm of flesh, and often rush to do what only You can do. Teach us to trust in You with all our hearts, and to lean neither on our own understanding nor on any person. Let us, in marriage, among friends, with our children, and at work, put You back in Your place. Blessed are those who trust in You; let us dwell in this blessing. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.

1. This chapter is compiled from Q&A-style material in the 2019 lecture PPT (slides 101–108): the question on dependence, the contrast between codependent and healthy marriage and friendship, the five steps for guiding children, the five reminders for the workplace, and the two types of root cause and the Scriptures for the steps to resolution all come from the original manuscript, with no new cases added.
2. "Burdens" in Galatians 6:2 (baros, G922) and "load" in 6:5 (phortion, G5413) are two different words in the original text, consistent with the original manuscript's reading that assigns verses 1–2 of chapter 6 to "burdens" and verses 3–5 to "load."
3. "Trusts" in Jeremiah 17:7, in Hebrew bāṭaḥ (בָּטַח, H982), in the Old Testament mostly refers to trusting and entrusting oneself in security, forming a direct contrast with "trusts in man and makes flesh his strength" in 17:5.
