Brothers and sisters, peace to you all! I am so glad to gather with you today in the house of the Lord to explore together the manifesto of the Kingdom of Heaven given to us by the Lord Jesus in the "Sermon on the Mount."

Please turn your Bibles to Matthew chapter 5, verses 4 to 5; let us read together:

"Blessed are those who mourn, For they shall be comforted. Blessed are the meek, For they shall inherit the earth." (Matt. 5:4–5)

Brothers and sisters, to be honest, if you just walked into church today, or if you stand in today's society with its brutally fierce competition where everyone is caught in rat races, hearing these two sentences for the first time, you would think this is completely "counterintuitive." [Emphasize]

How does this world teach us? People of the world will tell you: "Only those who are wealthy and happy are blessed! Only those with power and influence are blessed! You must be ruthless enough, tough enough, and domineering enough to seize the market and snatch up resources!"

Yet, the King of the Kingdom of Heaven, the Lord Jesus, spoke from a mountain two thousand years ago to all who followed Him, saying, "No! Blessed are those who mourn; blessed are the meek!" [Pause for 3 seconds]

How does that classic hymn we all know so well, "Amazing Grace," go?

[Singing a cappella from the pulpit / Speaking softly]

"Amazing grace! How sweet the sound that saved a wretch like me! I once was lost, but now am found; was blind, but now I see."

Brothers and sisters, the sweet grace of the Kingdom of Heaven was never prepared for those who think they are formidable; the grace of the Kingdom of Heaven breaks forth precisely from the very moment we acknowledge before God that we are lost and acknowledge that we are utterly helpless!

When speaking about the Beatitudes, Pastor Tong Liu of the River of Life Christian Church in Silicon Valley once said something that cuts right to the heart (original video quote):

"The Beatitudes of the Kingdom of Heaven were never moral commandments given to us by the Lord Jesus, nor are they a law calling us to grit our teeth and endure by our own flesh; the Beatitudes are the DNA of the Kingdom of Heaven naturally flowing forth after the Holy Spirit performs 'life pruning' deep within a person's life. When you know how to weep before God (mourning), and when you learn to yield your strength back to God (meekness), only then will God's mighty power and spiritual inheritance truly break forth!"

Today, through proverbs across the Old and New Testaments (with original-language exegesis), biblical stories, and seven everyday life scenarios we face daily—the elderly, children, mother-in-law and daughter-in-law, husbands and wives, work, business, and health—let us examine together these two world-subverting kingdom characters!

(08:00 - 30:00)
1.1 What Is "Mourning" in the Kingdom of Heaven?
First, let us understand: what is "mourning"?

In Matthew 5:4, the Greek word for "mourn" is Πενθοῦντες (transliteration: Penthoountes / Pai-en-suo-en-tai-si). In ancient Greek, this word refers to an intense grief that cannot be suppressed or hidden, tearing from the deepest place of the heart (like the weeping over the loss of a dearest loved one).

Mourning in the Kingdom of Heaven is by no means a helpless despair over fate, much less lying flat and complaining.

Pastor Tim Keller put it very incisively: "Worldly sorrow is 'weeping over one's own losses,' whereas kingdom mourning is 'weeping before God over one's own sins and the brokenness of this world.'"

And the "comfort" promised by the Lord Jesus to those who mourn is Παρακληθήσονται in Greek (transliteration: Paraklēthēsontai / Pa-la-ke-li-sai-song-tai), which shares the very same root with "the Holy Spirit / Comforter" Paraklētos (Pa-la-ke-li-tuo-si)! It means: "A helper specifically called to stand by your side and step forward to support you!" [Emphasize]

1.2 Golden Scripture Passages and Original Word Analysis


"He who covers his sins will not prosper, But whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "covers" is Mekhasheh (Me-kha-sheh), referring to covering up breaches through rationalization, stubbornness, or acting as if nothing happened; the Hebrew word for "will have mercy" is Yuracham (Yu-ra-kham), derived from the womb (Rechem), referring to a love as deep as a mother's for her unborn child.

Spoken Pulpit Exposition: If a person refuses to soften before God (covers Mekhasheh), his spiritual life will surely be blocked; only when you dare to confess your own brokenness before God will God's love, as deep as a mother's womb (Yuracham), embrace and enfold you.



"A merry heart does good, like medicine, But a broken spirit dries the bones."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "medicine" is Gehah (Ge-hah), meaning healing and restoration; the Hebrew word for "broken" is Neke'ah (Ne-ke-ah), referring to a state of being struck down or crushed.

Spoken Pulpit Exposition: Worldly, hopeless internal friction (Neke'ah) directly destroys your immune system; but mourning in the Kingdom of Heaven means bringing this internal friction before God, pouring it out, and receiving in exchange God's true medicine of joy (Gehah).



"The heart knows its own bitterness, And a stranger does not share its joy."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "bitterness" is Marat (Ma-rat), referring to an extremely bitter, piercing, agonizing pain.

Spoken Pulpit Exposition: Much of the bitterness (Marat) in family, marriage, or physical health cannot even be shared with relatives and friends. But when you weep before God in the secret chamber behind closed doors, no one can take away the joy God gives you!



"For godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death."

Original Word Analysis: The Greek word for "repentance" is Metanoia (Me-ta-noi-a), referring to a complete 180-degree turnaround in the direction of one's life.

Spoken Pulpit Exposition: Feeling sorrow because one's face or pride is hurt is worldly sorrow that produces death; feeling sorrow over having sinned against God brings about a complete turnaround in life's direction (Metanoia)!

"The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit, a broken and a contrite heart—these, O God, You will not despise."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "contrite" is *Nidkeh*, meaning to be thoroughly crushed, pulverized.

Pulpit Spoken Elaboration: God does not want our outward shell of pretended piety; what God wants is a heart willing to be completely crushed (*Nidkeh*) before Him, no longer desperately clinging to face or pride.

1.3 Detailed Introduction of Biblical Examples

David, Israel's greatest king, committed a terribly dreadful sin at the pinnacle of his career: he seized Bathsheba, the wife of his warrior, and to cover up the scandal, he murdered the faithful Uriah by the sword of the enemy. For a whole year, David stubbornly refused to confess his sin, pretending nothing had happened. He wrote in Psalm 32: "When I kept silent, my bones grew old through my groaning all the day long... My vitality was turned into the drought of summer."

It was not until the prophet Nathan confronted him face to face, "You are the man!" that the hypocritical mask over David's heart instantly shattered. As king, he could entirely have put Nathan to death to silence him, but he did not. He knelt in the dust and wept aloud: "I have sinned against the Lord!"

In his secret chamber he penned Psalm 51: "Have mercy upon me, O God, according to Your lovingkindness... The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit, a broken and a contrite heart—these, O God, You will not despise!" David's weeping and mourning were not out of fear of losing his throne, but out of genuine heartbreak over having broken his relationship with God. God valued the brokenness of his heart, forgave his sin, and spared his life!

[Pulpit Micro-Response A Cappella Singing]

"Jesus, keep me near the cross, there a precious fountain; free to all, a healing stream, flows from Calv'ry's mountain." [Pause 3 seconds]

Four days after the Lord Jesus' dear friend Lazarus died of illness, Jesus arrived in Bethany. As soon as Mary, Lazarus's sister, saw Jesus, she fell down at His feet weeping: "Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died." When Jesus saw Mary and the Jews who came with her all weeping, the Bible recorded an immensely stirring sentence: "Jesus wept." (John 11:35)

Jesus already knew He was going to raise Lazarus from the dead; why did He weep? Because as Jesus stood before the tomb, He deeply experienced the profound suffering and tearing that sin and death brought upon mankind! His tears were the heavenly King's deepest empathy and mourning for suffering humanity. Then Jesus cried out with a loud voice: "Lazarus, come forth!" And he who had died came out! Jesus' mourning brought forth the power of resurrection!

1.4 Real-Life Applications (Health, Children, Marriage, Business)
[Pulpit Interactive Question]
"Brothers and sisters, let me ask everyone: When you face a sick family member, a rebellious child, or a broken marriage at home, when was the last time you wept in prayer before God?" [Pause 3 seconds]

A middle-aged brother in our church was diagnosed with advanced cancer. At first, he was intensely angry, losing his temper at his family. Later, in prayer, he wept before God: "Lord, I am afraid, and I also confess that in the past I overspent my body and was too proud!" When he turned from "angry resistance" to "mourning and surrendering before God," the peace of the Holy Spirit came upon him. He cooperated with treatment, maintained a calm mindset, and even the doctors marveled at the miracle of his recovery.

Facing an adolescent child who refuses to go to school and is addicted to the internet, many parents choose shouting, scolding, and beating. One mother stopped shouting out of the flesh; instead, every day before entering her child's room, she first wept and prayed in the secret chamber for the child and for her own impatience. When she laid down her pride before the child and apologized with tears, the child's frozen heart instantly melted, and communication was restored.

A couple was facing divorce due to years of personality clashes; the husband always felt it was the wife's fault. During a prayer meeting, the husband saw his own coldness and selfishness in the marriage for the first time, kneeling on the floor and weeping aloud. After returning home, he embraced his wife and confessed his wrong. This spiritual mourning saved a family on the brink of collapse.

2.1 What Is "Meekness" in the Kingdom of Heaven?
Let us continue to look at verse 5: "Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth."

In Matthew 5:5, the original Greek word for "meek" is *Praus*.

In ancient Greek, *Praus* was used to describe a warhorse that was originally wild and untamable, but after rigorous training, could precisely obey the rider's commands! It still possessed powerful muscles and explosive strength, yet its power was tamed.

Therefore, meekness in the kingdom of heaven is by no means weak or incompetent, but rather "Power Under Control"! [Emphasize]

The Greek for inheriting "the earth" is *Klēronomēsousin tēn gēn*, referring to inheriting true spiritual inheritance and influence.

The hymn "Flowers of the Field" sings:

[Pulpit A Cappella Singing / Gentle Accompaniment]

"Look at the flowers of the field, what clothes do they wear? The birds of the air, where do they seek their food? Our loving heavenly Father cares for them day by day; His grace is boundless and abundant."

It is precisely because the meek deeply trust in the heavenly Father's care that they do not need to rely on their own flesh to seize and contend!

2.2 Golden Scripture Passages and Original Word Analyses

"A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "soft" is *Rakh*, referring to pliable, resilient communication that is free of sharp barbs.

Pulpit Spoken Elaboration: In an argument, meeting hardness with hardness will only cause an explosion; softness (*Rakh*) is like a soft cushion, able to defuse the other party's rage in an instant.

"He who is slow to anger is better than the mighty, and he who rules his spirit than he who takes a city."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "rules" is *Moshēl*, referring to exercising the authority of ruling and governing.

Pulpit Spoken Elaboration: Defeating others is no great achievement; being able to master your own temper and flesh (*Moshēl*)—that is the true hero!

"By long forbearance a ruler is persuaded, and a gentle tongue breaks a bone."

Original Word Analysis: The Hebrew word for "breaks" is *Tishbor*, meaning to shatter the hardest resistance.

Pulpit Spoken Elaboration: "A gentle tongue breaks a bone"—gentleness seems soft, yet it possesses a power that can pierce through the hardest of hearts!

"And a servant of the Lord must not quarrel but be gentle to all... in humility correcting those who are in opposition."

Original Word Analysis: The Greek word for "gentle" is *Ēpion*, referring to being kind, tender, and calm like a nursing mother cherishing her infant.

Pulpit Spoken Elaboration: Arguing out of the flesh may win the argument, but you lose the person; only gentleness like a mother caring for her child (*Ēpion*) can move and touch people's hearts.

"Who is wise and understanding among you? Let him show by good conduct that his works are done in the meekness of wisdom."

Original Word Analysis: The Greek word for "meekness/gentleness" is *Praytēti* (pra-oo-tay-tee), referring to the unity of inner humility and outward gentleness.

Pulpit Spoken Elaboration: "Wisdom" without meekness turns into harsh, caustic criticism; true spiritual wisdom is always accompanied by meekness (*Praytēti*).

2.3 Detailed Presentation of Biblical Examples

Moses was the leader who brought a million Israelites out of Egypt, wielding supreme authority. Yet in Numbers 12, his own sister Miriam and his brother Aaron publicly confronted him and challenged his status because he had married an Ethiopian woman: "Has the Lord indeed spoken only through Moses? Has He not spoken through us also?"

This was an extremely serious provocation. Had it been a worldly king, they would have been arrested long ago. But Scripture writes: "(Now the man Moses was very humble, more than all the men who were on the face of the earth.)" (Num. 12:3). Moses did not use his authority to strike back; he chose silence, entrusting the judgment to God. As a result, God Himself spoke on Moses' behalf from the pillar of cloud and brought punishment upon Miriam. When Aaron pleaded for mercy, Moses did not gloat; instead, he immediately fell facedown and prayed: "Please heal her, O God, I pray!"

Moses possessed immense power, yet he restrained that power in obedience to God—this is the meekness of the kingdom of heaven!


Late that night in the Garden of Gethsemane when He was arrested, the apostle Peter acted on impulse, drew his sword, and cut off the ear of the high priest's servant. Jesus immediately stopped him, saying, "Put your sword in its place, for all who take the sword will perish by the sword. Or do you think that I cannot now pray to My Father, and He will provide Me with more than twelve legions of angels?" (Matt. 26:52–53).

Facing the false witnesses' frame-ups and the scourging, Jesus completely fulfilled the prophecy in Isaiah 53: "He was led as a lamb to the slaughter, and as a sheep before its shearers is silent." With a single word, Jesus could have summoned twelve legions of angels to destroy His enemies, but to accomplish redemption, He restrained His own power in obedience to the Father God.

Jesus went to the cross in meekness, and as a result, He broke the power of death and sin, was exalted to the highest place, and obtained the true inheritance of all the earth!

2.4 Real-Life Applications (Mother-in-law & Daughter-in-law, The Elderly, Work, Business)
Pastor Tong Liu's exact words (video presentation):

"River of Life Christian Church has always emphasized one principle: 'Pray & Obey.' True meekness is daring to surrender control back to God in interpersonal relationships and ministry conflicts. Worldly people think they must fight in the flesh to seize territory (the earth), but God says that only the meek will truly 'inherit the earth'—because God will grant people's trust and spiritual authority to the meek person who does not rely on the flesh, but restrains their own heart!"


A young daughter-in-law, faced with her mother-in-law's faultfinding, used to contradict her to her face, throwing the household into turmoil. Later, she practiced "a soft answer." When her mother-in-law nagged, she no longer argued, but gently said, "Mom, thank you for your concern; I've taken note of it." That evening, she even quietly bought knee warmers for her mother-in-law. Won over by the daughter-in-law's gentleness, the mother-in-law praised her to everyone she met, and the family atmosphere was completely transformed.


Caring for an elderly father suffering from Alzheimer's disease with an eccentric temper, the son taking care of him was often exhausted and felt like losing his temper. In prayer, the son asked the Lord to grant him the meekness to "rule his own spirit." When his father smashed dishes again, the son did not shout; instead, he crouched down and gently said, "Dad, it's okay, we'll just get a new one." This gentleness gave the ailing elderly father a tremendous sense of security.


During a corporate project collaboration, the partner suddenly changed their mind and shirked responsibility. Instead of acting out of the flesh by sending lawyer's letters to wrestle with them, the brother who was the supervisor invited the person in charge to meet. He communicated solutions with an extremely gentle attitude yet with crystal-clear facts (a gentle tongue breaks a bone). Moved by the brother's poise, the other party not only bore the losses, but also added an exclusive cooperation agreement for the following year.


A brother in the clothing wholesale business encountered a customer who demanded an unreasonable return while hurling abusive words. The brother chose to restrain the flesh; he gently processed a full refund for the customer and included a small gift. Six months later, after dealing with other unscrupulous merchants, the customer deeply realized this Christian businessman's integrity and gentleness, and transferred all of his purchasing orders to this brother (inheriting the earth).
