Sermon Title: A Virtuous Woman, a Hidden Hero, a Pioneer in Bi-vocational Ministry
When we read about the virtuous woman in the Bible, most people feel both hope and apprehension: How did she balance her time? How did she grow to be like this? Does such a woman really exist? In fact, many of these doubts or concerns stem from faith. Let us look at a woman in the Bible today—Priscilla.

Several passages in Scripture:
Acts 18:2	And he found a certain Jew named Aquila, born in Pontus, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla (because Claudius had commanded all the Jews to depart from Rome); and he came to them.

Simon Peter is the only apostle who is relatively explicitly recorded as being married, yet the Bible does not mention his wife's name. Here, however, Aquila's wife is specifically mentioned, which shows that in the understanding of later generations, this couple was exceedingly important.

A Brief Overview of Aquila's Life
1. Background of His Birth

Aquila was "born in Pontus," meaning he was born in Pontus, an ancient region located along the Black Sea coast of modern-day northern Turkey (cf. Acts 18:2).

He was a Jew, skilled in tentmaking, which was a common trade at the time.

2. Expulsion from Rome

Aquila and his wife Priscilla originally lived in Rome, Italy, but were forced to leave because Roman Emperor Claudius commanded all Jews to depart (around A.D. 49). They relocated to Corinth, where they met Paul (Acts 18:1–3).

3. Establishing a Relationship with Paul

Because Paul was of the same trade (tentmaking), he stayed with them and worked with them. Because of their faith and ministry, they became spiritual partners.

Afterward, they went together to Ephesus (Acts 18:18–19), continued to serve the church, and established a meeting place in their home (1 Cor. 16:19).

4. Instructing Apollos

Using their home as a base, they received and instructed Apollos, explaining to him the way of God more accurately (Acts 18:24–26).

5. Journeying with Paul

They are mentioned multiple times in Paul's epistles; for instance, Rom. 16:3–4 refers to them as "fellow workers" who "risked their own necks for my life," showing their loyalty and sacrifice for Paul.

6. Final Mention

In 2 Tim. 4:19, Paul still mentioned them near the end of his life, showing that they had been faithfully serving all along, and also indicating that they later returned to Ephesus.

Aquila's Spiritual Significance and Example
1. An Example of Family Unity

Almost every time Aquila is mentioned in the Bible, he appears together with Priscilla; the two served with one heart, and their home became a base for the church, serving as a model of the "house church."

2. An Example of Ministry

Aquila was a "bi-vocational missionary." Without abandoning his trade, he planted churches and equipped disciples everywhere. He and Priscilla welcomed Paul, instructed Apollos, and hosted a house church, standing as an outstanding biblical model of workplace ministry.

3. An Example of Faithfulness and Sacrifice

In Romans 16, Paul commends them as having "risked their own necks for my life," showing that in times of peril they remained faithful to the gospel of Christ. Not only did they support Paul, but all the churches were grateful to them as well.


Out of the five passages in the Bible where Aquila is mentioned, in three of them he is placed after his wife, Priscilla, which was extremely rare in those days.


Acts 18:18 So Paul still remained a good while. Then he took leave of the brethren and sailed for Syria, and Priscilla and Aquila were with him. He had his hair cut off at Cenchrea, for he had taken a vow.
Acts 18:26 So he began to speak boldly in the synagogue. When Aquila and Priscilla heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately.

1 Cor. 16:19 The churches of Asia greet you. Aquila and Priscilla greet you heartily in the Lord, with the church that is in their house.

"Greet Priscilla and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their own necks for my life, to whom not only I give thanks, but also all the churches of the Gentiles." (Rom. 16:3–4)

Who would dare give such an evaluation? Yet as we look at the scriptures above, it was indeed so.
It can be said that Priscilla was widely recognized in her day as a virtuous woman.

Hidden Heroes
1. A Good Homemaker


Priscilla is just like the virtuous woman described in Proverbs 31; the heart of her husband safely trusts her, so he will have no lack of gain, and she does him good and not evil all the days of her life. Balzac said: A good woman is born with the genius to create a good husband; having her is worth more to a husband than millions in wealth.


According to Jewish tradition, Priscilla came to faith in the Lord before her husband. Yet whenever Paul mentions the church in their house, Aquila is always placed first; only in personal greetings is Priscilla's name mentioned first. This shows that Priscilla was humble and submissive, never regarding herself as the spiritual leader. Perhaps her spiritual maturity was higher than her husband's, yet she always honored her husband as the head, keeping herself in the supportive role of a helper.


The Bible records the names of this couple six times in total: Acts 18:2, 18, 26; Romans 16:3; 1 Corinthians 16:19; and 2 Timothy 4:19. Among these six times, Priscilla's name comes first three times, and Aquila's name comes first three times—a truly equal standing. Augustine said: If God had intended woman to rule over man, He would have made Eve from Adam's skull; if God had intended woman to be man's slave, He would have made Eve from Adam's foot bone; but God made Eve from Adam's rib, indicating that He intended woman to be a helper to man, equal in standing.


Priscilla and Aquila are truly a good model for us. Between them, there was no precedence of rank or superiority, nor was there any division of "yours" and "mine"; they truly lived out a life of being one flesh and bearing the same yoke together. Socrates said: A married couple is like two people rowing a small boat together; if their goal is not united and their actions do not coordinate, the boat will surely capsize. Thinking of our own marital life: is it devoid of any merit like Nabal and Abigail (1 Sam. 25)? Or is it like Ananias and Sapphira, conspiring together to do evil (Acts 5)? We should be like Zechariah and Elizabeth (Luke 1), serving God devoutly with one heart and one mind, and God will surely grant His blessing.


2. A Good Teacher


After staying in Corinth with Paul for a year and a half, Priscilla and her husband sailed together with him to Syria. Upon arriving in Ephesus, the couple stayed there. Soon a Jew named Apollos came to Ephesus. Born in Alexandria, he was an eloquent man and mighty in the Scriptures. Furthermore, being fervent in spirit, he often went to the synagogue to speak boldly, speaking and teaching accurately the things of the Lord. It was only a pity that his knowledge of the full truth was not clear enough: he knew only the baptism of John and did not know the work of the filling of the Holy Spirit. Perhaps he only understood the Christ introduced by John the Baptist, but did not yet have a deep understanding of the Lord's death, resurrection, and ascension.


Priscilla and her husband went to the synagogue to listen, and hearing that this young preacher's knowledge of Christ was still superficial, they did not publicly rebuke him, nor did they privately

criticize him in front of others; instead, they took him to their home and explained to him the way of God more accurately.


Presumably, they first received him with love, invited him to eat, showed care for his travel life, and only then taught him, hoping that he would proclaim the gospel more completely. This is the model of a good teacher. Many people love to speak the truth, yet forget to speak it in love; as a result, the truth turns into caustic criticism, making it hard for people to accept. Moreover, having lost love, speaking the truth becomes unfair faultfinding, bringing no benefit but only harm to others and to oneself. When we look at Apollos's subsequent progress—how he greatly helped those in Achaia who had believed through grace, vigorously refuting the Jews in public and demonstrating from the Scriptures that Jesus is the Christ—we know that the teaching Priscilla and her husband gave him bore good fruit.




II. Pioneers of Bi-Vocational Ministry
1. Pontian, Skilled Workers, Immigrants
Priscilla was born in Pontus, which is northeastern Turkey today. Like her husband Aquila, they were persecuted for their faith and expelled from Italy. The Bible records that "by their occupation they were tentmakers" (Acts 18:3); they were skilled laborers, "workplace people."

The workplace is not an obstacle to faith, but a mission field established by God.

Because Paul was of the same trade, he stayed with them, and a spiritual connection was thereby forged. In other words: workplace skills were the platform through which they responded to the calling; the home was the starting point of their service to the Lord.

III. Life Characteristics of Priscilla
1. A Good Homemaker: Submission and Unity in Spiritual Demeanor
The "virtuous woman" of Proverbs 31 is a true portrait of Priscilla.

Although tradition says she came to faith first, and Paul also placed her name first in three passages (such as Acts 18:26), she never overstepped her place nor drew attention to herself.

Husband and wife were of one heart; among the six passages, Aquila is placed first three times, and Priscilla is placed first three times, showing their mutual respect and their being members of one another.

Application: In marriages today, is there such a spiritual partnership? Are you partners who build the church with one heart, or business partners who each go their own way? Is there unity in godliness, or tension under unequal spiritual maturity?

2. Good Teachers: Teaching in Love, Shepherding Through the Home
In Ephesus, they met Apollos—a learned preacher whose understanding of the truth was nonetheless incomplete. Priscilla and Aquila did not refute him publicly, nor did they judge him privately; rather, they "took him aside" into their home and corrected his understanding of the truth in love (Acts 18:26).

Teaching given in love is more capable of producing change;

Shepherding done in the home carries greater spiritual influence.

This was not a one-time lecture, but a process of life influencing life.

How do we treat younger believers today? How do we treat coworkers whose theological understanding is not yet complete?

3. Good Coworkers: Laying Down Their Lives in Hospitality, Making the Home a Church
Romans 16:3–4: They "risked their own necks" for Paul's life; they were workers willing to risk death for the Lord.

Their "church that is in their house" appears three times (Rom. 16:5; 1 Cor. 16:19; Acts 18:26)—the home became the church, and the home was a spiritual altar.

Theirs was not a "part-time ministry," but wholehearted commitment. They did not build a "ministry platform," but rather built a "spiritual family."

Bi-vocational Pioneers:

III. Good Coworkers


In Romans chapter 16, Paul says: Greet Priscilla and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their own necks for my life, to whom not only I give thanks, but also all the churches of the Gentiles. Likewise greet the church that is in their house.


In the original text, Paul still uses an affectionate diminutive name for Priscilla here, which shows the intimacy between them. They were originally of the same trade, and they lived together for over a year. Both of these circumstances make it difficult to coexist in peace; nevertheless, they radiated the fragrance of Christ, lived out a beautiful testimony, and went further to become good coworkers (tónggōng), rather than "attacking one another" (tónggōng).


When Paul first went to seek refuge with them, it was precisely when he had suffered severe attacks in Athens, yet they took Paul in regardless of their own safety. Not only that, Priscilla's home always hosted a church; she was willing to open her home to receive coworkers as well as fellow believers on the Way.


In the early days of the church back then, the number of people gathering in various places was small, and there were not yet chapels dedicated specifically for gatherings, so churches were all established in homes. Unexpectedly, in mainland China today, due to the political regime, the same phenomenon has arisen. For us overseas, Chinese people are scattered everywhere with inconvenient transportation, so there is also this kind of need. It is said that in Los Angeles there are more than a hundred Chinese churches, mostly of the house-church type. Perhaps the area where you live needs such a place where believers can gather, study the Bible, and pray together with one heart. Are you willing to open your home to be used by the Lord?


Church history records: On July 8, A.D. 66, Priscilla and Aquila were beheaded and martyred together on the outskirts of Rome. The two of them were not only coworkers, but also shared one life and one death. Today in the catacombs of Rome there is a cave specifically commemorating the deeds of Priscilla. In the city of Rome there are also churches and stone monuments commemorating her. But we know with certainty that what she took greatest pride and joy in was that her name is written in the Book of Life, and that she received the crown of righteousness.  

IV. Priscilla's View of Service and Its Insights for the Church Today
1. The Church Is Not Merely a Building, but an Extension of the Home
The early church mostly gathered in homes. Today, in our urban and immigrant environments, we are also often under the constraint of "meeting without a church building." This is not an obstacle, but a return to the essence—the church is a community life "centered on the home."

Are you willing to open your home and let it become a fellowship? Let the coffee table become a pulpit? Let the kitchen become a bridge for the gospel?

2. Service Is Not a Matter of Title, but a Response to Calling
Priscilla was not a "preacher," yet with her home, her profession, and her life, she responded to the call of the gospel. She had no pulpit, yet she had the actions of preaching; she had no title, yet she had a crown upon her head.

3. Husband and Wife of One Heart: The Workplace Is the Mission Field
Wherever Priscilla and Aquila moved, they were always planting churches. They left the traces of "house churches" in Corinth, Ephesus, and Rome.

This is also the model we ought to emulate today in immigrant churches across North America, Asia, the Middle East, and Europe: Family Missions + Workplace Shepherding = Today's Discipleship Strategy.

V. Conclusion: Her Mark Is Not Merely a Tombstone, but a Crown
According to church tradition, Priscilla and her husband were martyred together in Rome in A.D. 66. They left behind no worldly property, but they left behind a spiritual heritage. Their tombstone is on earth, but their names are in the Book of Life.

Are we willing, like Priscilla:

To build the church with our homes;

To bear witness in the workplace;

To instruct people in the true way;

To walk together with one heart in the Lord?

Application Questions:
Am I willing to open my home to become a place for small group or Bible study gatherings?

Has my workplace become a "tentmaking workshop" used by the Lord? Or am I merely bustling about for a livelihood?

Are my spouse and I walking together with one heart spiritually?

Am I able to instruct in love and correct in gentleness?
