# What Doth the Lord Require of Thee — A Sermon on Deuteronomy 10

> English first translation · First draft, pending review （英文首译·初译待校）
>
> Title determination: Five candidates were considered — (1) *What Doth the Lord Require of Thee*, taken from the question in verse 12, "what doth the LORD thy God require of thee?"; (2) *Circumcise the Foreskin of Your Heart*, taken from the command in verse 16, covering only the second half of the chapter; (3) *He Is Thy Praise*, taken from the concluding sentence in verse 21, covering only the final section; (4) *God of Gods, and Lord of Lords*, taken from the title in verse 17, covering only the praise section; (5) *As the Stars of Heaven for Multitude*, taken from the closing sentence in verse 22, covering only the final verse. Candidate (1) is adopted: the argument of all twenty-two verses has only one axis. Chapter 9 has just dismantled "not for thy righteousness"; chapter 10 then answers positively — what, after all, does God require of this people? The answer is set out in two halves: the first half is given by the narrative (the covenant tables rewritten, the people spared, Levi set apart, the command to journey given again — what God did first), and the second half is given by the question (fear, walking in His ways, love, serving Him with all the heart and with all the soul, keeping the commandments — what God then asks for). The two halves together close with the circumcision of the heart and with "He is thy praise." English title: What Doth the Lord Require of Thee (from KJV 10:12, "what doth the LORD thy God require of thee").
>
> Text and collation note: The Chinese source text follows the Chinese Union Version with New Punctuation (CUNPSS) as its base text. In this English translation, every Scripture quotation is taken verbatim from the King James Version (KJV), checked directly against KJV text, and is never translated from the Chinese. Scope of checking: all twenty-two verses of the chapter were checked verse by verse. Cross-reference quotations (Exod 34:4, 28; Num 18:20; Deut 18:1–2; Gen 46:27; Exod 1:5; Gen 15:5; Deut 1:10; Neh 9:23; Exod 22:21; Lev 19:34; Rom 2:11; Acts 10:34; Heb 11:12; 1 Kgs 8:9; Heb 9:4; Num 33:30–33; Deut 4:37; Deut 7:7; Deut 30:6; Lev 26:41) were registered through the cross-reference columns of the verse-by-verse pages for this chapter; where a source is named, the quotation is given in full, and the rest are registered by summary only. Particulars of the KJV wording, registered separately: (1) The forms of address differ within the chapter: in verse 9 and at the first occurrence in verse 12 the base text reads "the LORD thy God" (in the Chinese base text, without the possessive particle at those two places); at the second occurrence in verse 12, and in verses 14, 20 and 22, "the LORD thy God"; in verse 17, "the LORD your God"; in verse 21, only "thy God" — registered separately. The KJV lines are registered from the KJV itself and are not adjudicated against the Chinese forms. (2) Quotation marks and parenthesis structure of the discourse: the whole chapter is Moses' discourse; verse 1 introduces God's words in quotation marks, and verse 2 closes them at "in the ark."; at the end of verse 5 the base text opens a parenthesis — verses 6–9 are a parenthetical appendix (the journey and Levi, in two parts) — and verse 9 closes the parenthesis; verse 10 opens the discourse again in quotation marks, verse 11 places God's words in inner quotation marks, and the end of verse 22 closes the whole chapter with a full stop and quotation mark — registered separately. In the KJV itself verses 6–9 are likewise set within the parenthetical movement of the discourse in many printings; the KJV wording is registered as it stands. (3) In verse 3 the base text reads "made an ark of shittim wood": in KJV, "And I made an ark of shittim wood" — the wood is šiṭṭîm. (4) In verse 4 the base text reads "the ten commandments" (literally, the ten words); KJV: "the ten commandments." (5) In verse 9 the base text reads "no part nor inheritance" (KJV: "no part nor inheritance"), the same form as Deut 18:1. (6) In verse 11 the base text reads, in effect, "Arise, take thy journey before the people": the Hebrew noun massaʿ means "journey, setting out"; KJV: "Arise, take thy journey before the people." (7) In verse 13 the base text reads "keep his commandments and statutes," with the pronoun "his," not "the LORD's"; the closing phrase "for thy good" renders Hebrew ləṭôḇ lāk (for your benefit) — registered separately. In KJV verse 13 the subject is explicit: "To keep the commandments of the LORD, and his statutes, which I command thee this day for thy good?" — the difference between the KJV line and the Chinese line at this point is registered, not harmonised. (8) In verse 16 the base text renders freely, "circumcise the foreskin of your heart" being the literal sense: the Hebrew ûmaltem ʾet-ʿorlat ləḇaḇkem is literally "you shall circumcise the foreskin of your heart"; KJV gives the direct rendering, "Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart." Deut 30:6 uses the same sentence pattern for God's own action (registered from the cross-reference column) — registered separately; this is the most important separate registration in the chapter. (9) In verse 17 the base text reads "a great God, a mighty, and a terrible": the Hebrew hāʾēl haggādōl haggibbōr wəhannôrāʾ is three adjectives with the definite article in a row, used as a title; "which regardeth not persons" renders Hebrew lōʾ-yiśśāʾ pānîm, literally "does not lift up faces" — registered separately. (10) In verse 19 the base text reads "ye were strangers"; KJV: "for ye were strangers in the land of Egypt." (11) In verse 20 the base text's "cleave" renders Hebrew dāḇaq, to adhere, to cling closely; KJV: "to him shalt thou cleave." (12) In verse 21 the base text reads "He is thy praise": the Hebrew hûʾ təhillāṯəkā is literally "He is thy praise"; KJV: "He is thy praise." (13) In verse 22 the base text reads "threescore and ten persons," agreeing with the Masoretic reckoning of Gen 46:27 and Exod 1:5; Acts 7:14 reads threescore and fifteen (the Septuagint reckoning) — registered separately and discussed in Section Five.

## Main Text

Introduction (00:00–05:00). [Factual Layer] The chapter's place in the book is registered first: together with chapter 9 it belongs to the opening group of Moses' second great discourse (chapters 6–11), and narratively it is the direct continuation of chapter 9 — chapter 9 told of Moses coming down the mountain, breaking the tables, and falling down before the LORD forty days and forty nights, and there it stopped; chapter 10 opens with God commanding that the stone tables be hewn again: the story resumes at the place of the breaking. After the story is told (vv. 1–11), the second half of the chapter turns to the most concentrated group of positive commands in Deuteronomy (vv. 12–22): chapter 9's "not for thy righteousness" is the dismantling; this chapter's "what doth the LORD require of thee" is the building — the wrong ground is removed, and then the right response is given. The chapter has twenty-two verses in all, registered by structure: the covenant tables rewritten and placed in the ark (vv. 1–5, 5 verses); parenthetical appendix: the journey and Levi (vv. 6–9, 4 verses); the second ascent and the command to journey (vv. 10–11, 2 verses); what is required (vv. 12–15, 4 verses); the circumcision of the heart and love for the stranger (vv. 16–19, 4 verses); cleaving to Him and the stars (vv. 20–22, 3 verses) — the groups total twenty-two verses (5 + 4 + 2 + 4 + 4 + 3 = 22, Note 1; the sermon follows five sections: Section One, vv. 1–5; Section Two, vv. 6–11; Section Three, vv. 12–15; Section Four, vv. 16–19; Section Five, vv. 20–22). Only one party is quoted as speaking in the whole chapter: God's words in two passages (the command in vv. 1–2, and the journey command in v. 11); everything else is Moses' narration and exhortation — the people do not speak in this chapter; they need only hear two things clearly: what God has already done, and what God now requires. [Theological Layer] The order of this chapter is itself doctrine: first the narrative of grace, then the command to respond; first the broken tables rewritten, first the people who should have been destroyed spared, first the priestly office restored, first the people commanded to journey — and only then the question, "what doth the LORD require of thee?" God's requirement never hangs in the air: it always stands behind His finished work. [Summary Layer] The chapter's outline can be put this way: the tables can be hewn again, because the One who writes is still He; the people can be spared, because the One who hearkens is still He; and then what He asks for is not sacrifices, not silver or gold, but only five things — fear Him, walk in His ways, love Him, serve Him with all the heart and with all the soul, and keep His commandments — and it is for thy good. Since what is required is these things, what is done should be these things; once the heart has been circumcised, the hands will love the stranger, and the mouth will swear only by His name — because He is thy praise, and He has made seventy persons into the stars of heaven.

### I. The Covenant Tables Hewn Again (10:1–5; 05:00–15:00)

[Factual Layer] Verse 1 in full (KJV) — God's words: "At that time the LORD said unto me, Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first, and come up unto me into the mount, and make thee an ark of wood." Registered item by item: (1) The time word "At that time" nails this verse to the aftermath of the crisis in chapter 9 — the tables had been broken, the prayer had been heard (9:19), and God's first word is not an investigation but a re-hewing. (2) Three commands: hew the tables (like unto the first), come up the mountain, and make an ark of wood — the command to make the ark is mentioned before the ascent; the order of execution is in verse 3. (3) The word "hew" (pāsal) in this verse and Moses' execution of it in verse 3 are the same word: command and execution are registered with the same verb. [Factual Layer] Verse 2 in full (KJV): "And I will write on the tables the words that were in the first tables which thou brakest, and thou shalt put them in the ark." Registered item by item: (1) The division of labour, recounted by hand — three things done by man: hew the tables, make the ark, put the tables in the ark; one thing done by God: write the words. "I will write on the tables": the author of the content is still God; Moses only prepares the stone and the ark. (2) God Himself mentions the breaking here: "the first tables which thou brakest" — no evasion, no change of wording: the origin of the new covenant tables is precisely the shattering of the old ones. (3) This verse read against 9:10: the first set of tables was "written with the finger of God" (registered in that sermon); for the second set God says plainly, "I will write" — the author of both sets is the same, registered on record. [Factual Layer] Verse 3 in full (KJV): "And I made an ark of shittim wood, and hewed two tables of stone like unto the first, and went up into the mount, having the two tables in mine hand." Registered item by item: (1) The difference between the order of execution and the order in which the commands were mentioned: the commands were mentioned in the order hew the tables, come up, make the ark (v. 1); the execution runs make the ark, hew the tables, go up (v. 3) — the ark was made before the ascent; the similarity and difference are registered, not forced into an explanation. (2) The name of the wood in the base text is "shittim wood" (šiṭṭîm), the same name as the wood of the ark of the tabernacle (Exod 25:10; 37:1, checked directly in the cross-reference column of this verse's page). (3) When Moses went up the mountain the tables bore no writing yet: he carried blank stone up — the content of the tables waited for him at the top of the mountain, not in his hand. [Factual Layer] Verse 4 in full (KJV): "And he wrote on the tables, according to the first writing, the ten commandments, which the LORD spake unto you in the mount out of the midst of the fire in the day of the assembly: and the LORD gave them unto me." Registered item by item: (1) The explicit statement of the identity of the content is "according to the first writing" (Hebrew kammiḵtāḇ hāriʾšôn, as in the first writing) — what is rewritten is not a new commandment but the same one. (2) The content is named as "the ten commandments" (Hebrew ʿăśeret haddəḇārîm, the ten words), spoken to the whole assembly out of the midst of the fire on the day of the assembly (yôm haqqāhāl) (read against 9:10 and Deuteronomy 5, registered in those sermons). (3) The handing over of the tables is complete at this point: the God who writes, the man who receives the tables, in the same form as the first ascent (Exod 34:4, 34:28, checked directly in the cross-reference column of this chapter's verse pages — of which 34:4, KJV: "And he hewed two tables of stone like unto the first; and Moses rose up early in the morning, and went up unto mount Sinai, as the LORD had commanded him, and took in his hand the two tables of stone."; and 34:28, KJV: "And he was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights; he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments." The Chinese base text at 34:28 explicitly makes the LORD the subject of the writing; in the Hebrew text at that place the subject is not explicitly stated, which differs from this chapter's verse 2, "I will write" — set side by side in the Discussion Layer). [Factual Layer] Verse 5 in full (KJV): "And I turned myself and came down from the mount, and put the tables in the ark which I had made; and there they be, as the LORD commanded me." Registered item by item: (1) "And there they be" is a sentence of testimony: when Moses preached this sermon east of the Jordan, the tables were still resting in the ark — forty years of wilderness road had not lost them. (2) Mutual corroboration with 1 Kings 8:9 (checked directly in the cross-reference column of this verse's page): on the day Solomon dedicated the temple, what was seen in the ark was still only these two tables of stone. (3) "As the LORD commanded me" closes this section: from the command (v. 1) to the obedience (v. 5), there is not one sentence of Moses' self-defence in between. [Theological Layer] The inferences of this section: (1) Grace has a concrete shape in this section: the broken tables rewritten. What the people broke — strictly speaking, Moses broke the tables, but the people broke the covenant first (chapter 9) — God's answer is not to make a new covenant instead, but to write the same covenant over again: not one word reduced, not one word changed ("according to the first writing"); only the stone must be hewn again by man, and the ark made again by man. Restoration is not a cheap reset: man must prepare with his hands, and God writes the words Himself. (2) The blank stone is worth recording on its own: on his second ascent Moses had no commandments in his hand, only prepared blank tables — all man can ever prepare is the tables and the ark; the words are always written by God. This is an inference, marked as such here. [New Testament Reading Layer] The echoes of this section in the New Testament, registered at the reading layer only: Hebrews 9:4 records three things within the ark of the covenant (KJV): "Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron's rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;" — this differs in detail from verse 5 of this chapter and from 1 Kings 8:9, which record only the two tables of stone; the two passages are registered side by side (the reading of the difference is placed in this sermon's notes, not developed at this layer). Second Corinthians 3 treats the contrast between tables of stone and tables of the heart (registered by summary), joining the motif of "the tables" in this section. All are registered only, not read back as this chapter's original meaning. [Scholarly and Historical Discussion Layer] Two matters set side by side. (1) What "the ark" refers to — wooden ark and ark of the covenant: (a) The one-ark reading: the wooden ark Moses made in this chapter is the later ark of the covenant, and when the tabernacle was completed the tables were still in this ark (on the evidence of v. 5, "and there they be"). (b) The two-ark reading: the ark in this chapter is a wooden ark Moses prepared temporarily, and in Exodus 37 Bezalel made another ark overlaid with gold (its text checked in the cross-reference column of the 10:3 verse page), into which the tables were later moved. The two readings are set side by side; what can be determined is only the textual fact: this chapter calls it only "an ark of wood / the ark," not "the ark of the covenant" as a proper name in verses 1–5, and says plainly that the tables are in it, "and there they be." (2) The subject of the writing in Exod 34:28, set side by side: (a) The God-wrote reading: on the evidence of this chapter's verse 2 and of 9:10, "written with the finger of God," and because the Chinese base text at 34:28 explicitly takes the LORD as the subject of the writing, the writer in Exod 34:28 is God. (b) The Moses-wrote reading: on the evidence of the context of Exodus 34 (34:27 explicitly commands Moses, in effect, "Write thou these words"; comparisons beyond what is registered in the cross-reference column are not asserted, and this reading is registered as a position only). The two readings are set side by side, without adjudication. [Summary Layer] A summary of the section: the covenant was broken, and God did not change the covenant; He rewrote it — you hew the stone, you make the ark, I will write the words. Forty years later Moses pointed to the ark and said: the tables are still there. The preservation of grace can be pointed to in a physical object.

### II. The Journey and Levi in Parentheses (10:6–11; 15:00–24:00)

[Factual Layer] Verses 6–7 in full (KJV, the upper half of the parenthetical appendix): "And the children of Israel took their journey from Beeroth of the children of Jaakan to Mosera: there Aaron died, and there he was buried; and Eleazar his son ministered in the priest's office in his stead. / From thence they journeyed unto Gudgodah; and from Gudgodah to Jotbath, a land of rivers of waters." Registered item by item: (1) In the base text this passage stands in parentheses: the main line of the narrative (the rewriting of the tables — the second ascent) is interrupted by this journey appendix, placed between two great events — registered on record. The KJV wording is registered as it stands. (2) The account of the journey's stations: Beeroth of the children of Jaakan → Mosera → Gudgodah → Jotbath. (3) Aaron's death is given in this one sentence only: died, was buried, succeeded — one verb, "ministered in the priest's office" (kāhan, Eleazar serving as priest), completes the handover; this section records no funeral honours. (4) Comparison with the journey record in Numbers 33 (33:30–33, checked directly in the cross-reference column of this verse's page), KJV: "And they departed from Hashmonah, and encamped at Moseroth. And they departed from Moseroth, and pitched in Benejaakan. And they removed from Benejaakan, and encamped at Horhagidgad. And they went from Horhagidgad, and pitched in Jotbathah." — the order of the station names and the translated names both differ (Moseroth / Mosera, Horhagidgad / Gudgodah), and the place of Aaron's death there is Mount Hor (Num 20:28; 33:38, checked directly in the cross-reference column of the 10:6 verse page); the similarities and differences are placed in the Discussion Layer side by side. [Factual Layer] Verse 8 in full (KJV): "At that time the LORD separated the tribe of Levi, to bear the ark of the covenant of the LORD, to stand before the LORD to minister unto him, and to bless in his name, unto this day." Registered item by item: (1) "At that time" answers the "At that time" of verse 1 across the text: in the same stretch of days God did two things — rewrote the covenant tables, and separated Levi. (2) Levi's office is listed in three items in a row: bear the ark of the covenant, stand before the LORD to minister to Him, and bless in His name (Hebrew bādal, to separate; šāraṯ, to minister; bārak, to bless) — the content of the office is entirely directed toward the ark and toward the people; not one item is directed toward themselves. (3) "Unto this day" answers verse 5's "and there they be": the tables are still there, and the office is still there — on the day Moses preached, both could be verified. [Factual Layer] Verse 9 in full (KJV; the parenthesis closes here in the base text): "Wherefore Levi hath no part nor inheritance with his brethren; the LORD is his inheritance, according as the LORD thy God promised him." Registered item by item: (1) "No part nor inheritance" (ḥēleq wənaḥălâ) is Levi's definition of identity, in the same tone as the LORD's words to Aaron in Num 18:20 (checked directly in the cross-reference column of this verse's page), KJV: "And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel." — this verse is the discourse restatement of that sentence. (2) "The LORD is his inheritance": among the twelve tribes, only one tribe's inheritance is not land, but God Himself. (3) This form appears again in Deut 18:1–2 (checked directly in the cross-reference column of this verse's page), KJV: "The priests the Levites, and all the tribe of Levi, shall have no part nor inheritance with Israel: they shall eat the offerings of the LORD made by fire, and his inheritance. Therefore shall they have no inheritance among their brethren: the LORD is their inheritance, as he hath said unto them." — the same sentence: in this chapter it is placed in the journey appendix, in chapter 18 in the laws of office — registered on record. [Factual Layer] Verse 10 in full (KJV): "And I stayed in the mount, according to the first time, forty days and forty nights; and the LORD hearkened unto me at that time also, and the LORD would not destroy thee." Registered item by item: (1) The second forty days and forty nights is here: reckoned together with chapter 9's account — the first stay on the mountain (9:9, 11), the falling down in intercession (9:18, 25), the second stay on the mountain (this verse) — "forty days and forty nights" is mentioned five times across the whole series, covering three periods (Note 2). (2) "The LORD hearkened unto me at that time also" answers 9:19: two crises, two fallings-down and stays, two hearkenings — with the word "also" Moses strings the two into one account. (3) The verb in this verse, "would not" (ʾāḇâ, to be unwilling, to refuse), is registered: the Hebrew is not "could not" but "was unwilling / would not" — the destruction did not happen, not because God could not change His mind, but because He heard the intercession and would not lift His hand. [Factual Layer] Verse 11 in full (KJV) — God's words: "And the LORD said unto me, Arise, take thy journey before the people, that they may go in and possess the land, which I sware unto their fathers to give unto them." Registered item by item: (1) The crisis closes with a journey command: God's last word to Moses is not an explanation but a departure — "Arise (qûm), take thy journey before the people (Hebrew leḵ ləmassaʿ, go before them on the journey)." (2) This verse is in the same form as God's words in Exod 32:34 and 33:1 (checked directly in the cross-reference column of this verse's page): there too, in effect, "go, lead the people to the place of which I have spoken" — after both crises, God's closing word is the same sentence: the road is still to be walked. (3) "The land, which I sware unto their fathers to give unto them": the ground of the land is stated once more here — not the people's righteousness (chapter 9 has dismantled that), but God's oath to the fathers (šāḇaʿ). [Theological Layer] The inferences of this section: (1) The three things in the parentheses are all "succession": Aaron died, and Eleazar succeeded him; one station was finished, and the journey set out for the next; Levi had no land, and God Himself was his inheritance — after judgment, what God restored first was not territory but order and service: there was someone to bear the ark, someone to bless, someone to lead the way, and only then is entering Canaan spoken of. (2) Levi's "no inheritance" is a sample set before the whole people: among the twelve tribes, God deliberately kept one tribe that does not live by land but lives by Him; when the other tribes later, on the land, forget the Owner of the inheritance, this brother is a living reminder. This is an inference, marked as such here. [Factual Layer] Verse 16 in full (KJV): "Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked." ## Devotional Extract

Scripture (base text) — Deuteronomy 10:12–13 (Chinese base text, in translation): "And now, Israel, what doth the LORD thy God require of thee, but to fear the LORD thy God, to walk in all his ways, and to love him, and to serve the LORD thy God with all thy heart and with all thy soul, to keep the commandments of the LORD, and his statutes, which I command thee this day for thy good?"

Meditation: What does God require of you? Moses' answer is surprisingly simple: fear Him, walk in His ways, love Him, serve Him with all your heart and with all your soul, and keep His commandments — not one of them adds anything to Him, because the heaven and the heaven of heavens are already His. Even the purpose is spelled out: it is for thy good. What He requires turns out to be for our benefit. Only, all of this has a prior action: verse 16 says, circumcise the foreskin of your heart. Until the heart has first undergone that surgery, fear will turn into mere dread, love into mere words, and service into performance. And before you act, remember verse 18 first: He doth execute the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loveth the stranger, in giving him food and raiment — in His sight, you too were once a stranger. Today, surrender your heart, and find the "stranger" in your home and your church: as God has treated you, so treat them.

Prayer: LORD my God, the heaven and the heaven of heavens are Thine, yet Thou hast set Thy delight upon us and chosen us. Circumcise my heart, and let me be stiffnecked no more; teach me to fear Thee, to walk in Thy ways, to love Thee, and to serve Thee with all my heart and with all my soul. Give me also a heart that loves the stranger, for I was a stranger in the land of Egypt. In Jesus' name, Amen.

Reflection: What does the Lord require of you? Moses' answer is surprisingly simple: fear Him, walk in His ways, love Him, serve Him with all your heart and soul, and keep His commandments. None of it adds anything to God — the heaven and the heaven of heavens are already His. Even the purpose is spelled out: it is for your good. What He requires turns out to be for our benefit. But one thing comes first, in verse 16: circumcise the foreskin of your heart. Until the heart has undergone that surgery, reverence decays into mere fear, love into mere words, and service into performance. And before you act, remember verse 18: He executes judgment for the fatherless and the widow, and loves the stranger, giving him food and clothing — and in His sight, you too were once a stranger. Today, surrender your heart, and find the "stranger" in your home and your church: as God has treated you, so treat them.

Prayer: Lord my God, the heaven and the heaven of heavens are Thine, yet Thou hast set Thy love upon us and chosen us. Circumcise my heart, and let me be stiffnecked no more. Teach me to fear Thee, to walk in Thy ways, to love Thee, and to serve Thee with all my heart and with all my soul. Give me a heart that loves the stranger, for I was a stranger in the land of Egypt. In Jesus' name, Amen.
