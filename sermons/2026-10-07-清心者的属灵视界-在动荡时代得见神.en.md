"Blessed are the pure in heart, For they shall see God." (Matt. 5:8)

Brothers and sisters, I wonder if you have this feeling: Why is it becoming harder and harder for us to "see God" in this era? [Pause for 3 seconds]

Every day when we open our phones, information about layoffs, pay cuts, educational rat races, and interpersonal conflicts overwhelms us. In prayer, we feel God is far away; in life, we cannot see God's hand at work, feeling parched in heart, anxious, and uneasy.

How does the classic hymn "I'd Rather Have Jesus" go?

[Pulpit a cappella / soft spoken reflection]

"I'd rather have Jesus than silver or gold; I'd rather have Jesus than houses or lands; I'd rather have Jesus than all things, than today's wealth and empty fame."

This hymn sings of the purest longing in the kingdom of heaven. If in our hearts, besides Jesus, we also crave money, status, applause, and worldly security, our hearts are no longer "pure."

Pastor Tong Liu of River of Life Christian Church in Silicon Valley once said something very vivid in a sermon (verbatim from the video):

"Many people think that not hearing God's voice means God has stopped speaking; actually, God is speaking all the time! The problem is not that God's voice is too quiet, but that our hearts are too 'cluttered.' Our hearts are crowded with worldly calculations, panic about the future, self-ambition, and utilitarian mindsets. It is just like a pool of murky water: the surface is churning with waves, full of silt and sand—how could it possibly reflect the moon in the sky? Only when your heart is cleansed by the Holy Spirit and becomes 'pure in heart' (Pure in Heart)—pure in motive, single-mindedly seeking God alone—will your spiritual eyes instantly brighten. Not only will you hear God, but you will also 'see God's' sovereignty in any complex environment!"

Today, centering on "Blessed are the pure in heart," we will cite ten scriptures (including original language exegesis), biblical stories, and integrate seven major real-life scenarios—the elderly, children, mother-in-law and daughter-in-law, husband and wife, workplace, business, and health—to reshape our spiritual vision!

(08:00 - 28:00)
1.1 The Dual Meaning of "Purity of Heart"
In Matthew 5:8, the original Greek for "pure in heart" is *Katharoi tē kardia* (Ka-tha-roi te kar-di-a).

The root word *Katharos* (Ka-tha-ros) in ancient Greek has two extremely critical meanings:

Clean and blameless (Purified): like a mirror wiped spotless, without any dirt or impurities;

Pure and undivided (Unmixed): like pure gold without a trace of dross, or pure milk without a single drop of water added!

The Greek for "shall see God" is *Tōn Theon opsontai*, referring to spiritual eyes being opened to recognize God's work in the present moment.

Philosopher Søren Kierkegaard said: "Purity of heart is to will one thing."

1.2 Golden Scripture Passages and Original Word Analyses

"Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life." (Prov. 4:23)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew word for "keep" is *Netsor* (Net-sor), referring to an army guarding a fortress; the Hebrew word for "issues/springs" is *Totsa'ot* (To-tsa-ot), referring to a continuously flowing spring.

Spoken pulpit exposition: If the source (the heart) is dirty, the marriage, career, and health flowing out of it will all become sick! You must guard your inner purity just like guarding a fortress (*Netsor*).

"Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, But the LORD weighs the hearts." (Prov. 21:2)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew word for "weighs" is *Token* (To-ken), referring to accurately weighing on a scale.

Spoken pulpit exposition: We are best at making excuses for our selfish motives. But God does not look at how pretty your words are on the outside; what God accurately weighs (*Token*) is whether your underlying motive is pure!

"Who can say, 'I have made my heart clean, I am pure from my sin'?" (Prov. 20:9)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew word for "pure/cleansed" is *Taharti* (Ta-har-ti), referring to complete purity.

Spoken pulpit exposition: Relying on our own self-cultivation, we can never achieve absolute purity of heart. We must come before the Lord every day, asking the precious blood of Christ to cleanse (*Taharti*) us.

"He who loves purity of heart and has grace on his lips, The king will be his friend." (Prov. 22:11)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew for "purity of heart" is *Phor-Lev* (Phor-Lev), referring to a pure white heart without impurities.

Spoken pulpit exposition: People whose hearts are pure white (*Phor-Lev*) speak without twists and turns; Jesus, the King of Heaven, loves most to be friends with such people.

"Cleanse your hands, you sinners; and purify your hearts, you double-minded." (Jas. 4:8)

Original word analysis: The Greek word for "double-minded" is *Dipsychoi* (Di-psy-choi), literally meaning "people with two souls."

Spoken pulpit exposition: Being "double-minded" (*Dipsychoi*) means wanting God's blessings while also clinging to worldly lusts. This "dual motive" will completely blind your spiritual eyes.

1.3 Detailed Biblical Examples

In the days of Jesus, the Pharisees were considered the most devout people. They gave alms regularly, fasted frequently, and stood at street corners making long prayers. Yet, confronting these "devout" leaders, the Lord Jesus issued a most severe condemnation:

"Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For you cleanse the outside of the cup and dish, but inside they are full of extortion and self-indulgence... For you are like whitewashed tombs which indeed appear beautiful outwardly, but inside are full of dead men's bones..." (Matt. 23:25, 27)

The greatest tragedy of the Pharisees was that their motives were contaminated. They gave alms to be praised by others, and they prayed to display their piety. Their hearts were filled with utilitarianism and selfish ambition. And what was the outcome? When the incarnate God, Jesus Christ, stood right before them, the Pharisees, because of the murkiness of their hearts, were completely blind to the glory of God, and even conspired to crucify Jesus on the cross!

In stark contrast was the blind man begging by the roadside outside the city of Jericho—Bartimaeus. When he heard that Jesus was passing by, he began to cry out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

The crowd around him found him noisy and strictly warned him to be quiet. But Bartimaeus had no impurities in his heart, only a single, undivided motive: to seize hold of the Savior! He cried out all the more, "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

Jesus stood still and called him over, asking, "What do you want Me to do for you?" Bartimaeus threw aside his garment and said with utter purity, "Rabboni, that I may receive my sight!" Jesus said to him, "Go your way; your faith has made you well." Immediately Bartimaeus received his sight! Because of his single-minded purity (*Katharos*) of heart, Bartimaeus saw God with his own eyes!

(28:00 - 50:00)
2.1 Golden Scripture Passages and Original Word Analyses

"Trust in the LORD with all your heart, And lean not on your own understanding; In all your ways acknowledge Him, And He shall direct your paths." (Prov. 3:5-6)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew for "with all your heart" is *Bekhal-Lekha* (Be-khal-Le-kha), meaning pouring out one's entire mind and heart without any reservation.

Spoken pulpit exposition: When you stop playing the game of "relying half on God and half on your own cleverness," and trust wholeheartedly (*Bekhal-Lekha*), the path God directs becomes extraordinarily clear!

"Evil men do not understand justice, But those who seek the LORD understand all." (Prov. 28:5)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew for "understand" is *Yavinu* (Ya-vi-nu), meaning discerning deeply the essence of things.

Spoken pulpit exposition: For those whose hearts are blinded by selfish desires, the more they analyze, the more confused they become; but for those whose motives are purely seeking God, God grants them spiritual sight (*Yavinu*) to see through to the essence.

"Flee also youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart." (2 Tim. 2:22)

Original word analysis: The Greek for "pure heart" is *Katharas Kardias* (Ka-tha-ras Kar-di-as).

Spoken pulpit exposition: A pure heart needs companions! Spend more time with those "who call on the Lord out of a pure heart" (*Katharas Kardias*), and your spiritual eyes will not be contaminated by the world.

"Pursue peace with all people, and holiness, without which no one will see the Lord:" (Heb. 12:14)

Original word analysis: The Greek for "holiness" is *Hagiasmon* (Ha-gi-as-mon), referring to a state of purity set apart exclusively for God.

Spoken pulpit exposition: "Seeing the Lord" is not by physical eyes, but by the holiness (*Hagiasmon*) and purity of life!

"Who may ascend into the hill of the LORD? Or who may stand in His holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart, Who has not lifted up his soul to an idol, Nor sworn deceitfully." (Ps. 24:3-4)

Original word analysis: The Hebrew for "vanity / an idol" is *Lashav* (La-shav), referring to bubble-like worldly vanity and comparison.

Spoken pulpit exposition: "Not lifting up one's soul to vanity" (*Lashav*) means not being led astray by online comparisons, vanity, and anxiety. Only those with clean hands and a pure heart can see God!

2.2 Present-Day Real-Life Applications (The Elderly, Children, In-Laws, Couples, Workplace, Business, Health)
Pastor Tong Liu's verbatim words (video exposition):

"In our ministry training at River of Life Christian Church, we continually require our co-workers to do one thing: 'sweep clean your inner motives.' Are you teaching this class to show off your eloquence, or to edify souls? When you wash away 'seeking your own glory' and seek only 'God's glory,' you will find power upon the pulpit, because God manifests Himself among the pure in heart!"

[Pulpit Interactive Question]
"Brothers and sisters, in our daily lives, are our motives in doing things really pure?" [Pause for 3 seconds]

Many couples argue because their motives are impure—"I have sacrificed so much for you, so you must treat me well." A sister cleaned out the impurities in her heart, no longer treating her husband as a tool to fulfill her own sense of security, but serving him purely out of the love of the Lord. When her motives became pure, her husband felt genuine love free of pressure, and their marital relationship was miraculously restored.

A pure-hearted daughter-in-law stopped comparing her mother-in-law with her own mother in her heart and stopped haggling over self-interest. She cleared away suspicion and honored her mother-in-law solely for the Lord's sake. The defensive walls between mother-in-law and daughter-in-law were torn down, and even the non-believing mother-in-law said, "I have seen God in my daughter-in-law!"

Today, many parents push their children into studying this and that, driven by the motive of "having bragging rights on social media" (lifting up their souls to vanity, *Lashav*). A brother repented before God, cleaned out his vanity, and stopped comparing his child with others, instead accompanying him purely according to the gifts God gave him. As a result, the child's depression disappeared, and father and son experienced the abundance of life.

Caring for incapacitated elderly people easily breeds calculation and utilitarian motives. When caring for her paralyzed elderly mother, a sister regularly prayed a "prayer of motive cleansing": "Lord, I am not doing this for relatives to see; I am doing this unto You." When her motive became pure, caregiving was no longer a torment; instead, she experienced God's profound love.

A brother who is a software programmer insisted on praying a pure-heart prayer every day before going to work: "Lord, today I am not doing this to keep my job or please people; I am writing this code for You." Once his motive was cleansed and made pure, he broke free from panic and inner friction; his work efficiency became extremely high, and he became the most trusted breath of fresh air in his department.

A brother in business resolved to be a "pure-hearted businessman," cleaning out the dross of greed and upholding absolute integrity. Although he gave up some opportunities to "make quick money" in the short term, over the long haul he earned an exceptionally high reputation. Even large corporations took the initiative to seek long-term cooperation with him, and he saw God's abundant provision in business.

A sister suffering from severe heart disease emptied her heart of panic and the fear of death, looking up single-mindedly to God: "Lord, my life is in Your hands; whether I live or die, I belong to You." When her heart became calm and clear like still water, the surgery went exceptionally smoothly, and the speed of her recovery was hailed as a miracle even by doctors.
