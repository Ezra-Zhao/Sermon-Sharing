The highest point of full salvation is that God wants to save us out of "man" into "God," uniting us with Himself. Not only do we achieve union in eternal life, but this is also manifested in our present life in this age.

God's salvation here is to save us out of Adam into Christ—grafted into the life of Christ and brought under the name of Christ.

This chapter is divided into three parts: **Part 1—United into Christ** (saved out of Adam into Christ); **Part 2—Filled with the Holy Spirit** (saved out of the flesh into the Holy Spirit); **Part 3—Hidden in God the Father** (saved out of created man into the Creator God).

# Part 1: United into Christ

## 1. United into Christ by Faith

"But as many as received Him, to them He gave the right to become children of God, to those who believe in His name:" (John 1:12)

"Gave" means to bestow; "right" means privilege; "become" means to come to be.

The word "believe" refers to "believing into"; this verse indicates that believing means receiving. The "name" represents the entire person, so "believe in His name" means receiving Jesus Christ. "Right" here refers to special grace; it carries the meaning of assurance, dependability, and trustworthiness. "To become children of God" means that man possesses God's life and nature. Man is not born a child of God; only by receiving Christ can one obtain the right to become a child of God.

"who were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God." (John 1:13)

"Of" means out of; "blood" is blood (plural), referring to man's body of flesh and blood; "born" means to be begotten or brought forth (passive voice); "will of the flesh" is the will of the flesh.

"Born of blood" refers to man's body of flesh and blood. "Born of the will of the flesh" refers to what comes out of the corrupted will in man's natural life. "Born of the will of man" refers to what comes out of the good will in man's natural life; some commentators also consider that "the will of man" refers to help coming from man.

The Christian life is entirely spiritual, having absolutely nothing to do with man's flesh and blood or natural life.

"Not born of blood"—salvation cannot be passed down through bloodlines; we cannot say that because our parents are Christians, we are destined to be Christians by birth. "Nor of the will of the flesh"—salvation does not rely on the will of the flesh; although we need to make a decision when we are saved, a decision without repentance and faith still cannot save us. "Nor of the will of man"—salvation does not rely on the help of a preacher; although our hearing of the gospel is a form of help from others, without the illumination and moving of the Holy Spirit, it still cannot truly save us.

## 2. Grafted into Christ: The Wild Olive Grafted into the Cultivated Olive Tree

"You will say then, 'Branches were broken off that I might be grafted in.'" (Rom. 11:19) "Branches" refers to the unbelieving Jews.

"Well said. Because of unbelief they were broken off, and you stand by faith. Do not be haughty, but fear." (Rom. 11:20)

God can "graft in" and also "cut off"; He can cut off and graft in again (cf. v. 23). Everything is according to God's solemn sovereignty. This causes us to have a reverent heart, daring not to "boast" or be "haughty," but rather to "fear," looking solely to God's kindness.

Throughout history, the only requirement for fellowship with God has always been faith. Although good works, obedience, and love are all very important and are the proper expressions of a Christian's life, these are merely the fruit of faith, not the foundation of salvation.

Experience tells us that whenever we do not trust Him, but rely on ourselves, thinking we can manage, at that very moment our fellowship with God is severed; whenever we do not rely on ourselves, but depend closely on Him and draw upon all that He has accomplished for us, at that very moment we are able to stand.

"For if God did not spare the natural branches, He may not spare you either." (Rom. 11:21)

"Therefore consider the goodness and severity of God: on those who fell, severity; but toward you, goodness, if you continue in His goodness. Otherwise you also will be cut off." (Rom. 11:22)

"Consider the goodness and severity of God": God's heart is love, and God's deeds are righteous; love produces goodness, and righteousness manifests severity. In dealing with people of the world, God exercises both grace and majesty—severity within goodness, and goodness within severity. Our view of God must be balanced on both sides. If we overlook His goodness, God seems like a heartless tyrant; if we overlook His severity, He seems like an overly indulgent father.

How God treats a person depends entirely on how that person treats Christ. God is gracious to those who rely on Christ and love Christ, but severe to those who stumble by rejecting Christ and hating Christ. As long as something is not what the Lord desires or is incompatible with Him, He will never compromise or spare it! Oh, He is truly a severe Lord.

"And they also, if they do not continue in unbelief, will be grafted in, for God is able to graft them in again." (Rom. 11:23) "Do not continue in unbelief," literally translated from the original text, is "do not remain in unbelief," meaning repenting and believing that Jesus is the Messiah. This will take place at the Lord's second coming. "For God is able to graft them in again"—what is considered impossible with man is possible with God in all things.

"For if you were cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature, and were grafted contrary to nature into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these, who are natural branches, be grafted into their own olive tree?" (Rom. 11:24)

"Contrary to nature" shows that Paul knew this method of grafting was extraordinary. For the Gentiles to become members of God's household is indeed an extraordinary matter.

## 3. Incorporated into Christ: Sealed with the Holy Spirit

"In Him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; in whom also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise," (Eph. 1:13)

Literal translation of the original text: "In Him you also, having heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation in Him, having also believed, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise."

"Seal" refers to a brand, stamp, or seal. In those days, it signified ownership: Ephesus was near a port and conducted timber trade. Timber was cut from the mountains and floated down the streams to Ephesus. Merchants purchasing timber bought it on the mountain and stamped a "seal" with an iron hammer onto the purchased timber, and the buyer would then go downstream (to Ephesus) to claim his purchased timber according to the mark.

"The word of truth"—"Truth" in the original text is "true/truth"; in the universe, there is no truth apart from God. The word of truth is the word of truth, which reveals the God who is the truth of the universe to man, so that man may receive Him and see Him.

"The gospel of salvation"—Everything man gains on earth is false; only when one gains God does one gain reality. This is the gospel that brings salvation to man.

"Sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise"—Through faith, the Holy Spirit enters into us. This Holy Spirit is promised by God, planned and arranged by God, and is the sweetest thing in God's heart.

"Sealed with the Holy Spirit" has two meanings: (1) It serves as a sign that we belong to God, and that we are protected, cared for, governed, and directed by God; (2) In Paul's day, seals were engraved with words or patterns expressing the characteristics of the seal's owner; the seal of the Holy Spirit signifies that we have God's image, that we are like God, and that the people of the world can see God in us.

From this verse we can see: (1) Faith comes by hearing; (2) The word of truth can save people; (3) Christ Himself is the word of truth, and believing the word is believing in Christ; (4) Receiving the Holy Spirit occurs at the time of believing; there is no need to wait for a period of time after believing to receive the Holy Spirit.

Since believers have received God's seal—the Holy Spirit, they ought to submit to the Holy Spirit's management, rule, direction, and promptings in all things, and also trust in the Holy Spirit's help, teaching, and protection.

"Heard" and "believed in Him" express man's responsibility in preaching and receiving; man's salvation is the result of the cooperation between the objective means (the gospel) and the subjective means (faith).

"that the blessing of Abraham might come upon the Gentiles in Christ Jesus, that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith." (Gal. 3:14) If we believe in the redemptive grace of Christ Jesus, the curse immediately departs, and we can obtain two harvests: (1) Receiving the promised blessing obtained by Abraham through justification by faith; (2) Receiving the promised Spirit through faith.

## 4. Baptized into Christ

"Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death?" (Rom. 6:3)

"Baptized" should preferably be translated as "immersed" according to the original text, because the original meaning of this word is "to immerse into water, submerge under water, be overwhelmed by water." From this, it can be seen that the person being baptized is entirely submerged into water.

"Into" means entering into a new realm. There is only one condition for man to receive eternal life, and that is "faith"; but to be delivered from the power of sin, baptism must also be added. Baptism expresses through action an "entering" into another realm, thereby breaking free from the power of the original realm. We have been changed from being "in Adam" to being "in Christ" (1 Cor. 15:22), that is, conveyed from "the power of darkness" into the "kingdom of the Son of His love" (Col. 1:13). Baptism is the active declaration of this relocation.

"Baptized into His death"—The first meaning signified by baptism is being baptized into Christ's death; that is, acknowledging that just as Christ died, we who are baptized have also died together in Him. This death specifically refers to dying to sin, self, the world, Satan, and all negative things.

"Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death, that just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life." (Rom. 6:4)

"Buried with Him"—The second meaning signified by baptism is being buried together with Christ. Although the Bible does not explicitly give a clear pattern for the mode of baptism, from the descriptions "because there was much water there" and "came up out of the water," one can conceive that the person being baptized was entirely immersed in water. This mode corresponds precisely to the meaning of "burial" (being completely covered) mentioned in this verse.

"Burial" is clear proof of the fact of death. On one hand, it shows that since the baptized person has indeed been united into Christ's death, the past ought to be brought to an end; on the other hand, it shows that being buried in the earth is for the purpose of bearing new life.

"Even so we also should walk in newness of life"—The third meaning signified by baptism is having the newness of Christ's resurrection. After the baptized person's entire body is immersed into the water, he must also "come up out of the water"; since "water" signifies death, "coming up out of the water" signifies rising from the dead. Since death and burial remove and bring an end to things, rising again naturally means one is no longer the same as in the past. From then on, having received the new life of Christ, if we live by this life, we will naturally have a new walk of life. This is the newness of resurrection, which is a life of sanctification.

In the original text, the word "newness" is a noun and refers to something that did not exist before or is completely different from the old. This word is also used together with the word "life," expressing a new condition of life, emphasizing that the "newness" of the new life resurrected together with Christ refers to a spiritual or moral quality of life.

Regarding the new life obtained by a Christian, in terms of human sequential experience, it is "regeneration"; in terms of its nature and source, it is "born of God"; in terms of the manner in which it is manifested, it is "newness of life."

"Just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father"—"The glory of the Father" refers to God's manifestation; whenever God manifests Himself, He also displays His power. The resurrection of Christ was accomplished through God's power and also manifested God's glory.

The spiritual significance symbolized by the three steps of baptism is as follows: (1) Going down into the water—baptized into Christ's death; (2) Being submerged in the water—buried with Him; (3) Coming up out of the water—raised together with Him.

Since baptism is being united into Christ's death and buried together with Him, we who have been baptized should no longer return to our past way of living to live out the likeness of the old man. Without death there can be no life; if the natural does not die, the spiritual will not live; the more we are united in death, the more we will grow in life.

"For if we have been united together in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection," (Rom. 6:5)

Literal translation of the original text: "For if we have grown together with Him in the likeness of His death, certainly we also shall grow together with Him in the likeness of His resurrection."

"United" means planted together, grown together, growing together, grafted in. In "united together in the likeness of His death," the tense of this clause is perfect tense, so the word "if" points to an accomplished fact, conveying no doubt; "the likeness of His death" refers to the likeness of the Lord Jesus being crucified unto death, thus meaning being crucified with Him and dying with Him. Baptism signifies our union with Christ's death—the old man dying together with Christ.

"Certainly we also shall be in the likeness of His resurrection": the tense of this clause is future, yet it does not mean that we must wait until the future to be united with Him in the likeness of His resurrection; rather, it means that since the preceding fact of union in death exists, union in resurrection is certain. "The likeness of His resurrection" refers to that vivid likeness of Him who "was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore"; "united with Him in the likeness of His resurrection" means displaying the newness of life through His resurrection life. When we come up out of the water after baptism, it signifies our union with Christ's resurrection—the new man living together with Christ.

Baptism not only signifies "living together" with the Lord, but also signifies "growing together" with the Lord. There must first be the experience of "dying together" before there can be the experience of "living together." Today, some preachers never mention the message of the cross, but speak only of "living together with the Lord" and "living in the Spirit." This is a very dangerous thing, because one might be living according to the flesh while imagining they are living in an exalted spiritual realm.

"He has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love," (Col. 1:13)

"Delivered" means rescued, pulled out, and brought over; "power" (authority/domain) means ruling, being master; "conveyed" (translated/transferred) means relocated, moved away, transferred—in the original usage, it refers to the removal or transplantation of an entire people from one country or region to another. In ancient times, when one nation defeated another, it was customary to put the vanquished people in fetters and drive them into the territory of the conquering nation to reside.

"Delivered from the power of darkness" indicates being completely freed from the control of the power of darkness, severing all ties with Satan, the ruler of this world, and no longer being manipulated by him. "The kingdom of the Son of His love" indicates that this kingdom is established by the beloved Son and ruled by the beloved Son. "The Son of His love"—this phrase indicates that Christ is the center, the object, and the manifestation of God's love.

"in whom we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of sins." (Col. 1:14)

Literal translation of the original text: "In whom we have redemption through His blood, that is, the forgiveness of sins."

"Redemption" is buying out, the penalty-price, the ransom that sets a slave free; "sins" (transgressions) means missing the mark; "forgiveness" means causing to depart, canceling, remitting, blotting out, disregarding.

Christ's redemption: (1) The price: "Through His blood"—the shedding of blood is the evidence of bearing punishment, indicating that God's beloved Son bore the penalty of sin for us; (2) The meaning: "We have redemption"—it refers to how we, from a position condemned by God and deserving the penalty of sin, are bought back because the Lord Jesus paid the price with His blood; (3) The result: "The forgiveness of sins"—the ground of forgiveness is redemption; without redemption there is no forgiveness.

"Delivered" in verse 13 emphasizes being freed from the power of sin, while "redemption" in this verse emphasizes being exempt from the consequences of committing "sin."

Since God gave up His beloved Son to redeem us, this redemption is to save us to the extent that we become as lovely as His beloved Son. Both redemption and forgiveness are obtained "in the beloved Son"; in other words, if we depart from Christ, we cannot obtain redemption and forgiveness. After believers are saved, they may still sin and fall, but the efficacy of Christ's redemption has not ceased; the essential thing is that we must confess our sins and repent in order to restore fellowship with God.

In God's eyes, there are only two men: "The first man... the second Man..." (1 Cor. 15:47). The first man is the first Adam, who brought rebellion, condemnation, and death; the second Man is Jesus Christ, who brought obedience, justification, and eternal life. "For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive." (1 Cor. 15:22)

# Middle: Filled with the Holy Spirit

Being in Adam is objective; being in the flesh is subjective. The former pertains to position; the latter pertains to slavery (Rom. 7:14). God not only wants us to be freed from Adam, but God also wants to deliver us from the flesh. On the one hand, God wants us to sever ties with Adam, and on the other hand, He also wants us to crucify the flesh (Gal. 5:24). For since we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit (Gal. 5:25). Replace our own will with the inspiration of the Spirit, and take the things of the Spirit as primary. In this way, in the antagonism and warfare against the flesh (Gal. 5:17), we can overcome the flesh and live in the Holy Spirit. This is the salvation that God wants us to enjoy today: that we do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.

## I. Receiving the Law of the Spirit

"For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free from the law of sin and death." (Rom. 8:2)

Literal translation of the original text: "For the law of the Spirit of life..."

When we were still living in the flesh, we were bound by the law of sin in our members, unable not to sin, so that it bore the fruit of death; therefore, the law of sin is also called the law of death. But now, once we are moved from the flesh into Christ Jesus, where the Holy Spirit brings divine, uncreated life and releases an unchanging, constant power—what is called "the law of the Spirit of life"—we are easily freed from the law of sin and death, and have obtained release.

Romans 8:1–2 gives a clear account of how the Lord Jesus delivers us from the law of sin in our members:

(1) As long as we no longer struggle and strive on our own, but hide ourselves in Christ Jesus, we will not constantly be condemned by our conscience due to failure.
(2) Only when we can fully rest in Christ does His Spirit of life begin to work and produce effects.
(3) When the Spirit of life operates, it inevitably carries with it a constant and vast power; this power is called "the law of the Spirit of life."
(4) The "law of the mind" that we used to rely on stemmed from the "human" life that God originally gave when He created man, whereas the "law of sin in the members" stems from Satan's "demonic" life that usurped man's body. The power of demons is stronger than the power of man; therefore, it is no wonder that the law of the mind is often no match for the law of sin in the members.
(5) Only the "divine" life can overcome the "demonic" life. The "law of the Spirit of life" proceeding from God's life naturally overcomes the "law of sin and death" proceeding from the demonic life, enabling us to be delivered from "this body of death."

In the Holy Spirit there is life, and in life there is a law; the Holy Spirit, life, and the law—these three are three elements of one entity. If we want to enjoy the power of the "law" in life so that we can overcome sin and death, we must live and walk by the life of God; only in this way can the law in that life exert its efficacy.

The more God's life grows and matures within us, the more the power of the law can be manifested; therefore, if we want to be overcoming Christians, we must press on to pursue growth in life.

God's life is joined to the Spirit (cf. John 6:63); where the Spirit is, there life is also. The way for us to be saved in life lies in the Holy Spirit; in other words, the more we allow the Holy Spirit to move freely in us, the more we are saved in life.

If we do not live and walk under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, we are not living and walking in newness of life; and the Holy Spirit dwells in our spirit, so we must live in the spirit abundantly in order to understand the guidance of the Holy Spirit.

A "law" is a constant and unchanging power. The power generated by the human will can only temporarily overcome the power of a law, and will eventually be exhausted and surrender. We must counter "law" with "law," but the "law" in human life cannot overcome the "law" of sin and death; only the "law" of the Spirit of life of God can overcome it.

The "law" of the Spirit of life now dwells within us; the problem is that many believers do not know the treasure within themselves, but seek help from other external people, matters, and things.

The phrase "in Christ Jesus" is extremely crucial. Outside of the Lord, everything is vanity of vanities; even spiritual doctrines and knowledge are of no avail. Inside the Lord, all that God has belongs to our use—how rich!

If believers use their own resources, even if they exhaust all their efforts, they will certainly be defeated; but if they use the resources of life and power that already belong to them "in Christ Jesus," they can be more than conquerors.

Christ not only saved us once and for all from our sins (Matt. 1:21), but He also continues to deliver us daily and unceasingly from the law of sin and death.

"It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh profits nothing. The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life." (John 6:63)

"It is the Spirit who gives life"—after passing through death and resurrection, the Lord Jesus has now become the "life-giving Spirit"; the Christ whom believers receive gives life in the Spirit and serves as our life supply.

"The flesh profits nothing"—the Lord Jesus did not give us His physical, incarnate body to eat, because even if we actually ate His physical flesh, it would profit us nothing.

"The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life"—the "words" here refers to instantaneous, opportune, and subjective words (rhema); the words we read in the Bible are constant and objective words (logos), which must be turned into personal, subjective words to us before they can produce efficacy for us; at that time, the Lord's words are spirit and are life.

This verse shows that the Lord Jesus has now become the life-giving Spirit in resurrection, and this life-giving Spirit becomes concrete and real in His opportune words; if we believers receive the Lord's words with the right attitude, we will receive the supply of this Spirit of life.

The Lord has granted us two great gifts today: one is the Spirit dwelling within us, and the other is the Bible outside of us. The Spirit is the actual content of the Bible, and the Bible is the concrete description of the Spirit.

To read the Bible without contacting the Spirit within results in obtaining mere letters and forms; to speak solely of the Spirit while neglecting reading the Bible often leads to extremes, unrestrained by the truth. We ought to possess the right attitude, frequently studying the Lord's word and frequently contacting the Lord's Spirit; Spirit and word, word and Spirit, applied in balance—only in this way can we obtain the Lord's more abundant life.

## II. Obeying the Inward Inspiration of the Spirit

"Do not quench the Spirit." (1 Thess. 5:19)

"Quench" means to extinguish, pour water on, put out a fire, suppress, smother.

The Holy Spirit is like fire; operating within believers, He often moves people so that they are fervent in spirit, naturally treating others with warmth and serving with zeal; but if one does not obey His prompting, one will become cold or lukewarm.

Although the Holy Spirit burns like fire, it is still possible for Him to be quenched or extinguished by us; therefore, our cooperation as believers is needed. The more believers obey the Holy Spirit, the more the fire of the Holy Spirit is fanned into flame; if believers are hardened and resist the Holy Spirit, the fire of the Holy Spirit will be quenched.

"for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure." (Phil. 2:13)

Literal translation of the original text: "For it is God who works powerfully in you according to His good pleasure, so that you are able both to will and to work it out effectively."

"To will" is an inward resolving; "to do" is an outward performing. Combined, it means to work out outwardly according to what has been resolved in the heart.

"It is God who works in you"—indicates that not only the inward "willing," but also the outward "doing," are entirely based upon God's "working."

Do not be afraid of failing to work out your salvation, because the key lies with God. God's "working" comes first, followed by our "doing"; our power to live out salvation lies in God's working in our hearts.

God "exerts force" within us, and we "exert force" outward in accordance with Him—how effortless this is! But if God does not exert force, and we merely exert force by ourselves, how laborious it is!

Believers need only obey God's working within, because this working is of great power and might, and God will surely take full responsibility for us. God wants man to partner and cooperate with Him; therefore, on the one hand, there is God's mighty working, and on the other hand, there must be man's willing and doing. Many believers merely "will" according to God's working, yet rely solely on their own strength without "doing" according to God's working; as a result, failure is inevitable.

God's good pleasure is for us to live out from our being the salvation that we have received.

"Therefore do not be unwise, but understand what the will of the Lord is." (Eph. 5:17)

An "unwise person" is a person without understanding, a person devoid of common sense. From the words of this verse, we can know that a so-called "unwise person" is one who does not understand the will of the Lord.

Our living must take the Lord's will as its goal; all conduct outside the Lord's will is in vain, and only in the Lord's will can we redeem the time. A Christian's primary concern should be discerning what God wants him to do.

It is true that conduct comes from life, but having life still requires education so that conduct may be orderly and regulated. Although believers have God's life, they still need to understand God's will so that their walk may be worthy of God. God's will must be carefully examined in order to be known; a careless, negligent, and foolish person cannot understand it. Therefore, if we want to be spiritually discerning persons, we must redeem the time and make good use of precious time to understand the will of the Lord.

"And do not be drunk with wine, in which is dissipation; but be filled with the Spirit," (Eph. 5:18)

Literal translation of the original text: "And do not be drunk with wine, being caught up in which leads to unrestraint; but be filled with the Spirit."

Drunkenness is a temporary fervor that causes people to cross boundaries, act dissolutely, or ruin themselves, leaving them without restraint. "Be filled with the Spirit"—the opposite of drunkenness is being filled with the Holy Spirit; the Christian life should be a life lived out from being filled with the Spirit.

Regarding the verb "be filled," there are the following points to note: (1) It is passive: the Holy Spirit actively fills us, causing us to be controlled by the Holy Spirit and to act accordingly; (2) It is an imperative: although passive, we have the responsibility to "desire" and to "ask"; (3) It is in the present tense: indicating that we must continually and unceasingly be filled.

As a result of our being filled with the Holy Spirit, outwardly it may look somewhat like being drunk with wine, but in reality it is not like the unrestraint of drunkenness; a person who is truly filled with the Holy Spirit will certainly have his walk and conduct restrained by the Holy Spirit.

"Be filled" shows that it is not receiving a little bit, but receiving to the full; our receiving of the Holy Spirit is not just receiving a little bit, but receiving to the brim. D.L. Moody said, "In order to live an overcoming Christian life, we do not need to pull out inward sin, as long as we are filled with the Holy Spirit."

## III. Submitting to the Leading of the Spirit

"I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh." (Gal. 5:16)

"Walk" means walking, conducting oneself (present continuous tense, referring to daily routine, a continuing action).

If people choose to live in the spirit and let the Holy Spirit govern their daily conduct, they will not practice or satisfy the lust of the flesh, or continue to live in sin.

"For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God." (Rom. 8:14)

Being "led" by the Spirit of God refers to being guided, instructed, impelled, and controlled by the Holy Spirit; a person thus led by the Holy Spirit will certainly be able to put to death the deeds of the body and live a holy life well-pleasing to God.

Although we believers are distinguished as male and female according to the flesh, before God, all who obey the leading of the Holy Spirit are "sons" of God.

## IV. Setting the Mind on the Things of the Spirit

"For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit, the things of the Spirit." (Rom. 8:5)

This does not mean that walking according to the flesh is the same as minding the flesh. Walking according to the flesh is one thing, and minding the flesh is another; walking according to contains a passive sense of being compelled to do so, whereas minding contains an active sense of approval. In the original language, "to mind" is alternatively translated as "to set one's mind upon"; it is an inner assent and agreement in the heart and mind.

Now that we are free persons, we ought actively and proactively to set our minds on the right things—to set our minds on the things of the Spirit, not on the things of the flesh; that is, to set our minds on things above, not on things on the earth.

If we want to know whether a person walks according to the flesh or walks according to the Holy Spirit, we need only look at the things he sets his mind upon, whether they are of the flesh or of the Holy Spirit. What a person thinks in his heart reveals what kind of person he is.

Our minds hold the greatest sway over us. If our minds are set on other things instead of on the Holy Spirit, we can never walk according to the Holy Spirit. If we want to walk according to the Holy Spirit, we must set our minds upon Him and care for Him.

A mindset of the flesh has one characteristic: trusting in oneself—that is, knowing everything and being confident in everything. A spiritual mindset also has one characteristic: not trusting in oneself—that is, daring not speak presumptuously about anything, but doing everything with fear and trembling.

Whoever sets his mind on the Holy Spirit will naturally turn his actions toward spiritual things, and his life will naturally have a new goal and new affections.

The leading of the Spirit of God is not sporadic inspiration, but the normal experience of the believer and the principle by which the Christian life attains freedom.

"For he who sows to his flesh will of the flesh reap corruption, but he who sows to the Spirit will of the Spirit reap everlasting life." (Gal. 6:8)

Everything we do is "sowing," and everything will yield a "harvest"; the question is: whatever a man sows, that he will also reap. The spiritual life has certain laws: to walk and conduct oneself according to the flesh reaps corruption and death; to walk and conduct oneself according to the Holy Spirit reaps life and peace.

(Our daily priority is to manifest the power of the Holy Spirit, a power that operates through a life whose will is surrendered.)

# Part Two: Hidden in God the Father

## I. The Hope Hidden in God the Father

"looking for the blessed hope and glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ," (Titus 2:13)

Literal translation of the original text: "looking for the blessed hope, that is, the glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ."

"Looking for" means eagerly waiting with outstretched neck in fervent expectation.

"Looking for the blessed hope" means waiting expectantly for what has been received by faith. "And looking for the glorious appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ"—this phrase uses only one definite article, indicating that "God" and "Christ" here refer to the same Person; therefore, "our great God" can be viewed as demonstrating the deity of Jesus Christ. Moreover, nowhere in the New Testament is the appearing of God the Father mentioned; hence, some commentators believe that this refers to the appearing of the Lord Jesus Christ at His second coming.

The Lord Jesus is man and also God; His first appearing brought salvation to mankind, and His second appearing will bring the blessed hope to the believers who love Him. At Christ's first coming, He humbled Himself and stood in the place of sinners to bear the punishment for our sins; but His second coming will be full of glory, bringing us the blessed hope.

To a Christian, the present world is temporary, and evil forces run rampant within it; therefore, the Christian within it ought, on the one hand, to display the life of the new man in Christ, and on the other hand, to long for Christ's second coming, which will usher in a state of blessedness and glory.

## II. Hidden in God

"For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God." (Col. 3:3)

"Life" is spiritual life (*zoe*); "hidden" means buried, concealed, not outwardly displayed.

"For you died"—meaning that believers are no longer what they used to be, because the former old man has been crucified with Christ on the cross.

"Your life is hidden with Christ in God"—the believer's co-dying with Christ does not end there; it is followed by co-resurrection with Christ, receiving the divine life through regeneration. To the worldly, this regenerated life is something that cannot be seen, touched, or comprehended; hence, it is described by the word "hidden."

"Hidden in God"—meaning that this life is a matter hidden within the realm of God, a mystery that belongs entirely to God.

The people of the world cannot perceive any difference between the Christian's life and that of ordinary people, because the Christian's spiritual life is hidden. Living is the outworking of life; though life is hidden, the nature of life can still be expressed through one's living.

Believers ought to live out the life of God, bearing a glorious testimony for the Lord. Today, because the worldly cannot see the wondrous mystery of God's life within the Christian, it is no wonder that they often mock and despise us.

Since believers have already entered with Christ into a higher realm of life, our conduct and walk ought to be elevated to a higher level.

Since our life is "hidden with Christ in God," we ought to live together with Christ within God, taking God as the sphere of our living. God is in heaven; therefore, all who are hidden in God ought to be people living in heaven and must no longer "set their minds on things on the earth."

Our life is a life hidden in God; therefore, our living should also be a hidden living. For Christians who live by this life, many things they do are known only to God and are unknown to men. True Christians do not promote themselves or recommend themselves; they proclaim the gospel and recommend Christ.

"knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin." (Rom. 6:6)

"Done away with" means to render null and void, to make ineffective, to put out of employment.

"Knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him"—the "old man" refers to our natural fallen disposition inherited from Adam, which was corrupted by deceitful lusts. When Christ was crucified on the cross, our old man was also crucified with Him. In history, this is an accomplished fact, but the eyes of our hearts need to be opened to see this glorious fact. This spiritual seeing is what is meant by "knowing." If we do not have this seeing and knowing, we will attempt to reform our old man, which only results in stirring up the old man so that it lives out its corrupt deeds.

"That the body of sin might be done away with, that we should no longer be slaves of sin"—the "body of sin" refers to the physical body by which the old man lives and acts. Since the old man is corrupted, the deeds of its body are inevitably full of sinful defilement; hence, it is called the "body of sin." Since our old man has been crucified on the cross, sin, which reigns within man as king, can no longer enslave our old man to live out sinful deeds. Without the old man's cooperation and submission to its drive, the "body of sin" naturally loses its efficacy, lying paralyzed there and powerless.

If our physical body lives and acts under the command of the old man, it is the "body of sin"; if it lives and acts under the command of the new man, it is a holy body.

Many believers are completely mistaken in their experience: they continue to sin, cannot break free from sin's dominion, and their bodies are still as busily driven by sin as ever. This is because although the Lord has accomplished a complete way of salvation for us, we have not received His work, nor believed what He has accomplished, nor appropriated His victory by faith.

"For he who has died has been freed from sin." (Rom. 6:7)

Our release from the power of sin is through death; sin can no longer have dominion over someone who has died. This verse shows us three things: (1) Sin is a living power, like a master; (2) The old man is the slave of sin, habitually commanded and directed by sin, but is freed the moment he dies; (3) The body of sin is the instrument through which the old man acts; once the old man is dead, although the body of sin still exists, it is out of a job and has nothing to do.

What is vital is having the spiritual seeing to "know" the fact that the old man has died with Christ.

The Lord's substitutionary death on the cross delivers us from the penalty of sin; the Lord's co-death on the cross delivers us from the bondage of sin.

The Christian's way of deliverance from the bondage of sin is not through desperate struggles, nor through fighting and striving against sin, but through "death."

"Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him," (Rom. 6:8)

The word "if" here carries no negative connotation of "doubt," but rather contains the positive sense of "based upon this, it is proven that." "We believe that we shall also live with Him"—"believe" means to have faith; the "believing" here, like the "knowing" mentioned earlier, refers to spiritual seeing. However, "knowing" places more emphasis on seeing an accomplished fact, whereas "believing" places more emphasis on seeing a fact that is to be fulfilled hereafter; thus, "believing" carries the connotation of "trusting."

Without death there is no life; life comes only after death. We first see and experience the fact of co-dying with Christ, and only then do we see and experience the fact of co-living with Christ. Co-dying is negative, while co-living is positive; co-dying is meant to usher in co-living, and without co-living, co-dying would have little meaning.

"knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more. Death no longer has dominion over Him." (Rom. 6:9)

"Knowing" indicates that the reason we have the "believing" of the previous verse is because we have the "knowing" of this verse—that is, seeing in the spirit that Christ once died and is now alive, living forever and ever; therefore, we are confident that since death no longer has dominion over Him, we also believe death cannot have dominion over us. Whoever is in Christ has been set free from the power of death.

Our old man has two masters: one is sin, and the other is death. The experience of co-dying with Christ delivers us from sin; the experience of co-living with Christ delivers us from death.

"For the death that He died, He died to sin once for all; but the life that He lives, He lives to God." (Rom. 6:10)

"For the death that He died, He died to sin"—meaning that His death was to resolve the problem of sin; sin can no longer exercise dominion over one who has died. "Once for all" means only this once; it shows that His death has an once-for-all, eternal efficacy, and the dealing with sin is an eternally unchangeable fact.

"He lives to God"—meaning that God is the meaning and purpose of His living; "lives" in the original text has the sense of living forever; henceforth, He lives forever and ever.

"Likewise you also, reckon yourselves to be dead indeed to sin, but alive to God in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Rom. 6:11)

Literal translation of the original text: "Likewise you also, calculate yourselves to be dead to sin, but calculate yourselves to be alive to God in Christ Jesus."

The word "reckon" here in the original means "to calculate" or "to enter into an account"; since we already know and believe the fact of co-dying and co-living with the Lord, we must enter it into the ledger of our lives, verifying it accurately without error. Often, our seeing of an objective fact is one thing, and our subjective experience of that fact is another; therefore, we need to apply faith to compute the facts God has accomplished until they are realized in our life experience. And this reckoning of faith is to reckon ourselves dead to sin and alive to God; sin can do nothing more to us, and from now on, we have our own Master—the "God of the living."

Whenever we look at ourselves, we are still alive; but as soon as we reckon in Christ, we are dead. Therefore, we should not "look" at ourselves, but only "reckon" in Christ. If we look at the condition of our own living self, we will be under the bondage of sin and commit sin; if we reckon the fact of having died in Christ, we will be free from the power of sin and will not commit sin.

God reckons us as having died to sin; this is a fact. God has so reckoned, and we also need to reckon. Reckoning ourselves to be dead does not mean that we are not actually dead and are merely regarding it as dead, treating it as dead, imagining it to be dead, or treating it "as though yesterday were dead." Rather, it is because the death of the old man is something God has already done, and therefore I reckon it as true. Reckoning means: God reckons that I have already died, it is settled on God's part, and so I also reckon myself to be dead. God says I have been crucified; I believe this word and confess that I have died.

After verse 6 tells us the fact, verse 11 tells us what we ought to do. Verse 11 teaches us how to practice the way of verse 6. This way is to reckon what the Lord Jesus has done as real, and to accept the work of the Lord's cross in dealing with our bodies.

There must first be the seeing of "co-dying" in verse 6 before the "reckoning" of verse 11 can be effective.

Believers are alive only "in Christ Jesus"; apart from Him, we can do nothing, because we are dead.

## III. Entering into Glory

"For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and by whom are all things, in bringing many sons to glory, to make the captain of their salvation perfect through sufferings." (Heb. 2:10)

"Captain" means author, leader, commander, pioneer, forerunner; "perfect" means completed, accomplished; "fitting" means appropriate, suitable.

"For it was fitting for Him, for whom are all things and by whom are all things"—this phrase refers to God who created all things. All things were created for God, so all things in the universe belong to Him; all things also originate from Him, and He is the source of all things.

"In bringing many sons to glory"—"many sons" refers to all who through faith have become the sons of God across all ages; "to glory" refers to being enabled to be forever with God in the glory of God's manifestation.

"To make the captain of their salvation"—the "captain of their salvation" refers to Jesus Christ, the Author and Pioneer of salvation. He not only accomplished salvation for us, but He also leads us to fully attain the ultimate goal of salvation.

"Perfect through sufferings, was fitting"—"to make perfect" does not mean that His divine attributes and moral character were deficient and needed to pass through suffering to reach perfection; rather, it means that He needed to personally undergo suffering in order to have a complete human experience. "Was fitting" indicates that having had such experience, He is suitable to be the Captain of our salvation, leading us into glory.

He is the One "for whom are all things and by whom are all things"; not only do we exist for Him, but we also can only exist through Him; all that we are and all that we have should be for Him and through Him.

If anyone wants to enter into glory, he must first become a child of God, because the realm of God's glory is prepared for His children.

In order for the Lord Jesus to become the Captain of salvation for God's many children and lead them into glory, He Himself had to be made perfect through sufferings; we who follow Him must also go through many tribulations to enter the kingdom of God.

The Lord Jesus is the Captain of our salvation. The path He is to lead us on is one He has walked first Himself. Therefore, He knows clearly the circumstances that will be encountered along this path and has made full preparation for us. We need only follow in His footsteps, and we will surely reach the destination. Since He is the Captain, He will certainly take full responsibility for us, resolve every difficulty of those who follow Him, and supply all our needs. But we must completely obey the Captain's commands in faith, and constantly draw upon His supply through prayer.

"and if children, then heirs—heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with Him, that we may also be glorified together." (Rom. 8:17)

"And if children, then heirs"—"Children," "sons," and "heirs" carry slightly different meanings: "Children" refers to the fundamental position we have by virtue of life once we are born again; every person who believes in the Lord has the authority to become a child of God. "Sons" refers to a further stage of expression in life for God's children; believers, regardless of male or female, may become sons of God, and all who overcome in life shall become the "male child." "Heirs" refers to growing up and maturing in life, recognized by God as legally qualified to inherit the glory that shall be revealed in the future.

As soon as believers are saved, they have the consciousness of being children of God; but only those who are led by the Spirit of God are sons of God; and they must grow into full maturity to become heirs of God and inherit the inheritance.

"—heirs of God"—Since we are children of God, we all have the sonship; sooner or later, we will all become heirs of God in the future. "and joint heirs with Christ"—In the future we will inherit all things together with Christ. He is bringing many sons into glory; therefore, it says here that we are joint heirs with Christ.

"—if indeed we suffer with Him, that we may also be glorified together"—This Pioneer who leads us into glory was made perfect through sufferings. Therefore, we who follow Him should also have a mind to suffer and suffer with Him, so that we may also be glorified together with Him. "Suffer with Him" means being conformed to His death, denying self, disregarding the outward body's decay and affliction, wholeheartedly dealing with sin, rejecting Satan's temptations, and learning obedience to God.

"Children" is our identity, speaking of our relationship of life with God as Father and child; "heirs" is our blessing, speaking of our ability to inherit all of God's fullness, especially the ultimate glory. This is not our own illusion, but what the Scriptures state, and what the Holy Spirit seals.

"Children" in the first half of this verse is unconditional, while "heirs" in the second half is conditional—that is, suffering together with Christ: "if indeed we suffer with Him, that we may also be glorified together."

The indwelling and working of the Holy Spirit is the guarantee of our receiving the spiritual, glorious inheritance, and further enables us to foretaste the inheritance we are to receive in the future.

The identity of a glorious heir is "children of God." This speaks to the fact that only the divine life and the mystical union are the sole foundation for entering into glory; everything of the flesh, of the carnal, or of human will has no place here whatsoever.

The path of a glorious heir is to "suffer together... be glorified together." This shows that only the cross is the sole path to reaching glory. If we are to truly enjoy the blessing of being heirs, we cannot escape the death of the cross. As it was with the Firstborn, so it cannot be otherwise with the many sons, for this is God the Father's eternal ordination.

We may not like suffering, but we need suffering. Remember that suffering is grace in disguise. We should not be troubled because of suffering. If we suffer with Him, we shall also be glorified with Him.

"For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us." (Rom. 8:18)

Concerning the sufferings believers currently experience, the Apostle Paul describes them as "momentary and light," while the glory that shall be revealed in us in the future is "a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory" (2 Cor. 4:17). The difference between the two is beyond measure.

The meaning of this verse is that the sufferings of this present time are simply not worthy to be compared with the future glory.

If the future glory subtracts the present sufferings, the remaining glory is so vast that it cannot be calculated; therefore, today's sufferings are not worthy of concern.

The glory mentioned here, in the original text, is "the glory that is coming to be revealed in us." Therefore, this glory is not a sudden temporal transformation that we must simply wait for; rather, it is an ever-growing spiritual reality that we can experience daily, reaching its full consummation only on that Day. Reality tells us that whenever we truly experience the suffering of the death of the cross, we genuinely sense the glory of Christ.

If we truly have this revelation of "the glory which shall be revealed in us," how wonderfully our feelings and our "calculating" will transcend the "sufferings" we endure, enabling us to triumphantly declare likewise: "They are not worthy to be compared!"

## IV. Becoming One

"I in them, and You in Me; that they may be made perfect in one, and that the world may know that You have sent Me, and have loved them as You have loved Me." (John 17:23)

"That they may be made perfect in one"—"Perfect" in the original text means "being perfected." This statement acknowledges a reality: although Christians possess the life of oneness, they still lack the expression of oneness, and thus still await perfection; the full expression of oneness awaits the complete obedience of you and me to the Lord who indwells us.

"And that the world may know that You have sent Me"—The purpose of believers' oneness is "that the world may know," which goes deeper than "that the world may believe." When believers manifest complete oneness, that is also when Christ is fully glorified in all the believers; at that time, the world will not only believe, but they will surely know that Christ was sent by God.

"And have loved them as You have loved Me"—The oneness of believers has an evangelistic purpose, which is not only related to the mission of the Lord Jesus, but also to God's love for humanity and for Christ. In other words, God's love plays a crucial role in the matter of believers' oneness.

Only by allowing Christ to reign and be Lord inside every one of us believers can we be perfected into true oneness. The oneness of Christians is a glorious testimony that will surely have a tremendous impact on the world. God's love is the driving force of believers' oneness; genuine oneness will inevitably manifest God's love.

"Him we preach, warning every man and teaching every man in all wisdom, that we may present every man perfect in Christ Jesus." (Col. 1:28)

"All" [wisdom] means everything; "every man" means all people, every single person; "perfect" means adult, mature, complete.

"Preaching" includes "warning" and "teaching": "warning" is to correct or keep from straying from the right way; "teaching" is to explain the truth.

"Perfect"—The word "perfect" was originally terminology used in heretical teachings, referring to those who "were initiated into all the secret mysteries," carrying a boastful connotation. Paul uses it to refer to believers in Christ, that everyone can reach the state of being perfect in God's eyes.

Christ is the theme and content of the preaching message; any sermon without Christ is merely an ordinary speech, flattering words, and empty talk.

Preaching Christ requires the exercise of "all wisdom"; any preaching that is prepared without spending heart and effort is derelict preaching.

Preachers must not be like spiders, constantly spinning webs without endurance; nor should they be like ants, only knowing how to store up materials without knowing how to use them; rather, they should be like bees, working hard to collect nectar while knowing how to brew it into their own honey, wisely turning the Word of God into spiritual food that supplies.

Preachers must not only focus on public proclamation from the pulpit, but also pay attention to warning and teaching each individual in private.

Preachers do not present people before themselves, but "present them to God"; and they do not present people in an adequate or slipshod manner, but present them "perfect."

Our being saved is not the finish line, but the beginning of the road leading to God.

The previous verse says Christ is in us, and this verse says we are in Christ; the more believers live in Christ, the more Christ is manifested in us; the more He is manifested in us, the more we live in Him.

## Conclusion

The final consummation of complete salvation is that the creature enters into the Creator.

Speaking from past position, we were saved out of Adam into Christ; speaking from present experience, we are saved out of the flesh into the Holy Spirit; speaking from final consummation, we are saved out of the created man into the Creator God.

Although this mystery still awaits future fulfillment, we already experience it partially here on earth today.

## Responsive Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You for saving us out of Adam into Christ, and out of the flesh into the Holy Spirit. Please grant that each day I may reckon myself to be dead to sin and alive to You; walk according to the Spirit, mind the things of the Spirit; and in hope, wait for the manifestation of Your glory, making us completely one, and presenting us before You. Amen!
