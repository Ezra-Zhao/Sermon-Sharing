"And though I bestow all my goods to feed the poor, and though I give my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing." (1 Cor. 13:3)

"Bestow all my goods to feed the poor" refers to giving up material possessions, but the motive does not stem from loving one's neighbor as oneself; rather, it arises out of vanity, to draw people's attention and win human praise.

"And though I give my body to be burned"—"give my body" in the original text means handing over one's own body, that is, sacrificing oneself; "to be burned" in the original text is similar to "that I may boast," thus some say it means laying down one's life for vanity.

"But have not love, it profits me nothing"—Paul's meaning in this verse is that giving up material possessions, and even sacrificing oneself, such noble moral manifestations do not equate to "love." If not motivated by love, they cannot add to my profit before God.

Here it is clearly pointed out that human good works and God's love are two different things. In God's love there will certainly be good works, but human good works do not necessarily stem from God's love. If we pursue God's love, good works will naturally follow; if we only pursue good works, at best we are no different from unsaved moral gentlemen, possessing no spiritual value before God.

A philanthropic spirit and a martyr's spirit are not necessarily love; God Himself alone is the source of love. Giving alms and self-denial often increase the element of "self"; only almsgiving and self-sacrifice born of love will increase the element of the "Lord."

A "self" without God's love is the greatest enemy—everything is for me; a self that has God's love does everything for God, and naturally there is no more self.

Verses 1 to 3 show us that no matter what I can speak, what I know, what I can do, or what I am willing to give up, if I do not have love, it is entirely useless—it remains of no profit to others, and counts for nothing to myself.

God is love, and so God's children and God's people also love; if there is no love, they do not resemble God's people. It is only normal for children to resemble their parents; otherwise, it is abnormal.

Verses 1 to 3 also show us the three major branches of Christianity today, each having its own focus: the Charismatics emphasize the gift of speaking in tongues; the Fundamentalists emphasize doctrine, knowledge, and faith; the Social Gospel movement emphasizes self-sacrifice and helping others. Yet without love, all is empty and profitless—regardless of the direction.

## 1. Love Is Living the Way God Commands Us

"This is love, that we walk according to His commandments. This is the commandment, that as you have heard from the beginning, you should walk in it." (2 John 6)

Literal translation from the original text: "This is love, that we walk according to His commandments; this is the commandment, just as you have heard from the beginning, that you should walk in it."

"According to" means in accordance with, by means of; "walk" means to conduct oneself, to walk; "love" is benevolent love, dear love, charity (agape); "should walk in" is for practicing and obeying.

"This is love, that we walk according to His commandments"—"His commandments" in the original text is a plural noun, referring to all of God's commandments in the Bible; "this is love" expresses two characteristics of love: (1) obedient love—"according to His commandments"; (2) practicing love—"walk."

"This is the commandment, that as you have heard from the beginning, you should walk in it"—"heard... that you should walk in it" emphasizes once again the practice of love; "this commandment" in the original text is singular, referring to the commandment that you love one another (cf. John 13:34).

Love is not an uncontrolled emotion; true love must "walk according to His commandments"—that is to say, it must continuously be lived out within the standards and boundaries of God's commandments.

Authentic biblical love must be governed by God's various commandments and does not permit transgressing any single one of them; any behavior that conflicts with God's truth and moral law is by no means love.

Let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth (1 John 3:18). Practicing mutual love is practicing the Lord's love, and practicing the Lord's love is obeying the Lord's commandments.

We must be doers of the word, and not hearers only (Jas. 1:22); if we hear the word and do it, actually carrying it out, we will be blessed in what we do (Jas. 1:25).

Following God is a very joyful and blessed thing because it is in love. One must possess both this logic and this experience: brothers must know it rationally and also believe it; sisters, while having feelings, must also have knowledge, so that having known, they will not drift near and far, or rise and fall erratically based on feelings.

## 2. Life Is All About Love

Because God is love, the lesson He most desires us to learn on earth is how to love. When we love, we are most like Him; therefore, love is the foundation of all the commandments God has given us: "For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.'"

Having love is like clearing the meridian channels; it is like having a huge bank deposit (the source is with God, not within ourselves). When there is love, all laws are benevolent, possessing a soul and the warmth of humanity. With love, many touching stories can take place, entering into a virtuous cycle where love inspires love, and more love gushes forth.

Learning to love unselfishly is not an easy task, because it goes against our self-centered nature, so it takes a lifetime of effort to learn. God wants us to love everyone, but He especially wants us to love the members of God's family; this is the second purpose of our life. Peter said, "Love the brotherhood of believers," and Paul echoed this view, saying, "Whenever we have the opportunity to help others we should do it, but we should give special care to those who belong to the family of believers."

Why does God insist that we give special love and care to other believers? Why should we love them first? Because God desires His family to be known for love. Jesus said our best witness to the world is not the dogmas of our faith, but that we love one another. He said, "By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another."

In heaven we will enjoy God's family forever, but first on earth we need to strive to prepare ourselves for that eternal love. God gives us some "family responsibilities" to train us, the most important of which is learning to love one another.

God wants us to be in regular, close fellowship with other believers to learn the skills of love. One cannot learn to love in isolation; you must be among people—only by interacting with irritating, imperfect, and frustrating people can you learn. Through fellowship life, we learn three important truths.

## 3. Love Is the Best Use of Life

Love should be your top priority, your primary objective, and your greatest ambition. Love is not merely a "good" part of your life, but the "most important" part. The Bible says: "Make love your greatest aim." It is not enough to simply say, "One of my life goals is love"; that makes it sound as if love is just one of your top ten goals. Relationships must be first in your life. Why?

### 3.1 Life Without Love Is Worthless

Paul points out: "No matter what I say, what I believe, or what I do, without love, I have nothing." We often treat relationships as dispensable. We talk about finding time for our kids or carving out time to see friends. This makes relationships sound like just another calendar entry in life. But God says that relationships are what life is all about.

Four of the Ten Commandments deal with our relationship with God, while the other six deal with our relationships with people, but all ten commandments are about relationships! Jesus summarized what matters most to God as "loving God" and "loving people." He said, "‘You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind.’ This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like it: ‘You shall love your neighbor as yourself.’ On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets." Learning to love God (worship), and then learning to love others, is the second purpose of your life.

The most important thing in life is not achievement or wealth, but relationships. Why, then, do we so often allow our relationships to be sacrificed? When we are busy, we skimp on relationships, cutting back on the time, energy, and effort spent on loving relationships. What God considers most important has been supplanted by other urgent matters.

Busyness is the greatest enemy of relationships; our lives are often crammed full of earning a living, work, bills, and goals, as if these things were the point of life. In reality, the point of life is learning to love—loving God and loving people! Life minus love equals zero.

### 3.2 Love Lasts Forever

Another reason God wants us to make love our top priority is that love lasts forever. "And now abide faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love." Love leaves a legacy; how you treat other people, not your wealth or accomplishments, is the most enduring impact you can leave on earth. Mother Teresa said, "It is not what we do, but how much love we put into doing it." Love is the secret to what lasts forever.

I have sat at the bedside of many people in their final moments. When facing the edge of eternity, I have never heard anyone say, "Bring my diplomas; I want to look at them one more time. Bring me my awards, medals, and gold watches that I won in the past." When life is nearing its end, what people want is not to be surrounded by things, but to be surrounded by people—those we love and have relationships with. At the final moments of life, we all realize that relationships are what human life is all about. Those who learn this truth as early as possible are wise. Do not wait until you are on your deathbed to realize that relationships are what matter most.

### 3.3 God Evaluates Us Based on How We Love

The third reason to make learning to love the goal of your life is that God will evaluate us on how we loved. One of the ways God evaluates our spiritual maturity is by the quality of our relationships. In heaven, God will not say, "Tell me about your career, your bank balance, and your hobbies." Instead, He will examine how you treated people, especially those in need. Jesus said that to love Him is to love His family and care for their practical needs: "‘Inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these My brethren, you did it to Me.’"

When you step into eternity, you must leave everything behind; all you can take with you is your character. That is why the Bible says: "The only thing that counts is faith expressing itself through love."

That being so, I suggest that every morning when you get up, you kneel or sit by your bed and pray: "God, whether I get anything else done today, I want to make sure that I spend time loving You and loving people—because that is what life is all about. I don't want to waste this day." Why should God give you another day if you are only going to waste it?

## 4. The Best Expression of Love Is Time

The importance of something can be measured by how much time we are willing to invest in it. The more time you spend on something, the more you reveal its importance and value to you. If you want to know what a person values, just look at how they spend their time.

Time is your most precious gift because you only have a set amount of it in a lifetime. You can earn more money, but you cannot produce more time. Therefore, when you give someone your time, you are giving them a portion of your life that you will never get back, because time is life. So, time is the greatest gift you can give anyone.

Merely saying "relationships are important" with words is not enough; we must prove it by investing time in them—words alone are useless. "Let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth." Relationships take time and effort, and the best way to express love is to give time!

The essence of love is not what we think or do or provide for others, but how much of ourselves we give away. Most men do not understand this; many say to me, "I don't understand my wife and kids. I provide everything they need. What more do they want?" They want you! Your eyes, your ears, your time, your care, your presence, your focus! Nothing else can take the place of that.

The most desired gift of love is not diamonds, roses, or chocolate, but focused attention. Love is so intently focused on another person that for that moment, you forget yourself. Attention says, "You are so valuable that I do not hesitate to give you my most precious asset—my time." Whenever you give your time, you are making a sacrifice, and sacrifice is the very essence of love. Jesus set an example for us: "And walk in love, as Christ also has loved us and given Himself for us, an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweet-smelling aroma."

You can "give" without "love," but you cannot "love" without "giving." "God so loved the world that He gave..." Love means letting go—giving up your own preferences, comfort, goals, security, money, energy, or time for the benefit of someone else.

## 5. The Best Time to Love Is Now

Procrastination is sometimes the best way to handle trivial matters, but because love is the most important, it must be done immediately. The Bible repeatedly stresses: "As we have opportunity, let us do good to all," "take advantage of every opportunity to do good," and "Do not withhold good from those to whom it is due, when it is in the power of your hand to do so. Do not say to your neighbor, 'Go, and come back, and tomorrow I will give it,' when you have it with you."

Why is now the best time to express love? Because you do not know how many opportunities you will have left. Circumstances change, people die, children grow up, and you have no guarantee of tomorrow. If you want to express love, you had better act right now. Since you know that one day you will stand before God, you must seriously consider the following questions: How will you explain those times when you valued work projects or certain tasks more than people? Who is the person you need to spend more time with? What things do you need to cut from your schedule to make that possible? What sacrifices do you need to make?

Love is the best use of life, the best expression of love is time, and the best time to love is now.

## Chapter Reflection

**Point to Ponder**: Life is all about love.

**Memory Verse**: "For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.'" (Gal. 5:14)

**Reflection Question**: Ask yourself: Are relationships the most important thing in my life? How can I ensure their priority?

## Prayer of Response

Dear Father, thank You, for You are love. Please forgive me for the days when I used busyness as an excuse and pushed relationships aside. Grant that every morning when I rise, I may resolve to spend time loving You and loving others—giving time to those I love, and giving focus to those in need, because love is the best use of life, and the best time to love is now. Amen!
