# Faith Q&A Question 3: Can a Person Who Is Truly Born Again and Saved Blaspheme the Holy Spirit?

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «一个真正重生得救的人可能亵渎圣灵吗？».

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 3
**Adaptation:** Guangyi Zhao (retold in his own preaching style; the core views belong to the original author)

---

## Scripture

> "Do not quench the Spirit." (1 Thess. 5:19, NKJV)

> "And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom you were sealed for the day of redemption." (Eph. 4:30, NKJV)

> "'You stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears! You always resist the Holy Spirit; as your fathers did, so do you.'" (Acts 7:51, NKJV)

---

## I. The Answer First: It Is Impossible

Pastor Tong answers this question very crisply: **a person who is truly born again and saved cannot blaspheme the Holy Spirit.**

The logic is simple — the new birth is the work of the Holy Spirit. Since the Holy Spirit has already regenerated him, that means this person has already received the grace of forgiveness. **He will not regenerate a person and then take the forgiveness back from him.**

This question is in fact the natural extension of the previous one: Question 2 explained that blaspheming the Holy Spirit = rejecting to the end of one's life the forgiving grace the Holy Spirit applies; this question follows on: will a person who has already received that grace still do so? No.

---

## II. But Christians Really Do "Offend" the Holy Spirit — Look at the Levels

The matter does not end there. If blasphemy is impossible, does that mean Christians never have friction with the Holy Spirit? Of course not. Pastor Tong says that **"offending the Holy Spirit" has levels**, and Scripture names at least three levels, spoken to Christians:

**Level one: quenching the Spirit (1 Thess. 5:19)**
The Greek is ***sbennymi* (σβέννυμι)** — to put out, to extinguish, like pouring water on a fire. The Holy Spirit is at work in your heart, and you pour a basin of cold water over it: ignoring, disobeying, pretending not to hear. Paul says this **to the church** — it is a sin a Christian can commit.

**Level two: grieving the Holy Spirit (Eph. 4:30)**
The Greek is ***lupeō* (λυπέω)** — to cause sorrow, to cause grief. Note the emotional color of this word: you can only grieve someone who **loves you and is close to you**. A stranger will not grieve over you. **Grief presupposes a relationship.** This verse actually proves the point: the Holy Spirit counts you as one of His own; you hurt His heart and He is grieved — but He will not leave you because of it. The verse says "by whom you were sealed"; the seal is still there.

**Level three: resisting the Holy Spirit (Acts 7:51)**
The Greek is ***antipiptō* (ἀντιπίπτω)** — to collide with, to oppose, to push back head-on. When Stephen rebuked those religious leaders, saying "as your fathers did, so do you," he was pointing at precisely this state: **stiff-necked, uncircumcised in heart and ears**.

Romans 8:6 draws the same contrast: "For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace." The very fact that Paul wrote these words shows that **a Christian may fail to obey** — otherwise, to whom was he writing?

**But note the watershed:** quenching the Spirit's moving, grieving the Holy Spirit, resisting — a Christian may commit any of these. But at the level of **blaspheming the Holy Spirit and rejecting the salvation the Holy Spirit applies**, Pastor Tong is very clear: **that is what non-Christians do, not what Christians do.**

In one sentence: **offending the Holy Spirit runs from shallow to deep; only those standing outside the door of grace go as deep as blasphemy.**

---

## III. Are You Afraid? Your Fear Proves You Have Not Done It

This is Pastor Tong's comfort for the fearful, and it is beautifully put:

**A person who has truly blasphemed the Holy Spirit is a person who no longer fears blaspheming the Holy Spirit.**

Conversely: **if you fear that you have committed this sin, that proves you have not committed it.**

His illustration is direct: a person who fears death proves he is not dead yet — how would a dead man be afraid? By the same reasoning, a person who still fears blaspheming the Holy Spirit shows that the Holy Spirit's moving is still in him, that he still has feeling, that he still has hope.

This comfort is not permission to keep sinning; it is meant to help you **rise out of the devil's bondage and stand firm in the truth**. For the thing the devil loves most is to make earnest seekers live in groundless fear.

---

## IV. The Devil's Two Kinds of Bondage

Pastor Tong names two of the devil's methods with seekers, both worth remembering:

**Bondage one: oversensitivity — great fear over small faults, unable to tell big from small.**
To be greatly sensitive to small sins is the mark of a saint; to be insensitive to great sins is the mark of an unclean person. A saint's sensitivity to small faults is right. But the devil will push that sensitivity too far: terrified half to death over a small fault, living in fear, accepting the devil's accusations, unable to rise up again.

Pastor Tong gives an example: some young people fear marriage, thinking that the physical relationship between husband and wife after marriage is unholy and does not glorify God. **That idea is oversensitive.** Sex was created by God, and God permits people to enjoy it; **lust and illicit passion are wrong, but desire within marriage is permitted.** Do not treat what God created as unclean.

**Bondage two: mistaking the shadow for the substance — treating a sin one has not committed as a sin one has committed.**
This is an illusion many earnest seekers have: not a deliberate intention to sin, but the negative result of oversensitivity. You kneel down wanting to pray, and the devil whispers in your ear: "No use. You have already blasphemed the Holy Spirit; you will not be forgiven." — Pastor Tong says he knows this kind of suffering.

His answer: **do not rely on yourself; rely on God.**

---

## V. For Us Today

1. A person who is born again will not blaspheme the Holy Spirit — the Holy Spirit will not push out again a person He Himself has regenerated
2. But do not use this as a license: quenching the Spirit's moving, grieving the Holy Spirit, disobeying His guidance — a Christian can still commit these
3. If you fear over this, that fear itself is evidence — where there is still feeling, there is still hope
4. Do not fall for the devil's trick: oversensitivity is not holiness, and mistaking a shadow for the substance is not confession
5. Kneel down and pray; do not listen to that voice saying "No use"
