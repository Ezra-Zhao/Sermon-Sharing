## Content

How is the problem of sin solved? The problem of life is inward; the problem of sin is outwardly exposed. The former concerns essence; the latter concerns character. In His work of salvation, God on the one hand solves for us that corrupt problem of life, and on the other hand also solves for us that unbearable problem of sin. The so-called problem of sin is the problem of behavior and living. How does God solve these problems, so that salvation is manifested in us?

Justification (by grace (Titus), relying on the blood (Rom.), by faith (Rom.), given (Rom.), imputed (Rom.)). 1. Cleansing from sin: "He had by Himself purged our sins, [and] sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high" (Heb. 1:3, NKJV). 2. Forgiveness of sins: "everyone who believes in Him receives remission of sins [through His name]" (Acts 10:43, NKJV). 3. Bearing sins: "who Himself bore our sins in His own body on the tree" (1 Pet. 2:24, NKJV). 4. Covering sins: "Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered" (Ps. 32:1, NKJV). 5. Blotting out sins: "Repent therefore and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out" (Acts 3:19, NKJV). The meaning of justification is to declare [one] not guilty, or, to count [one] as righteous. How can God do this? It is because God, through His Son's death and shedding of blood on the cross, on the one hand causes us to "be justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Rom. 3:24, NKJV), and on the other hand releases us from sin so that we become slaves of righteousness, unto righteousness. "Who shall bring a charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies. Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died" (Rom. 8:33-34, NKJV).

(Chosen (Eph.), the precious blood (Heb.), God accomplishes (1 Thess.)). 1. Made holy through Christ: "...God has made Him [Christ] to be for us [sanctification]". 2. Made holy through the truth: "Sanctify them by Your truth" (John 17:17, NKJV) (1 Peter). 3. Made holy through the Holy Spirit: "...moved by the Holy Spirit, [become] holy" (cf. 2 Pet. 1:21; 1 Pet. 1:2, NKJV). Justification is negative [in aspect]; sanctification is positive. Because of justification, we can come before God and be accepted by God; because of sanctification, we can live among men and bear witness for the Lord. The position of sanctification is had by faith; the experience of sanctification is obtained by pursuing. But there is one fundamental truth concerning sanctification that we must not forget: Christ is our holiness! When His abundant life is manifested in us, we show forth holiness. Such holiness is an outflow, not something manufactured; it is natural, not forced! (Whatever kind of life one has, that is the kind of living one will live.)

Being glorified (through the path of suffering to the realm of glory; the valley of tears becomes a place of springs) (predestined (1 Cor.), through suffering to glory (2 Cor.)). 1. Entering into glory: "[the Captain of salvation] bringing many sons to glory" (Heb. 2:10, NKJV). 2. Obtaining glory: "Moreover whom He justified, these He also glorified" (Rom. 8:30, NKJV) (1 Peter). Sanctification and glorification both deal with the problem of sin. If sin has not been forgiven, how can one be justified? If one still lives in sin, how can one be sanctified? Carrying sin, one also will not be glorified. Thank God: in the salvation He has carried out, we "have been washed, ... have been sanctified, ... have been justified" (1 Cor. 6:11, NKJV). We are now waiting for the glory that is to be revealed to us! (1 Peter).

Homework questions: [Original text partially illegible.] Dealing with our sin. What is the original meaning of justification? In salvation, who justifies us? What is the difference between sanctification and justification? What does it mean that Christ is our holiness? What is the difference between the position of sanctification and the experience of sanctification? If the problem of sin has not been dealt with clearly, can one obtain glory?
