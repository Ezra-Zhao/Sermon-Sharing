# Faith Q&A Question 10: From the Standpoints of Science and History, How Can the Resurrection of Jesus Be Proved?

## Scripture

> "And if Christ is not risen, then our preaching is empty and your faith is also empty." (1 Cor. 15:14, NKJV)

> "But now Christ is risen from the dead, and has become the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep." (1 Cor. 15:20, NKJV)

> "This Jesus God has raised up, of which we are all witnesses." (Acts 2:32, NKJV)

## I. State It Accurately First: It Is Not That the Resurrection Is Unscientific; It Is That Science Cannot Reach Here

The person asking wants to prove the resurrection "from the standpoint of science." Pastor Tong's first step is not to look for evidence; it is to dismantle this way of asking: **time cannot be recovered.** What happened two thousand years ago has passed; today you can only obtain some clues; there is no way to move it back into a laboratory and re-enact it for you to watch.

So the key is not "whether the resurrection fits science," but that this method, science, by its nature cannot reach a one-time historical event. Science is good at things that can be repeatedly observed and repeatedly tested; a historical event happens only once, and once past, it is past, leaving behind records, remains, and the words of witnesses.

Pastor Tong's example is very everyday: even the fact that your great-great-grandfather was your great-great-grandfather cannot be proved by science. That is not a lie; it is something your genealogy, your records, and the testimony of your elders tell you. Many of the most important things in the world — your parentage, your origins — do not rely on laboratories; they rely on historical evidence. If a man will only accept what experiments can prove, he must first throw away his own genealogy.

This is not belittling science. Science is very useful within its scope; when it is used outside its scope and still insisted on as the only measuring stick, what is wrong is not the resurrection — it is the measuring stick being used in the wrong place.

**Original-language analysis:** "Resurrection" in Greek is ***Anastasis* (ἀνάστασις)**, composed of *ana* (again, anew) and *histēmi* (to stand); its literal meaning is "to stand up again." The resurrection Scripture speaks of is not a feeling, not "the spirit does not die"; it is a man who died and was buried standing up again. In 1 Corinthians 15 Paul leaves no retreat: if Christ is not risen, everything preached and everything believed is empty (1 Cor. 15:14). From the very beginning, Christianity left itself no back door of a "symbolic resurrection" — either this thing is true, or the whole faith collapses; there is no middle ground.

## II. Where Is the Historical Evidence? In Whether the Witnesses Would Change Their Testimony

Historical events rely on witnesses. So what test can the testimony to the resurrection stand up to?

Pastor Tong says the most important evidence is this: **if Jesus had not risen, then all those who were killed for preaching that Jesus had risen would be fools.**

This sentence must be heard precisely. Men will die for things they deeply believe are true — in every age there are people who die for all kinds of convictions. But the disciples' position was different: they were not later generations who believed after hearing a legend; they were the parties involved. If the resurrection was fabricated, they were the ones who fabricated it, and **no one will die, one after another, for a lie he fabricated with his own hands and knows full well is false.** If just one man had been willing to relent and say one sentence — "actually, we hid the body" — the lie would have been exposed on the spot, and his life would have been preserved. Yet not one did. One after another they were killed and would not bow their heads; the remaining John was exiled to the island of Patmos, where he received the vision of Revelation.

(Weigh the weight of this: the details of the apostles dying as martyrs one by one come mostly from early church tradition; what Scripture expressly records includes James being killed, see Acts 12:2, and John being exiled to Patmos, see Revelation 1:9. This piece follows Pastor Tong's narrative in using the church tradition, but the weight of the argument is not in the number of people; it is in the point that no one defected.)

**Original-language analysis:** "Witness" in Greek is ***Martys* (μάρτυς)**, whose basic meaning is a person who testifies in court. Before the Lord ascended, He said: "You shall be witnesses to Me" (Acts 1:8), using exactly this word. Later this word, in language, directly became "martyr" — it was not that the word's meaning changed; it was that that generation of witnesses really testified with their deaths, and the word went with them. The fate of a single word is itself a piece of history: when testimony is made unto death, "witness" becomes "martyr."

Paul also left behind the earliest list of witnesses: Christ "was seen by Cephas, then by the twelve. After that He was seen by over five hundred brethren at once" (1 Cor. 15:3–5). Note, this is not one man hiding in a cave saying he saw something; it is several hundred people, in the same period of time, whose testimony could be cross-checked with one another. That is exactly what investigating a historical event relies on.

## III. Even Judas, Who Betrayed the Lord, Never Said Jesus Was a Deceiver

There is one more corroborating testimony, coming from the most unlikely person.

Judas betrayed Jesus; afterward he regretted it, threw the thirty pieces of silver down in the temple, and said: "I have sinned by betraying innocent blood" (Matt. 27:4). Then he went out and hanged himself.

The weight of this sentence must also be weighed accurately: what Judas's words directly prove is not the resurrection — he died before the resurrection. What they prove is another thing: **the person with the greatest motive to expose Jesus, to the point of death only admitted that he himself was guilty; he did not say Jesus was guilty, still less that all of this was a fraud.** If Jesus had been a deceiver, Judas, who had followed Him for more than three years and personally betrayed Him, was the person who knew the inside story best and had the most reason to spill it. Words left at death by an enemy that do not favor the enemy's case — testimony of this kind is, precisely, the hardest kind to fabricate in a court of law.

Set the two sides together and look: on one side are those who followed Him, who would rather die than change their testimony; on the other side is the one who betrayed Him, who at death would not retract. For a single lie to withstand this pincer attack from both sides at once — there is no second example of it in history.

## IV. The Final Layer: The Risen Lord Has Changed Living People

Pastor Tong closes with a very personal testimony. He quotes a line from the hymn "He Lives": "You ask me how I know He lives? He lives within my heart." Then he says: if Jesus had not risen, I would not be standing here today. Jesus Christ has risen, and He has changed the lives of thousands upon thousands of people.

That this layer of evidence is placed last is a matter of order: it cannot replace the historical testimony before it; it is the fruit that the historical testimony bears today. If a thing happened only two thousand years ago and has had no movement at all today, people could still enshrine it as an antique. But the resurrection is not an antique — for two thousand years it has kept changing people: changing men's sins, men's fears, men's way of living. Pastor Tong standing on the pulpit himself is the evidence of his generation; in our generation, the question becomes: do your life and mine count as evidence?

So the answer of this piece can be gathered into one sentence: **the resurrection cannot be proved by science, because science cannot reach history; the resurrection can be investigated by history, and what history has left us is the blood of the witnesses, the silence of the enemy, and two thousand years of human hearts that cannot stop being changed.**

---

## For Us Today

1. Distinguish the tools first: what can be repeatedly tested, use science; what happens only once, use history — with the wrong measuring stick, everything measured comes out wrong
2. The weight of the disciples' deaths lies not in "willingness to die," but in "knowing the truth or falsehood and still refusing, even to death, to change their testimony" — they were the parties involved, not people who heard a legend
3. Weigh Judas's testimony accurately: what he proves is not the resurrection itself, but that even the man who betrayed the Lord could not point to any guilt in the Lord
4. The last link in the chain of evidence is life: if the resurrection shows no change in us, then however correctly we tell it, we have told only half of it
