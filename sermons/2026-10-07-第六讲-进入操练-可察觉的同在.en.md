"But you, when you pray, enter into thy closet, and when you have shut your door, pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly." (Matt. 6:6)

You have read the first five lectures — when you went back, did you practice?

Hearing the word and not doing it is like looking in a mirror — "he observes himself, goes away, and immediately forgets what kind of man he was" (James 1:23-24). This lecture is the landing point of the whole course: how to prepare, how to enter, how to practice. The "inner room" is not just a room; it is even more a state — from outside, turning inward; from noise, turning to quiet.

## I. Preparing to Receive: Seven Aspects

Before receiving dreams and visions, first prepare yourself. In the course, Brother Zhang Xun puts forward seven aspects:

**First, rest for the body.** Have quiet times of devotion. Bible study, prayer, and worship each have their time slots; long or short does not matter. When the body is too tired and the environment too noisy, the heart can hardly be quiet. First give yourself a period of undisturbed time.

**Second, concentration of the soul.** "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life" (Prov. 4:23). "But his delight is in the law of the LORD, And in His law he meditates day and night. He shall be like a tree..." (Ps. 1:2-3). Meditating on God's word day and night is not limited by place — on the subway, in the kitchen, the heart can turn toward God.

**Third, release of the spirit.** Thirst for the anointing of the Holy Spirit, and practice releasing the anointing of inner faith. It is not waiting until the feeling comes to believe; it is believing first, and the anointing is released.

**Fourth, cleansing by the precious blood.** No entanglement of sin, entering boldly. If there is unconfessed sin in the heart, first confess and repent, be cleansed by the Lord's precious blood, and then come before God boldly and without fear.

**Fifth, the formation of a personal prayer life.** At the beginning one may attend more prophetic meetings, to be led and to be ignited; but the long-term plan lies in a personal prayer life in secret (Matt. 6:6). Meetings are the gas station; the secret room is home.

**Sixth, devotion before sleep.** Thinking on heavenly things is an important preparation for receiving dreams. Falling asleep with God's word, God is very willing to speak to you in the night (see Job 33:15-16).

**Seventh, praying in tongues often.** This makes the spiritual man strong and the senses sensitive (see Lecture Five). This is the daily "basic skill."

These seven aspects are not a law; they are preparation. It is like an athlete warming up before a race — the warm-up is not the race, but without warming up, one cannot run well.

## II. Two Steps for Entering the Practice

Having prepared, how do we enter? The course gives **two steps**:

**Step One: Enter God's presence inwardly, and light "the lamp of the LORD."**

Worldly thoughts and ideas gradually stop, consciousness "turns inward," and attention concentrates on God. The key lies in **thirsting for God** — aiming the direction of one's spirit toward God's throne.

Three methods help concentration: worship music with a prophetic anointing, praying in tongues softly, and meditating on Scripture (one may meditate on God's throne, or on the cross; which verse to use can be asked of the Holy Spirit).

**Step Two: Fellowship in the spirit.**

If God gives a vision, receive it: **participate, inquire, respond, record** — enter into the vision, ask the Holy Spirit what it means, respond to Him, and write it down.

If there is no vision for the time being, do not be disappointed: simply immerse yourself in God's presence and converse with God in spiritual fellowship. Ask God: "Lord, today, for whom and for what matter do You want me to pray?" Use tongues and the understanding together. Ask God to continue communicating with you in dreams.

The general principle: **Any time spent on God is never in vain.** Thirst simply for God Himself, not for visions; worship with concentration, ignoring the surroundings; experience and enjoy the anointing of God's presence; keep joy and peace until falling asleep — God may well, because of your thirst, give a dream in the night.

Regarding "opening," the course states it very clearly: opening is an "interaction" between the Holy Spirit and the individual; **sovereignty, time, place, manner, and degree are all with God**. God's promise, plus the individual's thirst. The manner may be the anointing of the Holy Spirit, the laying on of hands, an angel conveying the anointing, or Jesus visiting in person; the place may be in devotion, prayer, fasting, worship, or sleep, or in a meeting; the time may be long or short.

The degree also differs: from "seeing light in the darkness," simple pictures, simple actions, simple thoughts (like a narration), to vivid colors, dialogue and action, a strong sense of involvement, even being caught away in the spirit. **When just beginning, it is mostly blurry pictures; do not easily deny or miss them.** Remember: the degree of the anointing is not the point; God's heart (the message) is; what matters is seeking the interpretation and the application.

## III. The Path of Advancement and the Weekly Practice

**First, the levels of practice for visions and dreams** (reference for advancement)

Visions have four levels: with eyes closed receiving a simple vision plus a basic interpretation (entry level) → with eyes closed entering into the vision, with questions and answers (using multiple spiritual senses) → without interference, with eyes open receiving a vision (necessary for entering church ministry) → receiving a vision at any time (the foundation of prophetic ministry).

Dreams have three levels: occasional dreams, with a simple interpretation after prayer (personal guidance) → dreams begin to follow a pattern, their scope expanding to the church and the nation → dreams almost every day, with an increase in dreams concerning the church and the nation (the prophetic calling manifested).

Regarding **prophet and seer** (prophet: original Hebrew נָבִיא, transliterated navi, Strong H5030; seer: original Hebrew רֹאֶה, transliterated ro'eh, Strong H7200; according to *BDB Lexicon*, "prophet / seer"): when immature, a simple division can be made — the prophet emphasizes prophecy, the seer emphasizes dreams and visions; when mature, prophecy, dreams, and visions interpenetrate and cannot be rigidly divided. "Formerly in Israel, when a man went to inquire of God, he spoke thus: 'Come, let us go to the seer'; for he who is now called a prophet was formerly called a seer" (1 Sam. 9:9). Samuel was both a prophet and a seer (1 Sam. 3:20; 1 Chr. 29:29).

One mark of a mature prophet is frequent visitation and warfare in the spiritual realm — but that is a result, not the goal. The goal is always: to know God more deeply, and to serve people better.

**Second, Bible cases: breakthroughs brought by visions**

- **The Macedonian vision** (Acts 16:9-10): At night Paul saw a vision — "Come over to Macedonia and help us"; immediately they concluded that God had called them to go, and from there the gospel entered Europe. One vision changed the map of the gospel.
- **The Corinthian vision** (Acts 18:9-10): In a vision the Lord said, "Do not be afraid, but speak, and do not keep silent; for I am with you" — the vision brought courage and comfort.
- **The angel in the storm** (Acts 27:23-24): The ship was about to sink, and an angel appeared and said, "Do not be afraid, Paul... God has granted you all those who sail with you" — in a desperate situation the vision brought peace and assurance.

**Third, the eight steps of weekly practice** (from Chapter Eleven of Zhang Xun's original work; this is the "homework" of the whole course)

1. First have a time of worship, entering God's presence, during which the understanding and tongues alternate in prayer;
2. Inquire of the Holy Spirit: for whom and for what matter should I pray;
3. Record in detail the visions and thoughts received;
4. Inquire of the Holy Spirit how to interpret;
5. Record the interpretation (best to ask for a verse of Scripture as confirmation), and pay attention to all kinds of confirmations the Holy Spirit gives through people, matters, and things;
6. Before sleep, have another fifteen minutes of worship, prayer, and meditation on the vision;
7. If God gives a dream, record it;
8. If what is received is not about the object of intercession (it may be about oneself), likewise record it and seek the interpretation.

Several important reminders: **Dreams and visions are "invitation letters to pray"** — having received one, go and pray; at first do not pray for family members and acquaintances, to avoid preconceptions; after receiving a dream or vision, do not immediately tell the other party; ask the Holy Spirit to indicate the method and the timing; a mere thought alone does not yet count as a vision.

**Fourth, the summary of the whole course**

"Dreams and visions begin from prayer, grow by the anointing, and are perfected in love." — This is the vision of Helpers of Joy Ministries, and it is also the general outline of these six lectures.

Do not limit the work of the Holy Spirit, and do not chase experiences themselves. Grow in love ("Pursue love, and desire spiritual gifts," 1 Cor. 14:1), grow in serving (1 Cor. 14:12), grow in obedience ("Let all things be done decently and in order," 1 Cor. 14:40). Practice under the covering of the church, to glorify God and benefit people, and to bear witness for Jesus — "for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (Rev. 19:10).

## IV. Life Application: Six Kinds of Readers

**Those who want to begin practicing but do not know where to start**: Begin today. Tonight before sleep, fifteen minutes of worship plus prayer, meditating on a verse of Scripture. The starting point is very small, but a starting point is a starting point.

**Those who practice for a while and then give up**: Ask yourself, is the method wrong, or are the expectations too high? When just beginning, it is mostly blurry pictures; do not easily deny them. Keep to the eight weekly steps, and give God time, and give yourself time.

**Those who have experiences only in meetings and none in their personal lives**: Meetings are the gas station; the secret room is home. The "inner room" of Matthew 6:6 is yours alone. Bring the fire from the meeting back to burn in the secret room.

**Those who sleep poorly and dream much at night**: Turn the trouble into a blessing. Devotion before sleep, thinking on heavenly things, keeping joy and peace until falling asleep — ask God to turn the dreams of the night into a channel through which He speaks.

**Those with a burden of intercession**: The eight weekly steps are prepared for you. At first do not pray for family and acquaintances (to avoid preconceptions); let God first give you the "invitation letter to pray."

**Church small-group leaders**: You may lead your group members to practice together — worship together, record together, seek interpretation together. Practicing within the covering and fellowship of a small group is the safest and the most solid.

## Conclusion

We have walked through the six lectures. From God's promise, to His purpose; from spiritual journey, spiritual life, and spiritual power, to the lamp of the LORD; from praying in tongues, to entering the practice. Today we bring all of this before God.

## Response Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You; in these six lectures of the course, You Yourself taught us. Lord, we confess that we often only hear and do not act, hearing and then forgetting. Forgive us, we pray.

Lord, from today on, we are willing to practice. Willing to enter the inner room, willing to be quiet, willing to record, willing to inquire. We do not chase experiences; we chase You. We do not limit the Holy Spirit; we obey the Holy Spirit.

Light the lamp within us, we pray, and open the eyes of our hearts. Let us grow in love, grow in serving, and grow in obedience. Under the covering of the church, to glorify God and benefit people, and to bear witness for Jesus.

In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
