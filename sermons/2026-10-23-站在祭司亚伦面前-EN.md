- Author: Written by Guangyi Zhao from the biblical text and commentaries

## Abstract

Chapter 1 numbers the men of twenty years old and upward who are able to go forth to war; chapter 2 sets the numbered people in their places round about the tabernacle; chapter 3 draws the lens back to the side of the tabernacle and numbers one tribe alone: the Levites. In the chapter's fifty-one verses, the first part records the sons of Aaron and the office of the Levites given to Aaron to serve him, together with the reason the Levites were chosen instead of all the firstborn of Israel; the middle part numbers the males of the three houses of Gershon, Kohath, and Merari from a month old and upward, house by house, assigning to each house its camping side, its chief, and the objects committed to its charge; the last part numbers the firstborn males of Israel, works out the number over and above, collects the redemption money head by head, and delivers it into the hands of Aaron and his sons. This sermon reads the chapter in the text's own order: first, how the Levites presented before Aaron the priest are entrusted; then, how the reason for the substitution reaches back to the night of the exodus; then, house by house, the three houses' numbers, sides, and charges; and finally the substitution account settled item by item — the Levites twenty and two thousand, the firstborn males twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three, the excess two hundred and seventy-three, the redemption five shekels apiece, a thousand three hundred and sixty-five shekels in all. The thread the sermon traces is this: this chapter's numbering is not for war but for service; those numbered are not troops but substitutes — one whole tribe taken out to stand in another's place, in the stead of all in Israel who were by right God's own.

## Sermon

### I. 00:00-08:00 The sons of Aaron and the tribe called near: ministering before Aaron the priest

(Fact) The chapter opens not with the Levites at large but with the house of Aaron: the generations of Aaron and Moses, in the day when the LORD spake with Moses in mount Sinai, stand at the head of the chapter (v. 1, Summary). Aaron had four sons, named here in the base text in order: Nadab the firstborn, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar (v. 2, Summary). All four were anointed priests, consecrated to minister in the priest's office (v. 3, Summary). Yet the list is no sooner given than the text appends a calamity: Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD in the wilderness of Sinai, when they offered strange fire before the LORD, and they had no children; so Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the priest's office in the sight of Aaron their father (v. 4, Summary). The full account of the strange fire stands in Leviticus 10 (Lev 10:1-2, Summary): the two men took their censers, put fire and incense in them, and offered strange fire before the LORD, which He commanded them not; and fire went out from the LORD and devoured them. This sermon cites Leviticus in summary only, never as a quotation; but the fact must be stated plainly: the assignments of office in chapter 3 begin from a priestly house with two of its members missing. The priestly company carries a wound from its first page — two of four sons gone, and the dead without sons. Office is not assigned amid unbroken peace; it is assigned beside the ashes of judgment.

(Application) This opening sets the tone for the whole chapter. Ahead lie great quantities of figures, names, sides, and lists of objects; without these four verses the chapter is easily read as an administrative document. With them, the reader knows first that every place beside the sanctuary stands between the two poles of "coming nigh" and "being put to death." Nadab and Abihu were no outsiders; they were anointed priests, the firstborn and the second son, the men best entitled to come near. They died not because they stood far off, but precisely because in coming near they crossed the commandment. The twice-repeated word of this chapter, that the stranger who comes nigh shall be put to death (vv. 10, 38, Summary), must not be read as boilerplate — it is the boundary line that Nadab and Abihu marked with their lives. The office of the sanctuary is first a solemn thing, and only then an orderly thing; order must be exact to the last item because the One at the centre is holy.

(Fact) The calamity recorded, the LORD speaks to Moses and commands the tribe of Levi to be brought near and given to Aaron (vv. 5-6). Verse 6 is the chapter's thematic verse, in the KJV: "Bring the tribe of Levi near, and present them before Aaron the priest, that they may minister unto him." (Num 3:6, KJV) Mark the structure of the sentence: the place to which the Levites are brought is not directly before God, but before Aaron the priest; and what they are to do there is to minister unto him. Verses 7-8 then unfold that ministry as a twofold charge: "And they shall keep his charge, and the charge of the whole congregation before the tabernacle of the congregation, to do the service of the tabernacle." (Num 3:7, KJV) — and they are likewise to keep all the instruments of the tabernacle and the charge of the children of Israel, to do the service of the tabernacle (v. 8, Summary). Verse 9 adds: "And thou shalt give the Levites unto Aaron and to his sons: they are wholly given unto him out of the children of Israel." (Num 3:9, KJV) Verse 10 then draws the other side tight: Aaron and his sons are appointed to wait on their priest's office, and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death (v. 10, Summary).

(Fact) The chapter's keywords must be set out one by one in this passage, because every office that follows grows out of them. First, the "charge" they keep is mišmereṯ (Strong's H4931), in its basic sense a post of guard, an entrusted duty, a responsibility of keeping. The chapter uses the word throughout: the Levites keep the charge of Aaron and of the congregation (vv. 7-8); the Kohathites are those "keeping the charge of the sanctuary" (v. 28); each house has objects committed to its charge (vv. 25, 31, 36); and Moses, Aaron, and Aaron's sons on the east are "keeping the charge of the sanctuary" (v. 38). One word running through the chapter declares the nature of the Levites' office: it is not odd-job labour but entrusted keeping — things are placed in their hands, and they answer for those things to the end. Second, the "service" of "the service of the tabernacle" is ʿăḇōḏāh (Strong's H5656), labour, service, the work of an office. In Exodus the same word is used of Israel's hard bondage in Egypt; here it is used of the tabernacle's service — the same hands, the same kind of labour, but with the object changed and the master changed, the labour's whole nature is changed with them. Third, "minister unto him" and "bring near" together state the Levites' position in full: they are men brought near, yet not near without limit; they come as far as standing before Aaron, and no farther — the priest's office beyond that point is not theirs. To be brought near is grace; to keep one's measure in standing is faithfulness; both are in this one sentence of verse 6.

(Application) Fix the Levites' identity in this passage exactly: they are not a lower grade of priest, nor a delegation of the congregation; they are a people given away — taken out of the children of Israel and given to Aaron. Being given is this chapter's first definition of the Levites, more fundamental than any object they will later carry or keep. A minister must first understand that he is entrusted: he did not choose this place to display his ability, nor drift into assistance for want of anything better; he was taken out of the crowd and placed in another man's hands, to fulfil that man's office. To stand before Aaron the priest means that Levitical service always has a concrete object, a definite counterpart; it is never loyalty performed toward empty air. The affairs of the tabernacle have a thousand threads, but the text lists none of them first; it fixes the relation first — first before whom they stand, then what their hands will hold. That is the chapter's order, and the order of all service: relation before objects, entrustment before lists.

### II. 08:00-24:00 The reason for the choosing: the Levites instead of all the firstborn of Israel

(Fact) Given to Aaron, the Levites are next given a deeper reason for their existence: they are not Aaron's assistants only; they are Israel's substitutes. In verses 11-13 the LORD declares to Moses, in the KJV: "And I, behold, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel instead of all the firstborn that openeth the matrix among the children of Israel: therefore the Levites shall be mine;" (Num 3:12, KJV) "Because all the firstborn are mine; for on the day that I smote all the firstborn in the land of Egypt I hallowed unto me all the firstborn in Israel, both man and beast: mine shall they be: I am the LORD." (Num 3:13, KJV) The "firstborn" here is beḵôr (Strong's H1060), the first to open the womb, the eldest son; the word recurs throughout the chapter in this sense. The declaration's ground reaches back to the night of the exodus: every firstborn in Egypt was smitten, while Israel's firstborn were preserved — and from that day they were hallowed unto the LORD and made His (Exodus 12-13, Summary). The firstborn therefore belong to God not by a newly invented rule but in settlement of an old account: their lives were the ones spared that night, and having been spared, they belong to the Lord who spared them.

(Fact) The reason stated, the numbering follows at once. In verses 14-15 the LORD commands Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, in the KJV: "Number the children of Levi after the house of their fathers, by their families: every male from a month old and upward shalt thou number them." (Num 3:15, KJV) Verse 16 records that Moses numbered them according to the word of the LORD, as he was commanded (v. 16, Summary). The verb "number" here is pāqad (Strong's H6485), the very word chapter 1 used for numbering the people, and this chapter uses it for the Levites and for the firstborn alike — one verb, three objects: the three numberings are three steps of one work by one Lord, not three unrelated pieces of statistics. Mark also where this numbering begins: chapter 1 numbered men of twenty years old and upward, able to go forth to war (Numbers 1, Summary); this chapter numbers the Levites from a month old and upward. A child one month old cannot go to war; he cannot even stand. Why number him? Because this numbering has nothing to do with military service and everything to do with belonging: the Levites are numbered not for what they can do, but for whose they are. Belonging knows no age, and so the numbering sets no threshold below its starting point — the child a month old is in the register too.

(Application) The doctrine of substitution in this passage must be preached through, for it is the hinge of the whole chapter. By the old rule of the exodus, the firstborn male of every Israelite house was to be hallowed to God and to stand, as it were, on the sanctuary's side of the camp. Now God takes one whole tribe — Levi — in the stead of the firstborn of every house: the Levites stand where the firstborn should have stood, and the firstborn of the several houses stay in their own homes and live the ordinary life of the people. God has not lowered His requirement; He has Himself provided the substitution. The requirement lacks not one man, the number lacks not one name; only the persons standing in that place have been exchanged. Substitution is not a shortcut; it is grace made into an institution: what God had the right to exact, He has chosen to have borne by a company of His own choosing. And note that the substitution is of a whole tribe for a whole class: it is not that this Levite exchanges for that firstborn, one for one, by private arrangement; the entire tribe of Levi is taken out over against the entire body of Israel's firstborn. The numbers still have to be reconciled, item by item — that comes in section IV — but the substitution's nature is fixed here first: choosing first, reckoning afterwards; grace first, arithmetic afterwards. Reverse the order, reckon first and speak of grace afterwards, and the chapter degenerates into a transaction. The text's own order is to declare first, "the Levites shall be mine," and only then, slowly, to count heads and compute redemption money.

(Application) One further layer must be spoken to today's hearers. In the ancient world the firstborn's status was an honourable one: the eldest son received the double portion and stood at the head of his house. But in this chapter the firstborn's status is first of all a claimed status: because he was preserved that night, his life belongs to God first, and only afterwards comes any talk of his honour. Grace received and belonging imposed are two faces of one event — there is no firstborn who was spared but does not belong, and none who is claimed but was not spared. The Levites, standing in their stead, act the lesson out for all Israel: the eldest sons of the houses can grow up safely at home because a whole tribe was taken out to stand in the post that was theirs. Substitution in this chapter is no abstract doctrinal noun; it has names, numbers, camping sides, and objects to keep — and to those we now turn, house by house.

### III. 24:00-42:00 The three houses numbered: Gershon in the west, Kohath in the south, Merari in the north, each keeping what was assigned

(Fact) The numbering begins with the pedigree. Verse 17 gives the names of the sons of Levi: Gershon, and Kohath, and Merari (v. 17, Summary). Verses 18-20 list the sons house by house (Summary): the sons of Gershon by their families, Libni and Shimei; the sons of Kohath by their families, Amram, and Izehar, Hebron, and Uzziel; the sons of Merari by their families, Mahli and Mushi. What follows sets out the three houses one by one, under four heads each — families, number, camping side, and chief with the objects of the charge — item by item from the text's own verses, with nothing added or taken away from memory.

(Fact) The first house: Gershon (vv. 21-26, Summary presentation). Of Gershon were the family of the Libnites and the family of the Shimeites; those numbered of them, all the males from a month old and upward, were seven thousand and five hundred (v. 22). Their camping side: the families of the Gershonites pitch behind the tabernacle westward (v. 23) — behind the tabernacle, that is, on its west. Their chief: Eliasaph the son of Lael, chief of the house of the father of the Gershonites (v. 24). The objects of their charge (vv. 25-26): the tabernacle and the tent, its covering, and the hanging for the door of the tabernacle of the congregation; the hangings of the court and the curtain for the door of the court, which is by the tabernacle and by the altar round about; and its cords for all its service. Run an eye over the list and its character is plain: the sons of Gershon keep all the tabernacle's cloth — curtains, tent, coverings, the hanging of the door, the hangings of the court, one whole line of fabrics. Cloth is the lightest of the materials, and the most easily torn, soiled, and dampened; the man who keeps cloth must have a light hand and a careful heart.

(Fact) The second house: Kohath (vv. 27-32, Summary presentation). Of Kohath were the family of the Amramites, the family of the Izeharites, the family of the Hebronites, and the family of the Uzzielites; in the number of all the males from a month old and upward were eight thousand and six hundred, keeping the charge of the sanctuary (v. 28). Their camping side: the families of the sons of Kohath pitch on the side of the tabernacle southward (v. 29). Their chief: Elizaphan the son of Uzziel, chief of the house of the father of the families of Kohath (v. 30). The objects of their charge (v. 31): the ark, and the table, and the candlestick, and the altars, and the vessels of the sanctuary wherewith they minister, and the hanging, and all the service thereof — the heaviest portion of the three houses: the ark at the head, then table, candlestick, and the two altars, all the core vessels within the sanctuary. The weight of the charge and the camping side answer one another here: the Kohathites keep what stands nearest the sanctuary's heart, camp on the south, and are in number the largest of the three houses at eight thousand and six hundred. One man is named singly after the house of Kohath: Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest is chief of the chiefs of the Levites, with oversight of those who keep the charge of the sanctuary (v. 32, Summary) — Eleazar, the eldest surviving son of Aaron after the death of Nadab and Abihu (compare vv. 2, 4, Summary), now stands over the three Levitical houses as supervisor. As for how the Kohathites were to carry the holy things, chapter 4 has its own express rule: the holy things must first be covered by Aaron and his sons, and only then do the sons of Kohath come to bear them — and bear them on their shoulders (Numbers 4, Summary). This chapter assigns the keeping only; the manner of bearing belongs to the next, and this sermon does not read the next chapter's word back into this one.

(Fact) The third house: Merari (vv. 33-37, Summary presentation). Of Merari were the family of the Mahlites and the family of the Mushites; those numbered of them, all the males from a month old and upward, were six thousand and two hundred (v. 34). Their camping side: they pitch on the side of the tabernacle northward (v. 35). Their chief: Zuriel the son of Abihail, chief of the house of the father of the families of Merari (v. 35, Summary). The objects of their charge (vv. 36-37): the boards of the tabernacle, its bars, its pillars, and its sockets, and all its vessels and all its service; and the pillars of the court round about, their sockets, their pins, and their cords. The sons of Merari keep the tabernacle's frame — boards, bars, pillars, sockets, the whole structural line: the heaviest, hardest, least conspicuous part of the whole, and yet the very thing by which the whole tabernacle stands up. Gershon keeps the cloth, Kohath the vessels, Merari the frame; put the three lists of objects together and they make one whole tabernacle: without cloth, no covering; without vessels, no worship; without frame, nothing stands at all.

(Fact) Beyond the three houses, verse 38 assigns the tabernacle's eastern side: those who encamp before the tabernacle toward the east, before the tabernacle of the congregation eastward toward the rising of the sun, are Moses, and Aaron and his sons, keeping the charge of the sanctuary for the charge of the children of Israel; and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death (v. 38, Summary). Read chapters 2 and 3 together and the whole circuit round the tabernacle is now assigned: on the east, Moses and the priestly house; on the south, Kohath; on the west, Gershon; on the north, Merari — and one ring farther out, the four camps of the twelve tribes of chapter 2. The inner ring now has its plan as well: the priests keep the east, facing the sunrise; the three Levitical houses keep south, west, and north between them. The east is given to none of the three houses, because the east is the tabernacle's front, where its entrance is; the front is kept by Moses and Aaron themselves — the place of greatest weight is stood in by the leaders in person, and farmed out to no tribe.

(Application) The three houses' division of labour deserves slow savouring. By order of birth Gershon is the firstborn, Kohath second, Merari the youngest; by weight of charge it is Kohath who keeps the most inward and holy things, Gershon the cloth, Merari the frame. The firstborn's house did not automatically receive the innermost office — the same principle as chapter 2, where Judah stands first and Reuben second: posts are assigned not by birth alone; God assigns by His own choosing. But do not read the division as rank and worth: the ark, holy as it is, must first be enclosed and covered by boards and curtains before it is a sanctuary at all; and the boards and bars, clumsy as they are, lack one piece and the tabernacle does not stand. When the Kohathite keeps his vessels, if he despises Merari's boards he despises the very lodging of the things he keeps. The tabernacle is one whole, and the three houses' charges are one whole: each house's keeping is, for the other two, keeping the precondition of their service. And mark this above all: nothing the three houses keep is their own property — the cloth is not the Gershonites' cloth, the ark is not the Kohathites' ark, the boards are not the Merarites' boards. They are keepers, not owners; and a keeper's faithfulness consists precisely in keeping what is not his own more carefully than his own.

(Application) The matter of the sides, too, must not be read past. The three houses are not scattered at random round the tabernacle; each keeps one side — west, south, north — and with Moses and Aaron on the east the circuit is complete. The Levites dwell between the tabernacle and the people: inwardly, they stand before Aaron the priest and minister to him; outwardly, they keep the charge of the children of Israel (vv. 7-8, Summary). That "between" is the Levites' life in one word: they belong neither to the outer ring of the twelve tribal camps nor to the innermost priestly office; they stand in the middle, buffer and bridge to both sides. The man in the middle feels the pressure from both sides, and must keep the boundary toward both: one step too far inward is the stranger's death for coming nigh; one step too far outward, and the sanctuary loses a layer of its guard. This chapter nails the three houses to three sides precisely so that this "between" has no gap anywhere: with every side kept, the sanctuary is secure on every side; and with Levites on every side between them and it, the people are kept from crossing the line unawares and dying. Order here is protection still — protection of the sanctuary, and protection of the people alike.

### IV. 42:00-56:00 The substitution account: not one man short, and the excess two hundred and seventy-three redeemed head by head

(Fact) The three houses numbered, verse 39 gives the Levites' total, in the KJV: "All that were numbered of the Levites, which Moses and Aaron numbered at the commandment of the LORD, throughout their families, all the males from a month old and upward, were twenty and two thousand." (Num 3:39, KJV) The total is twenty-two thousand. Now add the three houses' items together; in preparing this sermon every item was added by hand: Gershon seven thousand and five hundred (v. 22), Kohath eight thousand and six hundred (v. 28), Merari six thousand and two hundred (v. 34). Seven thousand five hundred plus eight thousand six hundred makes sixteen thousand one hundred; plus six thousand two hundred, twenty-two thousand three hundred. The items added together make twenty-two thousand three hundred, while the total recorded at verse 39 is twenty-two thousand — a difference of three hundred between them. This discrepancy a sermon must state exactly as it stands, and must not quietly alter any figure to make the account balance: the three item figures follow their verses, the total follows verse 39, both are the base text's own numbers, and this sermon touches neither.

(Scholarly and historical discussion) This difference of three hundred between the items' sum of twenty-two thousand three hundred and the stated total of twenty-two thousand has long been noticed by commentators, who have offered more than one explanation: some hold that one of the item figures was corrupted in transmission (that Kohath's eight thousand six hundred, for instance, should perhaps be eight thousand three hundred); some, that the total is given as a round number; some, that three hundred Levites who were themselves firstborn could not serve as substitutes for others and were therefore not counted in the total available for substitution. This sermon sets out the existence and the drift of these discussions only, and decides for none of them; nor does its argument rest on the three hundred. The text's own substitution reckoning that follows proceeds on verse 39's total of twenty-two thousand (see below), and this sermon states the matter on the text's own basis, without harmonising the two figures on its own authority.

(Fact) The Levites' total fixed, verses 40-43 turn to numbering Israel's firstborn: the LORD commands Moses to number all the firstborn males of the children of Israel from a month old and upward and take the number of their names, and to take the Levites for Him instead of all the firstborn, and the Levites' cattle instead of the firstborn of Israel's cattle (vv. 40-41, Summary). Moses numbered them as commanded (v. 42, Summary), and the result stands in the KJV: "And all the firstborn males by the number of names, from a month old and upward, of those that were numbered of them, were twenty and two thousand two hundred and threescore and thirteen." (Num 3:43, KJV) Firstborn males: twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three. With the figure out, the account is clear: Levites twenty-two thousand (the total of v. 39); firstborn twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three (v. 43); the firstborn are the more. Verse 45 states the exchange itself, in the KJV: "Take the Levites instead of all the firstborn among the children of Israel, and the cattle of the Levites instead of their cattle; and the Levites shall be mine: I am the LORD." (Num 3:45, KJV) And verses 46-47 state the redemption of the excess, in the KJV: "And for those that are to be redeemed of the two hundred and threescore and thirteen of the firstborn of the children of Israel, which are more than the Levites; Thou shalt even take five shekels apiece by the poll, after the shekel of the sanctuary shalt thou take them: (the shekel is twenty gerahs:)" (Num 3:46-47, KJV) In preparing this sermon the arithmetic was checked item by item by hand: twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three minus twenty-two thousand makes two hundred and seventy-three, in exact agreement with verse 46. And mark the text's basis of reckoning exactly: verse 46's "more than the Levites" is measured against the total of twenty-two thousand written at verse 39, not against the items' sum of twenty-two thousand three hundred — on the items' sum, the Levites would exceed the firstborn, and there would be no excess to redeem. The text's reckoning stands from first to last on the total it has itself written down; this sermon states that basis as the text gives it, neither substituting another base figure nor smoothing the tension between the two numbers away.

(Fact) How the two hundred and seventy-three were redeemed, verses 47-48 prescribe and verses 49-51 record: five shekels apiece by the poll, after the shekel of the sanctuary — the shekel of twenty gerahs — the money to be given to Aaron and his sons (vv. 47-48, Summary). Checked item by item: two hundred and seventy-three men at five shekels each — two hundred and seventy-three times five makes one thousand three hundred and sixty-five shekels. Verses 49-50 record that Moses took the redemption money from those over and above those redeemed by the Levites, and the total stands in the KJV: "Of the firstborn of the children of Israel took he the money; a thousand three hundred and threescore and five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary:" (Num 3:50, KJV) Verse 51 records where the money went, in the KJV: "And Moses gave the money of them that were redeemed unto Aaron and to his sons, according to the word of the LORD, as the LORD commanded Moses." (Num 3:51, KJV) The "redemption money" here is pidyôn (Strong's H6306), a ransom price; the chapter's closing paragraphs speak of redeeming again and again, and what is redeemed is the two hundred and seventy-three firstborn for whom no substitute stood available — their belonging does not lapse for want of a substitute; it is bought out in silver instead, and the silver goes to the priests, which is to say: their portion of service is rendered as an offering, and still belongs to God.

(Application) This account is to be savoured sentence by sentence. First, God's account is kept to the last digit: a reckoning across more than twenty thousand men lands at last on a remainder of two hundred and seventy-three, and the text does not say "about three hundred," does not say "let the rest go"; it numbers them one by one, takes five shekels for each one, and receives and delivers one thousand three hundred and sixty-five shekels without a farthing's difference. In God's account there is no "close enough": every man covered by a substitute is covered one for one; every man without a substitute is redeemed head by head, and not one leaks away in silence. Second, the excess men are not excess men — the two hundred and seventy-three are, in human eyes, the gap left where substitutes ran out; in God's eyes each of them has a name and is counted by name (v. 43 is a numbering "by the number of names"): every man in the gap is counted, priced, and redeemed, and not one becomes a written-off remainder for standing beyond the twenty-two thousand. Third, the redemption money is delivered into the hands of Aaron and his sons: the money is no fine; it is the certificate of belonging — the firstborn are God's by right, are served for by Levite substitutes, and the excess render silver in place of service; the silver entering the priests' hands is their portion belonging to God and entered on the sanctuary's account. The whole course of substitution is now complete: choosing, numbering, reconciling, making up the difference, delivering — five steps, and not one missing. Grace is no muddled account; grace is the most exactly kept account of all.

(Application) Set this account beside the three hundred's discrepancy among the houses' items, and note the lesson in their standing side by side. On the one hand the text leaves the tension of three hundred between items and total entirely unpolished; on the other, it reckons the remainder of two hundred and seventy-three without a shekel's error. Scripture's numbers were never recorded to show off numerical neatness, but to witness how the Lord who commanded the numbering treats His people: in the great matter, His sovereignty — He takes one whole tribe of Levi instead of the firstborn of all the people; in the small matter, His exactness — He gathers in the redemption price of even two hundred and seventy-three men, head by head. The preacher should learn this proportion: what the text has written plainly, state word for word; what the text leaves in tension, keep on record exactly as it stands, neither forcing a fit nor smoothing it away. The account is kept exactly because men's lives are precious; the tension is kept standing because honesty is more precious than tidiness.

### V. 58:00-60:00 Conclusion: those numbered are those set apart to be His

(Fact) The chapter runs from the list of Aaron's four sons to the delivery of the redemption money to Aaron and his sons: at its head, a priestly house's wound and survival; at its end, the substitution account received and delivered in full. Its three lines can now be gathered into one bundle. First, the Levites are brought near to stand before Aaron the priest and minister to him (v. 6) — service has its object and its entrustment first, and its list of objects afterwards. Second, the Levites stand instead of all the firstborn of Israel (vv. 12-13, Summary) — the place they stand in is the place where every house's firstborn should have stood; the substitution is of a whole tribe for a whole class, by choosing. Third, the account is settled to the very last man (vv. 43-51): twenty-two thousand against twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three, the excess two hundred and seventy-three redeemed head by head at five shekels, one thousand three hundred and sixty-five shekels delivered in full — not one firstborn left outside the belonging, and not one Levite standing outside his assignment.

(Application) Let this sermon gather the whole chapter into three sentences. First, to be numbered is not merely to be registered; it is to be set apart: chapter 1's numbering was for the march, chapter 2's encamping was for the surrounding, this chapter's numbering is for belonging — a child of a month old is numbered, because belonging asks not age nor ability, but whose a man is, first of all. Second, the place of service is given, not seized: the Levites stand before Aaron because they were brought near; the objects the three houses keep are theirs because they were assigned; even the sum of the redemption money is what it is because it was commanded. The minister's deepest rest is to know that he stands in an appointed place, keeping an entrusted portion, with no need to compare its weight with the house next door. Third, the grace of substitution is kept as the most exact of accounts: God, having provided the substitution, does not therefore reckon carelessly; He redeems every man over and above, openly and exactly — grace never cancels carefulness; grace makes carefulness possible.

(New Testament reading) Two New Testament passages are traditionally read by the church alongside this chapter; as throughout, the layer is labelled, stated as a layer of reading, and not read back into the text of Numbers. The first is Hebrews 12, with its assembly of the firstborn whose names are written in heaven (Heb 12:23, Summary) — the firstborn numbered by name in this chapter answer, in the New Testament reading, to that register of firstborn written in heaven: the earthly register counts out who is God's, the heavenly register records who is God's, and between them stands the hand of the same God who records the names. The second is Colossians 1 on Christ: He is the firstborn of every creature, and the head of the body, the church, the firstborn from the dead (Col 1:15, 18, Summary) — the firstborn of this chapter are a company redeemed and substituted for, while that one Firstborn Himself becomes the ransom and the substitute; the redemption money of Numbers is silver, while the redemption price of the New Testament is Himself. (Application) But the layers must be kept straight: Numbers 3 is first of all itself — the three Levitical houses' numbers, sides, and objects, and the redemption money of two hundred and seventy-three firstborn. The New Testament reading is the light in which the church, in Christ, looks back on it; it must not be used to dissolve this chapter's figures. The more solid this chapter's accounting — the houses' items addable one by one, the reckoning's basis checkable verse by verse, the redemption total verifiable by hand — the more weight the reading layer's comparison carries: one tribe standing in the place where all should have stood, one sum of money making up the lack of those over and above — with those two pictures standing there, the One in the New Testament who stands in the place of all and makes up every lack stands out with a new clearness.

(Application) As the sermon ends, this chapter must be handed back into every hearer's own hands. Most of us are not of Levi's blood, and have no boards or curtains of a tabernacle to keep; but this chapter's questions no man can escape: Whose are you? In what place do you stand? Is your portion one that another stands in for you, or are you yourself standing in another's place? The text gives us a twofold comfort. On the one hand, our salvation never rested on our having stood in the right place ourselves: Another was first taken out and fixed in the post, to bear in our stead what we should have borne. On the other, those who have been saved are themselves numbered and assigned, each with a charge to keep — as the three houses each kept one side, and each one line of objects — a charge that need not be great, nor conspicuous, yet lacks nothing that can be spared. May we first rest as the redeemed — our names in the register, our price paid in full; and then gladly be keepers — standing in the place appointed us, keeping the portion entrusted to us, until the Lord who records the names comes Himself to take the number.

## Devotional Excerpt

English (KJV-based): Numbers 3 first showed me only three lists: of Gershon, seven thousand and five hundred; of Kohath, eight thousand and six hundred; of Merari, six thousand and two hundred. Reading on, I saw that all that were numbered of the Levites were twenty and two thousand, and all the firstborn males by the number of names, from a month old and upward, were twenty and two thousand two hundred and threescore and thirteen — and the two hundred and threescore and thirteen over and above were not passed over in silence: for those that are to be redeemed of them, five shekels apiece by the poll were taken, a thousand three hundred and threescore and five shekels in all, and Moses gave the money of them that were redeemed unto Aaron and to his sons. What moves me most is where the numbering begins: every male from a month old and upward. A child in arms, who can do nothing and cannot even stand, already has his name in the register, because being numbered rests not on ability but on belonging. I often feel that I am the odd one over — not in the plan, not in the number, as if God's account, once it reached the remainder, could let the remainder go. But the text tells me the remainder, too, is numbered man by man and redeemed man by man, and not one is left out: in God's account there are no leftover people, only an odd number, and for the odd number a redemption is already provided. So I pray today: Lord, I thank Thee that my name was not missed; it was numbered by Thyself, and my redemption price Thou hast paid in full. Let me rest in the place Thou hast appointed me, as the Levites stood before Aaron the priest, keeping the small charge Thou hast entrusted to me — not weighing mine against another's, but asking only that when Thou comest to take the number, I may still be standing in my place.

## Endnotes

1. The KJV quotations in this manuscript (Num 3:6, 3:7, 3:9, 3:12, 3:13, 3:15, 3:39, 3:43, 3:45, 3:46-47, 3:50, 3:51) were checked verse by verse against the KJV text of Numbers 3. The Chinese original's quotations follow the New Punctuation Union Version as carried on wd.bible (Num 3:6, 3:39, 3:43, 3:46, 3:50, 3:51); verses not checked word for word in the Chinese are given in summary and factual presentation in the Chinese manuscript, never as quotations. Each manuscript quotes its own base text and neither is supplemented from the other. The houses' figures and the names of persons are stated as facts and lists: the figures follow the text verse by verse and were cross-checked between the two base texts; the names follow the base text exactly.
2. Title and Num 3:6: the tentative Chinese title joined "standing" and "before Aaron" into a phrase the base text does not contain in one unbroken sequence — in the Chinese base text of 3:6 the wording runs "stand before Aaron the priest," with the word for "priest" standing between "stand before" and "Aaron." The title was therefore corrected to the base text's own unbroken wording in that verse, "standing before Aaron the priest," which is also the verse in which the Levites' office is assigned — the thematic verse of the chapter. The KJV at 3:6 renders the same appointment "present them before Aaron the priest."
3. The three houses' figures and the sum of the items, checked item by item by hand: Gershon 7,500 (v. 22) plus Kohath 8,600 (v. 28) makes 16,100, plus Merari 6,200 (v. 34) makes 22,300. That is the sum of the three houses' items. The total of the Levites recorded at verse 39 is 22,000 — the Chinese base text's "twenty-two thousand in all," and the KJV's "twenty and two thousand," alike. The items' sum and the stated total differ by 300; section IV of the sermon sets the two figures side by side exactly as they stand, alters neither, and offers no harmonisation; the commentators' several explanations of the 300 (a corrupted item figure in transmission, a round total, the exclusion of firstborn Levites from the number available for substitution, and the like) are kept at the (Scholarly and historical discussion) level in the sermon, and this sermon decides for none of them.
4. The substitution arithmetic, checked item by item by hand: firstborn males 22,273 (v. 43) minus the Levites' total 22,000 (v. 39) makes 273, in exact agreement with verse 46's "more than the Levites" by two hundred and seventy-three; 273 men at five shekels apiece (v. 47, Summary) make 1,365 shekels, in exact agreement with the total collected at verse 50. The text's basis of reckoning must be stated expressly: the "excess" of verse 46 is computed on the total of 22,000 written at verse 39, not on the items' sum of 22,300; this sermon states the basis as the text gives it. Verse 47 also records that the shekel is twenty gerahs and that the money is taken after the shekel of the sanctuary (Summary); verse 48, that the money is given to Aaron and his sons; verse 49, that Moses took the redemption money from those over and above those redeemed by the Levites; verse 51, that the delivery was completed (v. 51 is quoted; the rest are Summary).
5. The three houses' camping sides and charges, with their verse references, item by item: Gershon — westward, behind the tabernacle (v. 23); charge: the tabernacle and the tent, its covering, the hanging for the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, the hangings of the court, the curtain for the door of the court, and its cords (vv. 25-26). Kohath — southward (v. 29); charge: the ark, the table, the candlestick, the altars, the vessels of the sanctuary, the hanging, and all its service (v. 31). Merari — northward (v. 35); charge: the boards of the tabernacle, its bars, its pillars, its sockets and all its vessels, and the pillars of the court round about, their sockets, their pins and their cords (vv. 36-37). On the east of the tabernacle: Moses, Aaron, and Aaron's sons (v. 38). The three chiefs: Eliasaph son of Lael for Gershon (v. 24), Elizaphan son of Uzziel for Kohath (v. 30), Zuriel son of Abihail for Merari (v. 35); Eleazar the son of Aaron is chief of the chiefs of the Levites, with oversight of those keeping the charge of the sanctuary (v. 32). That the sons of Kohath bear the holy things on their shoulders is the rule of Numbers 4 (Summary), not of this chapter; the sermon marks the level accordingly and does not import it into this chapter's presentation.
6. Aaron's four sons: Nadab the firstborn, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar (v. 2, Summary); all four anointed and consecrated to the priest's office (v. 3, Summary). Nadab and Abihu died before the LORD for offering strange fire, having no children, and Eleazar and Ithamar ministered in the sight of Aaron (v. 4, Summary); the strange-fire account stands at Leviticus 10:1-2 (Summary; not quoted word for word in this sermon). The names follow the base text exactly throughout.
7. mišmereṯ (Strong's H4931), "an entrusted charge, a post of keeping": the "charge" of Num 3:7-8, the "keeping the charge of the sanctuary" of v. 28, the charges of vv. 31 and 36, and the "keeping the charge of the sanctuary" of v. 38 are all of this word's family, checked against the Hebrew text. Sections I and III rest on this.
8. ʿăḇōḏāh (Strong's H5656), "labour, service, the work of an office": the "service" of "the service of the tabernacle" at Num 3:7-8 is this word, checked against the Hebrew text. Section I rests on this.
9. pāqad (Strong's H6485), "to number, to muster": the numberings of the Levites and of the firstborn at Num 3:15, 16, 39, 40, 42, 43 all use this verb, checked against the Hebrew text; it is the same verb as the numbering of the people in Numbers 1. Section II rests on this.
10. beḵôr (Strong's H1060), "firstborn, eldest son": the "firstborn" of Num 3:12, 13, 40, 41, 43, 45, 46, 50 is this word throughout, checked against the Hebrew text. Sections II and IV rest on this.
11. pidyôn (Strong's H6306), "ransom, redemption price": the "redemption money" of Num 3:49 and 3:51 is this word, checked against the Hebrew text; the acts of redeeming at vv. 46-48 are expressed with cognate forms of the same root, which this sermon does not merge with the noun's form. Section IV rests on this.
12. 13.
