"Now those who were scattered after the persecution that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to no one but the Jews only. But some of them were men from Cyprus and Cyrene, who, when they had come to Antioch, spoke to the Hellenists, preaching the Lord Jesus. ... For he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a great many people were added to the Lord. ... And when he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. So it was that for a whole year they assembled with the church and taught a great many people. And the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch." (Acts 11:19-20, 24, 26)

The word "church" in Greek is **Ekklēsia** (Greek ἐκκλησία, transliteration ekklēsia; Strong G1577; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "church, congregation"), which means "a people called out." And the name "Christian" — "one who belongs to Christ" — appeared for the first time not in Jerusalem, but in Antioch.

The church in Antioch was the first church made up mainly of Gentile believers, the first church to send out missionaries (Acts 13:1-3), and the first church that made the world exclaim, "These people belong to Christ!" Today, let us take Antioch as our mirror and look at nine aspects of building the church.

Before we set out, let us first ask a question: **What is the definition of a successful church?**

Is it building a large church? Founding a university? Writing a few bestsellers? Or is it this: we sing directly to God, the congregation truly hears the word of God, we all live holy lives, and Sundays are full of life and vitality?

Numbers can deceive, and the buzz will fade. The answer Antioch gives us is not found in size, but in life. When it comes down to it, there is only one ultimate test of church building: can it make the world exclaim — "Look, Christians!"

## I. The Church Is a Holy Thing

The church is a holy thing; it must not be treated lightly, just like the sacraments.

Paul said: "Do you not know that you are the temple of God and that the Spirit of God dwells in you? If anyone defiles the temple of God, God will destroy him. For the temple of God is holy, which temple you are." (1 Cor. 3:16-17)

The Greek word for "holy" is **Hagios** (Greek ἅγιος, transliteration hagios; Strong G40; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "holy, saints") — set apart, belonging to God. Today our sense of "holy" has grown far too faint: being late or leaving early doesn't matter, forming little cliques in the church doesn't matter, running the church by the world's methods doesn't matter. But Paul says: this temple is holy, and whoever defiles the temple of God, God will destroy him!

You need to know this: you are a small part of an eternal plan. The church is not your social club, not your psychological comfort station — the church is the temple of God, and it is holy. Every step you take into it should be taken with reverence.

## II. A Fellowship Bound Together in Life and Death

It may sound a little jarring, but the meaning runs deep: the church should be as tightly knit as a "gang" — not a criminal gang, but a gang bound together in life and death.

What was the mark of the early church? Loving one another. Jesus said: "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another; as I have loved you, that you also love one another. By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another." (John 13:34-35)

The mark of a gang is this: sharing blessings together, enduring hardships together, never betraying a brother. The "gang spirit" in the church is exactly this: your trouble is my trouble, I will wipe your tears, I will carry your burden. The disciples of Antioch were just such a band of people — scattered by persecution, yet preaching as they scattered; when they reached Antioch, they spoke to the Jews, and they also spoke to the Hellenists.

What today's church often lacks is not programs, but this "gang-style" love: the kind of love that lays down its life for one another.

## III. The Order of the Church: The Word and Prayer

The order of a healthy church is very simple: the word of God, and prayer.

**Bible study groups**: Let the power of God's word be released by the word itself. It does not depend on human eloquence; it depends on the power of the word itself. The book of Hebrews says that the word of God is living and powerful (Heb. 4:12). Once the word is opened, power is released.

**Prayer**: A praying church is a church where "everyone scrambles to take part." Note this: not "required to attend," but "scrambling to come." When the prayer meeting becomes the hardest gathering to sit through, it shows that the church has lost its heartbeat; when brothers and sisters rush to come and pray, it shows that this church's spirit is alive.

## IV. Servants: Raising Up Leaders, Not Soothing the Congregation

The workers of the church are servants. But what kind of servants?

**Let the Holy Spirit take part in the worship**: Worship is not a performance; it is the Holy Spirit Himself being present. Worship without the Holy Spirit, no matter how lively, is still just a show.

**Not caring for people, but building them up**: Many ministers have turned themselves into "caregivers" — visiting whoever is sick, helping whoever is poor, soothing whoever is upset. There is nothing wrong with care, but the core mission of a servant is to build up — to build people into disciples, to build them into leaders.

**Not a masseur, but a personal trainer**: A masseur makes you comfortable; a personal trainer makes you grow muscle. The church does not need more masseurs who make people comfortable; it needs more coaches who make people grow — pointing out your problems, pushing you to break through, sweating alongside you.

**Keep raising up outstanding leaders without ceasing**: Leaders are not born; they are raised. Barnabas of Antioch found Saul and brought him to Antioch, and they "taught a great many people" — for a whole year. Raising up leaders is the church's most important investment.

**Sending out workers**: A healthy church does not hoard talent — the church is a sending station, not a talent warehouse. Send out the leaders you have raised, to plant churches, to go on missions. Antioch sent out Paul and Barnabas (Acts 13:1-3); this is what "sending" means.

## V. Seven Marks of a Good Shepherd

Those who serve as shepherds will be judged more strictly: "My brethren, let not many of you become teachers, knowing that we shall receive a stricter judgment." (James 3:1)

A good shepherd after God's own heart has seven marks: **prayer, humility, love, being a coach, fullness of the Holy Spirit, mission-driven, and suffering**.

Prayer is the shepherd's breath; humility is the shepherd's true color; love is the shepherd's motive; being a coach is the shepherd's method; fullness of the Holy Spirit is the shepherd's power; being mission-driven is the shepherd's direction; suffering is the shepherd's seal — there is not one shepherd who has walked up to the pulpit by going around the cross.

## VI. Crucified with Christ

"I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me." (Gal. 2:20)

But remember: what we pursue is Jesus, not suffering; what we love is the people of this world, not suffering.

Some people have turned "suffering" itself into a spiritual medal, as if the more you suffer, the more spiritual you are — this is putting the cart before the horse. The purpose of the cross is to unite us with Christ, not to make us fall in love with pain. Suffering is the road, not the destination; being crucified with Christ is for the sake of rising with Him!

## VII. Open the Cage: Sending Instead of Keeping in Captivity

"Like a wild horse that has thrown off its reins" — this sounds fierce, but it hits the nail on the head.

Today's church has not trained up mighty, fearless soldiers who are willing to carry the gospel to the ends of the earth; instead, it has produced a batch of "disciples who never grow up": still depending on their parents for everything in their thirties, complaining all day long that the church has no singles' fellowship. When it comes down to it, how could a Christian who eats only one meal a week possibly survive outside the "cage" of singleness?

For the church, what we need to do is not to build bigger, prettier cages, nor to dress the cage up to look more like nature. It is time to open the cage, to awaken the nature and abilities God has given to these "animals," and to release them back into nature!

The best way to change the victim mentality in this "never growing up" mindset is not more feeding, but sending them out! Send out the congregation — the early church is our DNA.

From God's point of view: the greater the cost you see from God, the greater the power you will receive from God. God never sends anyone He does not equip.

In addition, children's Sunday school is of the utmost importance: the workers of the next generation must be grasped starting from the children.

## VIII. A Church Returned to Simplicity

The less you own, the more you are able to experience. This sounds counterintuitive, but it is true.

Deep down, we do not believe God will really move, and so we add all kinds of exciting "equipment" to our gatherings — lighting, sound systems, programs, procedures. Where faith is lacking, equipment makes up the difference. But Antioch had none of these, yet it had the Holy Spirit, it had love, and it had sending.

If we want to raise up more leaders, dividing into smaller congregations and planting churches is the road we must take. A healthy church should grow five kinds of people: **committed worshipers, a family that loves one another, well-equipped disciple-makers, missionaries full of the Holy Spirit, and suffering sojourners**.

The way of life of a church returned to simplicity looks like this: reading the Bible every day, meeting in homes, a multi-leader model; everyone discipled, everyone discipling others, everyone exercising their gifts; planting churches regularly, simple gatherings, sharing resources, receiving the mission.

A small-scale, streamlined church is the most vibrant form: fellowship centered on God's word is an everyday affair; they rarely discuss so-called good sermons, but often discuss the truths they have discovered in the Bible; they often spend hours or even days praying alone; they enjoy Jesus, and prayer meetings often "run overtime," with no one willing to leave; everyone is willing to open their homes to receive others; there can even be bivocational pastors — the office lies not in whether one is full-time or bivocational, but in whether one is faithful.

## IX. Overcoming Pride: Zero Tolerance for Division

The last point, the most severe, and also the most necessary: factions and divisions must never be indulged.

"Reject a divisive man after the first and second admonition, knowing that such a person is warped and sinning, being self-condemned." (Titus 3:10-11)

Most people think that driving someone out of the church is an unloving act. In reality, in the name of mercy, they are openly defying the teaching of the Bible (Matt. 18:15-20; 1 Corinthians 5; Titus 3:10-11). Stop deceiving yourselves: this is not mercy at all; it is rebellion. If we tolerate those who divide the church and let them remain in the church, we are profaning God's holy church. To sit by and do nothing is no different from silently permitting a sinner to split God's church — this is something God utterly detests.

Do not let tears deceive you: learn to distinguish between "the wounded" and "professional victims." The former need healing; the latter need to be dealt with.

Replace entanglement with worship: the root of many disputes is pride; the best way to deal with pride is to bow down in worship — humble yourself before God, and you will not be able to stir up storms before men.

## This Week's Practice

To put today's word into practice in your life, please take away two concrete exercises:

**First**, keep watch for the church. This week, pray by name for one leader of the church and one ministry, asking God to raise up workers and send out workers.

**Second**, claim one act of "sending." This week, take the initiative to shoulder one act of service — visiting, leading a Bible study, caring for the community — and shift from "being fed" to "feeding others."

## Conclusion

Let us return to Antioch. The disciples were called "Christians" not because they passed a resolution at a meeting to change their name, but because the world looked at their lives and recognized, "These people belong to Christ."

The ultimate test of church building lies not in numbers, not in buildings, not in programs — but in life: Is there holiness? Is there love? Is there the Holy Spirit? Is there sending? Is there the holy courage of zero tolerance for division?

May our churches all be like Antioch, so that the world exclaims: "Look, Christians!"

## Response Prayer

Dear Lord, we thank You! You have called us out to become Your church — Ekklēsia, a people called out.

Lord, forgive us for treating the church as a club, as a performance venue, as a social platform. Please restore in us a reverence for what is "holy": the church is Your temple, it is holy, and it must not be treated lightly.

Lord, please give us hearts that love one another, bound together in life and death like a "gang"; give us the order of the word and prayer; raise up servant-hearted leaders, and train generation after generation of workers; give our shepherds hearts of prayer, humility, love, and fullness of the Holy Spirit.

Lord, send us out, this band of "wild horses that have thrown off their reins"; do not keep us penned up in pretty cages. Let us return to simplicity, back to the simplicity of the apostles. And give us the courage to overcome pride, with zero tolerance for anything that divides the church.

May our church, like Antioch, let the world recognize: these people belong to Christ! In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen!
