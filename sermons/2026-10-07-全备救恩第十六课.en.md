## Content

The experience of salvation. How do I know that I am saved? Salvation is not a kind of knowledge; it is an experience. It is not that we can be saved because we have understood some theories of salvation; it is only by receiving salvation that we are counted as having received grace. A saved person can compare his experience of grace and his experience of salvation with the principles of salvation, to see whether his own experience accords with the word of God. This kind of experience is not deliberately contrived; it is a natural outflow. If there is the fact of salvation, there will naturally be the expression of salvation. "Your garments shall always be white, and your head shall lack no oil" (Eccl. 9:8, NKJV).

The joy of believing (a double joy) (John ): the joy of first meeting the Lord and the joy of obtaining the Lord. "So he brought them into his house... and he rejoiced, having believed in God with all his household" (Acts 16:34, NKJV). In the experience of salvation, the most evident thing is the joy that comes from faith. This is not an outward stimulation; it is an inward activation. Just as the prophet Habakkuk said: "Though the fig tree may not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines; though the labor of the olive may fail, and the fields yield no food; though the flock may be cut off from the fold, and there be no herd in the stalls — yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation" (Hab. 3:17-18, NKJV).

Peace (four kinds of peace: the peace of forgiveness of sins; the peace of promise; the peace of meeting the Lord; the peace of being sent). [Original text partially illegible at this point.] A saved person has the joy of believing and also the peace of forgiveness. The former will not fail because of adverse circumstances, and the latter will not be shaken by disturbances all around. For the joy is the joy of the Holy Spirit, and the peace is the peace of Christ: "In the world you will have tribulation" (John 16:33, NKJV), yet in the Lord there is peace.

The satisfaction of hunger and thirst (man originally lives amid every kind of hunger and thirst that cannot be satisfied; only in Christ is man satisfied, for God is love!). Jesus said: "...he who believes in Me shall never thirst" (John 6:35, NKJV). Before we were saved, our life was as if we were both hungry and thirsty; the emptiness within seemed as though nothing could fill it. But thank the Lord: when we come before the Lord, we obtain satisfaction from Him. The Lord once said to the Samaritan woman: "Whoever drinks of the water that I shall give him will never thirst" (John 4:13-14, NKJV). Truly, because of the Lord we are satisfied.

Rest for the soul (unless we rest in the Creator and Redeemer, man has nowhere to rest). "Come to Me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matt. 11:28, NKJV). Our life is a life of bearing heavy burdens. A hundred years looked back upon are but labor and sorrow and a sigh. But because of the Lord's call we come before the Lord, and we can cast all our care [upon Him], for He is the Lord who daily bears our burden.

A living hope (only with hope can we endure the hardships of life and lightly regard the rumors of the world). "...[God] has begotten us again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead" (1 Pet. 1:3, NKJV). A living hope is a hope that is alive. Formerly we had no hope; now we have great hope! And this hope goes beyond this life and continues into eternal life (1 Peter).

These are experiences every saved person must have. Although they differ in depth, we all have these experiences.

Homework questions: [Original text partially illegible.] Can one be saved [by this alone]? What is the joy of believing? What is the peace of forgiveness? What is the satisfaction of hunger and thirst? What is the rest of the soul? What is the living hope? Do you have these several experiences of salvation?
