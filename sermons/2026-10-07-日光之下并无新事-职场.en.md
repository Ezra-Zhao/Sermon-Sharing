# Nothing New Under the Sun: The Workplace

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：职场».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of the workplace

## Summary

For this lesson on the workplace, the figure the Bible gives is Daniel. His nation was destroyed and he was taken captive, falling from a nobleman to a prisoner, yet he passed through three dynasties — Babylon, Media, and Persia — and four kings, always standing in a high position. This chapter studies Daniel chapter 1, refining from him four things: preparation, perseverance, wisdom, and team. These four things are not success-study slogans, but an order: prepare well on ordinary days, and opportunities can be seized; begin perseverance from today, and boundaries can be held; persevere with wisdom from God, and one need not clash head-on; walk with a spiritual team, and one can go far.

## I. You Are the Light of the World

Speaking of the workplace, first make the identity clear. The Lord Jesus said:

> "You are the light of the world. A city that is set on a hill cannot be hidden. Nor do they light a lamp and put it under a basket, but on a lampstand, and it gives light to all who are in the house. Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven." (Matt. 5:14-16)

Here "light" in Greek is **phōs (φῶς)** (original text φῶς, transliterated phōs; Strong G5457; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "light, brightness"). For a Christian in the workplace, identity comes before strategy: you are not going to muddle along in a position; you are a person placed on a lampstand. Conduct is placed before people, and glory is given to the heavenly Father — this is the general outline of all methods in this chapter. Daniel's life is the footnote to this passage.

## II. Daniel's Circumstances

When Daniel appears, his circumstances could not be worse:

> "In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it. And the Lord gave Jehoiakim king of Judah into his hand, with some of the articles of the house of God, which he carried into the land of Shinar to the house of his god; and he brought the articles into the treasure house of his god." (Dan. 1:1-2)

The king selected young men from the Israelites, with conditions:

> "Young men in whom there was no blemish, but good-looking, gifted in all wisdom, possessing knowledge and quick to understand, who had ability to serve in the king's palace, and whom they might teach the language and literature of the Chaldeans. And the king appointed for them a daily provision of the king's delicacies and of the wine which he drank, and three years of training for them, so that at the end of that time they might serve before the king." (Dan. 1:3-5)

Among them were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah of the children of Judah (Dan. 1:6). The chief of the eunuchs gave them new names, calling Daniel Belteshazzar, and the other three each had Babylonian names as well (Dan. 1:7). Names could be changed, the food was the king's grace; it seemed everything was not up to them.

The Bible uses a whole chapter to record this matter, not to tell an inspirational story. Daniel's life passed through three dynasties — Babylon, Media, and Persia — and experienced four kings — Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar, Darius, and Cyrus; from a royal nobleman to a captive prisoner and servant, then to a powerful important minister, his life was full of climaxes, yet he also often experienced the valley of the shadow of death, and always turned danger into safety.

Put another way: Daniel passed through three board reorganizations, the chairman changed four times, and he still continued as general manager. Many people wanted to bring him down, yet never succeeded; on the contrary, he was promoted and given raises. In imagination, a person like him should be a person adept at social relations, smooth and slick in all directions. But from the Bible, he was not such a person. Then, by what ability could he continue to sit in his position? This chapter answers from four aspects.

## III. Preparation: When Opportunity Comes, Are You Prepared

The first thing is preparation.

Young brothers and sisters entering the workplace will all complain that the current general environment is not good. But there is no year when work is easy to find, no year without negative news. The key lies in: When opportunity comes, have we prepared ourselves?

Look at Daniel and it becomes clear. Many people see his very good performance in the workplace; in fact the general environment was not good to him at all — his nation perished, he became a captive prisoner, seemingly with no future. In such a situation, God still gave him opportunity; but whether he could seize the opportunity depended on his ordinary preparation. One of the king's selection criteria, "gifted in all wisdom, possessing knowledge" (Dan. 1:4), was not crammed out temporarily after being taken captive; it was read and learned day after day before the nation perished.

Therefore complaining about the environment does not solve problems. The environment is about the same for everyone, even worse for Daniel. The difference is only in one place: When opportunity knocks, is the person inside the house long prepared?

## IV. Perseverance: Begin From Today, From the Present Position

The second thing is perseverance, and the starting point of perseverance is the most famous sentence in chapter 1:

> "But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's delicacies, nor with the wine which he drank; therefore he requested of the chief of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself." (Dan. 1:8)

The king of Babylon took his own things to nourish these selected people, hoping that after three years these people could be used by him. The wine and food the king provided had been offered in worship to false gods, so Daniel purposed not to defile himself with these foods that had not been set apart as holy.

What is rare is his age and the timing. Daniel, so young, decided he could persevere for God; this was very not easy. At that time he had not yet entered the king's palace; he was only in the training period. Brothers and sisters, face perseverance in faith in the "present"; do not think that in the "future" you can persevere for the Lord, because in the future the things you consider will only be more complicated. Unless we begin from today, from the present position.

Hebrews speaks the same principle to everyone:

> "For we have become partakers of Christ if we hold the beginning of our confidence steadfast to the end." (Heb. 3:14)

Passing through three dynasties and four monarchs, Daniel could still stand in his position unshaken. He was not a person adept at social relations, smooth in all directions; God blessed Daniel — what we are to look at is precisely how Daniel persevered for the Lord.

## V. Wisdom: Perseverance Is Not Clashing Head-On

The third thing is wisdom, speaking of how to persevere.

Daniel purposed in his heart, but did not directly deny the chief of the eunuchs' command. For the chief of the eunuchs had his difficulties; he said to his face:

> "And the chief of the eunuchs said to Daniel, 'I fear my lord the king, who has appointed your food and drink. For why should he see your faces looking worse than the young men who are your age? Then you would endanger my head before the king.'" (Dan. 1:10)

Daniel did not force him. He went around to the steward whom the chief of the eunuchs had appointed, and proposed a verifiable plan:

> "Please test your servants for ten days, and let them give us vegetables to eat and water to drink. Then let our appearance be examined before you, and the appearance of the young men who eat the portion of the king's delicacies; and as you see fit, so deal with your servants." (Dan. 1:12-13)

> "So he consented with them in this matter, and tested them ten days. And at the end of ten days their features appeared better and fatter in flesh than all the young men who ate the portion of the king's delicacies. Thus the steward took away their portion of delicacies and the wine that they were to drink, and gave them vegetables." (Dan. 1:14-16)

This is what wisdom looks like: The bottom line is not yielded, the method can be discussed; do not push the other party into a corner, leave the other party a road he can walk; let ten days of results speak, do not let the volume of the voice speak.

If the motive is for God's glory, I am willing to use wisdom that comes from God to avoid some unnecessary conflicts and confrontations. If it is to fight for my own interests, a person will become unscrupulous. Therefore first examine the motive, then discuss the method. The Lord's words also set a boundary for speech:

> "But let your 'Yes' be 'Yes,' and your 'No,' 'No.' For whatever is more than these is from the evil one." (Matt. 5:37)

> "In the multitude of words sin is not lacking, but he who restrains his lips is wise." (Prov. 10:19)

> "If it is possible, as much as depends on you, live peaceably with all men." (Rom. 12:18)

The attitude of service in the workplace was also made clear by Paul:

> "Bondservants, be obedient to those who are your masters according to the flesh, with fear and trembling, in sincerity of heart, as to Christ; not with eyeservice, as men-pleasers, but as bondservants of Christ, doing the will of God from the heart; with goodwill doing service, as to the Lord, and not to men, knowing that whatever good anyone does, he will receive the same from the Lord, whether he is a slave or free." (Eph. 6:5-8)

Treating work as service to the Lord, perseverance is no longer contending with the boss, but giving account to the Lord. When the mindset changes, many conflicts naturally are avoided.

## VI. Team: Do Not Fight Alone

The fourth thing is team.

Even though Daniel was very outstanding, intelligent, and wise, he did not fight alone. They were four people in total. The Scripture says:

> "As for these four young men, God gave them knowledge and skill in all literature and wisdom; and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams." (Dan. 1:17)

When the three years were full, the chief of the eunuchs brought them before the king:

> "Then the king interviewed them, and among them all none was found like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah; therefore they served before the king. And in all matters of wisdom and understanding about which the king examined them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers who were in all his realm." (Dan. 1:19-20)

Note that the Scripture from beginning to end puts these four people together. They were taken captive together, renamed together, ate vegetables together, and stood together before the king. Daniel did not think that because he was smarter and more outstanding than others, why should he be with others; for he knew he could not fight alone, and needed spiritual companions.

In the workplace you also need spiritual companions. Going alone as a single soldier is easiest to be attacked; thinking oneself stands firm is easiest to fall. Do not only come on Sunday to hear a sermon; you need a team, you need to enter into a body, you need spiritual companions — "Flee also youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart" (2 Tim. 2:22). Facing many important decisions in life, you need people to pray with you, you need people to accompany you in spiritual warfare in prayer. You are encouraged to join a small group.

Many young people once loved the Lord very much, but after entering the workplace, they were quickly lost. We must be in one body, that is, in the church, connected together with one another. May the Lord help us grasp spiritual principles and glorify the Lord's name in the workplace.

## VII. Cycles and Patterns: Usually It Ends Here

After studying Daniel chapter 1, the source material specially left a reminder: Usually it ends here — this is only the part of self-discipline.

> "That which has been is what will be, that which is done is what will be done, and there is nothing new under the sun." (Eccl. 1:9)

The workplace also has its cycles and patterns. Daniel's experience first tells us a most basic thing: People who can stand firm in any cycle rely not on having understood every trend, but on having done the four works of preparation, perseverance, wisdom, and team beforehand. Trends will change, kings will change; these four do not change.

Jesus is even more the Lord of us who are in the workplace. Relying on Jesus, through prayer, we face all kinds of challenges; we believe the promises He gives us, landing the Bible's promises in our era. We are willing to trust Him and follow Him, leading us to live a life no longer the same, walking securely on the heights:

> "The LORD God is my strength; He will make my feet like deer's feet, and He will make me walk on my high hills." (Hab. 3:19)

## Conclusion

Daniel's workplace had no secret, only order. First preparation, then perseverance; perseverance must use wisdom, and on the road there must be companions. Each of these four things can begin today. The environment will not first become better, and opportunities will not wait for people — a person first prepares well, stands in the present position for the Lord, and the rest, God will cause to grow.
