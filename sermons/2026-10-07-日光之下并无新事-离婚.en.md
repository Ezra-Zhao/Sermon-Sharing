# Nothing New Under the Sun: Divorce

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：离婚».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of divorce

## Summary

God hates divorce — this is the starting point of this chapter, and also the bottom line. The Bible gives only two grounds on which divorce is permissible: unfaithfulness in the sexual relationship, and desertion by the unbelieving party. Apart from these, when people cry out in marriage, "There is no more love," the root is often not that the spouse has changed, but that the human heart is hardened. This chapter first makes the boundaries clear, then deals with two real questions: what should a person who divorced before believing in the Lord and has remarried do now; and why does a person who is unwilling to divorceinstead  feel guilty.

## I. God Hates Divorce

The words of Malachi are very heavy:

> "'For the LORD God of Israel says that He hates divorce, for it covers one's garment with violence,' says the LORD of hosts. 'Therefore take heed to your spirit, that you do not deal treacherously.'" (Mal. 2:16)

Here "hates" in Hebrew is **sane (שָׂנֵא)** (original text שָׂנֵא, transliterated sane; Strong H8130; according to the *Brown-Driver-Briggs Hebrew Lexicon*, "to hate, to detest"). This is not a word of emotion. God speaks of divorcing a wife and treating a wife with violence together — in His eyes, tearing up the marriage covenant and injuring a person by hand are matters of the same kind. Therefore, when talking about divorce, do not first talk about technique; first take heed to your own spirit, and do not deal treacherously.

Then, does the Bible give situations in which one may choose divorce? Yes, only two kinds.

**The first kind, unfaithfulness in the sexual relationship.** The Lord Jesus personally said:

> "And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another, commits adultery; and whoever marries her who is divorced commits adultery." (Matt. 19:9)

Note the weight of this sentence: It first establishes the general rule that "divorcing a wife and marrying another is committing adultery," leaving only one exception — for the cause of sexual immorality. The reason the exception is an exception is precisely that the general rule cannot be moved.

**The second kind, desertion by the unbelieving party.** When Paul dealt with marriages between believers and unbelievers, he said:

> "But if the unbeliever departs, let him depart; a brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases, but God has called us to peace." (1 Cor. 7:15)

Here the initiative is not with the believing party. The one who wants to depart is the unbeliever; the believing party is not under bondage — this is not encouraging separation, but God has called us to peace; if a person insists on leaving, he cannot be stopped, and one need not bind oneself with guilt either.

Apart from these two grounds, there is no third. Feeling unable to go on, incompatible personalities, feelings having faded — none is among them.

## II. Divorced and Remarried Before Believing in the Lord — What Should One Do Now

Someone divorced before becoming a Christian, and later married again. After believing in the Lord and reading the Bible's words on divorce, his heart is uneasy, and he asks: Should I divorce my remarried spouse and return to remarry my former spouse?

The response is very clear: No.

Deuteronomy draws this boundary very clearly:

> "When a man takes a wife and marries her, and it happens that she finds no favor in his eyes because he has found some uncleanness in her, and he writes her a certificate of divorce, puts it in her hand, and sends her out of his house, when she has departed from his house, and goes and becomes another man's wife, if the latter husband detests her and writes her a certificate of divorce, puts it in her hand, and sends her out of his house, or if the latter husband dies who took her as his wife, then her former husband who divorced her must not take her back to be his wife after she has been defiled; for that is an abomination before the LORD, and you shall not bring sin on the land which the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance." (Deut. 24:1-4)

Returning to remarry a former spouse after remarriage is what the LORD detests. God gave this command to warn men: Abandoning a wife bears consequences; marriage is not an experiment that can be overturned and restarted. Matters before believing in the Lord must be confessed before God, and His forgiveness received; but the direction of remedy is not to tear apart again a marriage already established. Using a new error to remedy an old error will only add error upon error. The lesson now is to be faithful in the present marriage.

## III. "There Is No More Love in My Marriage"

Before the thought of divorce arises, there is usually first a sentence in the heart: There is no more love in my marriage. Life is short; I have the right to pursue happiness and personal satisfaction elsewhere.

These words sound like awakening, but in fact they are the lesion. The diagnosis this chapter gives it is only four words in the original sense: hardness of heart.

> "Happy is the man who is always reverent, but he who hardens his heart will fall into calamity." (Prov. 28:14)

A hardened heart sees only problems in the spouse, and only grievances in itself; it no longer draws love from God, but only demands love from the other party; when it cannot obtain it, it announces this marriage is dead.

The right understanding is another road: God wants me to obtain love and strength from Christ who dwells within, and then go to love and respect my spouse; I am to look to God to satisfy my personal needs, rather than recording the whole account of satisfaction on my spouse's head. When love dries up, many times it is not that the other party has drained the well dry, but that one has first cut off the source oneself.

This road is walkable, not because human will is strong enough, but because God is faithful:

> "He who calls you is faithful, who also will do it." (1 Thess. 5:24)

Concretely, how to practice? The solution given in the source material is very plain: memorize Scripture, read repeatedly.

Memorize the words of Philippians:

> "I know how to be abased, and I know how to abound. Everywhere and in all things I have learned both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." (Phil. 4:12-13)

The secret Paul speaks of is standing firm in both abundance and need. If a person can stand firm in circumstances, he can also stand firm in the low valley of marriage. Read repeatedly Psalm 119:25-32, and let God's word lift up again the heart lying in the dust.

## IV. Unwilling to Divorce, Why Do Iinstead  Feel Guilty

There is another question, whose askers are often in more pain: I am unwilling to divorce, why do Iinstead  feel guilty?

First admit that this guilt is normal. Being unwilling to divorce yet feeling guilty does not show you are hypocritical; it only shows you are honest. For even if you are the innocent party, you will make mistakes in the marriage. In your life there may be some problems not yet realized, for which you need to bear responsibility. When a marriage reaches this step, rarely is one party entirely right and the other entirely wrong; admitting one's own portion is not exonerating the other party, but letting oneself first be healed.

Proverbs speaks this road through thoroughly:

> "He who covers his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy." (Prov. 28:13)

> "The ear that hears the rebukes of life will abide among the wise." (Prov. 15:31)

The one who covers spends all his strength proving he is not wrong, and the more he covers the more tired he becomes; the one who confesses first lays out his own problems before God, andinstead  obtains mercy. If guilt leads a person to confession here, it has completed its work; the rest is entrusted to God's mercy.

## Conclusion

On this matter of divorce, the Bible's attitude is consistent from beginning to end: God hates it, and only in the two situations of sexual immorality and desertion does He loosen the marriage covenant; all other roads are roads that walk back — back to Christ who dwells within to obtain love, back to God's word to obtain strength, back to confession to obtain mercy. There is nothing new under the sun; the struggles in marriages today, the Bible has already written the way out for them.
