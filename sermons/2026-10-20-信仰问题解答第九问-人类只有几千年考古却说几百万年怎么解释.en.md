# Faith Q&A Question 9: Mankind Has Only a Few Thousand Years, Yet Archaeology Speaks of Millions of Years — How Is This to Be Explained?

## Scripture

> "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." (Gen. 1:1, NKJV)

> "The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us and to our children forever, that we may do all the words of this law." (Deut. 29:29, NKJV)

---

## I. Dismantle the Question First: Scripture Never Gave This Number

The person asking has taken one thing for granted: as if Scripture clearly states that mankind, from the beginning until now, has only a few thousand years.

Pastor Tong's first sentence dismantles this premise: **Scripture has not told us how many thousands of years there have been from Genesis until now.**

Genesis opens by speaking of "God created"; it does not speak of "God announcing a chronology." It tells of creation with perfect clarity, yet it does not attach a timetable at the side, stating the first year, the first millennium, the sixth millennium. To take something Scripture has not said as something Scripture has said, and then take that sentence and go argue with archaeology — the argument has not even begun, and the starting point is already wrong.

This is the first lesson to learn: **what Scripture has not stated plainly, do not state conclusively on its behalf.** State it conclusively, and once that number cannot stand, bystanders will think it is faith that has fallen, when in fact what has fallen is only what we ourselves added.

## II. Who Calculated the Six Thousand Years?

Then where does "about six thousand years" come from?

Pastor Tong says it was calculated by a British bishop: he added the genealogies and the ages together item by item and reckoned about four thousand years from Adam to Jesus Christ; add the roughly two thousand years from Jesus until now, and you get the figure of about six thousand years for human history.

(A small note: the original manuscript recorded this bishop's name as "Hasse"; by historical fact it should be James Ussher, the seventeenth-century Irish archbishop. In 1650 he published his chronological calculation, fixing creation in 4004 BC. The name is corrected here according to historical fact; the argument is unchanged.)

But note carefully: calculations have never produced only one answer. Pastor Tong goes on to give an example: in 1912, another British scholar calculated 8,169 years from Adam to Jesus — the same Bible, the same set of genealogies, and the calculation differs by more than four thousand years.

What does this show? **These numbers are men's calculations, not Scripture's declarations.** A calculation can be studied, discussed, and wrong. Whoever elevates one particular calculated result into "the position of Scripture" has already passed his own arithmetic off as the word of God.

**Original-language analysis:** Genesis 5:1 says, "This is the book of the genealogy of Adam"; the "genealogy" here is, in Hebrew, ***Toledot* (תּוֹלְדוֹת)**, meaning generations, descendants, origins. This word recurs throughout Genesis, and its function is to carry a line of generations forward — remembering who begat whom and where the line is going. It records a lineage; it is not an addition problem prepared for later readers: add up the ages generation by generation and you will get a chronology of heaven and earth. Reading a genealogy as a chronology is a use the reader himself adds; the focus of the text was never there.

Look again at "In the beginning" in Genesis 1:1; in Hebrew it is ***Bereshit* (בְּרֵאשִׁית)**, which is simply "in the beginning." The first sentence of Scripture declares one thing only: the beginning is God, and all things begin from God. It does not go on to report a date — there is no "which year the beginning was," only "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." The opening is like this, and the use of the genealogies afterward is like this too: Scripture cares about who the source is, not about marking the source with a year.

## III. Pastor Tong's Counter-Question: From When Did Writing Exist?

Having put the Scripture side in order, look now at the archaeology side. Archaeologists say they have found human cultures of millions of years.

Pastor Tong was in no hurry to rebut them item by item; he asked a counter-question: **if human culture really has already lasted thousands, millions of years, why is it that genuinely written records only exist in these last few thousand years?**

This sentence must be heard precisely. He is not asking "whether men were active millions of years ago"; he is asking about **records**.

Unearth a stone tool, and its age is something later people have calculated; leave behind a piece of writing, and its meaning is something the people of that time wrote down themselves. These two are not the same thing: one is calculation, the other is record. Man's "recorded history," counted in full, is precisely these few thousand years since writing appeared. Everything traced back before that is inference made from remains — inference can stand, and it can also be revised; in archaeology, years being changed back and forth is a normal occurrence.

So Pastor Tong's question calls people to keep two levels separate: **calculated ages and recorded history are two different ledgers, and they cannot be reckoned together.**

## IV. Whose Problem Is This, After All?

Pressed to the end, Pastor Tong draws a crisp conclusion: **this is not Christianity's problem; it is a great difficulty evolution itself has run into.**

What he means is: on the Christian side, say it plainly first — Scripture has given no timetable, so do not carry a number Scripture has not given into battle. The side that truly has an accounting to give is the other side: if, as they claim, man has already been active on the earth for millions of years, then why, during the vast majority of that history, was no written record left behind, and why did man suddenly have writing and written history only in these last few thousand years? This long blank must be explained by those who put forward the claim of millions of years.

For the Christian, the gain of this piece is actually not in winning the argument with archaeology, but in returning to the way Scripture is read:

- What Scripture states plainly: stand firm, and do not retreat one step
- What Scripture does not state plainly: leave it blank, and do not fill in God's words for Him
- Men's calculations, whether a bishop's or a scholar's, are only calculations and are not fit to be guarded as doctrine

---

## For Us Today

1. Do not say on Scripture's behalf what it has not said — "mankind has only six thousand years" is a bishop's calculation, not Scripture's declaration
2. Keep calculation and declaration separate: a calculation off by thousands of years shakes the arithmetic, not the faith
3. Calculated ages and recorded history are two different ledgers — written records go back only a few thousand years, and that is where Pastor Tong's counter-question presses
4. Where Scripture leaves a blank, honestly leave it blank; that shows more reverence for God than forcing a number together
