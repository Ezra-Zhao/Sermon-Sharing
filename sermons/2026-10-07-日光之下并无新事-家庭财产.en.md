# Nothing New Under the Sun: Family Property

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：家庭财产».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of family property

## Summary

Family property is a blessing left to descendants; handled poorly, it can tear a family apart. This chapter first presents a real inheritance problem, then returns to the Bible to look at the rules of inheritance: the firstborn receives a double portion, if there is no son then daughters inherit, and if there are no close relatives, inheritance follows an order. Add advance arrangements, understanding, self-discipline, and reliance on God, and property can be left as a blessing rather than as a battlefield.

## I. An Inheritance Problem: Why Can an Only Daughter Inherit Only 7/8

> "A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children, but the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the righteous." (Prov. 13:22)

In the key Scripture, the word "inheritance" in Hebrew is **nachalah (נַחֲלָה)** (original text נַחֲלָה, transliterated nachalah; Strong H5159; according to the *BDB Lexicon*, "inheritance, inherited property"), which in the Old Testament mostly refers to that portion of land and family property handed down from the father's house for a family to live on. It is not only money; it is the foundation on which a family lives.

First look at the case in the original manuscript.

Xiaoli, an only child, ten years after her father's death, wanted to dispose of a house her father had left. She never expected: as her parents' only child, she could inherit only 7/8 ownership of this property; even for this 7/8, she still had to go through complicated notarization of inheritance rights before she could sell the property.

A simple idea — "an only daughter inherits her father's property" — when it lands in reality becomes division of shares, the legal order of inheritance, and a whole set of inheritance procedures. The original manuscript presents this matter not to discuss the legal details themselves, but to make a judgment: If the problem of property inheritance is not handled properly, it will turn a family originally full of warmth, joy, and happiness into the main battlefield of estate disputes, into a place where flesh and blood harm each other.

Where specifically is the risk? The original manuscript listed relevant provisions of marriage law and inheritance law, and the risks that may appear in lifetime gifts, statutory inheritance, and testamentary inheritance, summed up in four items: loss of control over the property; the second generation squandering the property; the second generation's marital risk causing family property to be divided; and confusion of premarital and postmarital property, causing family property to be lost. These four risks have one point in common — none is trouble caused by money itself, but the consequence of no advance arrangements and no clear rules.

Therefore, when a good man leaves an inheritance to his descendants, the word "leaves" means not only leaving things behind, but also leaving them properly and clearly.

## II. The Rules God Set for Inheritance

How family property is divided — the Bible does not only speak of principles; it has given very concrete norms.

**First, the firstborn inherits a double portion.** Deuteronomy sets it thus:

> "If a man has two wives, one loved and the other unloved, and they have borne him children, both the loved and the unloved, and if the firstborn son is of her who is unloved, then it shall be, on the day he bequeaths his possessions to his sons, that he must not bestow firstborn status on the son of the loved wife in preference to the son of the unloved, the true firstborn. But he shall acknowledge the son of the unloved wife as the firstborn by giving him a double portion of all that he has, for he is the beginning of his strength; the right of the firstborn is his." (Deut. 21:15-17)

The key to this law is that it does not permit changing the firstborn's status because of the parents' favoritism. When dividing property, it is easiest to act on feelings; the Bible first nails down the rule: status belongs to whom it belongs, not decided by favoritism.

**Second, if there is no son, daughters may inherit.** Numbers says:

> "If a man dies and has no son, then you shall cause his inheritance to pass to his daughter." (Num. 27:8)

Daughters are not an exception outside the sequence of inheritance; they are heirs written expressly into the law.

**Third, if there is no direct heir, inheritance follows the order of closeness.** The order given in the Bible is:

> "If he has no daughter, then you shall give his inheritance to his brothers. If he has no brothers, then you shall give his inheritance to his father's brothers. And if his father has no brothers, then you shall give his inheritance to the relative closest to him in his family, and he shall possess it. And this shall be to the children of Israel a statute of judgment, just as the LORD commanded Moses." (Num. 27:9-11)

**Fourth, land is not passed to another family.** In the absence of direct heirs, a favored servant or a more distant relative may receive the family property — when Abram had no son, he once said, "the heir of my house is Eliezer of Damascus" (Gen. 15:2); but according to the law, this land can never at any time be passed to another family. The reason, as the original manuscript explains clearly: the purpose of inheriting land is to ensure that the extended family has a means of support and survival. Property is first the foundation of survival, and only secondarily wealth.

Putting these four together, the Bible's view of inheritance is very clear: there is order, it is not by favoritism, it does not cut off a person's path, and it guards the foundation of the family's survival. Today's legal environment is different, and the provisions cannot be copied wholesale, but this sense of order and the consciousness of "first making the rules clear" is precisely what was most lacking in Xiaoli's case.

## III. Abraham's Practice: Clear Division During His Lifetime

In the Bible there is a positive demonstration: Abraham.

Abraham completed the arrangements for his property during his lifetime:

> "And Abraham gave all that he had to Isaac. But Abraham gave gifts to the sons of the concubines which Abraham had; and while he was still living he sent them eastward, away from Isaac his son, to the country of the east." (Gen. 25:5-6)

"While he was still living" — this sentence is key. What should be given to whom was given, who should be sent away was sent away, all handled while he himself was clear-minded and alive, not leaving a confused account for those after him to fight over. His earlier bringing Rebekah into Sarah's tent and taking her as wife for Isaac (Gen. 24:67) was the same thinking: what can be arranged, arrange in advance.

After his death, "his sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the cave of Machpelah" (Gen. 25:9). That the two sons could stand together before their father's grave is inseparable from that clear arrangement during his lifetime.

In the Bible there is also a fragment of Joshua dividing the inheritance: the people of the three and a half tribes obeyed all that Moses commanded, and Joshua sent them back to their tents, "to the land of your possession, which Moses the servant of the LORD gave you on the other side of the Jordan" (Josh. 22:1-4). Property was divided according to the promise, each returning to his own inheritance — the endpoint of dividing inheritance is that each family enjoys peace.

## IV. The Way to Resolution: Understanding, Self-Discipline, Biblical Principles, Reliance on God

The way to resolution for the family property problem given in the original manuscript lists several items: understanding, self-discipline, biblical principles, reliance on God, plus professional help and fellowship prayer.

- **Understanding:** First recognize clearly the nature of property — it is property entrusted by God, not the private property of any generation to dispose of at will; also recognize where the risks lie (control, second generation squandering, marital division, confusion of premarital and postmarital property).
- **Self-discipline:** Rules established must be carried out. How the will is to be written, how gifts are to be made — once decided, follow it, do not change course because of momentary softness or favoritism — Deuteronomy's prohibition on changing the firstborn's status guards precisely against this.
- **Biblical principles:** The order of inheritance, land not flowing outside, a good man leaving an inheritance to descendants — establish these principles in the heart first, then connect them with today's legal provisions.
- **Reliance on God:** However thorough the property arrangements, they cannot settle people's hearts; the final reliance is not in property.
- **Professional help and fellowship prayer:** What should be notarized, notarize it; whom should be consulted among professionals, consult them; at the same time bring the family's difficulties into the fellowship in prayer, do not carry it alone by sheer effort.

## V. Those Who Suffer in Property Turmoil

Property matters hurt people often not only in money, but also in the heart. When change comes to the family and disputes arise over property, a person's most natural reaction is to ask: Why did this happen to me?

Here the original manuscript gave several Scriptures, for those who suffer.

First, guard against the root of bitterness:

> "Looking carefully lest anyone fall short of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up cause trouble, and by this many become defiled." (Heb. 12:15)

In suffering, beware of self-pity, envy, jealousy, anxiety, and false guilt. If these emotions are not dealt with, they will grow into a root of bitterness. The first step of detoxification is to change one's perspective on one's own circumstances:

> "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the LORD, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope." (Jer. 29:11)

Further down is an understanding of suffering itself:

> "It is good for me that I have been afflicted, that I may learn Your statutes." (Ps. 119:71)

Finally is a word of comfort, and also the landing point of this chapter:

> "He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds." (Ps. 147:3)

Property can be divided, ownership can be calculated in shares — 7/8 or however much, all are earthly numbers. The brokenhearted, God Himself heals; their wounds He binds up with His own hand. This portion is not divided by shares.

## Conclusion

There is nothing new under the sun. Families thousands of years ago had to divide land; families today have to process notarization; the appearance of the problem has changed, but the principle has not: When a good man leaves an inheritance to his descendants, whether what is left is a blessing or a dispute depends mostly on whether during his lifetime the rules were made clear and arrangements were made plain. Handle property matters in order, and entrust the heart to the One who heals the brokenhearted — neither of these two things can be missing.
