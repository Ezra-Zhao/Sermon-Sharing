Please think about this: **Just now, what was in your mind?**

Were you thinking about the information you read today? Or were you thinking about what to eat at noon? Were you giving thanks for one thing, or anxious about another?

Our thoughts never stop for a moment. There is a sentence in the course that awakened me: **"Our mind is a continual battlefield."** On this battlefield, every day, every moment, two kinds of thoughts are at war: one pulls us toward the flesh, toward complaining; the other leads us toward the Holy Spirit, toward thanksgiving. And which one we choose determines which kind of life we live.

Paul says in Romans chapter 12, verse 2:

> "And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God." (Rom. 12:2, NKJV)

The word "mind" in Greek is **νοῦς (nous)** (original νοῦς, transliteration nous; Strong G3563; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "mind, understanding"), referring to man's intellect, the center of thought; "be transformed" in Greek is **μεταμορφόω (metamorphoō)** (original μεταμορφόω, transliteration metamorphoō; Strong G3339; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to transform, to change form"), from which the English word metamorphosis (transformation, metamorphosis) comes — like a caterpillar becoming a butterfly, a complete change. Paul says what we need is not to "control" our thoughts, but to let our thoughts "metamorphose."

## I. The Mind Is a Continual Battlefield

The course, at its opening, brings us onto the battlefield: **In our mind, there is a struggle between the desires of the flesh and spiritual thoughts.**

Romans chapter 8, verse 6 states the outcome of this war very clearly:

> "For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace." (Rom. 8:6, NKJV)

The word "to be minded" in the original has the meaning of "to set the mind on" (original φρονέω, transliteration phroneō; Strong G5426; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to think, to set the mind on"). Where you set your mind determines your outcome: set it on the flesh, the outcome is death; set it on the Spirit, the outcome is life and peace. Note, Paul did not say "to be carnally minded is weakness"; he said it is "death" — this is a battle of life and death.

The course used a very vivid metaphor: **Our mind is like a piece of land.** Whatever seeds of thoughts, emotions, and desires are sown on this land will bear corresponding fruit. Galatians chapter 6, verse 7:

> "Do not be deceived, God is not mocked; for whatever a man sows, that he will also reap." (Gal. 6:7, NKJV)

Sow complaining, and you will reap bitterness; sow thanksgiving, and you will reap joy. No one can sow complaining in his heart every day and expect thanksgiving to grow.

Then, how do we fight on the battlefield? 2 Corinthians chapter 10, verses 4-5 gives us our weapons:

> "For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds, casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ." (2 Cor. 10:4-5, NKJV)

Note "bringing every thought into captivity" — thoughts can be taken captive, and they can also be taken back. When you discover that your thoughts have been taken captive by anxiety, taken captive by complaining, taken captive by bitterness, do not resign yourself to fate; "take them back." How to take them back? The course gave us a concrete handle.

## II. The Tongue Is the Rudder of the Ship

There is a sentence in the course that left a deep impression on me: **"The importance of the tongue is like the rudder of a ship."**

James chapter 3, verses 4-5:

> "Look also at ships: although they are so large and are driven by fierce winds, they are turned by a very small rudder wherever the pilot desires. Even so the tongue is a little member and boasts great things." (James 3:4-5, NKJV)

The direction of a large ship depends on a small rudder; the direction of a person's life depends to a great extent on the small tongue. Proverbs chapter 18, verse 21 says it even more directly:

> "Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruit." (Prov. 18:21, NKJV)

The logic of the course is this: **Your thinking will change with your words.** We usually think that first there are thoughts, then there are words; but the course reminds us that the reverse is also true — the words you speak will in turn shape your thoughts and change your emotions. The study notes record: "You must control your emotions, and let your tongue submit to the word of God, rather than being swayed by your mood."

This is why the course lists "Give thanks out loud" as one of the seven practices. Do not only think silently in your heart; open your mouth and say it. At first, you may feel nothing — your heart is clearly still suffering, but your mouth says "Thank You, Lord." But the testimony of the course is: **If you keep saying it long enough, the inside will truly change.** What you are enjoying is precisely the fruit sown by your words.

So, on the battlefield of the mind, the tongue is our weapon. When you are besieged by negative thoughts, do not be silent; open your mouth and say: "Thank You, Lord!" If saying it once is not enough, say it ten times; if ten times is not enough, say it until your heart is filled with thanksgiving. This is not self-hypnosis; this is using the weapon God has given to take every thought captive and make it obedient to Christ.

## III. In Every Circumstance, Choose Thanksgiving

Victory on the battlefield must ultimately be worked out in choice. The course repeatedly emphasizes one sentence: **God never has to act according to our plans.** When our plans fall through is precisely the moment for us to choose thanksgiving.

Philippians chapter 4, verses 6-7 gives us the formula for thanksgiving in every circumstance:

> "Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Phil. 4:6-7, NKJV)

Note this order: prayer, supplication — **and thanksgiving**. It is not to give thanks after the matter is resolved, but to give thanks at the same time as supplication. Then, the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard our hearts and minds. The word "guard" in the original is a military term, like an army guarding a city (original φρουρέω, transliteration phroureō; Strong G5432; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to guard, to protect") — God's peace will, like an army, guard your heart and not let anxiety and complaining break in.

Then, what specifically should we think on? Philippians chapter 4, verse 8 gives us a "list for the mind":

> "Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there is any virtue and if there is anything praiseworthy — meditate on these things." (Phil. 4:8, NKJV)

This is the "diet for the mind" God has given us. When your mind is occupied by negative news, by memories of being offended, by fear of the future, compare it against this list: Is this true? Is it noble? Is it lovely? If not, replace it, and replace it with the contents of the list.

The course also mentioned a very practical practice: **a thanksgiving journal**. Record every day the things worthy of thanksgiving, and train the brain to discover grace. You will discover that the frequency of thanksgiving grows higher and higher — it is not that the circumstances have changed, it is that your eyes have changed.

Finally, let us look at an example in the Bible. In Luke chapter 17, ten lepers were healed, and only **one** returned, glorifying God with a loud voice and falling down on his face at Jesus' feet, giving Him thanks:

> "And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, returned, and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at His feet, giving Him thanks. And he was a Samaritan." (Luke 17:15-16, NKJV)

All ten people were healed, but only one returned. Jesus said to him: "Your faith has made you well." (Luke 17:19, NKJV) Many people receive grace, few people return to give thanks. May we all be the one who returns.

## IV. Application: Six Groups of People

**1. Brothers and sisters who have newly believed in the Lord:** Starting today, "stand guard" over your mind. Every night before bed, check against Philippians 4:8: Are the things I meditated on today the contents of the list?

**2. Brothers and sisters troubled by anxiety:** Anxiety is a signal that your thoughts have been taken captive. Do not only reason with anxiety; open your mouth: speak aloud three things for which you thank God. The tongue is the rudder; turn it, and the ship will turn.

**3. Servants who are in the habit of complaining:** As soon as a complaining word comes out of your mouth, it sows a seed of complaining in your heart. Try replacing every complaint with thanksgiving: "This coworker is really hard to get along with" becomes "Thank You, Lord, for using him to train my patience."

**4. Parents who worry for their children and family:** Turn worry into "prayer + thanksgiving." The formula of Philippians 4:6 is: give thanks at the same time as you make supplication. God's peace will, like an army, guard your heart.

**5. Seekers who are still observing:** You can do an experiment: for seven consecutive days, write down three things of thanksgiving each day, and see what change there is in your mood. The battlefield of the mind — everyone is in it.

**6. The next generation of the church:** Young people, your minds are bombarded every day by phones and social media. Learning to choose what to meditate on is the most important lesson for your age. Let Philippians 4:8 become the filter in your heart before you scroll on your phone.

## Conclusion

The mind is a battlefield, but we are not without weapons. God has given us His word, given us prayer, and given us thanksgiving. Choose spiritual thoughts, and give thanks in every circumstance — this is not inborn; this is trained.

## Response Prayer

Dear heavenly Father, thank You for reminding us that the mind is a continual battlefield. Lord, we confess that we often set our minds on the flesh, on anxiety, on complaining, and as a result reap restlessness and bitterness. Please forgive us.

Lord, please renew our minds. Teach us to take back the thoughts that have been taken captive and make them obedient to Christ. Teach us to steer with our tongues, to speak thanksgiving aloud until our hearts are filled with thanksgiving. Teach us to give thanks at the same time as prayer and supplication, and let Your peace, which surpasses all understanding, guard our hearts and minds.

We pray in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.
