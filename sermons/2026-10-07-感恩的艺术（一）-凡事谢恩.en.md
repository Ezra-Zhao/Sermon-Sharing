---

Please think about this: **When was the last time you sincerely said "thank You" to God?**

Was it when things went smoothly and your wishes came true? Or was it when you were in a valley and the way ahead was unclear?

For most of us, thanksgiving is conditional: when we are healed of illness, we thank the Lord; when work is settled, we thank the Lord; when our children are obedient, we thank the Lord. But once the circumstances are not as we wish, the words of thanks cannot come out of our mouths, and in our hearts we even begin to complain: Lord, where are You?

But the command the Bible gives us is unconditional. 1 Thessalonians chapter 5, verse 18:

> "In everything give thanks; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you." (1 Thess. 5:18, NKJV)

"In everything," not "in things that please us"; "give thanks" in Greek is **εὐχαριστέω (eucharisteō)** (original εὐχαριστέω, transliteration eucharisteō; Strong G2168; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to give thanks"), which means "to offer thanksgiving." The English word Eucharist (the Lord's Supper) also comes from this root — every time we give thanks, it is like offering a sacrifice.

At the opening of KPM Mission's *Art of Thanksgiving* series, the speaker Daniel said: **"The art of thanksgiving is the journey of forty years since I met Jesus Christ."** Forty years, learning one thing: thanksgiving. In this chapter, we begin from the three stops of this journey.

## I. Thanksgiving Is a Command, Not a Suggestion

Please note the second half of this verse: "**For this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.**"

The word "will" in Greek is **θέλημα (thelēma)** (original θέλημα, transliteration thelēma; Strong G2307; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "will, desire"), referring to God's desire, what God has purposed to accomplish. This is not a suggestion of "do it or not, as you please," but a command. God says: I want you to give thanks in everything.

Why does God insist so much? Because thanksgiving is not God's need, but our need. There is a very piercing sentence in the course: **When we forget thanksgiving, even for just a short moment, unpleasant things will happen one after another.** It is not that God is punishing us, but that when we stop giving thanks, our hearts turn toward complaining, toward self, and that is precisely where the enemy can most easily work.

We need to clarify a common misunderstanding: **"Give thanks in everything" is not "give thanks for evil things."** God has not asked us to give thanks for disease, for disaster, for sin itself. God wants us to give thanks "in" everything — in disease, thank God that He is the Healer; in lack, thank God that He is the Provider; in darkness, thank God that He is Light. The object of thanksgiving is always God's goodness, not the smoothness of our circumstances.

The course reminds us: even if we cannot understand, or the situation looks very bad, **do not hesitate; obey God and offer thanksgiving as a sacrifice**. Hebrews chapter 13, verse 15 says:

> "Therefore by Him let us continually offer the sacrifice of praise to God, that is, the fruit of our lips, giving thanks to His name." (Heb. 13:15, NKJV)

Note the words "as a sacrifice" — a sacrifice has a cost. To give thanks when everything is smooth is instinct; to still give thanks when you cannot see a way out is a sacrifice. And God delights in such a sacrifice.

## II. Thanksgiving Is a Journey, Not a Moment

"The art of thanksgiving" — why is it called an "art"? Because an art is not mastered in one day. The speaker Daniel said this is his **journey of forty years**. Thanksgiving is not a one-time decision, but a road that must be walked every day.

The course gave us a very practical goal: **Train thanksgiving to become a reflex**. Just as after you become skilled at driving you do not need to think about how to shift gears, after thanksgiving becomes skilled, it will come out of your mouth without needing a reminder. The study notes record: Training the brain to give thanks, you will feel the frequency of thanksgiving grow higher and higher — this is not mysticism, this is the power of habit.

How specifically should we practice? The course summarized **seven practices of thanksgiving**, whose English initials are ULPHIET:

1. **U – Give thanks for the Unexpected:** God often works outside our plans. God never has to act according to our plans — give thanks for those "unexpected" benefits.
2. **L – Give thanks Out Loud:** Do not only think it in your heart; say it out loud. Thanks spoken aloud has more power.
3. **P – Pinch it out with thanks:** The English word pinch means "to squeeze, to pinch out" — even in tightness and difficulty, squeeze out thanksgiving.
4. **H – Give thanks until your Heart is fully thankful:** Do not give thanks for three seconds and stop; keep giving thanks until your heart is truly filled with thanksgiving.
5. **I – Give thanks Immediately:** Do not wait, do not delay; as soon as something happens, give thanks immediately.
6. **E – Give thanks in Every area:** Family, work, health, relationships — no exceptions.
7. **T – Give thanks, thanks and bless others:** Thanksgiving does not stop with yourself; it must flow toward others and become a blessing.

The course also mentioned five daily activities for becoming a "master": **the word of God, persevering in prayer, meeting God every week, regularly attending church fellowship, and preaching the gospel to others**. A master of thanksgiving is not born; he is trained.

You can also begin your journey today: every night before bed, write down three things for which you thank God — this is the practice of a "thanksgiving journal." It does not need to be written very profoundly; what matters is persisting every day.

## III. Thanksgiving Is a Spiritual Weapon

Finally, the course lifts thanksgiving to a height we seldom think of: **Thanksgiving is a spiritual weapon**.

The study notes record several very weighty sentences, relaying the teaching of the course as it was, for readers to discern and receive for themselves:

- **"Giving thanks immediately is the key to preventing the enemy from destroying our lives."** The longer we complain, the more time we will need to spend searching for the blessing God has hidden.
- **"The place where we do not give thanks is our weakness, a breach in the city wall."** Wherever there is no thanksgiving, that place is easily lost.
- **"If we lose thanksgiving, we expose ourselves before the enemy; if we begin to complain about others, we are indulging the enemy to work in our lives."**

These words are very stern, but the logic behind them is clear: thanksgiving turns our hearts toward God, complaining turns our hearts away from God. Whomever the heart turns toward, that one reigns in our lives.

Connected with thanksgiving is also **blessing**. The course summarized the "Three Principles of Blessing":

1. **When we sincerely bless others, before the blessing reaches that person, it will first come to ourselves.** God first pours the blessing on the one who blesses, and then it flows to the one being blessed.
2. **If the blessing you release is not received by the other person, that blessing will return to you.** The Lord Jesus says in Luke chapter 10, verse 6: "And if a son of peace is there, your peace will rest on it; if not, it will return to you." (Luke 10:6, NKJV)
3. **When you pray for your own need, first bless and pray for those who have the same need.** Proverbs chapter 11, verse 25: "The generous soul will be made rich, and he who waters will also be watered himself." (Prov. 11:25, NKJV)

So the slogan of the course is: **"Give thanks, give thanks, and bless."** Thanksgiving is toward God, blessing is toward people. A person whose heart is filled with thanksgiving will naturally become a person who blesses others.

## IV. Application: Six Groups of People

**1. Brothers and sisters who have newly believed in the Lord:** Starting today, every night before bed write down three things for which you thank God. It does not need to be profound; what matters is persisting. This is the best start to your spiritual journey.

**2. Brothers and sisters who are in difficulty:** I know you may not be able to speak thanksgiving right now. But please remember, "give thanks in everything" is not to give thanks for suffering itself, but in suffering still to thank God for His goodness. Try to say one sentence: "Lord, although I do not understand, I thank You that You are with me."

**3. Servants who feel weary:** Check: in your ministry, is there more complaining or more thanksgiving? Complaining is exhaustion, thanksgiving is refueling. Return to thanksgiving and be strengthened anew.

**4. Those interceding for family and for others:** Try the "Three Principles of Blessing" — first bless the person you are interceding for, then bring your own need before God. The person who blesses others is himself blessed first.

**5. Seekers who are still observing:** A person who knows thanksgiving has a fragrance in his life. You can begin by observing: look at those Christians who sincerely give thanks — what is different about their lives?

**6. The next generation of the church:** Young people, do not wait until "everything is good" to give thanks. Learn to give thanks amid exam pressure and interpersonal troubles, and you will possess, earlier than your peers, a peace not controlled by circumstances.

## Conclusion

Thanksgiving is a command, it is also a journey, and even more it is a weapon. God set "give thanks in everything" as His will, not to make things hard for us, but to protect us and bless us.

## Response Prayer

Dear heavenly Father, thank You for giving us this gift of "thanksgiving." Lord, we confess that we often give thanks only when things are smooth, and complain in adversity. Please forgive us.

Lord, please teach us the art of thanksgiving. Let us train thanksgiving into a habit, speak thanks aloud, squeeze out thanksgiving even in lack, and keep giving thanks until our hearts are filled with thanksgiving. Let us give thanks in every area, and become people who bless others.

We pray in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.
