"God says: In the last days I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh. Your sons and daughters shall prophesy; your young men shall see visions; your old men shall dream dreams." (Acts 2:17)

Does God still speak to people through dreams today?

Some people get excited the moment they hear this topic — "I have had so many special dreams!" Others feel uneasy — "Isn't this too charismatic? Might it lead us astray?" Still others think this was all about Old Testament prophets and has nothing to do with them.

Let us not draw conclusions by feelings. Let us go back to the Bible and see what God Himself says.

## I. This Is God's Public Promise, Not the Privilege of a Few

On the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Spirit descended, Peter stood up and quoted the prophecy of Joel:

> "'And it shall come to pass in the last days, says God, That I will pour out of My Spirit on all flesh; Your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, Your young men shall see visions, Your old men shall dream dreams.'" (Acts 2:17, NKJV)

Please note three expressions: **"on all flesh"** — not a few prophets, but all who belong to God; **"your young men shall see visions"** — the Greek word is **horama (ὅραμα)** (original Greek ὅραμα, transliterated horama; Strong G3705; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "a vision, a sight"), referring to a picture received while awake; **"your old men shall dream dreams"** — the Greek word is **enypnion (ἐνύπνιον)** (original Greek ἐνύπνιον, transliterated enypnion; Strong G1798; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "a dream, what is seen in a dream"), referring to a message received in sleep. This is a way of communication that God Himself promised to initiate in the last days, written in black and white in the Bible.

Even earlier, God spoke plainly through Moses:

> "'Hear now My words: If there is a prophet among you, I, the LORD, make Myself known to him in a vision; I speak to him in a dream.'" (Num. 12:6, NKJV)

Visions and dreams are one of the normal channels through which God speaks to man.

The book of Job shows us God's heart even more:

> "'For God may speak in one way, or in another, Yet man does not perceive it. In a dream, in a vision of the night, When deep sleep falls upon men, While slumbering on their beds, Then He opens the ears of men, And seals their instruction... He keeps back his soul from the Pit, And his life from perishing by the sword.'" (Job 33:14-18, NKJV, excerpted)

Do you see it? When God speaks through dreams, **His purpose is often protection** — to keep a man from falling into the pit. He speaks once, twice; we do not pay attention, so He speaks again while we sleep quietly. This is the heart of a Father.

So what exactly are dreams and visions? Brother Zhang Xun gives this definition in the course: **A dream or a vision is a spiritual experience in which the inner spiritual man receives or perceives information originating from the spiritual realm.**

The natural man knows the material world through his eyes, ears, and other five senses; likewise, the Christian who has been born again has an inner spiritual man who also has the capacity to perceive. Hebrews says:

> "But solid food belongs to those who are of full age, that is, those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil." (Heb. 5:14, NKJV)

"Their senses exercised" — spiritual perception can be exercised and can grow. This is not a privilege belonging only to prophets; it is a path every believer can walk.

## II. What God-Given Dreams and Visions "Are" and "Are Not"

In order not to go to extremes, we must first distinguish clearly: what a God-given dream or vision **is**, and what it **is not**.

**It is:**

First, a way for God to communicate with man directly and to guide him. In the Bible, God's word includes **logos (λόγος)** (original Greek λόγος, transliterated logos; Strong G3056; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "a word, the Word") — the written Word — and also **rhēma (ῥῆμα)** (original Greek ῥῆμα, transliterated rhēma; Strong G4487; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "that which is spoken, a word, saying") — the word the Holy Spirit speaks to you here and now. Dreams and visions are often the vehicle of a rhēma (Job 33:14-18; Num. 12:6-8).

Second, it is God's grace, to be received by faith. "For in Him we live and move and have our being" (Acts 17:28). God delights to show us His presence; we are to reach out by faith and receive it.

Third, it is a manifestation of God's presence and love. The Lord said: "He who loves Me will be loved by My Father, and I will love him and manifest Myself to him" (John 14:21). His manifesting Himself to us is an expression of love.

Fourth, it is one of the pathways through which the gifts of the Holy Spirit operate — for example, distinguishing of spirits, the word of knowledge, the word of wisdom, and prophesying often come through visions and dreams.

Fifth, it is a path that believers with a prophetic calling must walk (Num. 12:6).

**It is not** (these ten points are guardrails against extremes; remember them firmly):

First, it is not a dream made by the natural man, not a fantasy of "thinking about it by day," and certainly not an illusion.

Second, **it is not a substitute for the Bible**. The Holy Spirit has come to guide us into all truth, and He never contradicts the Bible. Any "vision" that violates the Bible does not come from God.

Third, it is not the only way the Holy Spirit guides and confirms. God also speaks through the Bible, through circumstances, and through the members of the body.

Fourth, it is not the common way by which every person is led by God. There is no ranking in the Holy Spirit's guidance; He distributes to each one as He wills.

Fifth, **it is not a measure of spiritual life**. Jesus is the standard. Having dreams and visions does not mean a high spiritual life; not having them does not mean a low spiritual life.

Sixth, it is not prepared only for those deep in spiritual life.

Seventh, it is not prepared only for those who do not know the Bible.

Eighth, it is not prepared only for times of trouble — we need to walk with God every day.

Ninth, it cannot be interpreted by the flesh. "Do not interpretations belong to God?" (Gen. 40:8). The right of interpretation is in God's hand.

Tenth, it is not for personal glory, but for God's glory.

These "five is-es and ten is-nots" are our anchor for not deviating to the left or the right on this topic. Brother Zhang Xun especially emphasizes three principles of balance: **Do not oppose dreams and visions themselves; only guard against the work of the enemy; do not limit the work of the Holy Spirit and strip believers of the promise** (even with good motives, human zeal cannot accomplish God's work); **"For we can do nothing against the truth, but for the truth"** (2 Cor. 13:8).

Nor should we affirm or deny based on special cases: we cannot say they are unnecessary because some servants greatly used by God had no dreams or visions; nor can we turn it around, exalting experience and despising the Bible.

## III. Witnesses in the Bible

The Bible is full of such people. Let us look at only three:

**Joseph** (Gen. 37, 40-41): As a youth he had dreams; he was mocked by his brothers, sold as a slave, and put in prison. But in prison he interpreted the dreams of the cupbearer and the baker, and spoke that famous line: "Do not interpretations belong to God?" (Gen. 40:8). Later he interpreted Pharaoh's dream and saved the whole land from famine. A youth's dream, in God's hand, became a vessel to save the nations.

**Daniel** (Dan. 2, 7): He interpreted the king's dream and also received visions himself. Interestingly, even with an angel explaining, there were still things he did not understand, and God said to him, "Go your way" (see Dan. 12:9) — **we do not have to fully understand every vision; obedience is more important than understanding.**

**Peter** (Acts 10:17): He saw the vision of heaven opened, and "while Peter thought about the vision" — he was perplexed about what the vision he had seen meant. He did not understand at the time, but he continued to obey the Holy Spirit's leading, step by step, to the house of Cornelius, until that whole household believed in the Lord — only then did it dawn on him. **It does not matter if you do not understand; obey and follow, and God will make you understand along the way.**

## IV. Life Application: Six Kinds of Readers

**New believers who have never heard this kind of teaching**: Do not be afraid. This is not mysticism; it is the Bible. Begin by memorizing Acts 2:17, and store God's promise in your heart.

**Those prejudiced against dreams and visions, resistant in their hearts**: Ask God to remove your preconceptions. Remember the principle of balance — do not oppose them themselves, only guard against the enemy's work; do not limit the Holy Spirit and strip yourself of the promise.

**Those who have had special dreams but dare not tell anyone**: You are not a strange person. Write it down, bring it before God and ask Him, and you may also fellowship with a mature coworker.

**Church leaders and pastors**: When someone among the flock shares this kind of experience, do not beat it down with one blow, and do not accept it wholesale either. Test it with the Bible, and shepherd with love.

**Seekers still examining the faith**: God is a true and living God, and He still speaks today. You can pray: "God, if You are real, please reveal Yourself to me." He hears.

**Young people in the church**: The Scripture says plainly, "Your young men shall see visions." This promise is for you! To seek God at your age is a blessing for a lifetime.

## Conclusion

Dreams and visions are not a novelty; they are God's public promise in the last days, the way a Father protects His children and guides His children. We do not chase experiences; we chase the God who speaks. We do not limit the Holy Spirit; we keep watch with the Bible.

## Response Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You that You are a God who speaks. Lord, we confess that sometimes we are too rational and shut Your promise outside the door; and sometimes we are too careless, chasing experiences more than chasing You. Forgive us, we pray.

Lord, You promised in the last days to pour out Your Spirit on all flesh. Today, by faith, we receive this promise. Open our ears, we pray, as Job says, seal Your instruction in our hearts, and keep us from falling into the pit.

We place ourselves in Your hands: we will not limit You, and we will not make presumptuous demands. May Your will be done. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
