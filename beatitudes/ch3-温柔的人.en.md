## I. The Scripture Text

"Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth." (Matt. 5:5, NKJV)

This beatitude is the most easily misunderstood. When many people hear "meek," what comes to mind is someone with no temper, no opinions, a soft persimmon anyone can squeeze. But the meekness Jesus speaks of does not mean this. Moses was called the most humble man on earth, yet he still dared to stand before Pharaoh and speak; Jesus said that He is gentle and lowly in heart, yet He still overturned the tables of the money changers in the temple. Meekness is not the absence of strength; it is strength that has a Master.

## II. Exegesis of the Original Text

The Greek original: Μακάριοι οἱ πραεῖς, ὅτι αὐτοὶ κληρονομήσουσιν τὴν γῆν.

**1. "Meek" (οἱ πραεῖς, the nominative plural of praus; πραΰς, Strong G4235)**

The word πραΰς (praus) was also used in the Greek world to describe a tamed animal. Is a wild horse strong? Yes — but that strength only kicks, injures people, and wrecks things; once tamed, its strength is not one bit less, yet it heeds the reins, can pull a cart, and can travel a long road. Meekness is like this: it is not having the strength taken out of you; it is handing the strength over to be governed. Toward God, not arguing back; toward people, not losing control; when it is time to speak, able to speak; when it is not time to strike out, able to hold back.

The Lü Zhenzhong Translation renders it: "Blessed are the meek! For they shall inherit the land." Translating γῆ as "land" stays closer to the promised land of the Psalms' original meaning, and also reminds us: Jesus is not casually saying a word of good fortune here; He is quoting an ancient promise (see the cross-references below).

This word does not appear many times in the New Testament, but its weight is heavy. Moses is described in the Septuagint as "very meek" (Num. 12:3, Greek πραΰς σφόδρα); Jesus uses it of Himself personally: "I am gentle and lowly in heart" (Matt. 11:29); when He rode a donkey into Jerusalem, fulfilling the prophet's word, that King is said to be "meek" (Matt. 21:5, quoting Isa. 62:11 and Zech. 9:9). So meekness is not the self-consolation of a small person; it is the character of the King.

**2. "They shall inherit" (κληρονομήσουσιν, the future of klēronomeō; the verb κληρονομέω, Strong G2816; "earth" γῆ, Strong G1093)**

To inherit is to receive an inheritance. This word speaks not of seizing, not of snatching, but of a son receiving it from his father's hand. What is snatched is spoils of war — it cannot be kept, and one cannot sleep in peace over it; what is inherited is an estate — rightful and proper, and it can be passed on to one's children. Jesus does not say "they shall seize the earth," nor "they shall buy the earth"; He deliberately uses "inherit" — the final ownership of the earth is not in the hands of the one with the biggest fist, but in the hands of the heavenly Father. To whomever He gives it, that person truly possesses it.

The verb is in the future tense: they shall inherit. This is a promise that has not yet been fully cashed in, but has already been signed. A meek person may now have nothing; his name is not on any land deed — but it is in the heavenly Father's ledger.

English versions for comparison:
- KJV: "Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth."
- NIV: "Blessed are the meek, for they will inherit the earth."
- NRSV: "Blessed are the meek, for they will inherit the earth."

All three versions translate praeis as meek. The English word meek is also commonly misread today as weakness; when reading it, remember the meaning of the original: it is strength under control, not the absence of strength.

## III. The Place and Logic of This Beatitude in the Sermon on the Mount

The first three beatitudes are a road that goes down and then comes back up:

- The first beatitude (5:3), poor in spirit: admitting bankruptcy in spirit before God;
- The second beatitude (5:4), mourning: grieving in the heart over one's own sin and the brokenness of the world;
- The third beatitude (5:5), meekness: no longer avenging oneself, handing one's case over into God's hand.

Once a person has been bankrupt before God and has wept over sin, he can no longer be hard toward people. He knows he is a person who has received mercy, so he has no right to trample on others; he knows his case has been personally taken up by God, so he does not need to take revenge with his own hands. Meekness is the first character toward people produced by the first two beatitudes: the earlier beatitudes speak of man's attitude toward God; by this beatitude, it begins to be shown in how he treats those who offend him.

When the psalmist wrote Psalm 37, his circumstances were precisely that the wicked were making fortunes and the righteous were being oppressed. David did not teach the righteous to fight desperately; what he said again and again was: do not fret, do not do evil, wait on the LORD, and inherit the land (the gist of Ps. 37:1–11). Jesus moved this sentence, "the meek shall inherit the earth," just as it is into the Beatitudes, which is equivalent to condensing the whole lesson of Psalm 37 into one sentence: you simply entrust yourself; vengeance and distribution are God's business.

Immediately following is the fourth beatitude, hungering and thirsting for righteousness (5:6). The order is also interesting: only when the hands are loosened is there room to grasp God's righteousness; a person whose fists are clenched in demanding justice for himself has no room in his heart for longing after God's righteousness.

## IV. Cross-References

- Ps. 37:11 (numbering of the Hebrew Bible; numbering of the Septuagint is Ps. 36:11, i.e., LXX 36:11) — "But the meek shall inherit the earth, and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace." The direct source of the third beatitude; the wording of the Septuagint (LXX 36:11) is almost word-for-word identical to Matt. 5:5.
- Num. 12:3 — "Now the man Moses was very humble, more than all men who were on the face of the earth." The background of these words is precisely that Moses was being reviled to his face; he did not defend himself, and God personally stood up for him (Num. 12). That the meek do not plead their own case does not mean no one pleads it for them.
- Matt. 11:29 — "Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart." Meekness is not learning a set of speaking techniques; it is learning from a Person.
- Matt. 21:5 — "Behold, your King is coming to you, lowly, and sitting on a donkey…" The victorious King does not ride a war horse; He rides a donkey's colt. This is the way the kingdom of heaven wins.
- Ps. 25:9 — "The humble He guides in justice, and the humble He teaches His way." The meek do not only inherit the earth; they first inherit God's teaching — being able to listen to words is the first mark of meekness.
- James 1:21 — We are to "receive with meekness the implanted word." When the seed of the word is sown, only soft soil can receive it; for a person with a hard heart, no matter how right the words are, they cannot get in.
- Gal. 5:22–23 — Among the fruit of the Spirit there is also "gentleness" [meekness]. Its being listed in the fruit list shows that meekness is not an inborn temperament; it is worked out in a person by the Holy Spirit. A person cannot put it on by himself — over time, the disguise would show through.

## V. Life Application

**Scene One: When your credit is stolen, do not overturn the table first.** The most galling thing in the workplace is when the work was done by you, the credit is taken by someone else, and no one mentions your name in the report. The courage of the flesh tells you to confront them on the spot, to post a long message in the group chat, and you have even drafted your resignation letter. Meekness does not tell you to swallow the facts — what should be clarified can be clarified calmly: just lay out the process, the times, and the records. What is unnecessary is revenge: there is no need to sabotage behind the scenes, no need to quit in a fit of pique, and even less need to lose, through losing control, the reason that was originally on your side. When Moses was reviled, he did not hold a press conference; God Himself spoke up. Hand the case over, let the heart quiet down first, and the matter will actually be seen more clearly.

**Scene Two: At home, volume is not authority.** Many fathers take the anger they received at work and raise their voices at home; when a child does not listen, the first reaction is to shout. Shouting can subdue for a moment, but it cannot subdue the heart. A meek person is different at home: the words still have to be said, the rules still have to be set, but the voice comes down, and he first lets the child finish speaking; when he himself is wrong, he admits it. A father who can humble himself at home carries more weight than a father who only loses his temper — what a child remembers when he grows up is often not what you shouted, but whether you ever listened to him finish a sentence.

**Scene Three: For a matter waited on for a long time, hand over the timetable.** Some things, no matter how hard a person works, can only be waited for: a result that has had no word for a long time, an application sent out with no reply, an arrangement with no knowing when reunion will be possible. Waiting is when it is easiest to fret; seeing others go smoothly makes it even harder to bear. The lesson of Psalm 37 is for days like these: do not fret, lest it lead to evil; commit your matter to the LORD and trust in Him (the gist of Ps. 37:5). The meek are not without hope; they hand the time of hope's fulfillment over to God, and simply do well the duty that belongs to today. The earth is received by inheritance, not produced by anxiety.

## VI. Response Prayer

Lord, I confess that the strength within me is often ungoverned. When I am wronged, I want to get it back immediately; when I am misunderstood, I want to explain myself at once; at home my voice is louder than my reason. Yet You say that the meek shall inherit the earth.

Tame this wild horse of mine, I pray. Do not take the strength away, but let the reins be in Your hands. When it is time for me to speak, give me the courage to state the facts calmly; when it is not time for me to reach out my hand, let me be able to hold my hand back — learning from Moses to wait for You to stand up for me, and learning from the Lord Jesus to be gentle and lowly in heart.

I entrust to You those matters that have had no word for a long time, and that heart of crying out for justice for myself. The earth is in Your hands, and time is also in Your hands; I will simply listen to Your words today and walk Your road today. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I pray, Amen.
