"The spirit of a man is the lamp of the LORD, Searching all the inner depths of his heart." (Prov. 20:27)

Have you ever had this experience — suddenly a thought, a picture, arises in your heart, and later you discover that it was actually God speaking to you?

Many people have, but dare not be sure: "Could that just be my overthinking?" The Bible's answer is: there is a lamp inside you. "Lamp" in Hebrew is **nēr (נֵר)** (original Hebrew נֵר, transliterated ner; Strong H5216; according to *BDB Lexicon*, "lamp"), simply a lit lamp. The spirit of a man is the lamp of the LORD — God, through the spirit within us, enlightens us and guides us. In this lecture, let us consider this lamp, and how to exercise spiritual perception and distinguish between spirits.

## I. The Lamp of the LORD

Why is the spirit of a man called "the lamp of the LORD"? There are three layers of meaning:

**First, a lamp represents light and guidance.** "God is Spirit, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth" (John 4:24). God is Spirit; He does not manifest Himself to us through the eyes of the flesh, but guides us through the spiritual man within us. Once the lamp is lit, the road can be seen.

Paul said:

> "'Eye has not seen, nor ear heard, Nor have entered into the heart of man The things which God has prepared for those who love Him.' But God has revealed them to us through His Spirit." (1 Cor. 2:9-10, NKJV)

What the eye has not seen, the ear has not heard, and the heart of man has not conceived — what God has prepared for those who love Him exceeds the natural senses. Only the Holy Spirit can reveal it to us, and the "receiver" of the Holy Spirit's work is the spirit within us — this very lamp.

**Second, a lamp needs to be lit and maintained.** The Lord Jesus said:

> "'The lamp of the body is the eye. If therefore your eye is good, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eye is bad, your whole body will be full of darkness.'" (Matt. 6:22-23, NKJV)

A lamp can grow dim. Without God's continual illumination and guidance, the lamp in our spirit will grow dim, and the whole body will be full of darkness. Brother Zhang Xun says in the course: **The transformation of the whole earth must begin with the individual being enlightened by God.** Revival does not begin first with the big environment; it begins first with the lamp within you and me being lit.

**Third, every person has this lamp.** It is not a prophet's privilege and not a pastor's privilege. As long as you are a person born again and saved, you have this lamp within you. The question is not "whether you have it," but "whether it is bright" and "whether you use it."

## II. Spiritual Perception Can Be Exercised

The natural man knows the material world through his five senses; likewise, knowing the spiritual world depends on the perception of the inner spiritual man. And the key verse is:

> "But solid food belongs to those who are of full age, that is, those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil." (Heb. 5:14, NKJV)

"Exercised by reason of use" — spiritual perception can be exercised and can mature. In the course, Brother Zhang Xun systematically organizes **twelve kinds of spiritual perception**; the essentials are as follows:

**1. Spiritual hearing** (Rev. 2:7): "He who has an ear, let him hear what the Spirit says to the churches!" We must hear, and we must also discern the source of the voice — because voices from the second heaven and from the world will also come to interfere.

**2. Spiritual sight** (Eph. 1:18; 2 Cor. 4:18): "The eyes of your understanding being enlightened." The practice of dreams and visions puts first weight on this. Once the eyes of the heart are opened, spiritual pictures come in.

**3. Spiritual touch** (Mark 5:30): Jesus "perceived in Himself that power had gone out of Him." In the spirit, we can "feel" the anointing, faith, the presence of angels, and even the disturbance of evil spirits. Those who minister in spiritual power need to perceive when the anointing comes down and when it stops.

**4. Spiritual smell** (2 Cor. 2:14-16): "[God] through us diffuses the fragrance of His knowledge in every place." Believers have testified to smelling "the fragrance of roses" in worship — that is the Lord's visitation; others have smelled "the odor of sulfur" — that is a sign of an evil spirit's presence.

**5. Spiritual taste** (Ps. 34:8; Rev. 10:8-11): "Oh, taste and see that the LORD is good." Spiritual taste helps us judge spiritual information: hungering and thirsting for God's goodness, and being sensitive to sin.

**6. The sense of spiritual speech** (Col. 4:6): Not only hearing the words, but also grasping "what is being said." This helps us receive the "words" of the Holy Spirit or of angels.

**7. The sense of spiritual thought** (1 Cor. 2:16; 2 Cor. 10:5): God often "places" His thoughts in our hearts; but the enemy will also stuff in bad thoughts. Therefore we must "keep your heart with all diligence" (Prov. 4:23).

**8. Spiritual self-awareness** (Gal. 2:20): "It is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me." Practicing the presence of God is in fact practicing spiritual self-awareness — being conscious at all times that Christ is in me.

**9. Spiritual love** (Rom. 5:5; 1 Cor. 13): "The love of God has been poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who was given to us." Being able to feel love is God's gift; exercising it makes the heart that loves God and loves people grow, and also helps in spiritual discernment.

The remaining three kinds of perception are left in the course for deeper study, but the principle is the same: **Pay attention in God's presence, take notes, and ask the Holy Spirit to teach us to discern.**

Here one thing must be distinguished clearly: **A gift is different from exercise.** A prophetic gift (such as the word of knowledge, the word of wisdom, distinguishing of spirits) is often the Holy Spirit's special anointing of one aspect of spiritual perception — that is special grace; whereas general growth through exercise is something every believer can take part in. Do not refuse to exercise just because you do not have a "special gift"; and do not, because you exercise, think of yourself as having the gift.

In dreams and visions, we will experience the operation of all kinds of spiritual perception — this is precisely the Holy Spirit exercising us and expanding the stature of our spiritual man.

## III. Discernment: Always Test the Spirits

The more sensitive the perception, the more discernment is needed, because not every "feeling" comes from God. John said:

> "Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world." (1 John 4:1, NKJV)

What is the standard of testing? John goes on to say: Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God, and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not of God (1 John 4:2-3). **Christology is the touchstone** — only what exalts Jesus, the incarnate Word, comes from God.

Brother Zhang Xun especially reminds us in the course of the attitude of discernment:

First, **discernment comes from love and protection, not from fear or condemnation**. We discern in order to protect the flock, not to condemn.

Second, when you encounter a receiving that you do not understand or that makes you uneasy, **bring it under the covering of the church** and fellowship with mature coworkers. Do not draw conclusions alone, and still less rush to spread an unconfirmed receiving.

Third, a mere thought alone does not count as a vision; a receiving without confirmation is not to be preached as God's word.

God gives us a lamp, and He also gives us discernment. The brighter the lamp, the more careful we must be; the more sensitive the perception, the more humble we must be. This is the protection that "discernment" gives us.

## IV. Life Application: Six Kinds of Readers

**Those very sensitive to "feelings" but unsure how to discern**: Your sensitivity is a gift, but it needs the framework of the Bible. Begin with 1 John 4:1 and learn to test.

**Those afraid of being deceived, who therefore dare not practice at all**: Fear does not come from God. God gives you a lamp, and He also gives you discernment. Practicing under the covering of the church is safe.

**Those who have received a vision but do not know how to verify it**: Write it down, ask for a verse of Scripture as confirmation, and fellowship with a mature coworker. Do not draw conclusions alone, and do not rush to spread it.

**Church leaders and pastors**: You are the covering of the flock. When someone comes to you with a receiving, receive them with love and discern with the truth. Your attitude determines whether they dare to share honestly.

**Those who often intercede for others**: The thoughts and pictures received in intercession are to be recorded, inquired about, and confirmed. Your discernment grows through exercise after exercise.

**New brothers and sisters**: Begin with this promise of "the lamp of the LORD" — know that you have a lamp within you and that God delights to enlighten you. Practice can begin with five quiet minutes before sleep each day.

## Conclusion

God has placed a lamp within you. This lamp can be lit, can be exercised, and can grow brighter and brighter; at the same time, God has also given us the truth of discernment, so that we do not lose our way in sensitivity and do not deviate in fervor.

## Response Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You; You said that the spirit of a man is the lamp of the LORD. Lord, we confess that we often let this lamp grow dim — covered by busyness, pressed down by fear. Forgive us, we pray.

Lord, light the lamp within us, we pray. Open the eyes of our hearts, let us hear Your still small voice, and let us distinguish between spirits. Keep us from being deceived, and keep us from shrinking back in fear.

We are willing to practice under Your covering, to discern in love, and to grow in the truth. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
