# Nothing New Under the Sun: Extramarital Affairs

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：婚外情».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of extramarital affairs

**Summary:** An extramarital affair does not happen overnight; it begins with the transfer of affections and grows from rationalized excuses. This chapter first speaks of the root cause: thinking that one has the right to demand that a spouse meet all of one's needs is a wrong understanding; then it speaks to two sides: to the wavering party, turn back immediately and close the door decisively; to the faithful party, do not avenge yourself, do not blame yourself, pray for the Holy Spirit's leading, and learn to remember without bitterness — forgiveness does not equal forgetting.

The key Scripture is 1 Corinthians 6:18:

> "Flee sexual immorality. Every sin that a man does is outside the body, but he who commits sexual immorality sins against his own body." (1 Cor. 6:18)

The word "flee" in Greek is **pheugō (φεύγω)** (original text φεύγω, transliterated pheugō; Strong G5343; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to flee, to escape"). What Paul used is not "resist," but "flee." Some temptations cannot be wrestled with standing in place; one can only turn and run. This chapter on extramarital affairs, from beginning to end, speaks of this very thing.

## I. Root Cause: A Rationalized Wrong Understanding

The material locates the root of falling into an extramarital affair in understanding: "I have the right to satisfy my own basic needs for love, self-worth, and security; previous commitments cannot bind me, because my spouse did not meet all my needs."

Listen to this logic: The needs are real, the right is fabricated; the commitment still stands, yet is lightly dissolved by one sentence, "He did not satisfy me." The material points out the process within: rationalizing one's own wrong behavior, then indulging selfish lusts, so that the heart becomes hardened and cannot obey God's will. Proverbs says: "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the hearts." (Prov. 21:2) The deceptiveness of an extramarital affair lies precisely here: it gives a person a false satisfaction. "There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way of death." (Prov. 14:12) The cost was also written long ago: "Whoever commits adultery with a woman lacks understanding; he who does so destroys his own soul." (Prov. 6:32) Ephesians is even more direct: "But fornication and all uncleanness or covetousness, let it not even be named among you, as is fitting for saints." (Eph. 5:3)

The right understanding is: Building a true relationship with Jesus can satisfy the deepest needs of my heart. When I entrust myself to Christ and obey His will, He will give me right desires, and the ability to keep commitments. The Psalms say: "Delight yourself also in the LORD, and He shall give you the desires of your heart. Commit your way to the LORD, trust also in Him, and He shall bring it to pass." (Ps. 37:4-5) Proverbs chapter 5 portrays the fountain within marriage very vividly: "Drink water from your own cistern, and running water from your own well. Should your fountains be dispersed abroad, streams of water in the streets? Let them be only your own, and not for strangers with you. Let your fountain be blessed, and rejoice with the wife of your youth." (Prov. 5:15-18)

## II. When Affections Begin to Transfer: Turn Back Immediately

The material records a question: I have developed feelings for another man; what should I do to ensure my faithfulness to my marriage?

The answer is: When you notice your affections transferring from your husband to someone else, turn back immediately. The material uses a metaphor: We all know that the guardrails beside a mountain road, though they limit us, guarantee that we travel safely on the road and prevent us from driving off the road and falling down the cliff. In the same way, God has set moral boundaries for us because He loves us, to protect us from harm. Guarding your affections can prevent you from plunging headlong into the whirlpool of an extramarital affair. The Scripture is 2 Corinthians 5:14-15: "For the love of Christ compels us, because we judge thus: that if One died for all, then all died; and He died for all, that those who live should live no longer for themselves, but for Him who died for them and rose again." The management of affections does not rely on pressing them down by willpower; it relies on being compelled by the love of Christ, and changing to another way of living.

## III. One Who Has Already Fallen In: Do Not Blame Others, Come Out Immediately

Another question comes from a married person: A woman I dated several years ago came looking for me again, and we fell into an extramarital affair, but now I hate such deception. Why does this woman want to destroy my marriage?

The material's answer is unsparing: Some people often wash away their own guilt by blaming others. You yourself should know how to distinguish right from wrong. The more important question is: You clearly saw the warning sign outside the lion's cage that said, "Warning: Lions eat people," why did you still walk into the cage? You completely ignored truths you already understood, walked back into this relationship, and as a result were devoured by your own desires. It is not another person who destroyed your marriage; it is you yourself. We turned a blind eye to the warning sign, so we taste the bitter fruit we planted with our own hands. The conclusion is only one sentence: Come out immediately, close the door decisively, and do not look back. Proverbs says: "Do not lust after her beauty in your heart, nor let her allure you with her eyelids. For by means of a harlot a man is reduced to a crust of bread; and an adulteress will prey upon his precious life." (Prov. 6:25-26)

What the unfaithful party is to memorize is the key Scripture of this chapter, 1 Cor. 6:18; what he is to read repeatedly is Colossians 3:5-6: "Therefore put to death your members which are on the earth: fornication, uncleanness, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry. Because of these things the wrath of God is coming upon the sons of disobedience." An extramarital affair produces deep self-blame in the depths of many unfaithful persons' souls; Psalm 51 is their prayer: "For I acknowledge my transgressions, and my sin is always before me. Against You, You only, have I sinned, and done this evil in Your sight." (Ps. 51:3-4) If self-blame leads toward confession, it is a way out; if self-blame only circles in shame, it is still a dead end.

## IV. The Faithful Party: Six Steps Out of the Hurt

When a spouse has an affair, the one hurt most deeply is often the faithful party. The material lists six steps for the faithful party, and each step first corrects a wrong understanding.

**Step one, speak out your feelings, do not attack.** The wrong understanding is: "I should not suspect him, otherwise I might actually cause him to have thoughts of an affair." The right understanding is: Raising this topic both enables you to speak your own thoughts and will produce a deterrent effect for the future, and it will be used by the Holy Spirit to exhort the other party to repent; but do not attack and condemn the other party. The method is to use "I" to express your own feelings — "I feel you are hiding something from me," "I feel you no longer love me, and this hurts me very much." The Lord Jesus said: "Moreover if your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother." (Matt. 18:15)

**Step two, express anger in an appropriate rather than destructive way.** The wrong understanding is: "Anger is always wrong." The right understanding is: Anger is a natural response to hurt, fear, injustice, or rejection. The problem is not whether there is anger, but how it is expressed. Paul says: "Be angry, and do not sin: do not let the sun go down on your wrath." (Eph. 4:26)

**Step three, do not blame yourself because your spouse had an affair.** This is the most common mistake of the faithful party: What did I do wrong? The material says this is the most common view of unfaithful behavior, but it is wrong. The right understanding is: A spouse should not be responsible for the other party's irresponsible behavior; no one can make another person sin. "So then each of us shall give account of himself to God." (Rom. 14:12) Each person's sin, each person gives account to God.

**Step four, pray for the Holy Spirit's leading, then decide whether to stay or leave.** The wrong understanding is: "Even if my spouse keeps having affairs, I must maintain the marriage." The right understanding is: The Bible provides a basis for divorce — if the other party has behaved unfaithfully toward the marriage, one may divorce; in such a situation, Jesus does not command people to divorce, but He permits divorce. The Lord Jesus said: "Moses, because of the hardness of your hearts, permitted you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so. And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another, commits adultery." (Matt. 19:8-9) As for whether you personally should give up this marriage, pray and seek the Holy Spirit's leading, let Him direct you, rather than deciding from momentary anger or fear.

**Step five, rely on God to become a complete person.** The wrong understanding is: "In order to become a complete person, I must have a spouse." The right understanding is: Each person is complete, not a half or a part. In Christ, you can become a complete person. Rely on God, let Him be your Savior, the Lord who makes you complete, your Healer. Jeremiah says: "Heal me, O LORD, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved, for You are my praise." (Jer. 17:14) The Scripture the faithful party is to memorize is Isaiah 54:5: "For your Maker is your husband, the LORD of hosts is His name; and your Redeemer is the Holy One of Israel; He is called the God of the whole earth." The one to read repeatedly is Romans 12:19: "Beloved, do not avenge yourselves, but rather give place to wrath; for it is written, 'Vengeance is Mine, I will repay,' says the Lord." Entrust the matter of vengeance to the Lord, and your own heart will have room to be healed.

**Step six, choose forgiveness: remembering without bitterness.** The wrong understanding is: "In order to forgive, you must forget." The right understanding is: Forgiveness does not equal forgetting; the key is how to remember. Forgiveness is remembering without bitterness and hatred. The method is: Choose to forgive the other party from thought to action. Paul says: "Bearing with one another, and forgiving one another, if anyone has a complaint against another; even as Christ forgave you, so you also must do." (Col. 3:13)

In addition there are two questions often asked. One is: My husband had an affair and left me; how should I pray for him? The material's answer is: Your husband needs to recognize his own sin, so that he can realize he needs to leave the sinful way of life. Ask God to withdraw blessing until he repents; pray that he may experience helpless pain, and because of his sin have a heavy heart and deep guilt, to compel him to leave that woman's embrace and return anew to God's embrace; pray that God gives him sorrow, helps his conscience awaken, and come before God to confess and repent. For "godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; but the sorrow of the world produces death" (2 Cor. 7:10). The second is: My former husband had many affairs, and now hopes to remarry; should I agree? The answer is: If in the past you could not trust him, on what basis do you trust him now? Christians cannot deceive themselves; know that the past often foretells the future. God can change anyone who is willing to let Him take full control; God is the God who reconciles people, but reconciliation needs the participation of both parties. First Peter also reminds husbands to dwell with their wives with understanding and give honor to her, "that your prayers may not be hindered" (1 Pet. 3:7).

Finally, concerning setting boundaries. A wife whose husband had a sexual relationship with another woman and who hopes the marriage can continue may state the boundaries clearly: I love you, and I am faithful to you; but now you must make a decision — your relationship with this woman has already crossed the boundary. If you still want our family, you must leave her completely; if you choose her instead of our family, then you must leave here. This is not kicking him out of the home; it is letting him choose whether to stay or leave. Let him realize that his practice of having both in the sexual relationship — wanting the fish and the bear's paw at once — is an extreme disrespect to his wife, and will also shame the sacred marriage covenant. Whatever he decides, even if he does not respect you, you should respect him. Galatians says: "Do not be deceived, God is not mocked; for whatever a man sows, that he will also reap. For he who sows to his flesh will of the flesh reap corruption, but he who sows to the Spirit will of the Spirit reap everlasting life." (Gal. 6:7-8)

## Conclusion

God looks at divorce and at affairs very heavily: "'For the LORD God of Israel says that He hates divorce, for it covers one's garment with violence,' says the LORD of hosts. 'Therefore take heed to your spirit, that you do not deal treacherously.'" (Mal. 2:16) Take heed to your spirit — the defense line against an extramarital affair is not at the bedroom door, but in the heart. When the heart is guarded, within the guardrails is peace.

## Prayer of Response

Lord, please guard my heart. When my affections waver, let me remember Your boundaries are guardrails, protection, not bondage; for those already hurt, please heal them personally, let me entrust vengeance to You, learn to remember without bitterness, and move forward without bitterness. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I pray, Amen.
