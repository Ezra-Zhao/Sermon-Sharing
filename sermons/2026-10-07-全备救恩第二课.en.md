## Content

### II. Toward Oneself

1. Going one's own way (Isa. 53:6) (Rom. 3:12) (Deut. 11:16) (Isa. 56:11)
2. Groping in darkness (John 12:35) (Matt. 6:22–23)
3. Full of sin (Mark 7:21–22) (Eccl. 9:3) (Rom. 1:29–32)
4. No good in him (Rom. 7:18) (Hos. 4:1–3)
5. Without hope (Eph. 2:12) (Job 7:6) (Job 8:13) (Job 27:8)

Fallen man, having lost his normal relationship with God, has been alienated from the life of God (Eph. 4:18). He is like a blind man groping in the darkness (Job 12:25) (John 11:9–10), with no hope of ever seeing the light; and like a river cut off from its source, whose living water has turned into polluted water.

### III. Toward the World

1. Greedy for the world, loving the world (2 Tim. 4:10) (1 John 2:15) (Heb. 12:16)
2. Of the world, speaking of the world (1 John 4:5) (John 15:19) (Luke 16:8)
3. Following the world, conforming to the world (Eph. 2:2) (Mark 8:36–37)

Since man has forgotten God, man sees only the world. For this reason, man fixes his attention on the world and greedily loves the world; he forgets that he is a stranger and a sojourner. He takes the world as his hometown and the grave as his destination. Although in history some people have grown weary of the world and indignant at it, this is mostly because of their disappointment with the world. On the contrary, their negativity and resentment only reflect that they love this world all the more. (Eccl. 5:10) (Man is proud because of ignorance, and corrupted because of pride.)

### Homework Questions

1. From which two causes does man's corruption come?
2. Because of Satan's work, how do we stand toward God?
3. On the road of life, what is our path like?
4. What was our inward condition originally like?
5. When we were sinners, how did we stand toward the present world?
6. At that time, what hope did we have?
7. At that time, what did we take as our destination? What did we take as our hometown?
