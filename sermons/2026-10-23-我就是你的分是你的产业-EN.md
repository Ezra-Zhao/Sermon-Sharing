# I Am Thy Part and Thine Inheritance — A Sermon on Numbers 18

Author: Guangyi Zhao, composed from the biblical text and the commentaries
Status: Draft — awaiting cross-review (Sermon Production Batch 3, 23 October 2026, unpublished)
Text: Numbers chapter 18 (32 verses in full, in the numbering of the Chinese and English Bibles; the Hebrew canon was checked book by book and chapter by chapter and agrees with this chapter and these verses — see Base-text notes and Endnote 1)
Base texts: English quotations are embedded verbatim from the King James Version (KJV); the companion Chinese manuscript quotes the Chinese Union Version, New Punctuation Edition (verse by verse). The English text is not a back-translation of the Chinese: every quotation below is KJV wording, and where the two base texts differ in wording, the difference is registered, not harmonized.
Title verification: The title is taken from the LORD's own words to Aaron in verse 20, read as they stand in the base text: "Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel." (18:20). The whole chapter hangs on this verse: first the boundary of the offices (18:1-7), then the priests' portion listed item by item (18:8-19), and at verse 20 the ground of all provision is shifted from things to a Person — to have no part and no inheritance is not the end of the sentence; "I am" is. After it come the Levites' inheritance of the tenth (18:21-24) and their heave offering of a tenth part of the tithe (18:26); the whole chain of provision hangs on this one verse. Candidates examined verse by verse and set aside: "it is a covenant of salt for ever" (18:19) is only the closing characterisation of the priests' list and does not reach the Levites' paragraphs; "they are given as a gift" (18:6) covers only the giving of the Levites; "even a tenth part of the tithe" (18:26) is one procedural clause of the last paragraph; "lest ye die" (18:32) is only the final condition. The Chinese title is taken from the same verse in its own base text; at this verse the two base texts correspond closely (part/inheritance; the double negative before it), and each manuscript follows its own base text. Full record in Endnote 2.

KJV 18:2 reads "the tabernacle of witness," so also the Chinese ("the tabernacle of the testimony/ark"). KJV 18:9 lists "every meat offering... every sin offering... every trespass offering" and "reserved from the fire," in the same order as the Chinese. ## Summary

Chapter 17 closed with the people's cry of fear: "shall we be consumed with dying?" Chapter 18 is God's answer, and the answer comes not as a word of comfort but as the re-establishing of the offices. The LORD speaks to Aaron directly — four utterances in the chapter, three to Aaron and one directing Moses to address the Levites — and first draws the boundary: Aaron's house bears the iniquity of the sanctuary and of the priesthood; the Levites are joined to him to keep the charge of the tabernacle, yet must not come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar; a stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death, that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel (18:1-7). The boundary drawn, provision follows: the LORD lists the priests' portion item by item — the heave offerings, what is reserved from the fire of the most holy things, the wave offerings, the firstfruits, devoted things, and the firstborn — stating for each who may eat it and under what conditions (18:8-19), and seals the list as "a covenant of salt for ever" (18:19). Then the centre of the chapter: "Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel." (18:20) The Levites likewise have no land; their inheritance is the tenth, given for their service of the tabernacle (18:21-24); and those who receive provision must themselves offer: the Levites shall offer up a tenth part of the tithe as an heave offering unto the LORD, to be given to Aaron the priest (18:25-29). The chapter closes on a chain of conditions: heave the best of it, and ye shall bear no sin, and may eat in every place; pollute the holy things, and ye die (18:30-32). Chapter 17 asked how a people who die when they come near can live; chapter 18 answers: some bear, some keep charge, some are provided for, and some offer up — the office itself is God's way of life for the people.

## Main Text

### 1. Bearing and Keeping Charge: The Boundary of the Offices Drawn Again (Numbers 18:1-7) (00:00-08:00)

(Fact) The addressee changes at the head of the chapter. In chapter 17 the LORD spoke to Moses; here the chapter opens, as it stands in the base text: "And the LORD said unto Aaron," (18:1). God addresses Aaron directly, without Moses between; the arrangement is itself part of the answer to chapter 17's cry of fear — the people dreaded coming near and dying, and God first names the men who bear the iniquity. The two clauses of verse 1 stand side by side, quoted in full: "Thou and thy sons and thy father's house with thee shall bear the iniquity of the sanctuary: and thou and thy sons with thee shall bear the iniquity of your priesthood." (18:1) The bearing is in two circles: the iniquity of the sanctuary is borne by Aaron, his sons, and his father's house together; the iniquity of the priesthood by Aaron and his sons together — one circle wider, one narrower, and the weight rests on those nearest the sanctuary. In this chapter the priesthood's first word is not privilege but bearing: the office enters the chapter as the bearing of iniquity before any portion is named, and that order is registered exactly as it stands, because every portion that follows is lighter than this sentence that precedes it.

(Fact) Verses 2-4 set the Levites in their place, quoted verse by verse. Verse 2: "And thy brethren also of the tribe of Levi, the tribe of thy father, bring thou with thee, that they may be joined unto thee, and minister unto thee: but thou and thy sons with thee shall minister before the tabernacle of witness." (18:2) The Levites' identity is stated first: Aaron's brethren, of his father's tribe; their office is to be joined unto him and to minister unto him; ministering before the tabernacle belongs to Aaron and his sons. Nearness is in two grades within the one verse, set side by side and not merged. Verse 3 sets the Levites' limit: "And they shall keep thy charge, and the charge of all the tabernacle: only they shall not come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar, that neither they, nor ye also, die." (18:3) Two things they keep — Aaron's charge and the charge of all the tabernacle; two things they must not come nigh — the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar; and the consequence of crossing falls not on the crosser alone: "neither they, nor ye also" — if the Levites cross, the priests die with them; a charge badly kept is never one man's affair. Verse 4 draws the stranger's line: "And they shall be joined unto thee, and keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, for all the service of the tabernacle: and a stranger shall not come nigh unto you." (18:4) The three circles are now complete: priests before the tabernacle, Levites keeping the whole tabernacle, the stranger outside the circle.

(Fact) Verse 5 states the purpose of all this keeping, quoted in full: "And ye shall keep the charge of the sanctuary, and the charge of the altar: that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel." (18:5) The words "any more" are read as they stand: wrath has fallen already — the plague of chapter 16 and the "shall die" on the people's lips in chapter 17 are the background of that "any more." Keeping the charge of sanctuary and altar is, in this chapter, not ceremonial display but a work of defence: men stand between the sanctuary and the people, so that wrath need not fall on the people again. Verse 6 gives the Levites' origin from God's own mouth, quoted in full: "And I, behold, I have taken your brethren the Levites from among the children of Israel: to you they are given as a gift for the LORD, to do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation." (18:6) The items in order: the taker is "I" — God himself has taken them; their belonging is "for the LORD"; the manner of their giving is "as a gift" to Aaron's house; their use is the service of the tabernacle. The Levites are not helpers Aaron's house has hired; they are men God took out of the whole people, gave first to himself, and then gave to the priests. Verse 7 closes the section, quoted in full: "Therefore thou and thy sons with thee shall keep your priest's office for every thing of the altar, and within the vail; and ye shall serve: I have given your priest's office unto you as a service of gift: and the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death." (18:7) The priest's office covers every thing of the altar and within the vail; its nature God himself characterises as a gift — "a service of gift"; and the penalty of the boundary stands in the last clause: the stranger that cometh nigh shall be put to death. In these seven verses "gift" sounds twice (18:6, 7) and death twice (18:3, 7): the office is a gift, and the boundary is a line of life and death, and the two stand in one paragraph, neither to be read without the other.

(Application) The first lesson of this section is that bearing comes before privilege. When the church speaks of office, it commonly begins from the privilege side: what this office may decide, what respect it receives, what provision it draws. This chapter's order runs the other way: God's first word to Aaron is "shall bear the iniquity of the sanctuary," and the list of portions does not begin until the next section. The order is itself doctrine: an office that cannot bear has no right to any portion it receives, and a servant who asks first "what do I get?" has not yet entered the door of this chapter. Everyone who stands in any spiritual office should read verse 1 to himself first: what have I borne? Standing between the congregation and holiness, for whom have I intercepted wrath, for whom have I carried responsibility? The more real the bearing, the clearer the boundary kept, and the steadier the hand that receives the portion. An office that enjoys its portion and bears no iniquity has no place in this chapter.

(Application) The second lesson is the mercy inside the boundary. The people of chapter 17 read the boundary as a wall to shut them out: "Whosoever cometh any thing near unto the tabernacle of the LORD shall die." Chapter 18 tells the same boundary over again, and what comes out is protection: Levites keep charge in the middle, priests bear in front, "that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel." The boundary's purpose is not to keep men far away; it is to let them live in the camp. When the church handles holiness and boundaries today, it should learn this way of telling it: keep the boundary strict, and state the reason plainly — not "our group is the holier," but "some bear and some keep charge, so that none of us need die." The keeper who forgets that the boundary exists to protect turns it into a boast; the man stopped by the boundary, if he understands this layer of it, finds that the stop is not a humiliation: someone is standing between him and the danger. Strictness and mercy, in this chapter, are two faces of one thing.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only. "Bear" is nāśāʾ (H5375), to lift up and carry; it stands twice in verse 1 (bearing the sanctuary's iniquity, bearing the priesthood's), and the Levites' bearing in verse 23 and the "bear sin" of verses 22 and 32 are the same word. "Iniquity" is ʿāwōn (H5771), twice in verse 1 and once in verse 23. "Keep" is šāmar (H8104) in verses 3-5, and the "charge" kept is the noun mišmereṯ (H4931) — the same word as the "keeping" of the token in chapter 17: an office is a trust to be kept. "Gift" in verses 6 and 7 rests on mattānâ (H4979): the Levites are given as a gift, and the priest's office is a service of gift — one word binding the section's two "gifts" into a pair. The word for "stranger" is zār in form; its number was not separately verified for this manuscript and is therefore given without a number. All numbered items above were verified one by one before writing; the register is in Endnote 5.

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) The nature of the "bearing" in verse 1 has been discussed in more than one way, set out here without adjudication: some interpreters read it as the priests' bearing of the consequences of Israel's offences through their ritual service (a service-bearing); others read it as a declaration of where responsibility lies — if anything goes wrong within the sanctuary's sphere, the account falls on those who keep the sanctuary (a responsibility-bearing). The two readings differ on where the weight of "bear" lands; the text itself gives only the bearers and the sphere borne, and this sermon adjudicates neither. The relation of this chapter to chapters 16-17 is also commonly read in scholarship as the priestly tradition's codification of the order of office: narrative of contention first, statutes of office after. This sermon follows the order as written — the cry of fear in the narrative first, the re-establishing of the offices in the statute after — without first dismantling the sources and then preaching the text.

### 2. The Portion for Ever: The Priests' Portion Listed Item by Item (Numbers 18:8-19) (08:00-24:00)

(Fact) The second section's introduction stands in verse 8, as it stands in the base text: "And the LORD spake unto Aaron," (18:8) — God's second direct address to Aaron, and its subject is provision. Verse 8 is the head of the list, quoted in full: "Behold, I also have given thee the charge of mine heave offerings of all the hallowed things of the children of Israel; unto thee have I given them by reason of the anointing, and to thy sons, by an ordinance for ever." (18:8) Four items in the head: the source — "mine heave offerings," "all the hallowed things of the children of Israel"; the trust — the charge of them is given to Aaron: the priest is a steward before he is a receiver; the ground — "by reason of the anointing"; the tenure — unto Aaron and to his sons, for ever. Everything after this verse is the itemising of this head, and this sermon quotes it item by item without merging, because the precision of this chapter's provision lies exactly in its items and conditions: which class goes to whom, who may eat it, and in what place and state — verse by verse these differ, and to merge them is to misread the chapter.

(Fact) The first class is what is reserved from the fire of the most holy things. Verse 9, quoted in full: "This shall be thine of the most holy things, reserved from the fire: every oblation of theirs, every meat offering of theirs, and every sin offering of theirs, and every trespass offering of theirs which they shall render unto me, shall be most holy for thee and for thy sons." (18:9) Three items registered: the offerings named are three, in the base text's order — meat offering, sin offering, trespass offering; what goes to the priest is not the whole offering but what is "reserved from the fire" — the part burnt on the altar goes to the altar, the part reserved goes to the priest, one offering in two shares with the fire as the dividing line; and its character is "most holy," the highest grade-word of holiness in the chapter, registered in the base text's own wording without any scale of grades invented by this sermon. Verse 10 gives the conditions of eating this class, quoted in full: "In the most holy place shalt thou eat it; every male shall eat it: it shall be holy unto thee." (18:10) Three conditions: the place — in the most holy place; the eaters — every male; the attitude — it shall be holy unto thee. A difference between the base texts is registered here exactly: the KJV names the place expressly, while the Chinese base text says only "eat these as a most holy thing" and does not name the place; each manuscript keeps to its own base text, and the Chinese fact layer does not read the KJV's place into the Chinese text.

(Fact) The second class is the heave offering and the wave offerings. Verse 11, quoted in full: "And this is thine; the heave offering of their gift, with all the wave offerings of the children of Israel: I have given them unto thee, and to thy sons and to thy daughters with thee, by a statute for ever: every one that is clean in thy house shall eat of it." (18:11) Set it item by item against the class before, and every difference is the text's own: the recipients widen to sons and daughters (verse 9 said "thy sons"; this verse and verse 19 say sons and daughters — the Chinese base text likewise varies its wording between the verses, and each is quoted as it stands); the eaters widen to "every one that is clean in thy house" — no longer males only, the condition now hanging on cleanness; the tenure is still "for ever." Pause here in reading the list: in one and the same paragraph, one class is for males only and the next is for everyone clean in the house; the conditions are given class by class, not laid down once as a single rule for all. Whoever merges the conditions of the two classes has misread the chapter — half of this chapter's reverence is hidden in precisely this class-by-class distinguishing.

(Fact) The third, fourth and fifth classes are the firstfruits, devoted things, and the firstborn, quoted verse by verse. Verse 12: "All the best of the oil, and all the best of the wine, and of the wheat, the firstfruits of them which they shall offer unto the LORD, them have I given thee." (18:12) The firstfruits named are oil, wine, and wheat (the Chinese base text reads "grain" where the KJV reads "wheat"), and they must be "all the best" of them — firstfruits and best, two conditions laid on one verse. Verse 13: "And whatsoever is first ripe in the land, which they shall bring unto the LORD, shall be thine; every one that is clean in thine house shall eat of it." (18:13) The range widens from three named kinds to whatsoever is first ripe in the land; the eaters are again every one clean in the house. Verse 14 is one sentence: "Every thing devoted in Israel shall be thine." (18:14) Devoted things go to the priest as a whole class, with no condition attached in this verse, and this sermon adds none. Verses 15-18 treat the firstborn, the most detailed item of the list. Verse 15 lays down the general rule: "Every thing that openeth the matrix in all flesh, which they bring unto the LORD, whether it be of men or beasts, shall be thine: nevertheless the firstborn of man shalt thou surely redeem, and the firstling of unclean beasts shalt thou redeem." (18:15) The belonging is the whole class to the priest; the exceptions are two — the firstborn of man and the firstling of unclean beasts are to be redeemed. Verse 16 gives the manner of redeeming: "And those that are to be redeemed from a month old shalt thou redeem, according to thine estimation, for the money of five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary, which is twenty gerahs." (18:16) The time is from a month old, the estimation the priest's, the standard the shekel of the sanctuary, the sum five shekels. Verse 17 marks the class that may not be redeemed: "But the firstling of a cow, or the firstling of a sheep, or the firstling of a goat, thou shalt not redeem; they are holy: thou shalt sprinkle their blood upon the altar, and shalt burn their fat for an offering made by fire, for a sweet savour unto the LORD." (18:17) The firstlings of clean beasts may not be redeemed; their handling is blood sprinkled on the altar and fat burnt as an offering by fire. Verse 18 closes the firstborn class: "And the flesh of them shall be thine, as the wave breast and as the right shoulder are thine." (18:18) The flesh of the unredeemed belongs to the priest, on the same footing as the wave breast and the right shoulder — where the KJV reads "shoulder," the Chinese base text reads "right leg," a difference registered in the head of this sermon.

(Fact) The whole list is sealed in verse 19, quoted in full: "All the heave offerings of the holy things, which the children of Israel offer unto the LORD, have I given thee, and thy sons and thy daughters with thee, by a statute for ever: it is a covenant of salt for ever before the LORD unto thee and to thy seed with thee." (18:19) Three items in the sealing: the range returns to the general name "heave offerings," gathering the head of verse 8 and the items of verses 11-18 into one; the tenure is named again — this is the third "for ever" tenure spoken over Aaron's house in the list's frame (18:8, 11, 19); and the character rises to covenant: this provision is no temporary grant but "a covenant of salt for ever" — and the Chinese base text carries its own note on the word: salt means that which does not decay. The reliability of the provision is nailed, by the text itself, to the covenant: whether the priests' portion can fail does not depend on the mood of the givers but on whether the covenant decays; and a covenant defined by undecaying salt carries a portion defined by "for ever."

(Application) The first lesson of this section is the dignity of provision. Provision in this chapter is not charity; it is statute: source, class, condition, and tenure are written verse by verse into the law. How God's servants are to live is never left, in God's own arrangement, to the congregation's passing impulse; it is left to a portion God himself has ordained. The church's talk about the provision of ministers and fellow workers easily runs to two extremes: at one end, vagueness — "live by faith," which sounds spiritual and in practice hands what ought to be statute over to luck; at the other end, the marketplace — provision haggled into a wage bargain. This chapter gives a third road: provision comes from God's giving ("I have given" runs through this whole section), passes through the people's offering, and lands as the servant's portion for ever. The receiver receives without shame, because God ordained it; the giver gives without reluctance, because what he offers is first rendered unto the LORD. To speak provision plainly and set it in order is not worldliness; it is honouring the office in this chapter's own manner.

(Application) The second lesson lies in the conditions. The most holy things are for males and must be held holy; the heave and wave offerings are for everyone clean in the house; the firstfruits likewise — every class of provision carries its own "who may receive, and in what state." Provision in this chapter never falls into a pocket unconditionally: the man who receives holy things must himself stand in cleanness and separation. The receiver of a church's provision today should fear not that the provision is small, but that he receives it in the wrong state: holding in his hand what the people offered to God, yet treating it in his heart as ordinary income, to be spent carelessly and taken for granted. The chain of conditions in this chapter reminds every receiver: the more "for ever" the portion, the more trembling in the receiving — not fear of losing the portion, but fear of profaning its source. Everything the people offer passes first over the altar and is first rendered unto the LORD, and only then reaches the priest's hand; that path is the whole of the receiver's reverence.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only. "Heave offering" is tərûmâ (H8641), nine times in this chapter — the portion lifted out and separated from an offering; its verb root is the lifting up that appears in verses 26 and 30 ("offer up," "heaved"). The "holy" of "hallowed" and "most holy" rests on the root qdš: the noun qōdeš (H6944), with "most holy" its reduplicated intensive form, registered in the base text's wording without any grade table invented here. "Devoted" is ḥērem (H2764), what is wholly the LORD's and may not be redeemed. "Firstborn" is bəkôr (H1060), and the phrase "openeth the matrix" is peṭer reḥem (peṭer H6363, reḥem H7358). "Redeem" is pādâ (H6299) — note that this chapter's redeeming does not use the root gāʾal; the two roots are different, and only pādâ stands in this chapter (register in Endnote 5). "Covenant of salt" is bərîṯ melaḥ (bərîṯ H1285, melaḥ H4417). "Fat" in verse 17 and "the best thereof" in verses 29-30 are one word, ḥēleb (H2459), literally fat and by extension the richest part — one word, two uses, registered under each verse's own rendering. All numbers above were verified one by one before writing.

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) The background of the covenant of salt has several layers in scholarship, set out without adjudication: salt in the ancient Near East served for preservation, for purification, and for covenant meals; Leviticus 2 requires salt with the meat offering and speaks of the salt of the covenant not lacking (Lev 2:13, cross-reference layer), and 2 Chronicles 13 likewise speaks of the kingdom given to David by a covenant of salt (2 Chr 13:5, cross-reference layer). The Chinese base text's own note at verse 19 — "that which does not decay" — agrees with the preserving sense, and the sermon text argues from the base text's own note, keeping the background accounts on record only. The priests' list in this chapter and the priests' dues in Deuteronomy 18 (Deut 18:3-5) also differ in items — the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the maw of the peace offerings stand there and not here: the relation between the two lists in date and composition belongs to scholarly discussion; this sermon sets them side by side without harmonizing, and in preaching this chapter follows this chapter.

### 3. I Am Thy Part and Thine Inheritance: The Inheritance of Those Who Have No Land (Numbers 18:20-24) (24:00-42:00)

(Fact) Verse 20 is the centre of the chapter and God's third address to Aaron, quoted in full: "And the LORD spake unto Aaron, Thou shalt have no inheritance in their land, neither shalt thou have any part among them: I am thy part and thine inheritance among the children of Israel." (18:20) The structure is read as it stands: two negatives, then one declaration. The negative side is land: no inheritance in their land, no part among them — when the twelve tribes come to divide the land, Aaron's house is not in the division. The declaration side is a Person: "I am thy part and thine inheritance" — note the subject is not the list of provisions but "I": God does not say "I will give thee a portion and an inheritance"; he says he himself is that portion and that inheritance. Through twelve verses (18:8-19) the things stood in front; at verse 20 the things step back and the Giver of the things steps forward. Here is the foundation of the chapter's whole system of provision: the priests' portion is "for ever" not because the list is detailed, but because behind the list stands this "I am."

(Fact) Verse 21 turns to the Levites, quoted in full: "And, behold, I have given the children of Levi all the tenth in Israel for an inheritance, for their service which they serve, even the service of the tabernacle of the congregation." (18:21) The Levites' inheritance is set beside the priests' portion in plain contrast: the priests' inheritance is God himself (18:20); the Levites' inheritance is the tenth (18:21); the priests have no land, and the Levites have no land either — verses 23 and 24 will say it twice more. Four items in the giving of the tenth, as they stand: its range is "all the tenth in Israel," the whole and not a selection of kinds; its receivers are "the children of Levi," receivers by descent; its tenure-name is "for an inheritance" — the tenth in the Levites' hands is not small change for wages but the inheritance that stands in place of land; and its ground is "for their service" — the Chinese base text characterises it expressly as a reward for their labour: provision and service correspond in open terms in this chapter — serve the tabernacle's service, receive the tabernacle's inheritance. Verse 22 draws the boundary once more: "Neither must the children of Israel henceforth come nigh the tabernacle of the congregation, lest they bear sin, and die." (18:22) A boundary sentence inside the provision paragraph is no digression: precisely because the Levites alone do the tabernacle's service and alone receive the tabernacle's inheritance, the rest of Israel must not come nigh — office made single, provision made single, boundary made single: the three are one set.

(Fact) Verses 23-24 close the chain of reasons, quoted in full. Verse 23: "But the Levites shall do the service of the tabernacle of the congregation, and they shall bear their iniquity: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations, that among the children of Israel they have no inheritance." (18:23) Verse 24: "But the tithes of the children of Israel, which they offer as an heave offering unto the LORD, I have given to the Levites to inherit: therefore I have said unto them, Among the children of Israel they shall have no inheritance." (18:24) The direction of the reasoning is registered as it stands: it is not that land was first taken away and the tenth then offered as compensation to console them; the text's own sequence runs — the Levites do the tabernacle's service (service first), the tenth is given them for an inheritance (provision following), "therefore" they have no inheritance in land (landlessness is the consequence and the sign of the first two). Verse 24 adds one more layer of character to the tenth: it is first what Israel offers "as an heave offering unto the LORD," and only then given to the Levites to inherit — the people offer to God, and God passes it on to the Levites; the path is the same as with the priests' portion: all provision passes first through God's hand. By this verse "no inheritance" has sounded three times in the chapter (18:20 to Aaron; 18:23, 24 to the Levites), and "inheritance" six times in the KJV of the chapter; the distribution is in Endnote 4.

(Application) The lesson of this section is the three words "I am." A man reckons his inheritance by what can be counted: land, money, a way of retreat. Aaron's house is struck by God's own hand out of the land division, and God says to them: I am thy part. This is not a word to console the losers of the draw; it changes the definition of inheritance: other men's inheritance is land held in the hand; the priest's inheritance is the God who stands before him. The deepest temptation of those who serve today is not unbelief in God; it is quietly transferring their security to an inheritance of their own: with land, with savings, with another way back, service feels safe. This chapter's arrangement does the opposite — it strikes out the way back first, and then sets provision up as statute — not to make the servant live an ascetic life, but to teach him a different arithmetic: my portion is not on the list; my portion is the One who made the list. Lists grow and shrink, seasons have plenty and dearth, people have warmth and cold; "I am" neither grows nor shrinks. The man who has reckoned verse 20 clearly receives provision without panic and loses provision without murmuring — because what he truly had was never that portion alone, mistakable for the whole.

(Application) The second lesson stands on the Levites' inheritance. The tenth is the Levites' inheritance, and its ground is written with complete practicality: "for their service which they serve." Provision in this chapter is not mystified at all: labour and reward correspond, service and inheritance correspond. For the congregation today that is one lesson: providing for those who serve is not almsgiving and not favour; it is a correspondence written in the text itself — they do the tabernacle's service, and the congregation renders the tabernacle's tenth. For the servant it is also one lesson: inheritance and labour are received bound together; to receive the tabernacle's inheritance while not doing the tabernacle's service will not stand within this chapter's logic. Let both sides be calibrated by this verse: the giver does not make himself a benefactor, the receiver does not make himself a creditor; above labour and reward stands the God who first receives the tenth as an heave offering and then gives it on — both sides answer first to him, and the relation stays clean.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only, and the title-words of the chapter are distinguished one by one here, in the place where they are most easily mispair­ed. "Part" is ḥēleq (H2506), the share that falls to one. "Inheritance" is the noun naḥălâ (H5159), the hereditary landed inheritance; its verb is nāḥal (H5157), "to have/receive an inheritance," standing in verses 20, 23 and 24 — noun and verb of one root and two words, with different numbers, not to be interchanged. In the one sentence of verse 20, ḥēleq and naḥălâ stand together: no ḥēleq among them; "I" am thy ḥēleq and thy naḥălâ — the pairing itself shows the distinction: a part is the share received, an inheritance is the patrimony held. "Tenth/tithe" is maʿăśēr (H4643) — let the number be stated plainly: four-six-four-three, not four-six-four-five; the word derives from the root for "ten" and denotes the tithe-thing specifically. The "reward" word has two layers: the noun for wages/reward in verses 21 and 31 is śākār (H7939); while the "for" of verse 21 — "for their service," in exchange for — rests on ḥēlep̄ (H2500), a word near ḥēleq (H2506) in sound and spelling, differing only in its last letter: one is exchange, the other is portion — the first pitfall of mispairing in this chapter, verified and registered separately. All numbers above were verified one by one before writing; the register is in Endnote 5.

(New Testament Reading) The declaration "I am thy part" has its echoes later in Scripture, registered at the reading layer only, and its New Testament reception belongs here: the Levites' taking of tithes reappears in Hebrews 7, where the sons of Levi, who receive the office of the priesthood, are said to have a commandment to take tithes of the people according to the law (Heb 7:5, reading layer) — the New Testament writer treats this chapter's provision-order as the standing pattern of the old priesthood even while arguing beyond it. Behind that reception stand the Old Testament's own echoes: the psalmist's confession that the LORD is his portion and his inheritance (Ps 16:5, reading layer), and Ezekiel's restatement that God himself is the priests' inheritance (Ezek 44:28, reading layer). At the fact layer of this chapter, verse 20 is spoken to Aaron's house corporately and is bound to the office; it is not first read as a private devotional sentence and then poured back into the chapter. What the reading layer may say is only this: that God is a man's portion began as the charter of a priestly house, and in the later worship of God's people became words a singer could confess for himself — the source of the portion did not change; the company of those who claim it widened.

### 4. A Tenth Part of the Tithe: The Receivers Must Also Offer (Numbers 18:25-29) (42:00-56:00)

(Fact) The manner of speaking changes in the fourth section. Verse 25, as it stands: "And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying," (18:25) — the chapter's fourth utterance, and its only one addressed to Moses; its content is a direction to be passed on to the Levites. God speaks the offices and the portions to Aaron directly; the Levites' duty of offering he delivers through Moses — the chain of delivery is itself registered as it stands, without ornament added. Verse 26 is the head of the section, quoted in full: "Thus speak unto the Levites, and say unto them, When ye take of the children of Israel the tithes which I have given you from them for your inheritance, then ye shall offer up an heave offering of it for the LORD, even a tenth part of the tithe." (18:26) The items in order: the Levites' tithe is first restated as "which I have given you from them for your inheritance" — the duty of offering stands on the fact of receiving: first the inheritance was received, then the offering is spoken of; the proportion to be offered is "a tenth part of the tithe" — a tenth of the tenth, one hundredth of Israel's whole produce, rendered unto the LORD as an heave offering. The receivers of provision themselves stand inside the order of offering; this chapter leaves no class of receivers exempt: the people render the tenth, the Levites render a tenth of the tenth, and at every step down the chain of provision the duty of offering follows one step behind.

(Fact) Verse 27 gives the reckoning of this heave offering, quoted in full: "And this your heave offering shall be reckoned unto you, as though it were the corn of the threshingfloor, and as the fulness of the winepress." (18:27) The Levites have no land, no threshingfloor, no winepress; yet the tenth part they offer up is to be reckoned as though it were the corn of their own threshingfloor and the fulness of their own winepress — the standing of an offering does not hang on whether a man has land, but on whether what he has received is his inheritance. Men with no patrimony are still offerers; what they offer is no token coin but a true portion reckoned on their own "produce." Verse 28 gives the destination of the heave offering, quoted in full: "Thus ye also shall offer an heave offering unto the LORD of all your tithes, which ye receive of the children of Israel; and ye shall give thereof the LORD's heave offering to Aaron the priest." (18:28) The destination runs upward in one line: Israel's tenth to the Levites, the Levites' tenth unto the LORD, delivered in fact to Aaron the priest. Aaron is named here once in this section, and named as "Aaron the priest." Verse 29 gives the quality of what is offered, quoted in full: "Out of all your gifts ye shall offer every heave offering of the LORD, of all the best thereof, even the hallowed part thereof out of it." (18:29) What the Levites offer may not be what they have picked over: out of all their gifts, the best thereof, the hallowed part, is to be offered unto the LORD. The standard of quality is the same as the standard laid on Israel's firstfruits — "all the best" (18:12): as the chain of offering climbs, the quality is not discounted.

(Application) The lesson of this section is that receivers are not exempt. The Levites are men who live on the tenth, and to men who live on the tenth God says: of your tenth, offer up again a tenth part. The class most easily exempted today is precisely the class that receives provision: we preach giving, and preach it to the congregation; when it comes to ourselves, we say our income already comes from giving, and need not be divided again. This chapter recognises no such exemption: the more openly the inheritance is received, the more exactly — in proportion and in quality — it is to be offered from. That those who live on offerings themselves live inside offering first is not the statute's harshness; it is the order of faith: if I truly believe the tenth is God's way of providing for men, my own portion should pass through that way first. To preach giving to others is easy; to offer again out of what one has received is hard; and this chapter reserves the hard lesson for the receivers.

(Application) The second lesson stands on "shall be reckoned." What the Levites offer came, in men's eyes, from other men's hands; God says it shall be reckoned as the corn of their threshingfloor and the fulness of their winepress, exactly as if they had grown it themselves. Whether an offering counts does not depend on the origin of the thing, but on whether the offerer treats it as his own produce. Many today feel they have nothing to offer: the income is fixed, the ability ordinary, everything in hand a thing received. This chapter's answer is: what is received counts as your produce all the same; what is offered out of receiving is a true heave offering all the same. No man is too poor to offer, and no man's receiving is too small to yield a tenth part of the tenth. The question is never whether there is anything; it is whether the account is owned: do I count what I have received as my threshingfloor and my winepress? Own it, and the hand opens; disown it, and however much there is, it is always somebody else's.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only. Maʿăśēr (H4643) was registered in Section 3; here only its distribution in this section is added: three times in verse 26 (the tithes taken, "of it," "of the tithe") and twice in verse 28 — seven times in the chapter in all, each one counted, in Endnote 4. Tərûmâ (H8641) appears in every verse of this section's substance (18:26-29) as the fixed name of what the Levites offer up. The "gifts" of verse 29 is mattānâ (H4979), the same word as the "gift" of verses 6 and 7: everything the Levites have received is still called, in the original, a gift — and out of the gift received, the best is taken and offered up; the word itself binds receiving and offering together. "The best thereof" is ḥēleb (H2459), the same word as the "fat" of verse 17, registered in Section 2 and not set up again here, only noted as the correspondence.

### 5. Heave the Best of It, and Ye Shall Bear No Sin: The Close Between Eating and Polluting (Numbers 18:30-32) (58:00-60:00)

(Fact) The last three verses are the closing words God directed Moses to say to the Levites, quoted verse by verse in full. Verse 30: "Therefore thou shalt say unto them, When ye have heaved the best thereof from it, then it shall be counted unto the Levites as the increase of the threshingfloor, and as the increase of the winepress." (18:30) Note first that the chain of delivery has not broken: the LORD directs Moses, and Moses is to say to the Levites — the chain set up in verse 25 is named again in verse 30, "thou shalt say unto them." The content is verse 27's reckoning repeated and confirmed: once the best has been heaved from it, the remaining nine tenths "shall be counted" as the Levites' own threshingfloor increase and winepress increase — first heave, then reckon; reverse the order, and the whole account is wrong. Verse 31 gives the Levites their freedom in eating what remains: "And ye shall eat it in every place, ye and your households: for it is your reward for your service in the tabernacle of the congregation." (18:31) The words "in every place" are read with their full weight: set beside verse 10 — the most holy things eaten in the most holy place, by males — this portion of the Levites has no restriction of place and no restriction to males, and it extends to their households. The presence or absence of restrictions is still given by the text class by class: the grades of holiness differ, and so do the manners of eating; this sermon sets the two places side by side as the base text gives them and does not force the text into one uniform rule. Verse 32 is the chapter's last sentence, quoted in full: "And ye shall bear no sin by reason of it, when ye have heaved from it the best of it: neither shall ye pollute the holy things of the children of Israel, lest ye die." (18:32)

(Fact) The closing chain of conditions has two links, set side by side as they stand. The positive link: when ye have heaved from it the best of it — what was owed has been offered first — "ye shall bear no sin by reason of it": the sin that the Levites' enjoying of their portion might have raised is removed by the heaving of the best. The negative link: "neither shall ye pollute the holy things of the children of Israel, lest ye die" — the people's holy things do not change their nature in the Levites' hands; they are still holy things; to slight them or divert them is to pollute them, and the end is death. The chapter closes with death, answering at a distance the cry of fear in chapter 17 — but let the object be read clearly: those afraid of dying in chapter 17 were a people who desired to come near without the appointed way; those warned here "lest ye die" are men already standing in the office and holding the holy things. The nearer a man stands to the holy, the heavier the warning — this is no threat for effect; it is the logic that runs through the chapter from "shall bear" in verse 1 to "lest ye die" in verse 32: the nearer the office to holiness, the more the responsibility lands on the office-holder's own head. Within this chapter the LORD says nothing more to Moses; the statute is complete.

(Application) The closing lesson is: heave first, then eat. The order of verses 30-32 is the whole chapter's theology of provision in miniature: the best is heaved to God first, and the rest is then counted as one's own; God's account is reckoned first, and one's own account after. Reverse the order — eat first, and heave what is left — and what is heaved is no longer the best, and the sin is no longer lifted. Men handle income today in the opposite habit: spend first — house, living, children — and think of offering only if anything remains; and what remains is naturally not the best. This chapter's order runs the other way, and not in order to make God first and oneself miserable, but to give enjoyment its freedom: once the best has been heaved, the rest may be eaten in every place, eaten with a clear right, bearing no sin. The man who heaves first eats in freedom; the man who eats first heaves with a troubled conscience. What God wants was never the tenth part of the tithe itself — what addition or loss is that small thing to him? — he wants the order in the receiver's heart: whoever stands first is truly Lord.

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) The later history of the Levites' tithe is sparsely recorded, set out without adjudication: Nehemiah 10 records the people's covenant to bring the tithes into the storehouse, with a priest present when the Levites took them (Neh 10:37-38, cross-reference layer); Nehemiah 13 records the tithes stopping and the Levites fleeing, every man to his field (Neh 13:10, cross-reference layer); Malachi 3's "ye have robbed me" passage (Mal 3:8-10, cross-reference layer) likewise takes the tithe as its theme. These records show the statute's execution rising and falling across history; this chapter itself lays down the statute only and records no execution, and to judge the statute's force in this chapter by the later history of its execution belongs to the discussion layer, which this sermon does not adopt. The tithe of this chapter goes to the Levites (18:21-24); Deuteronomy 14 has, besides, the festival tithe and the third-year tithe (Deut 14:22-29, cross-reference layer); the two arrangements are set side by side in Endnote 6 and are not harmonized into one general rule.

## Devotional Excerpt

The house of Aaron had no land. When the tribes received their inheritance, their name was not on the list; yet the LORD said unto Aaron, "I am thy part and thine inheritance" (Num 18:20). The priest's portion was not taken away; it was given in another form: another man's portion could be measured in fields, but his portion was the God who stood before him. So it was with the Levites: their inheritance was the tenth, given unto them "for their service which they serve, even the service of the tabernacle of the congregation" (18:21); and having received it, they still had to "offer up an heave offering of it for the LORD, even a tenth part of the tithe" (18:26) — the receiver is never the end of the order. If today thou art troubled over thy portion — thy income, thy place, what others have and thou hast not — reckon the account as far as verse 20: if thy portion is written only in a list, when the list changes thou wilt tremble; if thy portion is the LORD himself, he neither waxeth nor waneth. Then learn of the Levites: out of what thou hast received, first heave "the best of it" (18:30); and the rest thou mayest eat in every place, in freedom.
