"And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to which also you were called in one body; and be thankful." (Col. 3:15)

"Rule" means to arbitrate, to judge; "one body" means a single body; "thankful" means grateful, giving thanks, feeling joyful.

Each one of you is a part of the body of Christ, chosen to live together in peace. Life is meant to be shared.

God's intention is for us to experience life together; the Bible calls this shared experience fellowship. Today, "fellowship" has lost its biblical meaning; now, "fellowship" often refers to "casual chat, socializing, having fun." Nowadays, "Where do you fellowship?" means "Which church do you attend?" "Please stay for fellowship" usually means "Please stay for refreshments." Genuine fellowship is not merely attending a gathering; it is also experiencing life together, which includes unselfish mutual love, sincere sharing, practical serving, sacrificial giving, compassionate comforting, and all the "one another" commands in the New Testament.

The number of people in a fellowship matters; smaller is better. Of course, you can worship with a large crowd, but one person is not a fellowship. When a small group exceeds ten people, someone will stop participating, and that person is often the quietest one, while the group begins to be dominated by a small handful of people. What the Lord Jesus trained was a small group of disciples; He could have called more people, but He knew that for everyone to participate, the small group could not exceed twelve people.

The body of Christ, just like our human body, is composed of many cells. The life of the body of Christ, also like our human body, lives within the cells. Therefore, every Christian needs to join a church small group, such as a home fellowship, Sunday school, or Bible study class. Real fellowship life happens here, not in large gatherings. If you compare the church to a large ship, the small groups are the many lifeboats on board.

God has many amazing promises for small groups of believers: "For where two or three are gathered together in My name, I am there in the midst of them." Sadly, even joining a small group does not guarantee that you will experience genuine fellowship life. Many Sunday schools and small groups merely stay on the surface, not knowing how to experience genuine fellowship life. What, after all, is the difference between genuine and superficial fellowship life?

## I. "Let the Peace of Christ Rule in Your Hearts"

"Let the peace of God rule in your hearts"—this means letting the peace of Christ be the master and umpire in all our conduct, and maintaining this peace as the standard for our interpersonal dealings. "The peace of Christ" refers to a peaceful and serene state of mind that exists in Christ.

If a person can live in Christ, peace will naturally abide in their heart. True rest is: letting the peace of Christ rule and reign in our hearts.

In judging things in personal or church life, we do not rely on right versus wrong, but on whether there is peace. When believers interact with people and handle matters, they must have a sense of peace before moving forward; once they feel the slightest lack of peace, they must stop regardless of anything else.

"Let the peace of Christ rule in our hearts"—the word "let" indicates that we have the responsibility to "allow" the peace of Christ to operate and work within us; if we do not "allow" it, there will be no peace.

"To which also you were called in one body"—this indicates that Christians are called into the body of Christ in order to manifest that Christ is the Lord of the whole body; all difficulties in the life of the body (the church) arise because the members do not obey the Head's direction and command, causing discord to appear among the members.

Believers are called to be "in one body." If members each act on their own and do not coordinate with one another, the body will inevitably suffer hardship. Therefore, in the church, one must not go their own way, but should obey Christ's direction in all things.

The corporate life of the church prioritizes unity, and the way to maintain unity lies in all members listening to the arbitration of the Head, Christ—not seeking in anything to get one's own way, but only seeking to have a heart of peace and thanksgiving.

Doctrines divide people, but Christ unites people—the unity of the church does not lie in debating doctrines and labels, but in Christ ruling in the hearts of each person.

"And be thankful"—that is, thanksgiving; when a person is thankful, they acknowledge God's grace, acknowledging that all good things come from God. In church life, our hearts toward others should be characterized by "peace" (another meaning of the original word for "peace"), and toward God, our hearts should be filled with "thanksgiving."

## II. Genuine Fellowship Life Allows Us to Experience Authenticity

Authentic fellowship life is not superficial, surface-level chitchat, but candid sharing of hearts, and sometimes even pouring out one's soul. This happens when people honestly reveal who they truly are and their life experiences. They can share hurts, pour out feelings, confess their failures, make their doubts public, admit their fears, recognize their own weaknesses, and ask for the help and intercession of others.

Such authenticity may be the exact opposite of what you see in some churches. Replacing an atmosphere of honesty and humility are phoniness, pretense, playing political games, and superficial politeness with shallow conversations. People wear masks, keep their guard up, and pretend that everything is happy and wonderful. Such attitudes cause fellowship to die. Only when we open up our lives can we experience genuine fellowship life. The Bible says: "If we walk in the light as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another... If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves." The world thinks intimacy happens in the dark, but God says it happens in the light. Darkness conceals our wounds, faults, fears, failures, and flaws; but in the light, we make all of these open and candidly confess who we truly are.

Of course, letting others know the real you requires courage and humility; doing so means we must confront the fear of exposure, of facing rejection, and of being hurt again. Why would anyone be willing to take this risk? Because it is the only way to spiritual growth and emotional health. The Bible says: "Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed." We can only grow by taking risks, and the most difficult risk among them is to face ourselves and others honestly.

## III. In Genuine Fellowship Life People Experience Mutuality

Mutuality is both giving and contributing; it is a two-way effort. The Bible says: "God has put the body together such that we understand how to live together in the church, and each part is interdependent." Mutuality is the heart of fellowship life: building reciprocal relationships, sharing responsibilities, and helping one another. Paul said: "I want us to help each other by faith, your faith helping me, and my faith helping you." When someone walks with us and encourages us, our faith becomes firmer. The Bible requires us to hold one another accountable, encourage one another, serve one another, and honor one another. More than fifty times, on various matters, the New Testament commands us to "one another" and "each other." The Bible says: "Therefore let us pursue the things which make for peace and the things by which one may edify another."

You do not have to be responsible for every believer, but you are responsible to them. God expects you to do your best to help them.

## IV. In Genuine Fellowship Life People Experience Compassion

Compassion is not providing advice or giving convenient, superficial help; compassion is entering into another's suffering and sharing it with them. Compassion is: "I understand what you are going through right now, and your feelings are neither strange nor crazy." Modern people call it "empathy," but the Bible uses "sympathy" (compassion). The Bible says: "Therefore, as the elect of God... put on tender mercies, kindness, humility, meekness, longsuffering."

Compassion can meet two basic human needs: the need to be understood and the feeling of being accepted. Every time you understand and validate the other person's feelings, you build fellowship. The problem is that we are always dealing with matters in such a rush that we often have no time to sympathize with others; or we are consumed by our own hurts. Self-pity dries up our compassion for others.

Fellowship life has different levels, each suitable for different occasions. The simplest level of fellowship is sharing and studying God's Word together. A deeper level is the fellowship of service, such as participating together in mission or caring work. The deepest, most intense level is the fellowship of suffering; in such fellowship, we enter into one another's suffering, grieving together and bearing one another's burdens. The Christians in the world who understand this level best are those who suffer persecution, despise, and martyrdom for their faith.

The Bible commands us: "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." In the most critical, painful, sorrowful, and doubting moments, that is precisely when we need one another the most; that is when we need our brothers and sisters in the Lord the most. We need a small group of friends to uphold us with faith to get through the difficulty. In a small group, even when God seems far away, the body of Christ is real and visible. This is precisely what Job desperately needed in his suffering; he cried out: "To him who is afflicted, kindness should be shown by his friend, even though he forsakes the fear of the Almighty."

## V. In Genuine Fellowship Life People Experience Mercy

Fellowship is a place of grace, where mistakes are not rebuked but erased. Fellowship occurs when mercy triumphs over justice. We all need mercy because we all stumble and fall, needing others to help us get back up. We need to extend mercy to one another and be willing to receive mercy from others. God says: "When someone sins, you should forgive him and comfort him, lest he lose heart and give up."

Without forgiveness, there is no fellowship life. God warns: "Do not hold a grudge against anyone." For bitterness and resentment destroy fellowship life. We are all imperfect sinners; when living together long-term, we will inevitably hurt each other; sometimes unintentionally, sometimes intentionally. But regardless, a great deal of mercy and grace is needed to create and sustain fellowship life. The Bible says: "You must bear with each other's faults and forgive the person who offends you. Remember that the Lord has forgiven you, so you must also forgive others."

God's mercy toward us is our motivation for showing mercy to others. Please remember that what God has forgiven you of is always far more than what you forgive others of. When you are hurt by someone, you can make a choice: Will I use my energy and emotions to retaliate, or to resolve? You can only choose one of the two.

Whether the other person asks for forgiveness or not, forgiveness must be timely. Trust, however, takes time to rebuild; trust requires a track record worthy of belief. If someone repeatedly hurts you, God commands you to forgive them immediately, but God does not expect you to trust them immediately, nor do you have to allow them to continue hurting you. They must demonstrate through time that they have changed. The best place to rebuild relationships is the environment of a supportive small group, which can provide encouragement and accountability.

A small group committed to genuine fellowship has many other benefits; it is an essential part of the Christian life that must never be underestimated. For over two thousand years, Christians have always gathered in small groups to live out fellowship. If you have never participated in any small group, you have missed out on far too much.

In the next chapter, we will explore how to cultivate this kind of fellowship life with other believers. Hopefully, this chapter will cause you to long to experience this genuine fellowship life of authenticity, mutuality, compassion, and mercy. You were created for deep fellowship with one another.

## Chapter Reflection

**Point to Ponder**: My life needs other people.

**Verse to Remember**: "Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." (Gal. 6:2)

"One another’s" means reciprocal; "burdens" refers to cargo on a ship, weight. The "burdens" in this verse are very likely related to "being overtaken in any trespass." The burden of the Mosaic law, no one could bear; but the burdens in the life of faith must be borne by one another.

"The law of Christ"—refers to the commandment given by the Lord to love one another (John 13:34). The Judaizers wanted people to bear the burden of the law, but Paul wanted people to bear the burden of love, and the burden of love is light.

Not only do the burdens of others need us to bear them, but our burdens may also need others to bear them; not only do the difficulties of others need our compassion, comfort, and help, but we also may have difficulties that need the compassion, comfort, and help of others. Bearing one another's burdens is an expression of love, and love fulfills the law.

**Question to Consider**: How should I take the first step to establish an authentic, open relationship with other believers?

## Response Prayer

Dear Father, thank You for not leaving me alone. You have placed me into the body of Christ, calling me to live together with all the members in the peace of Christ. Please grant me courage to be a real person in fellowship: open, authentic, sharing burdens, and showing mercy to one another. Also make me a help to others—upholding people with faith, and embracing people with mercy. Amen!
