## Summary (Approx. 100 words)

Thirteen years after Ishmael's birth, the LORD appeared to ninety-nine-year-old Abram, revealing Himself as Almighty God and reestablishing the everlasting covenant: Abram was renamed Abraham, Sarai was renamed Sarah, the promised son was named Isaac, and circumcision was given as the sign of the covenant. Abraham fell on his face and laughed, yet interceded for Ishmael, and that very same day circumcised all the males of his household. This sermon examines three realities: after silence, God first proclaims His almighty name; the sign signifies belonging, not merit; and renaming precedes transformation, demanding obedience that very day.

## (00:00 - 08:00) Opening · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Peace be with you, brothers and sisters!

In our last message, we talked about Hagar meeting the God who sees in the wilderness, the birth of Ishmael, and Abram being eighty-six years old. And then what? Then the Bible fell silent. Between the end of Genesis 16 and the beginning of Genesis 17 lies a gap of thirteen years—without a single divine appearance, without a single word from God. Abram went about his daily life with Ishmael, while the promise in his heart—"one who will come from your own body shall be your heir"—seemed to drift further and further away year after year. Brothers and sisters, have you ever experienced such a thirteen-year span? Prayers go unanswered, circumstances remain unchanged, and it feels as though God has forgotten you. Today’s passage tells us: silence is not absence. When God finally spoke after thirteen years, the very first name He proclaimed was "Almighty God." Then He changed a man's name, established the sign of the covenant, and anchored His promise more firmly than ever before.

Let us first read the key passages:

"When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the LORD appeared to Abram and said to him, 'I am Almighty God; walk before Me and be blameless. And I will make My covenant between Me and you, and will multiply you exceedingly.'" (Gen. 17:1–2, excerpt) [9]

"'As for Me, behold, My covenant is with you, and you shall be a father of many nations. No longer shall your name be called Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you a father of many nations.'" (Gen. 17:4–5, excerpt)

"'And I will establish My covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you in their generations, for an everlasting covenant, to be God to you and your descendants after you.'" (Gen. 17:7, excerpt)

"'Every male child among you shall be circumcised; this is My covenant which you shall keep, between Me and you and your descendants after you... and it shall be a sign of the covenant between Me and you.'" (Gen. 17:10–11, excerpt)

"Then God said to Abraham: 'As for Sarai your wife, you shall not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall be her name. And I will bless her and also give you a son by her;'" (Gen. 17:15–16, excerpt)

"Then Abraham fell on his face and laughed, and said in his heart, 'Shall a child be born to a man who is one hundred years old? And shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?'" (Gen. 17:17, excerpt)

"So Abraham took Ishmael his son... and all the men of Abraham’s house, and circumcised... that very same day, as God had said to him." (Gen. 17:23, excerpt)

Today we will look at this passage in three parts:

First, 17:1–8, I Am Almighty God—After thirteen years of silence, God proclaims His name, changes names, and reestablishes the everlasting covenant;
Second, 17:9–14, Circumcision as a Sign—The pledge of the covenant is carved into the flesh; the sign speaks of belonging, not merit;
Third, 17:15–27, Sarah and Isaac—The wife is renamed, the son is named in advance, the father intercedes for Ishmael, and that very day the whole household obeys and receives the sign.

Let us bow our heads in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. Abram waited thirteen years, and You did not forget. You appeared to him as Almighty God, changed his name, and confirmed Your everlasting covenant. We pray that You speak today to those among us who are waiting in silence. Cause us first to acknowledge Your almightiness, and then to receive the new name and new sign You give us, obeying that very day without delay. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

## (08:00 - 24:00) Part 1: I Am Almighty God—After Silence, Proclaiming the Name First, Then Renaming (17:1–8)

### 1.1 "When Abram Was Ninety-Nine Years Old": The Arithmetic of Thirteen Years of Silence (17:1)

The text begins by stating his age: "When Abram was ninety-nine years old, the LORD appeared to Abram."

Look at the arithmetic: he was eighty-six when Ishmael was born (Gen. 16:16), and now he is ninety-nine—thirteen years. Over these thirteen years, Ishmael grew from an infant into a youth, and Abram had every reason to think: surely this child must be the promised seed; surely this is how the promise is fulfilled. It is all too easy for people to scale down their expectations of God during periods of silence: not by losing faith altogether, but by quietly shrinking the promise until it fits the size of reality.

Yet God chose precisely this moment to appear. Had He waited just one more year, Abram would have been one hundred. Brothers and sisters, God’s timetable often presses right up against the limits of human possibility—not because He is late, but because He wants the word "Almighty" to be utterly unmixed with human effort. For a ninety-nine-year-old to father a child is a dead end for man; for Almighty God, it is the very setting in which to proclaim His name.

### 1.2 "I Am Almighty God": El Shaddai and "Walk Before Me and Be Blameless" (17:1–2)

God introduces Himself: "I am Almighty God; walk before Me and be blameless."

"Almighty God" in Hebrew is אֵל שַׁדַּי (*El Shadday*, H410 *El*, God + H7706 *Shadday*, the Almighty) [1]. This is the very first time the title "Almighty God" appears in the Bible, and it occurs precisely where human strength reaches its absolute end. God first reveals His own name before laying requirements on man—the exact same order seen in Genesis 15: first who He is, then what man should do.

The word "blameless" in "be blameless" is the Hebrew תָּמִים (*tamim*, H8549: blameless, unblemished, complete, whole) [2]; to walk "before Me" in the original means to walk back and forth in God’s presence, signifying intimacy, unashamed transparency, and continuous fellowship. This is not a call for Abram to attain perfection through his own moral striving—a ninety-nine-year-old man who had just stumbled in the matter of Hagar could never produce perfection on his own. What God means is: simply walk before Me with wholeness, hiding nothing, withholding nothing, not living half for Me and half for yourself; the power to fulfill the promise resides in My name, not in your age.

In Exodus 6, when God recalled to Moses how He had appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, it was by this very name, "Almighty God" (Exod. 6:3, summary)—it was as this Almighty One that the patriarchs came to know Him during their most unpromising days.

### 1.3 "Your Name Shall Be Abraham": Renaming Before the Transformation (17:4–8)

God continued to establish the covenant: "You shall be a father of many nations. No longer shall your name be called Abram, but your name shall be Abraham; for I have made you a father of many nations."

"Abram," Hebrew אַבְרָם (*Avram*, H87, exalted father); "Abraham," Hebrew אַבְרָהָם (*Avraham*, H85, father of many nations, related to the meaning of "father of a multitude")[3]. Notice the tense: God says, "I have made you a father of many nations"—it is "have made," not "will make." Yet what was the present reality? He had only one son, Ishmael; there was not even a trace of the promised Isaac yet. From then on, every time someone called him "Abraham," it was a reminder: God calls you according to what He will accomplish, not according to your present circumstance.

God even stated the covenant to its very ultimate end: "And I will establish My covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you in their generations, for an everlasting covenant, to be God to you and your descendants after you." The core of the covenant is not the land, nor the number of descendants, but "to be God to you"—relationship precedes inheritance. The land of Canaan would be given to his descendants as an everlasting possession (Gen. 17:8, paraphrase), but land can change hands; God being his God remains forever unchanged.

Concerning those who know the name of God, Psalm 91 promises: "Because he has set his love upon Me, therefore I will deliver him; I will set him on high, because he has known My name" (Ps. 91:14, paraphrase). On this day, Abraham first learned God's new name—El Shaddai, God Almighty—and only then received his own new name. Knowing God's name comes first, receiving one's own name comes second; this order remains the same today.

To summarize Part One in a single sentence: **After thirteen years of silence, God appeared by the name of the Almighty, first establishing an everlasting covenant, and then changing "exalted father" to "father of many nations"—God calls man according to what He will accomplish; before the change has even taken place, the name arrives first; what man is to do is to walk before Him completely, holding nothing back.**

## (24:00 - 42:00) Part Two: Circumcision as a Sign—Belonging Inscribed upon the Flesh (17:9–14)

### 2.1 "It Shall Be a Sign of the Covenant Between Me and You": The Sign Is Appointed by God, Not Conceived by Man (17:9–11)

With the covenant established, God immediately gave the sign: "Every male child among you shall be circumcised... and it shall be a sign of the covenant between Me and you."

"Circumcision," Hebrew verb מוּל (*mul*, H4135, to cut, to circumcise)[4]; "covenant," Hebrew בְּרִית (*berit*, H1285, covenant, alliance)[5], the same word as the covenant "cut" in Genesis 15. In Genesis 15, the covenant was established when God passed through the pieces of flesh alone; in Genesis 17, however, the sign required human participation: God bore the responsibility of keeping the covenant, while man was to receive the seal of the covenant in the flesh. Every time a child was circumcised, this household recited anew: We are a covenant people, and our God is a covenant-keeping God.

The location of the sign is also significant: inscribed upon the organ of reproductive capacity, it was equivalent to submitting the very channel of promise to the governance of the covenant—wherever descendants would come from, the seal of the covenant was stamped there first. This was not a religious ritual invented by Abraham; it was ordained by God: the content, scope, and timing of the sign were all determined by the covenant-making Party.

### 2.2 "He Who Is Eight Days Old" and "Bought with Money from Any Foreigner": The Scope of the Sign (17:12–13)

God drew the scope very broadly: "He who is eight days old among you shall be circumcised, every male child in your generations, he who is born in your house or bought with money from any foreigner who is not your descendant."

Two points must be seen. First, an eight-day-old infant understands nothing and has done nothing, yet the sign comes upon him first—belonging comes before understanding; the seal comes before the lesson. Second, the sign transcended bloodline: those born in the house and foreigners bought with money both received the exact same sign. From day one, the household of the covenant was determined not merely by blood, but by belonging. This line runs all the way into the New Testament: Paul says that neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but a new creation (Gal. 6:15, paraphrase); the external form of the sign would later give way to the inner reality of the heart, but God's intention to "demarcate a people of belonging by a sign" remains consistent from Genesis 17 to the New Testament.

### 2.3 "Shall Be Cut Off from His People": The Sign Is Not an Ornament, but the Boundary of the Covenant (17:14)

"And the uncircumcised male child... shall be cut off from his people; he has broken My covenant."

The words are spoken severely because the sign carries great weight. To despise the sign is to despise the covenant itself. Brothers and sisters, the way this passage speaks to us today must shift a level: the sign of the New Covenant is no longer physical circumcision—Colossians says that believers were also circumcised in Christ by putting off the body of the sins of the flesh, being buried with Him in baptism (Col. 2:11–12, paraphrase). The form has changed, but the boundary remains: whether one has the seal of the covenant, whether one has the pledge of belonging, is clear in one's own heart, and even clearer to God. We must not treat the sign as an ornament, nor have the name of belonging while practicing covenant-breaking in deed.

Here we must guard against a misreading: circumcision was not a merit that bought the covenant. Abraham was accounted righteous when he believed in Genesis 15, when there was not yet circumcision. In Romans 4, Paul deliberately makes the sequence clear: Abraham received circumcision as "a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while still uncircumcised" (Rom. 4:11, paraphrase)[10]—first righteousness, then the seal; first faith, then the sign. The sign seals grace already accomplished; it does not produce grace. If this order is inverted, the gospel becomes a transaction, and the Bible is the very first to prevent that.

To summarize Part Two in a single sentence: **Circumcision is the sign of the covenant appointed by God: an eight-day-old infant is sealed first, and foreigners are included in the same sign; to break the sign is to break the covenant; yet the seal always follows faith—Genesis 15 first accounts him righteous, and only in Genesis 17 is the seal affixed; the sign speaks of belonging, not of merit.**

## (42:00 - 52:00) Part Three: Sarah and Isaac—Renaming, Laughter, and That Very Day's Obedience (17:15–27)

### 3.1 "Her Name Shall Be Sarah": The Promise Writes the Wife in as Well (17:15–16)

God's word turned to Sarai: "As for Sarai your wife, you shall not call her name Sarai, but Sarah shall be her name. And I will bless her and also give you a son by her; then I will bless her, and she shall be a mother of nations; kings of peoples shall be from her."

"Sarai," Hebrew שָׂרַי (*Saray*, H8297); "Sarah," Hebrew שָׂרָה (*Sarah*, H8283, princess, noble lady; the meaning of mother of nations is connected with this name)[6]. After waiting so many years, and having stumbled in the matter of Hagar, God did not cross Sarai out of the promise. Instead, He renamed her and gave her a place: the mother of nations. On the path to fulfilling the promise, God restores people and also uses people—He not only changed the name of the master of the house, but He also wrote Sarai, who was both hurt and hurtful, right into the covenant. Twice He said, "I will bless her," sandwiched around "give you a son by her": the son had to come from her; the backup plan from thirteen years earlier would not do, and not a single path bypassing Sarah was viable.

### 3.2 Falling on His Face, Laughing, and Interceding: A Father's Heart, First Seeking Ishmael's Wellbeing (17:17–21)

Abraham's reaction was very real: "Then Abraham fell on his face and laughed, and said in his heart, 'Shall a child be born to a man who is one hundred years old? And shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bear a child?'"

"Laughed," in the original text, is related to צָחַק (*tzachaq*, H6711, to laugh, rejoice)[7]—the name of Isaac, יִצְחָק (*Yitzchaq*, H3327, he laughs), comes precisely from this root[8]. In this laugh, there was astonishment, there was disbelief, and there was irrepressible joy: falling on his face was reverence, laughing was human nature; the Bible records both. God did not rebuke this laughter, and later He even inscribed laughter into the child's name—God is not afraid that man laughs at His promise as being hard to believe; what He fears is that man does not even laugh, but simply turns around and walks away.

Immediately, Abraham interceded: "Oh, that Ishmael might live before You!" As a father, before the promised son had yet arrived, he first cared for the son right before his eyes. God's response was full of grace and completely unequivocal: "No, Sarah your wife shall bear you a son, and you shall call his name Isaac; I will establish My covenant with him... And as for Ishmael, I have heard you. Behold, I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful... He shall beget twelve princes, and I will make him a great nation." Ishmael received a great blessing, a great nation, and twelve princes; Isaac received the covenant. God did not mistreat Ishmael because of establishing the covenant, nor did He displace the covenant because of His affection for Ishmael—intercession was granted, the promise was not discounted, and in God's hands these two never conflict.

"At this set time next year, Sarah shall bear to you Isaac." After words without a timetable for thirteen years, this time it was given in full: next year, at this set time. Once God's silence ended, even the date was given crystal clear.

### 3.3 "That Very Same Day": Obedience Does Not Wait Overnight (17:23-27)

The chapter ends with action: "So Abraham took Ishmael his son, all who were born in his house and all who were bought with his money, every male among the men of Abraham’s house, and circumcised the flesh of their foreskins that very same day, as God had said to him."

The phrase "that very same day" is Abraham's spiritual fingerprint. A ninety-nine-year-old man circumcising every male in the household, including himself—pain, risk, shame, not one lacking; there were a hundred reasons to delay. He did not use a single one: God had spoken, so it was done that very day. This was the very same person as the hands that drove away the birds of prey in Gen. 15, and the feet that set out to battle before nightfall in Gen. 14: when he heard God's word, his action was faster than excuses.

The text concludes by recording the ages and the scope once more: Abraham was ninety-nine years old, Ishmael was thirteen years old, and all the men of the house were circumcised with him (Gen. 17:24-27, paraphrased summary). The picture of a ninety-nine-year-old man leading his entire household to receive the sign is the finest conclusion to this chapter: a new name is not mere talk; the person with the new name lives out the new sign that very day.

This is today's spark, and the call of the entire sermon.

Brothers and sisters, your new name may have been called for many years: a saved person, a child of God, a covenant people. You may be called that, but are you still living out the old name? The example Abraham gives us is: God changes the name before the transformation; man obeys within that very day. Today, not tomorrow—that very thing God gives you in Gen. 17: a habit that must be broken, a sign that must be observed, an allegiance that must be acknowledged—do it today, that very same day. At ninety-nine, he did not say it was too late; your age is even less of an excuse. Almighty God has already proclaimed His name, the everlasting covenant has been established, and now it is your turn: lay down the old name, and according to the new name, take the first step today.

To sum up the third part in one sentence: **Sarai is renamed Sarah; the unborn son is named Isaac beforehand; Abraham falls on his face and laughs, yet intercedes for Ishmael; God blesses both sons but establishes the covenant with Isaac; on that very same day, the whole household receives the sign—the new name comes first, obedience follows that very day, and laughter is ultimately inscribed into the name of the son of promise.**

## (52:00 - 58:00) Application: Six Categories of People

Today's chapter speaks to six categories of people:

**To Seekers:** Know another name of God: Almighty God. He appears when a person is ninety-nine years old and it seems too late for everything, telling you: He does not work based on your qualifications, but on His almightiness. If you feel that your life has already been set and it is too late, Gen. 17 was written precisely for people like you. Believe today first that He is Almighty God; as for the order of change, He will lead you step by step.

**To New Believers:** Having received a new name, you must receive the new sign and live in a new manner. Baptism is not merely a ritual; it is the seal of belonging, just as circumcision was to Abraham's household. Learn his obedience "that very same day": regarding the things God moves you to do, do not put them off until next week—what can be changed today, change today; what can be acknowledged today, acknowledge today. The seal follows faith, and your faith must have a sign testifying to it daily.

**To Longtime Believers:** Beware of "lowering expectations" during the thirteen years of silence: unconsciously scaling the promise down to the size of reality, leaving your faith as mere maintenance. God re-proclaims His name to you today: "I am Almighty God." Adjust your lowered expectations back up, and lay aside those make-do ways of living from the old name. Walking with integrity before God matters far more than the number of years you have served.

**To Parents:** Learn Abraham's intercession: he did not despise the Ishmael before him simply because the promised son was coming; instead, he first pleaded for him: "Oh, that Ishmael might live before You!" Bring every child in your family before God in prayer—those who bring joy, those who bring distress, those who came according to plan, and those who were unplanned. Also, impart the covenant sign and memory to your children early on: let them know from childhood whose house they belong to, and to which God they belong.

**To Those Striving in the Workplace:** A name is identity and positioning. The labels the world attaches to you—age, résumé, ceiling—are not your name; God calls you according to what He is going to accomplish. Do not sell yourself short to reality during periods of silence: thirteen years without movement does not mean thirteen wasted years. Fulfill your duty with integrity and walk before God; when the time is fulfilled, Almighty God will proclaim His name and accomplish it Himself.

**To Those Currently in Suffering:** The thirteen years of silence is the most comforting part of this chapter for you: God not speaking does not mean God is not working; when the revelation comes, He even gives the date clearly. If your situation is right in the midst of those thirteen years, first hold fast to this proclaimed phrase: "I am Almighty God." When human methods have reached their end, His name has only just begun. "At this set time next year" is His word; write it down first, and wait patiently for its fulfillment.

## (58:00 - 60:00) Conclusion · Response Prayer

Let us review today's three statements:

First, "I am Almighty God"—after thirteen years of silence, God proclaims His name, re-establishes the everlasting covenant, changes Abram's name to Abraham, and calls him according to what He is going to accomplish;
Second, circumcision as the sign—the evidence of the covenant is borne in the flesh, bringing in both the eight-day-old infant and the foreigner; the sign follows the righteousness already accomplished, speaking of belonging, not merit;
Third, Sarah and Isaac—the wife is renamed the mother of nations, the unborn son is named beforehand, the father's intercession is granted, and that very same day the entire household obeys and receives the sign.

Now let us bow our heads and respond to today's word in prayer.

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. You are Almighty God, who appeared to Abraham when he was ninety-nine years old, changed his name, established an everlasting covenant, and gave him the strength to obey on that very day. Forgive our hearts for lowering our expectations during the silence, and once again call our future by Your almightiness; teach us, having received a new name, to live out the new sign, doing what ought to be done this very day without further delay. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

Amen.

## Chapter Notes

[1] El Shadday: Hebrew אֵל (*El*, H410, God, Mighty One) combined with שַׁדַּי (*Shadday*, H7706, Almighty); Gen. 17:1, "I am Almighty God," marks the first occurrence of this divine title in the Bible; definitions according to BDB, HALOT. Its etymology is still debated in scholarship; this manuscript adopts the common translation "Almighty God."

[2] tamim: Hebrew תָּמִים (*tamim*, H8549), blameless, without blemish, complete, upright; Gen. 17:1, "and be blameless"; definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[3] Avram and Avraham: Hebrew אַבְרָם (*Avram*, H87, Abram, exalted father) and אַבְרָהָם (*Avraham*, H85, Abraham); in Gen. 17:5, God Himself interprets its meaning as "father of many nations"; definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[4] mul: Hebrew מוּל (*mul*, H4135), to circumcise, cut off; Gen. 17:10-11, "shall be circumcised"; definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[5] *berit*: Hebrew בְּרִית (H1285), covenant, alliance; Gen. 17:2, 7, 10, etc., "make a covenant, an everlasting covenant"; lexical definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[6] *Saray* and *Sarah*: Hebrew שָׂרַי (*Saray*, H8297, Sarai) and שָׂרָה (*Sarah*, H8283, Sarah, meaning princess, noblewoman); name changed in Gen. 17:15, and 17:16 promises that she shall be a mother of nations; lexical definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[7] *tzachaq*: Hebrew צָחַק (H6711), to laugh, rejoice, play; Gen. 17:17 "Abraham fell on his face and laughed"; lexical definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[8] *Yitzchaq*: Hebrew יִצְחָק (H3327), Isaac, meaning "he laughs," sharing the same root with *tzachaq* (H6711); Gen. 17:19 God commands that his name be called Isaac; lexical definitions according to BDB, HALOT.

[9] Regarding Gen. 17:1 "Almighty God": Chinese Union Version rendering; English comparisons: KJV reads "I am the Almighty God"; NIV reads "I am God Almighty"; NRSV is substantially the same (English quotations transcribed according to standard publicly available texts). Lu Zhenzhong's version was consulted for this verse, though its translation is not directly cited in this manuscript.

[10] Regarding the order of justification and circumcision (Rom. 4:11): the Chinese Union Version renders the meaning as the circumcision Abraham received was a seal of the righteousness of the faith which he had while uncircumcised; Part 2 of this message affirms on this basis that "first comes righteousness, then comes the seal"; cf. the sequential citation of Gen. 15:6 and Gen. 17 throughout the entirety of Romans 4.
