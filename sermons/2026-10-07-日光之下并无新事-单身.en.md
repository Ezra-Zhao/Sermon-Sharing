# Nothing New Under the Sun: Singleness

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：单身».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of singleness

**Summary:** The deepest anxiety of singleness is often not the absence of a partner, but placing completeness, security, and self-worth in marriage. This chapter begins with three wrong longings, and points out that human love is incomplete; only God's everlasting love can satisfy a person. It also gives concrete steps for facing the anxiety of singleness: godliness with contentment, acknowledging limitations, giving thanks in everything, entrusting the future to God, and seeking first His kingdom and His righteousness.

The Preacher says there is nothing new under the sun. The anxiety of singleness — the ancients had it, and people today have it too. The material takes this anxiety apart and finds that underneath it are hidden three longings, and in each longing is mixed a wrong understanding. Correct the understanding, and the anxiety loosens.

The key Scripture is Jeremiah 31:3:

> "The LORD has appeared of old to me, saying: 'Yes, I have loved you with an everlasting love; therefore with lovingkindness I have drawn you.'" (Jer. 31:3)

"I have loved you with an everlasting love" — here "love" in Hebrew is **ahavah (אַהֲבָה)** (original text אַהֲבָה, transliterated ahavah; Strong H160; according to the *BDB Lexicon*, "love"). God's love is not a momentary emotion; it is "everlasting love": with no beginning of calculation and no end of term. The love people give changes; God's love does not change. This is the foundation of everything discussed in this chapter.

## I. The Longing for Unconditional Love: Human Love Is Incomplete, God's Love Is Everlasting

The first wrong understanding is: "I need to get married in order to feel that my life is complete, secure, and loved." It sounds very natural, yet it places marriage in a position it cannot bear.

The right understanding is: All human love is incomplete. However good a spouse may be, he is a limited person; he will tire, he will misunderstand, and there will be parts he cannot give. If we press the whole hope of "completeness" onto one person, before marriage has even begun it will already be carrying a burden destined for disappointment. What we are to rely on is God's perfect, unconditional, eternal love — the kind spoken of in Jeremiah 31:3. Only after being caught by such love can a person love others out of fullness, rather than demanding out of lack.

## II. The Longing for Self-Worth: Satisfaction Comes from Obedience and Growth

The second wrong understanding is: "I must get married for my life to have meaning and purpose." It is as if the days of singleness are only a waiting area, and life only officially begins after marriage.

The correction given in the material is very direct: Only when a person grows in obedience to God's will and shapes his character after the example of Jesus will he experience true satisfaction. Paul says:

> "For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brethren." (Rom. 8:29)

The meaning of life is not in a change of status, but in the growth of character. Singleness is not a blank period; it is a shaping period. When we recognize our identity in Christ, our worth has a root. The Bible says: "The Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we are children of God." (Rom. 8:16) The identity of God's children does not need marriage to be issued as a supplement.

## III. The Longing for Security: Security Comes from Intimacy with God

The third wrong understanding is: "I need a partner to make me feel secure." The right understanding is: The only lasting security comes from an intimate relationship with God. Deuteronomy says of Benjamin:

> "The beloved of the LORD shall dwell in safety by Him, who shelters him all the day long; and he shall dwell between His shoulders." (Deut. 33:12)

The ground for "dwelling in safety" is that the LORD shelters him all the day long, not that someone is beside him. A partner can accompany, but cannot shelter; can share the burden, but cannot become the foundation. Build security on the relationship with God, and though circumstances change, the foundation does not move. First Peter also says: "Casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you." (1 Pet. 5:7) The One who cares for us is God Himself.

## IV. Steps for Dealing with the Anxiety of Singleness

Once the understanding is corrected, there must also be daily practice. The material gives several steps, all plain and concrete.

First, **read God's word repeatedly, and be content and joyful**. Second Peter says: "As His divine power has given to us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of Him who called us by glory and virtue." (2 Pet. 1:3) The things that pertain to life and godliness have already been given. Paul also says: "Now godliness with contentment is great gain." (1 Tim. 6:6) Godliness with contentment is the most practical wealth of single life. First Corinthians chapter 7 discusses singleness and marriage in the whole chapter, and is worth reading repeatedly and chewing over slowly.

Second, **acknowledge difficulties, and acknowledge human limitations**. Jeremiah says: "O LORD, I know the way of man is not in himself; it is not in man who walks to direct his own steps." (Jer. 10:23) That steps are not directed by oneself is not negativity; it is laying down the desire to control. The material also reminds: What one cannot obtain is not necessarily good. Paul says on marriage: "But if you marry, you have not sinned; and if a virgin marries, she has not sinned. Nevertheless such will have trouble in the flesh, but I would spare you." (1 Cor. 7:28) Marriage has its sweetness, and also its trouble; singleness has its difficulties, and also its freedom — there is no need to imagine the grass on the other side is all green.

Third, **have a thankful heart**. "Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, in everything give thanks; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you." (1 Thess. 5:16-18) Thankfulness is the most direct remedy for anxiety: when the eyes are fixed on the grace already given, the heart is not easily led away by what is lacking.

Fourth, **entrust the future to God, and seek opportunities to serve**. Romans chapter 12 says that we, being many, are one body in Christ, and individually members of one another, having gifts differing according to the grace given to us — whether prophecy, ministry, teaching, or exhortation — each should be done with singleness of purpose; it also says, "Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation, continuing steadfastly in prayer" (Rom. 12:12). The years of singleness are not a waiting room; they are a field of service. The Lord Jesus said: "But seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness, and all these things shall be added to you." (Matt. 6:33) Seek first the kingdom and righteousness, and God Himself will add the rest in His time.

## Conclusion

Singleness is not a defect, nor is it a punishment. Entrust completeness to marriage, and marriage cannot bear it; entrust security to a partner, and a partner cannot give it in full. God loves us with everlasting love; this love first catches us, and only then can we live steadily in any circumstance, and only then, in the future, whether or not we enter marriage, can we love others out of abundance rather than grasping out of thirst.

## Prayer of Response

Lord, You love me with everlasting love — this is not a word of comfort, it is a fact. Forgive me for the heart that entrusted completeness, security, and worth to marriage, and let me first be satisfied in Your love. Teach me godliness with contentment, to give thanks in everything, to entrust my steps to You, and to seek first Your kingdom and Your righteousness. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I pray, Amen.

## Notes

1. The structure of this chapter is based on the 2019 compiled and edited material: the section on singleness is divided into three groups contrasting wrong and right understandings — "the longing for unconditional love, the longing for self-worth, the longing for security" — with an attached "Anxiety of singleness — steps to resolution." At the end the original manuscript raises the question, "What is God's purpose in letting us date?" without expansion; it belongs within the scope of the "Dating" chapter of this series, and this chapter does not answer it in its place.
2. Hebrew word note: ahavah (אַהֲבָה), Strong H160, according to Brown-Driver-Briggs, *A Hebrew and English Lexicon of the Old Testament* (BDB), meaning "love"; parenthetical notes in the text of Jer. 31:3 such as "in the original, 'me'" are retained as in the Chinese Union Version original.
