## Executive Summary (approx. 100 words)

In Genesis chapter 9, God blesses Noah and his sons, repeating after the Flood the Chapter 1 blessing to "be fruitful and multiply." God values life, setting boundaries even regarding blood. God takes the initiative to establish a covenant with humanity and all living creatures, promising never again to destroy all flesh by a flood. He places the rainbow in the clouds, not merely for humans to see, but as a sign for Him to remember. This message explores three truths: blessing starts anew upon the ruins; establishing a covenant means God binds Himself first; and the rainbow shows the bow set down—after judgment, God hung remembrance in the heavens for all to see.

## (00:00 - 08:00) Introduction · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Peace be with you all, brothers and sisters!

In our last sermon, we finished Genesis chapter 8. When Noah stepped out of the ark, his first act was not building a house, but building an altar. The Lord smelled the soothing aroma and said in His heart: "I will never again curse the ground for man's sake."

Today we turn to Chapter 9. That word spoken in God's heart is now formally voiced, ratified into a covenant, and marked with a sign hung in the heavens.

What was the world like after the Flood? The earth was desolate, only one human family remained, and living creatures had to multiply from scratch. In such moments, the thought that most easily surfaces in the human heart is: Is it too late? Can we really start over? God's response at the beginning of Chapter 9 is not to settle old scores, but first to bestow His blessing.

Let us read the key passages together:

"So God blessed Noah and his sons, and said to them: 'Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth.'" (Gen. 9:1)

"'Every moving thing that lives shall be food for you. I have given you all things, even as the green herbs. But you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood.'" (Gen. 9:3-4)

"'Surely for your lifeblood I will demand a reckoning; from the hand of every beast I will require it, and from the hand of man. From the hand of every man's brother I will require the life of man. Whoever sheds man's blood, by man his blood shall be shed; for in the image of God He made man.'" (Gen. 9:5-6, excerpted)

"'And as for Me, behold, I establish My covenant with you and with your descendants after you, and with every living creature that is with you... Thus I establish My covenant with you: Never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of the flood; never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth.'" (Gen. 9:9-11, excerpted)

"'I set My rainbow in the cloud, and it shall be for the sign of the covenant between Me and the earth... The rainbow shall be in the cloud, and I will look on it to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth.'" (Gen. 9:13, 16, excerpted)

Today, we will examine three parts:

First, 9:1-7, the blessing is spoken once more—fruitfulness and multiplication return, while boundaries for life are simultaneously established;
Second, 9:8-11, God covenants with every living creature—the covenant is initiated by God, and humanity is not its only beneficiary;
Third, 9:12-17, the rainbow as a sign—God sees the rainbow and remembers; the bow is hung in the clouds, and judgment is not allowed to have the final word.

Let us bow our heads in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. When the floodwaters receded, You did not govern this world through terror; You began anew with blessing, with a covenant, and with a rainbow. We ask that You teach us today to hear Your blessing upon the ruins, to lift our eyes after the storm to behold Your sign, and to trust that the covenant You established is one that You Yourself will remember. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

## (08:00 - 24:00) Part 1: The Blessing Spoken Anew upon the Ruins (9:1-7)

### 1.1 "So God blessed Noah and his sons": The Words of Chapter 1 Spoken Again

The opening verse of Chapter 9 dovetails seamlessly with Chapter 1: "So God blessed Noah and his sons, and said to them: 'Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth.'" (Gen. 9:1)

"Be fruitful and multiply" in Hebrew is *peru urevu*, from *parah* (佩拉, H6509, to bear fruit, be fruitful) and *ravah* (拉瓦, H7235, to multiply, flourish) [1]. In Genesis 1:28, God used this exact phrase to bless the first human couple. Though the Flood swept over the entire earth, God did not swap in a scaled-down, make-do plan. What He spoke to Noah's family was the very same word He spoke to mankind at the beginning.

Brothers and sisters, hear this clearly: God's blessing is not nullified by human failure. People may make a mess of things, and floodwaters may sweep the earth bare, but God's original purpose has never changed. He still desires this earth to be filled with the humanity He created, and He still entrusts the mandate to rule over the earth and care for living creatures back into human hands (cf. Gen. 9:2).

Warren W. Wiersbe, in his exposition of Genesis 9 in his commentary, reminds readers that the post-flood blessing is a reaffirmation of the creation blessing; the new world is not one of lowered standards, but one that continues to operate according to God's original order. (cf. Warren W. Wiersbe, *The Bible Exposition Commentary: Genesis*)

Here is an immediate application: If your life feels as though it has been swept away by a flood—whether through divorce, severe illness, business failure, or spiritual coldness—never assume that all God has left for you is a compromised version. In Chapter 9, He teaches us: Starting over is not about living on scraps; it is about hearing the original words of blessing spoken once more.

### 1.2 "But you shall not eat flesh with its life... its blood": Life Is So Precious That Even Eating Has Boundaries

In verse 3, God broadens the scope of their food: every moving thing that lives may serve as food, just like the green herbs. Yet verse 4 immediately sets a boundary: "But you shall not eat flesh with its life, that is, its blood."

"Blood" is the Hebrew *dam* (达姆, H1818, blood) [2]; "life" is *nephesh* (内弗什, H5315, life, living creature, soul) [3]. The biblical logic is this: blood represents life, and life belongs to God. Humans may make use of created things, but they must never treat life as an ownerless commodity to be disposed of at will. Eating is permitted; disregarding life is not.

This boundary is later articulated even more thoroughly in Leviticus: "For the life of the flesh is in the blood" (Lev. 17:11). In Acts 15, among the instructions given by the Jerusalem Council to Gentile believers, the prohibition against consuming blood was preserved (cf. Acts 15:20, 29). From Genesis to the New Testament, Scripture maintains a consistent posture toward life: life is not a consumer good; it is God's possession.

### 1.3 "Whoever sheds man's blood": Safeguarding Life Means Safeguarding the Image of God

Verse 6 applies this boundary to human beings with the utmost gravity: "Whoever sheds man's blood, by man his blood shall be shed; for in the image of God He made man."

Pay close attention to the reason. Scripture prohibits murder not merely because society requires order, but because man bears the image of God (Gen. 1:26-27). To harm a human being is to strike out against God's image. This foundation predates the legal codes of any era and runs deeper than any human jurisprudence.

Proverbs 6 lists things the Lord hates, among which are "hands that shed innocent blood" (Prov. 6:16-17, excerpted). Psalm 139 declares that God cares for unborn life in the womb: "You covered me in my mother's womb" (Ps. 139:13, excerpted). From a boundary on diet to a solemn decree against bloodshed, the first half of Chapter 9 establishes a matter of supreme importance: the foundation of the new world is reverence for life.

A person is precious not because they are useful, but because they are created in the image of God. This worth neither increases nor diminishes with outward circumstances.

To summarize this first part in one sentence: **The first lesson after the Flood is not to rush to claim territory, but to listen anew to the blessing and show renewed reverence for life—the blessing is repeated by God, and the boundary is established by God Himself; embracing both together is the only way to live in the new world.**

## (24:00 - 42:00) Part 2: God Establishes a Covenant with Every Living Creature (9:8-11)

### 2.1 "I establish My covenant with you": God Is the One Taking the Initiative

In verse 9, God says: "And as for Me, behold, I establish My covenant with you and with your descendants after you."

"Covenant" is the Hebrew *berith* (贝里特, H1285, covenant, alliance) [4]. In Genesis, whenever God establishes a covenant, the initiating party is always God. It was not that Noah performed well by offering sacrifices and God grudgingly signed an agreement; rather, God smelled the soothing aroma, resolved it within Himself, and spoke of His own accord. What Noah's family needed to do regarding this covenant was primarily to receive it, remember it, and live accordingly.

Brothers and sisters, much anxiety in the faith comes from getting the order reversed: always fearing that if we fail to keep it, God will stop keeping it too. The covenant in chapter 9 sets the order straight first: the foundation of the covenant rests on God's faithfulness, not on human stability. In Isaiah 54:9–10, God Himself brings up the Noahic covenant as an analogy: “For as I have sworn that the waters of Noah would no longer cover the earth... My kindness shall not depart from you, nor shall My covenant of peace be removed.” The One who made the covenant remembers it, and the One who keeps the covenant is also Him.

### 2.2 The Parties to the Covenant: Even the Living Creatures Are Included

The detail most easily overlooked in this covenant is the list of beneficiaries.

“and with every living creature that is with you: the birds, the cattle, and every beast of the earth with you, of all that go out of the ark, every beast of the earth.” (Gen. 9:10)

When God makes a covenant, it is not only with mankind, but also with every living creature. Birds, cattle, and wild beasts are all within the promise. The Creator's heart is far broader than man's. After being saved, humans are most prone to caring only about themselves, yet on the very first day of salvation, God reminded Noah: this world was not reopened just for your family alone; the living creatures are in the covenant as well. As you steward them, you must do so with covenantal awe.

Psalm 145:9 sings of this: “The LORD is good to all, and His tender mercies are over all His works.” Psalm 36:6 says: “O LORD, You preserve man and beast.” The Noahic covenant is the root of these Psalms: from the day the covenant was made, God’s mercy covered far more than just human beings.

### 2.3 “Never Again Shall All Flesh Be Cut Off by the Waters of the Flood”: The Specificity of the Promise Brings Peace to the Human Heart

In verse 11, the promise becomes very concrete: “Never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of the flood; never again shall there be a flood to destroy the earth.”

God did not promise that from then on there would be no rain, no hardship, no tears. He promised this one thing: a flood-style wipeout will never come again. The earth will still experience cold and heat; human beings will still go through birth, old age, sickness, and death, gain and loss, success and failure. But the end of the world will no longer be swallowed up by water, and the end of mankind will no longer be wiped out by condemnation.

When 2 Peter 3 looks back at the flood, it says that the world of that time perished, being flooded with water, but the heavens and the earth which are now preserved by God's word are reserved for fire, awaiting the new heavens and new earth He promised (cf. 2 Pet. 3:5–7, 13). The Noahic covenant is the first guarantee: God would rather bind Himself by a covenant than determine to use extinction as the end again.

Let us ignite this truth for a moment.

Brothers and sisters, some of you are sitting here today, and the sentence you fear most in your heart is: *It’s over, everything is going to be ruined again.* Another fight in your marriage—is it going to be ruined again? Another crisis with your child—is it going to be ruined again? Another bad medical report—is it going to be ruined again?

Listen to this word in Genesis 9:11: *Never again.* Never again cut off by the waters of the flood. God’s own “never again” carries far more weight than the hundred times your heart cries, “It’s over!” Today—not tomorrow—receive that “never again” today, and place it right over the very thing you fear most. The rain will still fall, but the flood is no longer your end; the waters will still rise, but the covenant made in the ark has not been revoked. The God of the covenant has not gone back on His word; do not go back on it for Him.

To sum up the second part in one sentence: **The covenant was initiated by God, its scope is broad enough to include every living creature, and its promise is as concrete as “never again wiped out”—the security of the human heart is not built on the weather, but on the faithfulness of the Covenant-Maker.**

## (42:00 - 52:00) Part Three: The Rainbow in the Cloud—God Looks upon It and Remembers (9:12–17)

### 3.1 “I Set My Rainbow in the Cloud”: The Sign Is Given by God, Not Manufactured by Man

Verses 12 and 13: “And God said: ‘This is the sign of the covenant which I make between Me and you, and every living creature that is with you, for perpetual generations: I set My rainbow in the cloud, and it shall be for the sign of the covenant between Me and the earth.’”

“Sign,” in Hebrew *oth* (H226, sign, mark)[5], is the very same word used for the mark of Cain in chapter 4. Cain’s sign stopped vigilante vengeance; the rainbow’s sign stopped the flood. One was placed on a face, the other hung in the sky; one protected a murderer, the other protects the entire world. When God establishes a sign, it has always been to halt destruction.

“Rainbow,” in Hebrew *qesheth* (H7198, bow)[6]. Notice that the original word is simply “bow”—a warrior’s bow. In the original text, a rainbow is not a decorative ribbon; it is a battle bow. Many biblical scholars point out this dimension: God hung His bow in the clouds. Hanging up the bow means the battle is over; with the bow pointing upward and not aimed toward the earth, the weapon of judgment has been put away.

Ezekiel, by the river of Babylon seeing the vision of the glory of God, said: “Like the appearance of a rainbow in a cloud on a rainy day, so was the appearance of the brightness all around it” (Ezek. 1:28, excerpt); Revelation 4:3 writes of the heavenly throne, “and there was a rainbow around the throne, in appearance like an emerald.” From Genesis to Revelation, the rainbow consistently encircles the space between God's throne and the earth: it reminds us that the One seated on the throne remembers the covenant first.

### 3.2 “When I See It, I Will Remember”: God Himself Is the First to Remember

The most moving dimension of verses 15 and 16 is often read past: “The rainbow shall be in the cloud, and I will look on it to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living creature of all flesh that is on the earth.” (Gen. 9:16)

The sign was not given primarily to bolster man's courage; it was spoken by God for His own remembering. Humans erect monuments because they fear they will forget; God set the rainbow to declare to mankind: *I will not forget. I have hung My remembrance in the public sky, so that every time it rains, whenever man looks up, he runs right into My remembrance.*

In chapter 8, we explained that *zakar*, “to remember,” means God acting in accordance with His covenant. Chapter 9 turns that remembrance into something visible. From that moment, God’s remembrance took on a form: on rainy days, before the clouds have even scattered, the bow is already hung out. The memory of judgment and the remembrance of mercy share the very same sky, but mercy is hung in the far more prominent place.

The sign of the Noahic covenant demonstrates that the initiative lies with God. Remembering and keeping covenant are both actions God takes first; human beings simply respond within God's faithfulness.

### 3.3 The Everlasting Covenant: From Noah to Christ

Verse 16 calls this covenant an “everlasting covenant.” The Noahic covenant upholds the whole earth and every living creature. It has not yet reached the step of the forgiveness of sins—that would wait for God’s covenant with Abraham and its fulfillment in Christ. But the direction was already set: God chose covenant, not annihilation, as His way of relating to the world.

When the Lord Jesus instituted the Lord's Supper, He said: “This cup is the new covenant in My blood” (1 Cor. 11:25, excerpt; cf. Luke 22:20). The covenant of the rainbow guarantees that the earth will never again be destroyed by water; the covenant of Christ’s blood guarantees that those who believe in Him will never again be destroyed by sin. One is hung in the clouds, the other established in the cup; one is a sign, the other is the substance. Every time you partake of the Lord’s Supper, you are receiving the covenant that the bow in chapter 9 ultimately pointed toward.

Remembering God's covenant is not nostalgia; it is reconfirming in the midst of every storm who God is and who we are.

To sum up the third part in one sentence: **The rainbow is not merely an afterthought of the weather; it is God’s remembrance hung in the sky: the bow is put away, the covenant stands firm, and when He sees the rainbow He remembers—on rainy days when you look up, what you should see first is the covenant, not fear.**

## (52:00 - 58:00) Application: Six Groups of People

Today’s chapter speaks to six groups of people:

**To seekers:** You might feel that God is very distant and very harsh. Genesis 9 invites you to know this facet of Him first: after judgment, the very first things He did were to bless, make a covenant, and hang a rainbow. He hung His remembrance in the sky so that even those who do not yet know Him can look up and see it. Accept this truth today: God remembered you long before you ever remembered Him.

**To new believers:** Having just come to faith, what you fear most is that you won’t be able to hold on, and God will cast you aside. Set the order straight: the covenant was established by God, and the remembering is what God did first. Your responsibility is to receive the covenant and live by it, not to renew the covenant by your performance. This week, read Genesis 9:11 once every day, and speak the words “never again” out loud.

**To mature believers who have walked with the Lord for years:** When you have believed for a long time, it is easy to read the covenant as just a familiar story, and when you look up and see a rainbow, all you want to do is take a picture. This chapter reminds you: signs are meant for remembering. Taking Communion, reading Scripture, Sunday worship—these are all signs given by God. Don't let these signs become so familiar that you feel nothing. Every single time, receive it as God reiterating His covenant directly to your face.

**To parents:** When Noah's family came out of the ark, the very first words the children heard were blessing and covenant. After storms pass in your home, do your children hear rebuke first from your mouth, or blessing? Establish a rule in your home: once a hardship has passed, gather the whole family together to count God’s remembrances first before discussing any critiques. When children grow up under the rainbow, their heart's knowledge of God will be entirely different.

**To those striving in the workplace:** The boundary of life in chapter 9 translates in the workplace into the boundaries of honesty and respecting others: do not climb the ladder by stepping on others, and do not treat colleagues and clients as expendable consumables. The image of God is upon every person you interact with. Work with covenantal awe; then, beyond your professional skill, you will have a testimony.

**To those currently in suffering:** You are still in rainy days right now, and the clouds are so thick that you cannot see the bow. Please hold fast to this one thing: the rainbow does not wait until the sky is completely clear to appear; it is hung right when the clouds are still present and the rain has just passed. Just because you cannot see it today does not mean it is not there. Lean on the testimony of others for now and believe: the God of the covenant has not revoked His covenant, and your story will not end in a flood.

## (58:00 - 60:00) Conclusion · Response in Prayer

Let us review today’s three sentences:

First, the blessing is repeated verbatim over the ruins—being fruitful and multiplying comes once again, while the boundary of life is established at the same time;
Second, the covenant is initiated by God—its scope broadens to every living creature, and its promise is specific down to never again destroying them;
Third, the bow is in the cloud, and when God sees it, He remembers—the battle bow is put away, and the first One to remember is He Himself.

Now let us bow our heads and respond to today's word in prayer.

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. After the flood, You spoke the blessing first, You established the covenant first, and You hung the bow in the cloud first. Thank You that Your remembering does not depend on our feelings; You hung the sign in the heavens, so that on rainy days we need only look up to see it. Please forgive us for the days when we stared only at the clouds and forgot the covenant. Teach us to begin anew upon the ruins, to believe first in Your 'never again' amidst the wind and rain, and teach us to honor the image You have placed upon every person. Within the new covenant in Christ, today we entrust ourselves anew into Your covenant-keeping hands. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

Amen.

## Chapter Notes

[1] perah / ravah: perah is the Hebrew פָּרָה (H6509), to bear fruit, reproduce; ravah is the Hebrew רָבָה (H7235), to multiply, flourish; Gen. 1:28 and 9:1 "be fruitful and multiply" use this pair; lexical meanings based on BDB, HALOT.

[2] dam: Hebrew דָּם (H1818), blood; see Gen. 9:4–6; lexical meanings based on BDB, HALOT.

[3] nephesh: Hebrew נֶפֶשׁ (H5315), life, living creature, soul; in Gen. 9:4 "flesh with its life, that is, its blood," "life" translates this word; lexical meanings based on BDB, HALOT.

[4] berith: Hebrew בְּרִית (H1285), covenant, treaty; see Gen. 9:9–17, and Isa. 54:9–10 referring back to the Noahic covenant; lexical meanings based on BDB, HALOT.

[5] oth: Hebrew אוֹת (H226), sign, mark; see Gen. 4:15 (the mark of Cain), 9:12–17 (the rainbow as a sign); lexical meanings based on BDB, HALOT.

[6] qesheth: Hebrew קֶשֶׁת (H7198), bow; rendered "rainbow" in Gen. 9:13–16, originally meaning a bow, frequently referring to a battle bow in Scripture (cf. Ps. 46:9, God breaks the bow); lexical meanings based on BDB, HALOT; "the bow hanging in the cloud signifies the cessation of judgment" is a common exposition, not an explicit statement of the text.

[7] Regarding Gen. 9:13 "I set My rainbow in the cloud": Chinese Union Version text; English comparison: KJV reads "I do set my bow in the cloud"; NIV reads "I have set my rainbow in the clouds"; NRSV reads "I have set my bow in the clouds."
