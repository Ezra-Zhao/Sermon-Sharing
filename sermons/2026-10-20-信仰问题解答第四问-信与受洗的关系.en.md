# Faith Q&A Question 4: Faith Saves, Baptism Does Not? — The Relation Between Faith and Baptism

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «信能救人，洗礼不能？——信与受洗的关系».

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 4
**Adaptation:** Guangyi Zhao (retold in his own preaching style; the core views belong to the original author)

---

## Scripture

> "He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned." (Mark 16:16, NKJV)

> "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit." (Acts 2:38, NKJV)

> "Assuredly, I say to you, today you will be with Me in Paradise." (Luke 23:43, NKJV)

---

## I. The Answer First: Faith Saves; Baptism Does Not

Pastor Tong answers this question very crisply, with a clean cut:

**What saves is faith** — truly receiving, truly repenting, truly believing, truly receiving the new birth of the Holy Spirit. When these four "truly"s are all present, whether you have been baptized or not, you are a Christian.

**Baptism itself cannot save you.** Water does not have that power.

But the second half of the sentence is equally crisp: **a saved person ought to be baptized.**

In one sentence: **baptism is not the ticket; it is the uniform.** The ticket is issued by faith; the uniform is worn after you are saved — you cannot use the uniform as a ticket, and you cannot take the ticket and refuse to wear the uniform.

---

## II. Look at the Greek First: Two Words, Two Weights

This is Ezra's old habit in preaching: before writing, look at the original text.

**"Believe": *pisteuō* (πιστεύω)** — not merely nodding agreement in the mind, but "to trust, to entrust oneself," wagering one's whole person. So the "true believing" Pastor Tong speaks of is not "I know God exists"; it is "I have handed my life over."

**"Be baptized": *baptizō* (βαπτίζω)** — its basic meaning is "to immerse, to submerge," not sprinkling a few drops of water, but putting the whole person into the water. So when the early church practiced immersion, it had a lexical basis; the word itself carries the picture of "buried with Christ, raised with Christ" (Rom. 6:3–4).

But Pastor Tong immediately turns the wheel back: **whether sprinkling or immersion, both are forms, not the point.** What is the point? It is "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" — if the name is right, do not contend over the form; if the name is wrong, being immersed a hundred times is useless.

The most important sentence is this: **"This is God's command, and we must obey it."**

---

## III. Having the Opportunity and Not Being Baptized? Pastor Tong Says You Are Being "Naughty"

Pastor Tong's original words are delightful: **a person who has the opportunity to be baptized and is not baptized is a "very naughty Christian."**

"Naughty" is not heresy; it is like a child being willful — knowing full well that his father told him to do it, and simply refusing. Baptism is God's command; to be willful in the face of a command is not called wisdom; it is called naughtiness.

One ought to be baptized in order to **show obedience to God**.

---

## IV. What Is Baptism Actually Doing? A Fivefold Declaration

Pastor Tong presents the meaning of baptism in full dimension — it is not going through a ceremony; it is making a declaration simultaneously before five audiences:

- **Before God:** You commanded it, and I have obeyed
- **Before men:** before everyone, I am brought under the name of Christ
- **Before the angels:** heaven and earth both know it
- **Before the world:** from now on I draw a clear line between myself and the world
- **Before myself:** toward myself and toward the world, I am no longer the old person

Baptized in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit — **entering the body of Jesus Christ, which is the church.**

So baptism is not "adding a sense of ceremony"; it is a **public oath of naturalization**: from now on you are a person with a citizenship, and that citizenship is called "the body of Christ."

---

## V. One Witness on Each Side

Pastor Tong gives two extreme examples and nails the logic down completely:

**Witness one: baptized, yet not saved — Simon the sorcerer (Acts 8)**
Simon was baptized, yet Peter said to him: "'Your money perish with you... You have neither part nor portion in this matter.'" (Acts 8:20–21, NKJV)
The water went down; the heart did not. **Baptism washes the body; it cannot wash greed.**

**Witness two: not baptized, yet saved — the thief on the cross (Luke 23)**
He never had a chance to be baptized in his life, yet Jesus said to him personally: "Today you will be with Me in Paradise."
Salvation lies in faith, not in water.

Pastor Tong adds one sentence aimed squarely at loophole-seekers: "You say, thank the Lord, I don't need to be baptized — **but sadly, you are not that thief. He had no opportunity; you do.**"

In one sentence: **Simon proves baptism cannot save a person; the thief proves salvation does not depend on water. But since you and I do have the opportunity, do not use the thief as a shield.**

---

## VI. For Us Today

1. Get the order right first: faith comes before baptism; faith saves, baptism does not
2. Do not show off baptism as a ticket, and do not use "the thief was not baptized" as an excuse for laziness
3. Baptism is a public declaration: before God, men, angels, the world, and yourself, you are brought under the name of Christ and enter the church
4. Stop arguing over sprinkling and immersion: if the name is right (in the name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit), do not contend over the form; in the face of a command, just obey
5. If you have believed and have not yet been baptized, arrange a time with the church — do not be a "naughty" Christian
