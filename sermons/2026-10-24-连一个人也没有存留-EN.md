## Summary

At the end of chapter 25 the plague is stayed, and the dead are twenty-four thousand. Chapter 26 opens with four words' worth of setting: "after the plague" (26:1) — and the first thing God does after the plague is not to pursue blame but to number: he commands Moses and Eleazar the priest to take the sum of all the congregation, from twenty years old and upward, all that are able to go to war (26:1-4). The numbering is carried out in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho, registered tribe by tribe, family by family: Reuben 43,730, Simeon 22,200, Gad 40,500, Judah 76,500, Issachar 64,300, Zebulun 60,500, Manasseh 52,700, Ephraim 32,500, Benjamin 45,600, Dan 64,400, Asher 53,400, Naphtali 45,400 — a total of 601,730 (26:5-51). Set into the middle of the list are a few short notes: Dathan and Abiram and the company of Korah were swallowed by the earth, notwithstanding the children of Korah died not (26:9-11); Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan (26:19); Zelophehad had no sons, but five daughters, whose names are written down one by one (26:33). The numbering done, God at once issues the principle for dividing the land: the land is to be divided according to the number of names — to many the more inheritance, to few the less — and yet by lot (26:52-56). The Levites are numbered separately, 23,000 males from a month old and upward; they are not numbered among the children of Israel, because no inheritance was given them among the children of Israel (26:57-62). The last three verses are the chapter's verdict: among these that were numbered there was not a man of those whom Moses and Aaron numbered in the wilderness of Sinai — the sentence that that generation should surely die in the wilderness has been fulfilled to the last man, and, save Caleb and Joshua, there was not left a man of them (26:63-65). Forty years; a roll of six hundred thousand changed over entire; the count is short by only 1,820, and the promise has not retreated a single step.

## Main Text

### 1. After the Plague: Numbered Once More (Numbers 26:1-4) (00:00-10:00)

(Fact) Verse 1 — the time and the persons, quoted in full as the base text stands: "And it came to pass after the plague, that the LORD spake unto Moses and unto Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest, saying," (26:1). Registered item by item: first, the word of time is only "after the plague" — it fastens the chapter directly to the plague at Shittim in the preceding chapter (25:9, twenty-four thousand; earlier-chapter layer of this book); the chapter gives no further date, and the narrative simply nails the two chapters together: the plague first, the numbering after. Second, those addressed are two men — Moses, and "Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest": at the first numbering the man who stood beside Moses was Aaron (earlier-chapter layer, Numbers 1); in this chapter Aaron's name appears only in his standing as a father, and the man now in office is his son Eleazar. The succession is not among the people only; it is in the priest's tent as well. Verse 2 — the content of the command, quoted in full: "Take the sum of all the congregation of the children of Israel, from twenty years old and upward, throughout their fathers' house, all that are able to go to war in Israel." (26:2). The standard is registered in its three items as it stands: throughout their fathers' house, from twenty years old and upward, all that are able to go to war — the same standard as the first numbering (earlier-chapter comparison). Verse 3 — the place and manner of execution, quoted in full: "And Moses and Eleazar the priest spake with them in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho, saying," (26:3). The place is entered on the record: the plains of Moab, by Jordan, with Jericho on the far side — the closest stand to the promised land since the exodus. Verse 4 — the two men restate the command to the people, quoted in full: "Take the sum of the people, from twenty years old and upward; as the LORD commanded Moses and the children of Israel, which went forth out of the land of Egypt." (26:4). The phrase "the children of Israel, which went forth out of the land of Egypt" points backward here: the fathers of the generation now being numbered are precisely the generation that came out of Egypt; the command names their origin, and names with it the chapter's undertone — their origin is the exodus, but their standing must be numbered afresh.

(Application) The words "after the plague" speak first to every community that has just passed through loss. The plague has only just taken twenty-four thousand; there is mourning in household after household in the camp; and the first sentence God speaks is neither comfort nor blame but "Take the sum." This is not coldness; it is precisely the order of shepherding: first count clearly who remains. After a great loss, what a person, a family, a church is most tempted to do is to shut the door and nurse the wound, or to pretend nothing has happened. God's way is to open the roll and number again, tribe by tribe: who is still here, who is no longer here, who has stepped up. Numbering is a way of acknowledging loss — the one who is missing is missing on the roll, unvarnished; and numbering is also a way of setting out again — those who remain are counted by God by name, one by one. If a "plague" has just passed through your life, the first word God speaks to you may well be this one: count what remains. Do not refuse to move forward until you have counted how much you have lost; count clearly the people who remain, the grace that remains, the opportunity that remains — and that total is the starting point of your road again.

(Application) Look next at the succession of the numberers. The first time, Moses and Aaron side by side; this time, Moses and Eleazar side by side. Aaron has already died on Mount Hor (earlier-chapter layer, Numbers 20); his office has not stood empty — his son has taken the place where he stood. This sets the rule for every handover in service: the office does not die with the man, and the work does not wait for men to be complete. The older generation will leave the stage — this whole chapter is about that — but God's numbering does not stop: with one hand he moves Aaron's name back into the position of "father," and with the other he brings Eleazar forward. What drains a church most today is treating office as private property: the older generation gripping it and not letting go, the younger with no place to stand, until the man is gone and the work collapses all at once. This chapter's roll shows the healthy shape: the father's name stays in the son's designation ("Eleazar the son of Aaron the priest"), and the son's hand takes up the father's pen. The same holds for Moses himself: he is still numbering, yet he himself is not on this roll's list of promise (later-chapter layer; Numbers 27 records the handing over of the staff separately) — the faithful leader is the man who numbers others into the promised land.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only. "Number / be numbered" is pāqad (H6485), to register by name, to muster; the sentence pattern of each tribe's subtotal in this chapter is built from this word's passive participle. "House / family" is mišpāḥâ (H4940), family, clan, the unit of this registration. "Plague" is maggēpâ (H4046; its number was verified in the sermon on Numbers 25). "Spake" is registered in the base text's rendering; it occurs twice in this chapter (26:1, 52). "Eleazar" is ʾElʿāzār (H499), "Aaron" is ʾAhărōn (H175), "Moab" is Môʾāḇ (H4124), "Jordan" is Yardēn (H3383), "Jericho" is Yərîḥô (H3405) — each verified one by one (for the method, see Endnote 5).

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) The place of the first numbering was the wilderness of Sinai (Num 1:1, earlier-chapter layer); this one is in the plains of Moab (26:3). The number of years between them this chapter does not state; it marks the lateness only by "after the plague" and by the verdict of generational change (26:64-65). The standard is the same in both — from twenty years old and upward, all that are able to go to war (26:2, compared with Num 1:3, earlier-chapter layer) — except that this numbering is expressly joined to the dividing of the land (26:52-56): the purpose widens from preparation for war to the apportioning of inheritance. This is registered as a comparison of the two chapters' texts; as for the administrative details of numbering (the registrars, the schedule), this chapter does not record them, and only the blank is registered (Endnote 8).

(Fact) This section closes by registering the chapter's skeleton of speech: the LORD speaks directly in two passages — the command to number (26:2) and the command to divide the land (26:53-56); Moses and Eleazar relay one passage to the people (26:4); the remaining sixty verses are all list and short notes, with no man speaking. Few chapters in the whole book are as quiet as this: God speaks two passages, and men answer by writing down names one by one across sixty verses. The list itself is the response — six hundred thousand names numbered in are the obedience to those two speeches.

### 2. The New Account of Six Tribes: Reuben to Zebulun (Numbers 26:5-27) (10:00-24:00)

(Fact) The list begins with the tribe of the firstborn. Verses 5-7, Reuben, with four families registered as the base text gives them: the family of the Hanochites, the family of the Palluites, the family of the Hezronites, the family of the Carmites; the subtotal, quoted in full: "These are the families of the Reubenites: and they that were numbered of them were forty and three thousand and seven hundred and thirty." (26:7). Verses 8-11 carry the list's first short note — Eliab the son of Pallu, and the sons of Eliab, Nemuel, Dathan, and Abiram, quoted in full: "And the sons of Eliab; Nemuel, and Dathan, and Abiram. This is that Dathan and Abiram, which were famous in the congregation, who strove against Moses and against Aaron in the company of Korah, when they strove against the LORD:" (26:9). (The Chinese base text gives the striving's order as against the LORD first and against Moses and Aaron after; the KJV gives Moses and Aaron first and the LORD last — each text as it stands, registered in the head of this sermon.) Verse 10 — the outcome, quoted in full: "And the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up together with Korah, when that company died, what time the fire devoured two hundred and fifty men: and they became a sign." (26:10). Verse 11 — one sentence of reversal, quoted in full: "Notwithstanding the children of Korah died not." (26:11). Verses 12-14, Simeon, five families: the Nemuelites, the Jaminites, the Jachinites, the Zarhites, the Shaulites; the subtotal as it stands: "These are the families of the Simeonites, twenty and two thousand and two hundred." (26:14). Verses 15-18, Gad, seven families: Zephon, Haggi, Shuni, Ozni, Eri, Arod, Areli; the subtotal is forty thousand and five hundred (26:18). Verses 19-22, Judah: first the short note, "The sons of Judah were Er and Onan: and Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan." (26:19); then the families of Shelah, Pharez, and Zerah, and of the sons of Pharez the families of Hezron and Hamul; the subtotal is threescore and sixteen thousand and five hundred (26:22), the largest of any tribe in this chapter. Verses 23-25, Issachar, four families: Tola, Pua, Jashub, Shimron; the subtotal is threescore and four thousand and three hundred (26:25). Verses 26-27, Zebulun, three families: Sered, Elon, Jahleel; the subtotal is threescore thousand and five hundred (26:27). The subtotals of the six tribes in this section run in order: 43,730, 22,200, 40,500, 76,500, 64,300, 60,500, totalling 307,730 (for the count in detail, see Endnote 4).

(Fact) Set against the first numbering (earlier-chapter layer, Numbers 1, the tribes' numbers registered item by item): Reuben 46,500 → 43,730, down 2,770; Simeon 59,300 → 22,200, down 37,100, the largest decrease in the chapter; Gad 45,650 → 40,500, down 5,150; Judah 74,600 → 76,500, up 1,900, and first in the chapter in both rolls; Issachar 54,400 → 64,300, up 9,900; Zebulun 57,400 → 60,500, up 3,100. This sermon registers only the changes of number and rank; it assigns no causes for the increases and decreases in this paragraph — whether Simeon's sharp fall is connected with Zimri's being of the tribe of Simeon in the preceding chapter (25:14, earlier-chapter layer) the text does not say, and the question is left to the discussion layer below.

(Application) The short notes inside the list are the chief thing to preach in this section. In a roll of six hundred thousand the compiler stops, precisely under Reuben's name, and spends three verses on a rebellion forty years old: Dathan and Abiram were swallowed by the earth, the company of Korah — two hundred and fifty men — was devoured by fire, "and they became a sign." A roll is not a running account; a roll is memory: behind every name stands a stretch of history. Before God lets the new generation enter Canaan, he has them read a warning on their own ancestors' roll — your forefathers did not die in the wilderness in the abstract; their names, their families, and the matter in which they died are all written in the roll by which you divide the land. Men today least like reading history; they find lists tedious and old matters irrelevant. But God nails the warning into the list precisely because he knows: the man who forgets how his forefathers fell will fall again in the same place. Your family's roll, your church's history, your own record are all to be read this way: not only counting how many remain, but remembering who stumbled at which lesson — to remember is the warning still at work.

(Application) The one sentence of verse 11, "Notwithstanding the children of Korah died not," is to be preached on its own to everyone of "bad origin." Korah is the most notorious rebel leader in the whole book; the earth opened her mouth and swallowed his company. By man's reckoning his sons should have been struck off with him, never to rise again. Yet under Reuben's entry the roll records a deliberate stroke: his children died not — they were not among the swallowed; and Korah's descendants later had a share in the singing service of the temple (cross-reference layer: the titles of several psalms are given as psalms of the sons of Korah; this sermon registers only their existence). The father's sin settled the father's end; it did not settle the sons' standing: when God numbers, he numbers one by one, not judging clan by clan. This stroke matters greatly for the church today: we deal with men habitually by prejudice — by origin, by family, by "whose son he is"; God's roll does not keep accounts that way — the rebel's sons may still be numbered in, may still stand in the ranks of worship. If you were born in a broken family and carry your fathers' name as a burden, read verse 11 first: your name has its own independent line in God's roll; it is not written after your father's sin, but within God's numbering.

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) Simeon's fall from 59,300 to 22,200 — a decrease of 37,100, the largest single-tribal change between the two rolls — is explained in the commentary tradition along several lines, set out together without adjudication: one view connects it with Simeon's deep involvement in the plague at Shittim (Zimri, who died in the preceding chapter, was a son of a chief house of Simeon, 25:14, earlier-chapter layer), the Simeonites forming a disproportionate share of the plague's twenty-four thousand dead; another holds that the tribe suffered further unrecorded losses across the forty years; another, that the number traditions of the two rolls themselves come from different sources. This chapter gives the number and no cause; all three are later inference; this sermon sets them out and adopts none in its text. Again: Dathan and Abiram are attached in this chapter to the Pallu line of the tribe of Reuben (26:8-9), and how that stands with the narrative of Numbers 16, where the two are in company with Korah (earlier-chapter layer), is a matter of comparing two texts; this sermon sets the two side by side and makes no harmonization.

(Fact) This section closes its account of persons: the individuals named in this section are nine in all — Eliab, Nemuel, Dathan, Abiram, Korah, Er, Onan, and the ancestral names on which the tribe-names rest, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun. Of living men's names there is not one: the short notes record only the dead. This is not an omission; it is the form: the roll registers the living by number and the dead by name and by the matter of their death. The new generation, reading its own roll, reads first not its own glory but its forefathers' epitaphs — whom the earth swallowed, whom the fire devoured, who died in the land of Canaan — all written before the dividing of the land.

### 3. The Sons of Joseph and the Total: Six Hundred Thousand and a Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty (Numbers 26:28-51) (24:00-36:00)

(Fact) From verse 28, the sons of Joseph, Manasseh and Ephraim, are listed separately. Verses 29-34, Manasseh: the family of Machir, the family of Gilead, and the six families of the sons of Gilead — Jeezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Shemida, Hepher; the subtotal is fifty and two thousand and seven hundred (26:34). The list here inserts the chapter's second short note on persons, quoted in full: "And Zelophehad the son of Hepher had no sons, but daughters: and the names of the daughters of Zelophehad were Mahlah, and Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah." (26:33). Verses 35-37, Ephraim: the families of Shuthelah, Becher, and Tahan, and of the sons of Shuthelah the family of Eran; the subtotal is thirty and two thousand and five hundred (26:37), closing with the words "These are the sons of Joseph after their families" (26:37). Joseph's two tribes against the earlier-chapter layer: Manasseh 32,200 → 52,700, up 20,500, the largest increase in the chapter; Ephraim 40,500 → 32,500, down 8,000; the two together rise from 72,700 to 85,200 (for the count, see Endnote 4). Verses 38-41, Benjamin: Bela, Ashbel, Ahiram, Shupham, Hupham, and of the sons of Bela, Ard and Naaman — seven families in all; the subtotal is forty and five thousand and six hundred (26:41), against the former 35,400, up 10,200. Verses 42-43, Dan: one family only, Shuham; the subtotal is threescore and four thousand and four hundred (26:43), against the former 62,700, up 1,700 — the number of a single family, second in the whole chapter. Verses 44-47, Asher: Jimna, Jesui, Beriah, and of the sons of Beriah, Heber and Malchiel — five families in all; a short note records "And the name of the daughter of Asher was Sarah." (26:46); the subtotal is fifty and three thousand and four hundred (26:47), against the former 41,500, up 11,900. Verses 48-50, Naphtali: Jahzeel, Guni, Jezer, Shillem — four families; the subtotal is forty and five thousand and four hundred (26:50), against the former 53,400, down 8,000. Verse 51 — the total, quoted in full: "These were the numbered of the children of Israel, six hundred thousand and a thousand seven hundred and thirty." (26:51). The twelve tribes' subtotals, added item by item, come to 601,730, agreeing with the total (added by hand; for the detail, see Endnote 4). Against the total of the first numbering, 603,550 (Num 1:46, earlier-chapter layer), this numbering is less by 1,820.

(Application) The cell of the total must be lingered over. From 603,550 to 601,730: forty years of wilderness, plague, war, and rebellion; a whole generation laid low; and the total is down by only 1,820. Judged by the number alone, it looks almost as though nothing had happened; yet verse 64 will tell us that among these 601,730 there is not one man of the 603,550 of forty years before — the men have all been changed, the count has barely changed. This is exactly God's way of preserving his people: he does not promise that every individual shall not die — the old generation really did die; he promises that his people as a whole shall not be destroyed — the new generation really has been supplied. Looking at the church, the family, one's own service today, the same sight is needed: do not pin hope on "my generation shall never fade" — that is not the Bible's promise; pin hope on "God's roll shall not stand empty" — one generation falls, another is numbered in; the names change, the promise does not. Much of our anxiety comes from confusing these two things, supposing that one's own leaving the stage is God's work leaving the stage. This chapter's total says: you leave the stage, God does not; the roll turns a page, and the number is still six hundred thousand.

(Application) Zelophehad's five daughters are to be preached to everyone who is "not up to specification" on the list. The standard of this roll is males from twenty years old and upward, able to go to war (26:2) — the five daughters do not meet the standard in a single particular: they cannot go to war, and their father has no son, so by rule this family's name ends here. Yet the roll stops its pen for them by exception, and writes all five names in, not one missing: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. This is the chapter's only short note that writes out the names of the living one by one in full (the other is Sarah, the daughter of Asher, 26:46). God's roll has its standard, but above the standard there is remembrance: the person who does not qualify for the count is not therefore unremembered by God. Their story has no sequel in this chapter — what is to be done about the inheritance, they must go and ask themselves in the next chapter (later-chapter layer of this book, Numbers 27; this sermon registers only its existence). Register this stroke today: beside every list that counts only "useful people," God keeps another pen, reserved for those whom the specification would pass over. If you feel yourself the superfluous one on the list, remember first where Tirzah's name stands — beside Manasseh's total of fifty-two thousand seven hundred, God wrote her name first, and the total after.

(New Testament Reading) When the apostle Paul argues for God's remnant by election, he says that God has reserved a people to himself — seven thousand men (Rom 11:2-5, cross-reference reading layer; Paul's words cite the matter of 1 Kings 19). At the reading layer one comparison is registered: the Old Testament roll displays God's faithfulness by a complete change-over — the old generation gone entire, the new generation at full number; Paul's remnant displays the same God's faithfulness by preservation — in the midst of general departure, God reserves to himself a company who have not bowed the knee to Baal. The two registers run in opposite directions and point to one thing: the people's continuance never rests on the inertia of numbers, but on God's numbering with his own hand and preserving with his own hand. The reading layer registers only how the New Testament answers this chapter's theme of numbering; it does not pour the doctrine of the remnant back into this chapter as a fact of its count.

(Fact) This section closes by registering the distribution of the chapter's short notes: the short notes on persons in the whole chapter are four in this part of the roll — Dathan, Abiram, and the company of Korah (26:9-11), Er and Onan (26:19), the five daughters of Zelophehad (26:33), and Sarah the daughter of Asher (26:46) — with the household of Jochebed in the Levi paragraph (26:59-61) to be registered in the next section. Of the four notes, two record deaths (rebellion and early death) and two record daughters (the daughters of a house without a son) — the places where the roll stops its pen are of two kinds only: to set a warning for the dead, and to keep a name for the weak. A great roll of six hundred thousand, willing to stop its pen for these two kinds of people — that is this list's gentlest feature of form.

### 4. According to the Number of Names: The Dividing of the Land and Levi Without Inheritance (Numbers 26:52-62) (36:00-48:00)

(Fact) Verse 52 — the second "spake," quoted in full: "And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying," (26:52). Verse 53 — the general rule of the dividing, quoted in full: "Unto these the land shall be divided for an inheritance according to the number of names." (26:53). Verse 54 — the rule of proportion, quoted in full: "To many thou shalt give the more inheritance, and to few thou shalt give the less inheritance: to every one shall his inheritance be given according to those that were numbered of him." (26:54). Verses 55-56 — the rule of the lot, quoted in full: "Notwithstanding the land shall be divided by lot: according to the names of the tribes of their fathers they shall inherit. According to the lot shall the possession thereof be divided between many and few." (26:55-56). The two rules are registered side by side: the size of the inheritance follows the number of people (settled by men), the place of the inheritance rests on the lot (settled by the lot); and the title under which a tribe receives its inheritance is "according to the names of the tribes of their fathers" — the land given to the living carries the ancestors' names. Verses 57-62, the Levites in a separate roll: verse 57, three families, Gershon, Kohath, Merari; verse 58 continues with five more families — the Libnites, the Hebronites, the Mahlites, the Mushites, the Korathites — and records "And Kohath begat Amram." Verse 59 — Amram's household, quoted in full: "And the name of Amram's wife was Jochebed, the daughter of Levi, whom her mother bare to Levi in Egypt: and she bare unto Amram Aaron and Moses, and Miriam their sister." (26:59). Verse 60: unto Aaron was born Nadab, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Verse 61 — the short note, quoted in full: "And Nadab and Abihu died, when they offered strange fire before the LORD." (26:61). Verse 62 — the Levitical subtotal and its standing, quoted in full: "And those that were numbered of them were twenty and three thousand, all males from a month old and upward: for they were not numbered among the children of Israel, because there was no inheritance given them among the children of Israel." (26:62). The standards are registered in comparison: Levi is numbered from a month old and upward (26:62), unlike the twelve tribes, numbered from twenty years old and upward, of those able to go to war (26:2); and Levi's total does not enter the 601,730. Against the former Levitical number, 22,000 (Num 3:39, earlier-chapter layer), this one is more by 1,000.

(Application) Dividing the land "according to the number of names" is this section's first lesson. God did not say: divide by merit, divide by seniority, divide by whose voice is loudest; he said: according to names — the land is divided to "these" people, and every name numbered in is joined to a share of inheritance. This rewrites the new generation's identity outright: their fathers stood on the roll as "able to go to war"; they stand on the roll first of all as heirs of an inheritance. The numbering in verse 2 still uses the military standard, yet the moment the numbering is done, God's first sentence turns the roll into a roll for dividing the land: these people are numbered not in order to go and die in the wilderness again, but in order to enter the land and receive their portion. When God numbers his children today, it is likewise not in order to enrol us on a service list and consume us without end; he records our names because there is an inheritance to be divided to us by name. If your name is on God's roll, it means there is a share for you in his arrangement — neither more nor less, according to your name. Say no more that "God's promises are for the great men": this chapter's rule is that everyone has a share by name — even Zelophehad's five daughters, who have no brother, have their names on the roll (26:33) — and the gate of the inheritance is entered precisely at the gate of the name.

(Application) The stroke of the lot is to be preached to everyone afraid of an "unfair division." Numbers decide the size; the lot decides the place — which tract lies by the river, which against the hills, is not fought over by men but settled by the lot. The arrangement blocks two diseases: first, the strong enclosing the land — since the place is settled by lot, even Judah's 76,500, the most numerous in the chapter, cannot pick the best valley first for itself; second, the weak resenting their fate — the small tribe's land is small by the numbers, the fairness in plain sight, and where its land lies is settled by the lot, with no man to resent. When church and family divide resources, posts, and opportunities, the hardest part is never that there is not enough to divide; it is that everyone stares at the other man's tract. This chapter's order is worth learning: first work out the fairness clearly (by number), then hand the part that cannot be worked out back over (by lot) — man doing his duty in what can be calculated, and receiving in peace what cannot. Today's "lot" need not take the form of drawing lots; its spirit is this: give the settling of place back to God, neither grabbing nor resenting. A place grabbed cannot be sat in securely; days resented cannot be lived well; the share divided to you by name is the place where you can stand.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only. "Inheritance" is naḥălâ (H5159), the portion of land received as an inheritance; "lot" is gôrāl (H1486), a lot, a lot-cast portion; "name" is šēm (H8034); "Levi" is Lēwî (H3878); "Amram" is registered in the base text's rendering; the original-language number of "Jochebed" this sermon marks as to be verified; see Endnote 5. Every number entered above was verified one by one before writing (for the method, see Endnote 5).

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) Two matters are set out without adjudication. First, the executive details of the lot (the lot's form, its officiant, its order): this chapter gives the principle only (26:55-56) and records no procedure; a later part of the canon records Joshua's dividing of the land by lot at Shiloh (Joshua 18, later-text layer), and how the two join is a judgment of cross-volume synthesis; this sermon registers only the principle and the existence of the later text, and does not pour the later procedure back into this chapter. Second, how Zelophehad's five daughters obtained land after the principle of division was issued, this chapter does not say; in chapter 27 the five daughters plead their case before Moses in person and receive a ruling (later-chapter layer), and chapter 36 adds a supplementary ruling (later-chapter layer) — this sermon registers only this chapter's roll-note and the general rule of division; the case's ruling stands in the later text and is not drawn on in advance at this chapter's fact layer.

(Fact) This section closes by registering the division between the two rolls: this chapter in fact has two rolls — one for the twelve tribes, numbered from twenty years old and upward of those able to go to war, totalling 601,730, joined to an inheritance in the land; and one for Levi, numbered of males from a month old and upward, totalling 23,000, joined to no inheritance. On which roll does Moses' own name stand? In the genealogical short note of the Levi roll (26:59, "she bare unto Amram Aaron and Moses") — the man who numbers all Israel has his own name appear in a single line of a genealogy, and the roll to which he belongs has no inheritance. The leader receives no land on the roll he himself has numbered: that is this section's quietest stroke.

### 5. There Was Not Left a Man of Them (Numbers 26:63-65, with the Whole Chapter) (48:00-60:00)

(Fact) Verse 63 — the numbering's closing sentence, quoted in full: "These are they that were numbered by Moses and Eleazar the priest, who numbered the children of Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho." (26:63). The executors are still the two men of verses 1 and 3, and the place still the plains of Moab — the chapter's head and tail share the same place and the same men. Verse 64 — the upper half of the verdict, quoted in full: "But among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron the priest numbered, when they numbered the children of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai." (26:64). Verse 65 — the verdict's lower half and its exception, quoted in full: "For the LORD had said of them, They shall surely die in the wilderness. And there was not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun." (26:65). Registered item by item: first, the words quoted in this verse, "They shall surely die in the wilderness," are God's standing verdict on the Sinai generation (earlier-chapter layer, Numbers 14); this chapter is its statement of account: the verdict said they should all be gone, and the roll proves them all gone. Second, the exceptions are two men only, their descent given in full order as it stands — "Caleb the son of Jephunneh" and "Joshua the son of Nun": both men stood on the first roll in their day (earlier-chapter registration), and they are now the only two names of the old roll left on the new. Third, verse 64's "there was not a man" and verse 65's "save..." stand side by side within the two verses: the universal statement comes first, and the exception declares itself with the text; this sermon registers in the text's own order, neither deleting the exception to make the universal stand nor abolishing the universal for the exception's sake. Fourth, Moses is not listed among the exceptions: in his day he did not stand on Sinai's roll of fighting men (his Levitical standing; earlier-chapter layer, set against this chapter's 26:62), and his own end this chapter does not state (later-chapter layer, Numbers 27); here only the text's silence is registered.

(Application) "They shall surely die in the wilderness" — the sentence was spoken nearly forty years ago, and this chapter is its receipt. God's words, of judgment and of promise alike, must at their time be accounted for stroke by stroke: those who were to fall have fallen, not one remaining; Caleb and Joshua, who were to be preserved (earlier-chapter layer, Num 14:24, 30), have been preserved, not one missing. What should make us tremble most in reading this chapter is not the old generation's end but the exactness of God's bookkeeping: more than six hundred thousand men, forty years, and he has the count perfectly clear — who is on the old roll, who is on the new, who is an exception, who is not even an exception — without the least blur. Our readiness today to treat God's word lightly is mostly a wager that his memory is poor and that time will blur it. This chapter opens the ledger for you to see: he does not blur. Every word he spoke over the old generation has been fulfilled; and in the same way, every word he has spoken over the new generation — "Unto these the land shall be divided for an inheritance according to the number of names" (26:53) — will be fulfilled. The word of judgment does not fall empty, and the word of promise does not fall empty; they are two pages of one and the same ledger. Whichever page you fear shows which page you believe is real; the Bible would have us believe both pages — trembling because of the judgment, rejoicing because of the promise.

(Application) The two names Caleb and Joshua are to be preached to every "minority." Forty years ago twelve spies came back; ten brought an evil report, and two said, in Caleb's words, "Let us go up at once, and possess it; for we are well able to overcome it" (Num 13:30, KJV, earlier-chapter layer). Forty years later this chapter settles the account: the generation those ten represented is gone entire, and these two men are still here. Standing with the majority has never been the same as standing with the preserved; this chapter's rate of preservation is two in six hundred thousand — whether a man is preserved does not depend on how many stand with him, but on whether he believed that one word to the end. Caleb and Joshua had no stronger bodies than their contemporaries to guarantee them forty years of life; what they had was forty years without changing their confession: as God said, so they believed, believing on until their contemporaries' graves were heaped full, and still standing in the plains of Moab waiting to cross the river. Those in the church today who hold to one word of God for ten years, for twenty years, often feel foolish, feel that the world has changed and only they have not — read verse 65: on the day the account is settled, the names left on the roll are precisely these "unchanged" people. It is the generations that change, not the word; the man who lays hold of the word is not changed when the generations change.

(Summary) The words of this section are stated as philology only. "Caleb" is Kālēb (H3612); "Joshua" is Yəhôšuaʿ (H3091); "Jephunneh" is Yəpunneh (H3312); "Nun" is Nûn (H5126). The original word behind "was not left" belongs to the śārad family; its exact word-form and number this sermon marks as to be verified; see Endnote 5. Every number entered above was verified one by one before writing (for the method, see Endnote 5).

(Scholarly and Historical Discussion) Two matters are set side by side without harmonization. First, the juxtaposition of verse 64's "there was not a man" with verse 65's "save Caleb… and Joshua…": the commentary tradition mostly reads it as a universal statement at once limited by the exception the text itself sets, the two verses read together being complete; another reading takes verse 64 to refer to the fighting men "among these that were numbered," the two exceptions belonging separately to a special preservation under promise. Both routes presuppose reading the two verses together; the difference lies in how the rhetoric is read, and this sermon's text registers in the text's own order, presupposing neither route as fact. Second, the nature of the two rolls' numbers: 601,730 and 603,550 are both regular count-totals, and scholarship differs over the nature of ancient roll numbers (actual count, round number, or editorial form); this sermon argues and compares on the base text's numbers as they stand, and the numbers' historical character is kept at the discussion layer, set out and not adjudicated.

(Fact) The whole chapter's data are closed, every item already counted (for the detail, see Endnote 4): the chapter has 65 verses; the five sections cover 4 (26:1-4), 23 (26:5-27), 24 (26:28-51), 11 (26:52-62), and 3 (26:63-65) verses, totalling 65. The twelve tribes' subtotals occur twelve times (26:7, 14, 18, 22, 25, 27, 34, 37, 41, 43, 47, 50), and added together come to 601,730, agreeing with the total verse (26:51). The Levitical subtotal occurs once (26:62), 23,000, not entering the total. "Spake" occurs twice (26:1, 52); the LORD speaks directly in two passages (26:2; 53-56); Moses and Eleazar relay one passage (26:4); and beyond these, one sentence of God's old verdict on the former generation is quoted at the chapter's end (26:65). The short notes on persons are five (26:9-11, 19, 33, 46, 59-61). Registrations of death: those swallowed by the earth and the two hundred and fifty devoured by fire (26:10, referring back to a former matter), Er and Onan's dying in the land of Canaan (26:19), Nadab and Abihu's dying for offering strange fire (26:61), and the whole of the old generation covered by the chapter's verdict (26:64-65). The closing comparison with the chapters on either side: the preceding chapter ends with a command not yet executed (to vex the Midianites, 25:17), this chapter ends with an account already settled (there was not left a man of them, 26:65) — the command points to the future, the account closes the past; and Israel stands between the two, its feet in the plains of Moab, Jericho on the far side of the river.

(Application) The whole chapter closes with one sentence left for each of three kinds of people. First, for those who feel they have been missed out of the count: this roll stopped its pen even for Zelophehad, who had no son, and wrote his five daughters' names down; God's numbering misses no one — that you are not on men's list does not mean you are not on God's list; men's rolls count by usefulness, God's roll counts by name. Second, for those standing at a generational change: the old generation will pass entire — this chapter's heaviest and most honest sentence; stake neither church, family, nor work on any one generation never leaving the stage; stake them on God's word — his word sent one generation away and will surely bring another in; if you are of the new generation, prepare to receive your share by name; if you are of the old, learn of Moses: finish numbering the others, and settle the matter of handing over the staff (recorded in the later-chapter layer). Third, for those who hold on to the end: on the preservation list of two in six hundred thousand, God did not write "the two most capable," but "the two who believed forty years ago and are still believing forty years after." The road is still long, the word still stands — stand firm: on the day the account is settled, your name will be on the roll.

## Devotional Excerpt

On the roll of forty years ago there were 603,550 names; forty years later, Moses and Eleazar number again by Jordan, and count out 601,730 — the number has barely changed, but the men have all been changed. The chapter's last verse says: "But among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron the priest numbered, when they numbered the children of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai. For the LORD had said of them, They shall surely die in the wilderness. And there was not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun." (26:64-65). Not one word of God has fallen empty: the word of judgment has been fulfilled, and the word of promise has been fulfilled — Caleb and Joshua did not change their confession for forty years, and so they stand in the midst of the new generation, preparing to cross the river. Today God reminds us: generations will pass entire; his word will not pass. Do not pin your hope on men — men leave the stage; pin your hope on God's word — he who said he would divide the inheritance to you according to the number of names (26:53) will surely divide it to you. If you are passing through the grief of sending a generation away, remember this roll: with one hand God has closed the old account, and with the other he has already written the new; your name — he has counted it.

English (KJV-based):

"But among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron the priest numbered, when they numbered the children of Israel in the wilderness of Sinai. For the LORD had said of them, They shall surely die in the wilderness. And there was not left a man of them, save Caleb the son of Jephunneh, and Joshua the son of Nun." (Num 26:64-65, KJV) One whole generation passed away, yet the total barely changed — God replaced a generation without shrinking His promise by an inch. Do not pin your hope on people, who pass; pin it on His word, which does not. He still counts His people by name, and the portion assigned to your name will not be lost.

The tribal subtotals were re-added by hand for this translation: 43,730 + 22,200 + 40,500 + 76,500 + 64,300 + 60,500 + 52,700 + 32,500 + 45,600 + 64,400 + 53,400 + 45,400 = 601,730, agreeing with 26:51; against Numbers 1 the comparison total is 603,550, a net decrease of 1,820; Levi 23,000 (26:62).
