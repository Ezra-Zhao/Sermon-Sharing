# Faith Q&A Question 5: Why Did God Not Create an Adam Who Could Not Sin?

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «为什么上帝不造一个不会犯罪的亚当？».

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 5
**Adaptation:** Guangyi Zhao (retold in his own preaching style; the core views belong to the original author)

---

## Scripture

> "And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, 'Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die.'" (Gen. 2:16–17, NKJV)

> "I call heaven and earth as witnesses today against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing; therefore choose life, that both you and your descendants may live." (Deut. 30:19, NKJV)

**A note on the original words:** The Hebrew *Adam* (אָדָם) is both a personal name and the word for "man, mankind," and its root comes from *adamah* (אֲדָמָה, ground, soil). Genesis 2:7: "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being." — Adam is "the man who came out of the ground," yet God endowed him with something immense: **the capacity to choose**.

---

## I. The Question Is Well Asked, but It Hides a Logical Trap

"Since God knows everything, why did He not simply create an Adam who would not sin?"

This question binds two things together: whether God **can** create something that cannot sin, and whether God **wants** to create a "man" who cannot sin. Pastor Tong sees through it at a glance and separates the two things straight away.

---

## II. Things That "Cannot Sin" — God Already Created Heaps of Them

Pastor Tong's answer is crisp from the start: **for God to create something that "cannot sin" is far too simple, and He already created them long ago — stones and wood; these have never sinned in their whole existence.**

But have you thought about this: would you point at a stone and say, "This stone is very moral"? No. Because the word "morality" never gets a turn with it — it has never had the opportunity to be immoral.

**Where there is no choice, there is no morality to speak of.**

---

## III. Moral Victory Means Overcoming Sin

This is the core of the original answer, and not a word of it can be changed:

> What cannot sin is not called morality; **to be able to sin and yet overcome sin — that is moral victory**. Moral victory is the overcoming of sin; only being able to overcome sin and temptation counts as morality.

So what God created was a "man of moral character" — not a perfect machine, not a vase that shatters at a touch, but a person **who would face temptation, who would choose, and who could overcome**.

In one sentence: **stones do not need morality; men do. What God wants is not a pile of stones that cannot sin, but a company of children who overcome in the midst of temptation.**

---

## IV. Omniscience Does Not Mean Your Choice Is Not Allowed

Follow the question one step further: God knew in advance that Adam would fall; why create him anyway?

The answer is: **foreknowledge is not compulsion.** You know your child may fall while learning to walk, yet you still let go and let him walk on his own — your knowing is yours; the choice is still his. Otherwise, not a single second of his growing up would be called freedom; it would be called a puppet on a string.

God's omniscience has not abolished man's responsibility; on the contrary, precisely because man has freedom of choice, Adam's choice was real, and salvation became necessary.

---

## V. For Us Today

1. Do not treat "the existence of temptation" as a bug in God's design — temptation is standard equipment for freedom; where there is no temptation there are only stones and heaven
2. True morality is not "never having had an evil thought," but having had one and still choosing what is right — victory is a verb, not a state
3. Stones do not need salvation, but stones are not fit for a crown either; what God has reserved for man is something higher: **able to sin, and able to overcome**
