# He Will Not Forsake You — A Sermon on Deuteronomy 31

> Status: First translation · Initial translation pending proofreading (首译·初译待校). This is the first English translation of the finalized Chinese sermon on Deuteronomy 31, "He Will Not Forsake You," which passed the Chinese strong re-review (ZH 强复核) on 2026-10-25. All Scripture quotations in this English text are taken verbatim, including punctuation, from the King James Version (KJV), which serves as the base text for quotations in this translation; quotations are never re-translated from the Chinese. Cross-references quoted in full (Josh 1:5, 6, 9; 2 Tim 4:6–8; Deut 34:7; Exod 7:7) were fetched first-hand from BibleGateway/Bolls KJV in this round. Hebrews 13:5 is, as in the Chinese source, registered only and not quoted. Hebrew transliterations and Strong's numbers are carried over from the Chinese source as registered there (99 items). Counts are re-counted from the KJV text itself and registered on the KJV's own account; where the English form differs from the Chinese fixed text's form, both are stated side by side and neither is smoothed over (see note 2 and the production record at the end).

> Verification of the title: Three candidates were considered. (A) "He Will Not Forsake You" — from the promise sentence that occurs twice, at 31:6 and 31:8, "he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee," the one sentence in the whole chapter spoken once to the people and once to the leader: the man changes, this sentence does not. (B) "The LORD Will Go Before Thee," from the opening of 31:8, the picture of God going ahead of Joshua. (C) "Put It Beside the Ark," from 31:26, the order for placing the book of the law. Candidate (A) is adopted: verse 6 speaks it to all the people, verse 8 to Joshua; the two negative verbs standing side by side twice — "will not fail" (rāpâ, H7503) and "nor forsake" (ʿāzab, H5800) — are the covenant floor of this chapter's handover: Moses himself may not cross over, yet he hands this promise, which he cannot use for himself, into the hands of the one who will cross.

## Introduction (00:00–06:00)

[Summary layer] At the end of chapter 30 Moses sets life and death before the people and says, choose life. Chapter 31 opens with the question changed: the setting-out still stands, but the speaker is about to leave. Moses is a hundred and twenty years old; he says of himself that he can no more go out and come in, and God has long since told him plainly that he shall not go over this Jordan. This chapter is the chapter of the handover — the people will cross the river, the leader will stay behind, and the whole chapter is a handover list settled item by item: the successor stood up publicly (vv. 1–8), the law written into a book and delivered, with the institution of the septennial reading established (vv. 9–13), God himself appointing the successor in the tabernacle and giving the forecast of the coming breach of covenant in advance (vv. 14–22), and at the end the book of the law placed beside the ark, and Moses speaking the written song in full before all (vv. 23–30). Chapter 32 is the song, chapter 33 the blessing, chapter 34 the death of Moses; this chapter is the settling that comes before all of that. [Factual layer] Four structural facts are registered first. (1) The chapter has thirty verses; the name of Moses occurs ten times in nine verses in the Chinese fixed text, and the name of Joshua five times in four verses (note 2; for the KJV's own count, which differs, see note 2). (2) "Be strong and of a good courage" occurs three times in the chapter: at verse 6 to the people, at verse 7 from Moses to Joshua, at verse 23 from God to Joshua — the same exhortation transmitted through three levels: the congregation, the individual through Moses' mouth, the individual from God's own mouth. (3) "Witness" occurs four times in four verses in the Chinese fixed text (vv. 19, 21, 26, 28): the song is a witness, the book is a witness, heaven and earth are witnesses — the keyword of this chapter is not "leading" but "witness." (4) The orientation of the time-words is the reverse of chapter 30's: chapter 30 has "today" seven times; this chapter has it only once (v. 27), and in its place stand "thy days approach that thou must die" (v. 14), "the same day" (v. 22), "after my death" (vv. 27, 29), and "in the latter days" (v. 29) — the time axis unfolds behind the speaker. [Theological layer] The course of this sermon's four sections: Section One reads verses 1–8 and looks at the man of the handover — first his own "I can no more," then God's "will" and Joshua's "shall," the one sentence "he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" spoken separately to the people and to Joshua. Section Two reads verses 9–13 and looks at the book and the institution handed over. Section Three reads verses 14–22 and looks at the appointment and the forecast in the tabernacle — the successor does not take up the staff with his eyes shut. Section Four reads verses 23–30 and looks at the appointment completed and the book set in place. This sermon's argument rests only on the text itself: its verb chains, counts, sentence forms, and the form of the fixed text; wherever the relations between texts or readings are in dispute, they are stated side by side and not adjudicated.

## Section One (06:00–18:00): The Handover at a Hundred and Twenty Years (31:1–8)

[Factual layer] Verse 1 is the door of the whole chapter; the KJV text is given in full: "And Moses went and spake these words unto all Israel." Registered item by item: (1) The chapter opens with Moses' going — "went" is the first verb of his last public action: a handover is not announced sitting in one's place. (2) The audience is "all Israel" — with the public appointment of verse 7, the gathering of the elders in verse 28, and the whole congregation in verse 30, this forms the first of the chapter's four public occasions. [Factual layer] Verse 2 gives Moses' account of himself; the discourse's quotation opens at this verse; the KJV text is given in full: "And he said unto them, I am an hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in: also the LORD hath said unto me, Thou shalt not go over this Jordan." (In the Chinese fixed text the quotation levels are: outer layer, Moses' discourse; inner layer, God's earlier words to Moses.) Registered item by item: (1) "I can no more go out and come in" is in the original yākal (H3201) with the infinitives "to go out" and "to come in" — going out and coming in is the vocabulary of a leader who leads the host out to battle and brings them in (Num 27:17–23 has the same phrase; registered only, note 6). (2) Moses' "can no more" is twofold: that of years, and that of God's command — the quotation is nested inside his self-account: his not crossing is not only that he is old; God has said it. (3) "Go over" is ʿābar (H5674): the river Moses may not cross is the river that in verse 3 God and Joshua will lead the people over — the tension of the whole chapter hangs on this word (note 4). (4) The Chinese fixed text reads "thou shalt surely not go over," with the word "surely"; the 2010 edition reads "thou shalt not go over" without it; the two forms are registered separately (note 3). [Factual layer] Verse 3 gives the twofold "going over"; the KJV text is given in full: "The LORD thy God, he will go over before thee, and he will destroy these nations from before thee, and thou shalt possess them: and Joshua, he shall go over before thee, as the LORD hath said." Registered item by item: (1) The "go over" of the original is in both cases the participial form, "the one going over before thee" — God goes over ahead, Joshua goes over ahead, the sentence form occurring twice in parallel: the successor's place is to stand where God has already stood. (2) "Destroy" (šāmaḏ, H8045) and "possess" (yāraš, H3423) are both set down with God as the one acting and the people as the beneficiaries: what comes after the crossing is settled in advance in the words of the handover. (3) The 2010 edition at this verse reads "go over before thee" and "remove these nations from before thee," a different rendering from the Chinese fixed text's "lead you over"; registered separately (note 3). [Factual layer] Verses 4–5 use history as the warrant of the handover; the KJV text is given in full: "And the LORD shall do unto them as he did to Sihon and to Og, kings of the Amorites, and unto the land of them, whom he destroyed." "And the LORD shall give them up before your face, that ye may do unto them according unto all the commandments which I have commanded you." Registered item by item: (1) Sihon and Og are two victories this generation experienced itself — Moses cites the battle history one last time before stepping down, not to boast, but to give the successor his warrant: as it was before, so it will be hereafter. (2) The subject of "shall give them up" (nātan, H5414) is still the LORD: what Joshua takes over is a victory already handed over, not a battle yet to be fought out. (3) At verse 5, "according unto all the commandments which I have commanded you" — miṣwâ (H4687) and ṣāwâ (H6680) stand in the same sentence: the authority Moses hands over is God's word, not his personal arrangements (for the six occurrences of ṣāwâ and their division of labour, see note 4). [Factual layer] Verse 6 is the promise spoken to the people; the KJV text is given in full: "Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them: for the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee." (The discourse pauses here; Moses turns to speak to Joshua, and the quotation of this stretch closes at the end of verse 8.) Registered item by item: (1) "Be strong and of a good courage" is the pairing of two imperatives, ḥāzaq (H2388) and ʾāmaṣ (H553), the first of the phrase's three occurrences in the chapter, spoken to all the people. (2) The prohibitions of fear come in two groups: "fear not" (yārēʾ, H3372) and "nor be afraid" (ʿāraṣ, H6206) — the latter is the trembling before a strong enemy; the text names in advance both kinds of fear the people have not yet spoken. (3) The reason clause does not rest on the people but on God: "for the LORD thy God, he it is that doth go with thee" (hālak, H1980) — the ground of being strong and of good courage is not the people's strength but who their companion is. (4) "He will not fail thee" (rāpâ, H7503) and "nor forsake thee" (ʿāzab, H5800), two negative verbs side by side: to fail is to let the hand slacken and leave a thing uncared for; to forsake is to abandon and depart; both forms of abandonment are excluded by the one promise at once. (5) At the end of verse 6 the Masoretic paragraph marker petuhah was seen first-hand (note 1): the words to the people form a paragraph of their own in the layout — the people are settled first, and then Joshua is appointed in public. [Factual layer] Verses 7–8 are the public appointment; the KJV text is given in full: "And Moses called unto Joshua, and said unto him in the sight of all Israel, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou must go with this people unto the land which the LORD hath sworn unto their fathers to give them; and thou shalt cause them to inherit it." "And the LORD, he it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed." Registered item by item: (1) The occasion of the appointment is "in the sight of all Israel" — the successor's legitimacy is established in public; the word "called" (qārāʾ, H7121) is used in this chapter also for "read" (v. 11) and for "call" (v. 14); the same word in its separate uses is registered in note 4. (2) The "be strong and of a good courage" spoken to the people at verse 6 is spoken again here to Joshua, and its content becomes specific in proportion: what the people need is not to fear; what Joshua needs is to bring this people in and cause them to inherit the land (nāḥal, H5157, in the causative stem). In the Chinese fixed text there is an exclamation mark after the exhortation and a comma after its word for "for" — a modern-punctuation edition has no such comma; registered separately (note 3). (3) The opening sentence of verse 8 turns the "go over" of verse 3 into "doth go before thee": God does not only go with him; he walks ahead; and then the promise given to the people at verse 6 is spoken again to Joshua, with only "doth go with thee" exchanged for "doth go before thee … he will be with thee." (4) The verse ends with two prohibitions: "fear not" and "neither be dismayed" (ḥātat, H2865) — dismay is the fear in which the heart has already collapsed; the leader's prohibition goes one level deeper. (5) The quotation of Moses' discourse to the congregation closes at the end of verse 8. [Theological layer] The order of this section's handover is worth pausing over: first his own "I can no more" (v. 2), then God's "will" and Joshua's "shall" (v. 3), the battle history set down as warrant (vv. 4–5), and only then the separate exhortations to the people (v. 6) and to Joshua (vv. 7–8). Moses' way of stepping down is to point every sentence at someone else — he himself stays east of the river, and there is not one sentence of self-pity. Note also the symmetry of verses 6 and 8: to the people it is "doth go with thee"; to Joshua, "doth go before thee" together with "he will be with thee" — the same God, two modes of presence; and the one sentence "he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" is used by both sides exactly as it stands, the promise neither increased nor diminished according as its hearers are a congregation or one man. [Summary layer] This section is the closing ceremony of the whole book's discourse: the man who has spoken for thirty chapters does only three things in his last stretch of public discourse — he hands over the staff, he hands over the ground, and he hands over his heart. The staff goes to Joshua; the ground is that God goes before; and the words in which he hands over his heart are one sentence only: fear not; he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee. [New Testament reading layer] The echo of this section's promise in the New Testament (the related wording of Heb 13:5) was not verified word by word in this round; it is registered only, not quoted (note 6); the parallel texts verified word by word for this section are concentrated in the discussion layer (Joshua chapter 1). [Scholarly and historical discussion layer] Three items stated side by side. First, the hundred and twenty years set beside Deuteronomy 34:7 — "And Moses was an hundred and twenty years old when he died: his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated." (Deut 34:7, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) Chapter 34 says that at the time of his death his eye was not dim and his natural force was not abated, yet in this chapter, verse 2, Moses says of himself, "I can no more go out and come in": one reading takes verse 2 as an inability of office (the charge of going out and coming in must be handed over; it is not a decline of physical strength); another reading takes the two passages as each presenting one side, standing together unharmonised. The two readings are stated side by side, not adjudicated. Second, the reading of the stages of his life is registered — "And Moses was fourscore years old, and Aaron fourscore and three years old, when they spake unto Pharaoh." (Exod 7:7, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) Eighty years old at the exodus, forty years in the wilderness, and at this chapter exactly a hundred and twenty; later readers divide his life on this basis into three forties (Egypt, Midian, the wilderness); this sermon registers only the correspondence of the numbers (80 + 40 = 120) and makes no assertion of fact from it. Third, the chain of transmission of "be strong and of a good courage" is stated side by side — after its three occurrences in this chapter, Joshua chapter 1 has God restate it to Joshua: "There shall not any man be able to stand before thee all the days of thy life: as I was with Moses, so I will be with thee: I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee." (Josh 1:5, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) "Be strong and of a good courage: for unto this people shalt thou divide for an inheritance the land, which I sware unto their fathers to give them." (Josh 1:6, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) "Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the Lord thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest." (Josh 1:9, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round; the fetched BibleGateway text prints "the Lord thy God" where the chapter anchor of Deuteronomy 31 prints "the LORD") All three verses were fetched first-hand in this round: the "I will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" of Joshua 1:5 is, like verses 6 and 8 of this chapter, rāpâ together with ʿāzab; the causing this people to inherit the land of Joshua 1:6 is, like verse 7 of this chapter, nāḥal in the causative stem; the "neither be thou dismayed" of Joshua 1:9 is the same word as verse 8 of this chapter. Whether the two passages are the before-and-after promulgation of one appointment, or Joshua chapter 1 is an independent appointment at the river — the relation of transmission is stated side by side only, not adjudicated.

## Section Two (18:00–29:00): Written Down, and Read Every Seven Years (31:9–13)

[Factual layer] Verse 9 gives Moses' other identity; the KJV text is given in full: "And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it unto the priests the sons of Levi, which bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD, and unto all the elders of Israel." Registered item by item: (1) "Wrote" (kātab, H3789) occurs in the Chinese fixed text five times in four verses (note 2; for the KJV's own forms, see note 2); its first occurrence is here: the man who has spoken for thirty chapters has as one of his last actions writing — discourse retires with the speaker; a book does not. (2) Those to whom it is delivered are two groups: the priests the sons of Levi who bear the ark of the covenant (nāśāʾ, H5375, "bare"; ʾārôn, H727, ark; kōhēn, H3548, priest) and all the elders of Israel (zāqēn, H2205) — its keepers are men of the liturgical and the governing lines together; the law (tôrâ, H8451) is not made a private collection; it is handed into an institution. (3) The 2010 edition at this verse reads "wrote down" and reorders the phrase as "the Levite priests"; the wording is registered separately from the fixed text (note 3). [Factual layer] Verses 10–11 establish the cycle and the occasion of the reading; the command's quotation opens at verse 10; the KJV text is given in full: "And Moses commanded them, saying, At the end of every seven years, in the solemnity of the year of release, in the feast of tabernacles," "When all Israel is come to appear before the LORD thy God in the place which he shall choose, thou shalt read this law before all Israel in their hearing." (The quotation runs across verses 10–13 and closes at the end of verse 13.) Registered item by item: (1) The cycle is seven years: the reading falls in the year of release (šəmiṭṭâ, H8059) at the feast of tabernacles (ḥag, H2282; sukkôt, H5521) — the institution of release is in Deuteronomy 15; this sermon registers only the relation between the chapters and does not quote its text (note 6). (2) The môʿēd (H4150) of "in the solemnity" is the same word as the môʿēd of "the tabernacle of the congregation" in verses 14 and 15: one word, two uses; the same word in its separate entries is in note 4. (3) "Read" is qārāʾ — the same word as "called" for Joshua in verses 7 and 14: calling men to come and reading to men to hear are in the original one and the same verb (note 4). (4) "To appear" is the passive form of rāʾâ (H7200), the same word in two directions as God's "appeared" in the tabernacle at verse 15; registered separately in note 4. (5) The bāḥar (H977) of "the place which he shall choose" has the same root as the "choose life" of chapter 30: God chooses the place, man chooses life — only the shared root is registered; no argument is built on it. (6) In the Chinese fixed text verse 11 has a full stop after "him" ("appear before him. At that time…"), where a modern-punctuation edition runs on with a comma; registered separately (note 3). [Factual layer] Verse 12 gives the list of the hearers; the KJV text is given in full: "Gather the people together, men, and women, and children, and thy stranger that is within thy gates, that they may hear, and that they may learn, and fear the LORD your God, and observe to do all the words of this law:" Registered item by item: (1) "Gather … together" (qāhal, H6950, verb, causative stem) is the same word as Moses' last gathering in verse 28 — the first step of the reading institution and Moses' last action bear the same name: gather. (2) The list has four classes: men, women, children (ṭap, H2945), and the stranger within thy gates (gēr, H1616) — the stranger is named within the hearers; this is the widest list in the chapter: qualification as a hearer is not graded by descent or by age. (3) The chain of purposes has three steps: "that they may hear" (šāmaʿ, H8085), "that they may learn" (lāmad, H3925), "and fear" — hearing comes before learning, and learning before fearing, and "observe to do" (šāmar, H8104) closes the chain. (4) In the Chinese fixed text "observe, to do" carries a pause mark where other editions write the two words together; and its "gather them" has as its object in the original "this people," where the 2010 edition reads "assemble the people"; both are stated side by side in note 3. [Factual layer] Verse 13 gives the institution's next-generation clause; the command's quotation closes at the end of this verse; the KJV text is given in full: "And that their children, which have not known any thing, may hear, and learn to fear the LORD your God, as long as ye live in the land whither ye go over Jordan to possess it." Registered item by item: (1) The children "which have not known" (yāḏaʿ, H3045) any thing — the institution writes the generation not yet of understanding into the list of hearers in advance: the reading is not a ceremony of remembrance; it is a preparation for those not present in understanding. (2) "As long as ye live" is the ḥay (H2416) family, "the days of living" — the institution's term of validity restarts with the lifetime of each generation. (3) At the end of verse 13 the paragraph marker petuhah was seen first-hand (note 1): the reading institution stands as a large paragraph of its own in the layout; the book's institution is a piece by itself. [Theological layer] A handover hands over not only a man but an institution. Joshua will grow old and die, but the rhythm of a reading every seven years can walk on from generation to generation — the succession of leaders depends on appointment; the continuance of the faith depends on reading. What Moses arranges is not a farewell ceremony but a cycle. The width of the list of hearers especially deserves attention: women, children, and strangers are all named, and even the next generation, "which have not known," is registered in advance — this institution assumes that every generation needs to hear it from the beginning. Note also the order of the verbs: hear, learn, fear, observe to do — it is not that fearing comes first and hearing after; it is hearing first, and learning, and fearing follows. [Summary layer] This section pairs with Section One: Section One hands over the man who leads; Section Two hands over the content that leads. The man can change; the book abides — and the book is not merely kept: verse 9 hands it over, and verses 10–13 at once give its instructions for use: at fixed times, in public, for all the people, across the generations. [New Testament reading layer] No New Testament quotation of this section was confirmed by word-for-word first-hand verification; the scene of public reading of the Scriptures in the New Testament era (the narrative of Luke 4) was not verified word by word and is registered only, not quoted (note 6). [Scholarly and historical discussion layer] Two items stated side by side. First, the year of release and the year of reading are the same year: the release of Deuteronomy 15 handles the release of debts and of servitude, and this chapter fixes the reading in the same year — whether the fresh start in economic matters and the fresh hearing of the word, falling in the same year, are two sides of one thing, the text does not say plainly; stated side by side only, not adjudicated. Second, the records of this chapter's reading institution being carried out in later history (2 Kings 22–23; Neh 8) were not verified word by word in this round; only the index is registered (note 6), with no quotation and no adjudication of the correspondence.

## Section Three (29:00–41:00): The Appointment and the Forecast in the Tabernacle (31:14–22)

[Factual layer] At verse 14 the scene moves from before the eyes of all to inside the tabernacle; God's speech opens and closes within the verse; the KJV text is given in full: "And the LORD said unto Moses, Behold, thy days approach that thou must die: call Joshua, and present yourselves in the tabernacle of the congregation, that I may give him a charge. And Moses and Joshua went, and presented themselves in the tabernacle of the congregation." Registered item by item: (1) "Thy days approach that thou must die" is in the original "thy days approach, thou must die" (qārab, H7126; mût, H4191) — Moses' day of death is announced by God himself, without evasion and without being passed on through another's report. (2) The appointment has two scenes: verse 7 before the eyes of all, verse 14 in the tabernacle before God — the successor is first established before men, then comes before God to receive his charge. (3) The "present yourselves" (yāṣab, H3320, reflexive stem) is the posture of standing by in attendance to receive orders; the tabernacle, ʾōhel (H168) môʿēd, is the same word as the "solemnity" of verse 10, already seen in note 4. (4) The ṣāwâ of "that I may give him a charge" — God personally charges Joshua with the word Moses habitually used: the authority to command passes from this point from God directly to the successor, no longer through Moses' hands (note 4). (5) In the Chinese fixed text a semicolon divides the verse after "thy days approach that thou must die," where the 2010 edition divides with a full stop; registered separately (note 3). [Factual layer] Verse 15 gives the appearing; the KJV text is given in full: "And the LORD appeared in the tabernacle in a pillar of a cloud: and the pillar of the cloud stood over the door of the tabernacle." Registered item by item: (1) The pillar of a cloud (ʿammûḏ, H5982; ʿānān, H6051) is the mark of the Presence all the way from the exodus; on the day of the handover it is still in its place — what changes is the leader; what does not change is the pillar of cloud. (2) "Appeared" is the passive form of rāʾâ, standing in exact correspondence with the people's "to appear" at the feast in verse 11 (the same word, passive form): men appear before God at the feast; God appears to men at the appointment (note 4). (3) "Stood" (ʿāmad, H5975) is "over the door of the tabernacle" (petaḥ, H6607) — the pillar stands at the doorway, neither entering nor withdrawing; it is the position of a witness. (4) At the end of verse 15 the paragraph marker setumah was seen first-hand (note 1): the appointment scene is closed off with a small paragraph. [Factual layer] Verse 16 opens God's long forecast; the quotation opens at this verse and closes at the end of verse 21; the KJV text is given in full: "And the LORD said unto Moses, Behold, thou shalt sleep with thy fathers; and this people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land, whither they go to be among them, and will forsake me, and break my covenant which I have made with them." (The quotation runs across verses 16–21; an extract is given here verse by verse.) Registered item by item: (1) Death is spoken of as "sleep with thy fathers" (šākab, H7901) — Moses' death is narrated within the sequence of the fathers; it is not an ending but a rejoining of the ranks. (2) The subject of the forecast at once changes to the people: rise up (qûm, H6965), go a whoring after the gods of the strangers (nēkār, H5236) of the land (zānâ, H2181) — the verb chain of the breach of covenant unfolds from here. (3) "Forsake" (ʿāzab) here changes its subject: at verses 6 and 8 God "will not forsake" men; at verse 16 men "will forsake" God — the same word is first negated in the promise, then falls upon men in the forecast (note 4). (4) "Break my covenant which I have made with them" — pārar (H6565, causative stem) with bərît (H1285); the kārat (H3772) of making the covenant is in the same sentence in the original: the covenant is God's making; the breaking is man's. [Factual layer] Verse 17 gives the hiding of the face and the people's questioning of themselves; the KJV text is given in full: "Then my anger shall be kindled against them in that day, and I will forsake them, and I will hide my face from them, and they shall be devoured, and many evils and troubles shall befall them; so that they will say in that day, Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us?" Registered item by item: (1) After "my anger shall be kindled" (ḥārâ, H2734; ʾap, H639), God's response comes in three steps: I will forsake them (ʿāzab again — man's forsaking comes first, and God's forsaking is the response, note 4), I will hide my face (sātar, H5641), and they shall be devoured (ʾākal, H398). (2) "Evils and troubles" is the paired phrase rāʿâ (H7451) and ṣārâ (H6869), occurring in this chapter three times in the Chinese fixed text (the pair at vv. 17 and 21, and "evil" used alone at v. 29), standing in the same sentence as "befall" (māṣāʾ, H4672; the anchor's marginal note reads "befall: Heb. find"). (3) The people's embedded self-questioning is the chapter's only stretch of inner single-quoted speech in the Chinese fixed text: in the day when the evils come, the people themselves make the attribution — "Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us?" (ʾayin, H369, "is not") — the attribution is half right: the evils are indeed connected with God's presence, but as to the direction of the causation, verse 18 corrects it at once. (4) The 2010 edition gives this verse's embedded speech in the singular ("these evils come upon me … is my God not among me?") and renders "hide my face" as "turn my face away"; registered separately (note 3). [Factual layer] Verse 18 is the correcting sentence; the KJV text is given in full: "And I will surely hide my face in that day for all the evils which they shall have wrought, in that they are turned unto other gods." Registered item by item: (1) "I will surely hide" is in the original the intensifying construction of the infinitive absolute with the imperfect (hastēr ʾastîr) — paired with the intensifying construction of "ye will utterly corrupt yourselves" at verse 29: the chapter has two intensifications, one concerning God's hiding of his face, one concerning man's corruption (note 5). (2) The reason for the hiding of the face is stated by God himself: "in that they are turned (pānâ, H6437) unto other gods" — the turning appears again at verse 20: first the turning, then the hiding. (3) The people ask at verse 17, "Is it not because God is not among us?"; God answers at verse 18, "I will surely hide" — it is not God's absence; it is God's turning of his face; it is not that the circumstances changed; the relation changed first. [Factual layer] Verse 19 is the arrangement within the forecast — write a song; the KJV text is given in full: "Now therefore write ye this song for you, and teach it the children of Israel: put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel." (The Chinese fixed text closes this verse with a semicolon, God's speech continuing into verse 20.) Registered item by item: (1) The breach of covenant having been forecast, God's arrangement is not to write another discourse but to write a song (šîr, H7892): "write" (kātab) occurs once in this verse, and in the original it is a plural imperative (kiṯḇû, "write ye"); the Chinese fixed text has the singular "thou," and the 2010 edition the plural "write ye" — who the co-writers are is stated by both parties side by side in note 5; the quotation follows the KJV in this translation, which has the plural "write ye." (2) "Teach" (lāmad, causative stem) has the same root as the people's "learn" at verses 12 and 13: teaching and learning are two sides of one word (note 4). (3) "Put it in their mouths" is in the original "put it in their mouth" (śîm, H7760, with peh, H6310, mouth), and the KJV reads "put it in their mouths" — the song's place of storage is not on a scroll but in the mouth (note 3). (4) The song's office is to be "a witness" (ʿēd, H5707) — the first of the four occurrences of "witness" in the Chinese fixed text: a song is set up as a witness. [Factual layer] Verse 20 gives the path of the breach of covenant; the KJV text is given in full: "For when I shall have brought them into the land which I sware unto their fathers, that floweth with milk and honey; and they shall have eaten and filled themselves, and waxen fat; then will they turn unto other gods, and serve them, and provoke me, and break my covenant." Registered item by item: (1) The path has four steps: they shall have eaten and filled themselves (śābaʿ, H7646), and waxen fat (dāšēn, H1878), then will they turn unto other gods (pānâ again), and serve them (ʿābad, H5647), provoke and break — what makes men break covenant is not want but fulness; the most piercing item in the forecast is that abundance itself became the temptation. (2) For "provoke me," the wording is registered separately: the original word nāʾaṣ (H5006) means to despise, to treat with contempt, where the Chinese fixed text reads "despise me" (note 3). (3) The zûb (H2100), ḥālāḇ (H2461), and dəḇaš (H1706) of "that floweth with milk and honey" are the very words of the promise at the exodus — the promised land has not changed; what changed is man after he has eaten his fill. (4) "Sware unto their fathers" — the sworn promise occurs four times in the chapter in the Chinese fixed text (note 2): God's words keep returning to that one oath. [Factual layer] Verse 21 is the close of the forecast; God's quotation closes at the end of this verse; the KJV text is given in full: "And it shall come to pass, when many evils and troubles are befallen them, that this song shall testify against them as a witness; for it shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed: for I know their imagination which they go about, even now, before I have brought them into the land which I sware." Registered item by item: (1) "This song shall testify … as a witness" — the "testify" is ʿānâ (H6030), whose basic sense is to answer aloud: the song is not a silent document; it is a witness that will open its mouth and speak in the day of the evils. The KJV reads "testify against them," the Chinese fixed text "be a witness before them," and the original preposition is "before"; the forms are registered side by side (note 3). (2) "It shall not be forgotten out of the mouths of their seed" — the negated form of šākaḥ (H7911) with zeraʿ (H2233, seed): a book may be scattered and lost; a song is kept in the mouth, preserved by being sung. (3) "Their imagination" (yēṣer, H3336) and "even now, before" (ṭerem, H2962): what God knows in advance is not the behaviour but the imagination; registered to this point, with no extension. [Factual layer] Verse 22 is the execution on that same day; the KJV text is given in full: "Moses therefore wrote this song the same day, and taught it the children of Israel." Registered item by item: (1) "The same day" — the command of verse 19 and the execution of verse 22 are completed on the same day; the handover is not delayed; the writing and the teaching are both on that day. (2) The two verbs "wrote" and "taught" correspond item by item to the command of verse 19; Moses appears for the last time in the chapter in the role of executor, executing God's pre-positioned witness. [Theological layer] This section is the heaviest in the chapter: the appointment and the forecast of the breach of covenant take place on the same occasion — the day of appointment is the day of the forecast, and the successor takes over a charge whose outcome is already known. But the forecast is not given in order to produce despair: every step of the breach is matched in advance with a witness — the song is written, taught, and put into their mouths before the people have even entered the land. The order deserves to be seen clearly: it is not that the people first break covenant and God then supplies a remedy; God first sets up the witness, and men afterwards break covenant; the witness exists before the offence — and that is exactly the dividing line between "witness" and "revenge." Note also that the people's attribution at verse 17 and God's correction at verse 18 are recorded side by side: the people say the evils are because God is not among them; God says the hiding of the face is because men turned aside — this sermon registers only the textual positions of the two attributions and makes no adjudication in application (note 9, item four). [Summary layer] Section Two handed over the book and the institution; Section Three hands over the knowledge of the future. Joshua takes up his post carrying three things: God's personal charge (v. 14), the foreknowledge of the breach of covenant (vv. 16–21), and a pre-positioned song (vv. 19, 22). Knowledge is not a privilege; it is a burden: he is to lead them over the river in the full knowledge that the people will in the future break covenant. [New Testament reading layer] Of the parallels between this section and New Testament farewell texts, only one is taken that has been verified word by word — Paul's account of himself in 2 Timothy: "For I am now ready to be offered, and the time of my departure is at hand." (2 Tim 4:6, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) "I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith:" (2 Tim 4:7, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) "Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his appearing." (2 Tim 4:8, KJV, fetched first-hand in this round) All three verses were fetched first-hand in this round and are stated beside this chapter: both speakers make their dispositions before departing; Moses forecasts the breach of covenant and the pre-positioning of the witness; Paul recounts the fight fought and the crown laid up — the tone of the one is heavy, of the other at rest; which draws on which, and the relation of echo, is registered only, not adjudicated (note 7). [Scholarly and historical discussion layer] Three items stated side by side. First, the two parties' readings of the "hiding of the face" — a judicial-hiding reading (the hiding of the face is one link in the covenant sanctions) and a relational-withdrawal reading (the hiding of the face is the withdrawal of the Presence); the causal linking of the question in verse 17 and the answer in verse 18 also has two readings accordingly; stated side by side, not adjudicated. Second, the three subject–object configurations of ʿāzab (note 4, item one): whether coincidence of rhetoric or deliberate contrast, the two readings are stated side by side; this sermon argues from textual position only as far as "order," and does not adjudicate structural intent. Third, the correspondence between the song established in this chapter and the song of Moses in the next chapter (chapter 32) — commanded to be written at verse 19, written the same day at verse 22, read aloud publicly at verse 30, with chapter 32 being the song's full text: the connection between the chapters is registered here only; the text of chapter 32, verse by verse, awaits first-hand verification in the next sermon, and this sermon does not quote it.

## Section Four (41:00–53:00): Put It Beside the Ark (31:23–30)

[Factual layer] Verse 23 is the carrying out of God's charge in the tabernacle; the quotation opens and closes within the verse; the KJV text is given in full: "And he gave Joshua the son of Nun a charge, and said, Be strong and of a good courage: for thou shalt bring the children of Israel into the land which I sware unto them: and I will be with thee." Registered item by item: (1) The one sentence "be strong and of a good courage" is at verse 7 spoken by Moses in public, and at verse 23 spoken by God himself — the transmission of the exhortation here completes its last level: God did not let Joshua take up his post on Moses' words alone. (2) The Chinese fixed text marks the subject explicitly as "the LORD," where the KJV carries it on with the pronoun, "he gave Joshua … a charge"; the two forms are registered separately (note 3); the announcement of verse 14, "that I may give him a charge," is here fulfilled literally with the word ṣāwâ, "gave … a charge." (3) "I will be with thee" closes the verse: the words relayed by Moses at verse 8 have at verse 23 become God's first person — the promise passes from relay to his own mouth. (4) In the Chinese fixed text a semicolon follows "the land," where the 2010 edition has a comma and reads "enter"; registered in note 3. [Factual layer] Verse 24 gives the completion of the writing; the KJV text is given in full: "And it came to pass, when Moses had made an end of writing the words of this law in a book, until they were finished," (in the Chinese fixed text the verse ends with a comma, the sentence continuing into verse 25.) Registered item by item: (1) The writing of the chapter is gathered up to this point (note 4, item five): at verse 24, "had made an end of writing … until they were finished" — kālâ (H3615) and tāmam (H8552) stand in one sentence; a lifetime of discourse is here set in its final form. (2) "A book" (sēper, H5612) — the words of the law leave Moses' mouth and enter a book that can be handed over, placed, and read again; verse 24 runs on into verse 25 with a comma: the finishing of the writing and the delivery are one breath. [Factual layer] Verses 25–26 are the placing order; Moses' words to the Levites open their quotation at verse 26; the KJV text is given in full: "That Moses commanded the Levites, which bare the ark of the covenant of the LORD, saying," "Take this book of the law, and put it in the side of the ark of the covenant of the LORD your God, that it may be there for a witness against thee." (The quotation runs across verses 26–29 and closes at the end of verse 29.) Registered item by item: (1) In verse 25 the Chinese fixed text omits the subject and carries on with "then"; the 2010 edition supplies "Moses commanded"; registered separately (note 3). The KJV, by contrast, names Moses expressly: "That Moses commanded the Levites." (2) The ṣaḏ (H6654, miṣṣad, "at the side of") of "put it in the side" — the book of the law is not inside the ark of the covenant but beside the ark: a single word's difference of position; the KJV reads "put it in the side of the ark," its wording "in the side," stated beside the Chinese fixed text's "beside" (note 5). (3) The purpose of the placing is the same as the song's: "for a witness against thee" (ʿēd, the third of the four occurrences of "witness" in the Chinese fixed text): a book bears witness in the sanctuary, a song bears witness in the mouth — the same word, and the same verb śîm, "put" (note 4). (4) The object of the witness: the original is the singular collective "thee" (bəkā); the KJV reads "against thee," and the Chinese fixed text renders it expressly as "a witness against Israel"; the three forms are stated side by side (note 5). [Factual layer] Verse 27 is Moses' account of himself; the KJV text is given in full: "For I know thy rebellion, and thy stiff neck: behold, while I am yet alive with you this day, ye have been rebellious against the LORD; and how much more after my death?" Registered item by item: (1) "I know" occurs three times in the chapter in the Chinese fixed text (note 2): at verse 21 God knows men's imagination; at verses 27 and 29 Moses knows men's rebellion — the knowledge runs both ways; only men do not know their own future, and must be reminded of it by the song and the book. (2) "Thy rebellion" (merî, H4805) and "thy stiff neck" (ʿōrep, H6203, with qāšeh, H7186) are Moses' closing characterisation of this generation, and the sentence closes with the verb "rebellious" (mārâ, H4784). The chapter's only "today" in the Chinese fixed text occurs here — Moses' "today" has only this day left. (In the KJV "this day" occurs at verses 2 and 27 alike; registered on the KJV's own account in note 2.) (3) The original has hēn, "behold," at the head of the sentence, which the Chinese fixed text does not translate and the 2010 edition has; registered separately (note 3). The KJV does translate it: "behold, while I am yet alive with you this day." [Factual layer] Verse 28 is the last gathering; the KJV text is given in full: "Gather unto me all the elders of your tribes, and your officers, that I may speak these words in their ears, and call heaven and earth to record against them." Registered item by item: (1) Those gathered are the elders (zāqēn) and officers (šōṭēr, H7860) of every tribe (šebeṭ, H7626) — the whole governing level is present to hear Moses' last words. (2) The direction of "speak these words" is "in their ears" (ʾōzen, H241) — the words must enter the ears; with the "read this law before all Israel in their hearing" of verse 11 and the "spake in the ears of" verse 30, this belongs to the vocabulary of hearing: the chapter's transmission of information runs entirely through the ears, not the eyes. (3) "Call heaven and earth to record" (ʿûḏ, H5749, causative stem; the fourth of the four occurrences in the Chinese fixed text) — heaven and earth are witnesses longer-lived than the congregation; Deuteronomy 4:26 and 30:19 also have this phrase; they were not re-verified word by word, and are registered only (note 6). (4) The Chinese fixed text reads "speak unto them," where a modern-punctuation edition reads a variant of the same phrase; registered separately (note 3). [Factual layer] Verse 29 is Moses' last forecast; the quotation closes at the end of this verse; the KJV text is given in full: "For I know that after my death ye will utterly corrupt yourselves, and turn aside from the way which I have commanded you; and evil will befall you in the latter days; because ye will do evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger through the work of your hands." Registered item by item: (1) "Ye will utterly corrupt yourselves" is the intensifying construction of šāḥat (H7843), paired with God's "I will surely hide" at verse 18 (note 5): the text speaks man's corruption and God's hiding of his face in one and the same grammar. (2) "Turn aside from the way" (sûr, H5493; dereḵ, H1870) — the object of the turning aside is "the way which I have commanded you": Moses' command (ṣāwâ, its sixth occurrence) stands here as the boundary marker of the last station. (3) Word order, registered separately: the Chinese fixed text places "ye will do evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger through the work of your hands" (kāʿas, H3707; maʿăśeh, H4639; yāḏ, H3027) before "evil will befall you in the latter days," while the Hebrew and the KJV have the opposite order (speaking of the evil befalling first, and the doing of evil after); a modern-punctuation edition follows the Chinese fixed text's order, and the 2010 edition divides it into two sentences — stated side by side as it stands, not adjudicated (notes 3 and 5). The KJV quotation above follows the KJV order. (4) The "befall" of "evil will befall you" is qārâ (H7122); "the latter days" is ʾaḥărît (H319) — Moses' last sentence of forecast falls upon the days after his death. [Factual layer] Verse 30 is the close of the whole chapter; the KJV text is given in full: "And Moses spake in the ears of all the congregation of Israel the words of this song, until they were ended." Registered item by item: (1) "Spake" (dābar, H1696, verb) — the song written at verse 22 is here read aloud in public: the delivery of the song and the placing of the book of the law are completed on the same day, the one out of the mouth, the other into its place beside the ark. (2) "All the congregation" is qāhāl (H6951, noun), standing in the same root as, but separate from, the verb qāhal, "gather," of verses 12 and 28. (3) "Until they were ended" — the original has ʿaḏ tummām, "until they were completed," which the KJV renders "until they were ended"; the Chinese fixed text gathers it up in a single word for "all," with no word-for-word correspondence; registered separately (note 3). (4) At the end of verse 30 the paragraph marker petuhah was seen first-hand (note 1): the whole chapter closes with a large paragraph — the handover is completed, the scroll is shut. [Theological layer] The architecture of witness in this section is here gathered complete: the song in the mouth (vv. 19–22), the book beside the ark (vv. 24–26), heaven and earth on the scene (v. 28) — a threefold witness pointing to one and the same function: not to prove that God is in the right, but to leave the generation that breaks covenant without excuse, and to leave the generation that returns in a later day with something to stand on. In Moses' last words there is not one sentence of self-defence: he knows the people's rebellion (v. 27), knows that after his death they will utterly corrupt themselves (v. 29), and still finishes speaking the song and puts the book in its place — the handerover's duty is not to guarantee the outcome but to set the witness in its place. This section has one further hidden line, "know": God knows men's imagination (v. 21), Moses knows men's rebellion (vv. 27, 29), and the one thing men are required to know is this — this law: the direction of knowing from above downward is judgment and mercy; from below upward it is learning and fearing. [Summary layer] The four sections together, and the handover list is gathered complete: the man (Joshua, vv. 1–8), the institution (the septennial reading, vv. 9–13), the knowledge (the forecast of the breach and the song, vv. 14–22), and the material evidence (the book of the law placed beside the ark, vv. 23–30). Before Moses steps down, all four things are in place, and not one of them remains in his hands — in the next sermon, chapter 32, he will sing the song this chapter has already written. [New Testament reading layer] The echo of this section (vv. 23–30) in the New Testament — such as the mention of "the song of Moses" in Revelation — was not verified word by word in this round; it is registered only, not quoted (note 6); the word-for-word side-by-side statements of this sermon's New Testament reading layer are concentrated in Section Three (2 Tim 4:6–8), and by discipline are not read back into this section. [Scholarly and historical discussion layer] Two items stated side by side. First, the reading of the book's "placing beside" — the book is placed beside the ark of the covenant, not inside the ark: what was placed inside the ark was not verified word by word in this round; only the question itself is registered (the two parties: placed beside, outside, and placed within the side), with no quotation and no adjudication (note 8). Second, the two forms of verse 29's word order, and the question of identifying the forecast's fulfilment: the identifications in later history of this chapter's forecasts (breach of covenant, hiding of the face, evils) have always belonged to the disputed layer of reading, and the several identifications differ; this sermon identifies no concrete historical event, registering only the forecasting structure of the text itself (note 9, item two).

## Conclusion (53:00–60:00)

[Theological layer] The whole chapter read, we return to the title to close. This chapter has no miracle and no great battle, only a man of a hundred and twenty years handing his work over. The quality of a handover is not in its bustle but in every item's being set in place: the man standing before the eyes of all, the book placed beside the ark of the covenant, the song put into the people's mouths, the institution fixed in a seven-year rhythm. "He will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" is said twice — once to the people, once to Joshua; and Moses, who speaks it, cannot use it himself: he may not go over. But the measure lies exactly here: he did not, on account of his own ending, speak the promise lightly or speak it short; he handed the whole sentence, exactly as it stands, to the next generation, and also handed over and set in place in advance the coming breach of covenant, the hiding of the face, the evils, and the two witnesses — so that that generation, in the day of the evils, might be without excuse, and in the day of remembrance might have something to stand on. The use of this chapter is delimited as in note 9: it identifies no personnel change of today, and asserts the destiny of no community; it permits only that every hearer ask himself — the witness handed into my hands, have I set it in place? Moses' "today" has only one day left, and yet "after my death" is spoken twice — a leader's last responsibility is, while one day still remains, to hand everything over. In the next sermon, Deuteronomy 32, Moses will sing in public the song this chapter has written — heaven and earth are to be its hearers, and the song is to begin its work as a witness.

Note 9 — The boundaries of present use (the prohibitions on applying the handover and forecast sections, registered in their fixed form). (1) The prohibition on identifying persons — the succession of leaders in this chapter (Moses stepping down, Joshua succeeding) may not be used to identify or to judge the personnel arrangements and handover disputes of any church or institution of today; this chapter is a narrative within the covenant, not a warrant for individual cases. (2) The prohibition on identifying the forecast — the forecast of the breach of covenant at verses 16–21 and 29 may not be used to identify any community of today as "a rebellious generation" or to assert its destiny; the forecast's textual setting is God's disposition delivered to Moses in the hearing. (3) The positive delimitation of the promise's application — "he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee" at verses 6 and 8 may be used only as comfort and exhortation for the hearer himself: the hearer asks himself whether, amid handover and change, he still trusts this God who goes before; it may not be turned into an assertion or a guarantee about another's circumstances. This prohibition has the same intent as note 9 of every sermon in this series, and the sermons that follow register it likewise.

## Devotional Extract

Deuteronomy 31 is the record of an old man's last stretch of work. Moses is a hundred and twenty years old; he can no more go out and come in as before, and he knows clearly that he will not go over this Jordan; but he leaves not one sentence for himself, handing over everything that had to be handed over: he sets Joshua before the eyes of all the people, writes the law into a book and puts it beside the ark of the covenant, puts a song into the people's mouths, and establishes a reading every seven years, writing even the children who have not known this law into the list of hearers in advance. He even gives account in advance of what comes after his own death: the people will break covenant, and the evils will come, but the two witnesses, the song and the book, have been set in place a step ahead. The heaviest word he leaves to the next generation is one promise spoken twice: once to all the people, "he will not fail thee, nor forsake thee," and once again to Joshua, who has to lead the way. The man who leads changes; the God who goes before does not change. Our lives also have their seasons of handover: a post will be handed over, a responsibility will change hands, and someone we value will go a step ahead of us. If that day comes, may we learn not to grasp tightly but to set things in place well — speak the exhortation to the one who takes up the staff, leave the witness for those who come after — and then step down in peace. For the LORD will go before us; he will be with us; he will not fail us, nor forsake us. While today is still in our hands, to say first what must be said and leave what must be left — that is the soundest work of our stretch of the course.
