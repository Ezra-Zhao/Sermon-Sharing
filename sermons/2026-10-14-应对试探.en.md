(00:00 - 08:00)

Preacher:
Peace be with you, brothers and sisters! I am so glad to gather and worship together with all of you today in the house of the Lord.

The theme I want to share with everyone today is: "Dealing with Temptation."

Please reflect for a moment: in your life, have there been moments when you clearly knew you shouldn't do something, yet you simply couldn't control yourself? When you clearly knew that path would lead to a dead end, yet your feet kept moving forward as if having a mind of their own? This is temptation.

Temptation is not the exclusive domain of a few; it is a lesson every single person must face. Even our Lord Jesus was tempted by the devil in the wilderness for forty days (Matt. 4:1–11). But please note: being tempted does not equal sinning. The Lord Jesus was tempted, yet without sin; but when we are tempted, we often collapse completely. Where is the difference? The difference lies in whether we have learned how to "deal with it."

When James wrote this epistle, the brothers and sisters receiving it were scattered across various places, living in persecution and hardship. Some fell and sinned amidst trials, but they shifted the blame onto God, saying, "God is tempting me; God caused me to sin." Hearing this, James immediately took up his pen and wrote the passage of scripture we are reading today—James 1:13–18. This is a classic passage on the "theology of temptation," telling us where temptation actually comes from and how we ought to overcome it.

Today, we will look at three critically important spiritual principles, three laws for overcoming temptation:

**First, consider God's judgment—see through the four-step trap of temptation;**
**Second, consider God's goodness—see through disguised benefits;**
**Third, consider the divine nature within—let the new life overcome the old desires.**

Before we begin, let us bow our heads and pray together.

"Dear Heavenly Father, we thank You, because Your word is a lamp to our feet and a light to our path. Today as we reflect on how to deal with temptation, we pray that Your Holy Spirit would speak directly to us: illuminate the hidden desires within us, strengthen our weak knees, and grant that as we go forth today, we will no longer be defeated casualties of temptation, but overcomers through the Lord. Thank You, Lord. In the name of Jesus Christ we pray, Amen!"

---
(08:00 - 28:00)

To overcome temptation, the first vitally important thing is to learn to "consider God's judgment"—to see clearly how temptation drags a person step by step into death.

Please turn to James 1:13–16: "Let no one say when he is tempted, 'I am tempted by God'; for God cannot be tempted by evil, nor does He Himself tempt anyone. But each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own desires and enticed. Then, when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death. Do not be deceived, my beloved brethren."

📖 Scripture Exegesis 1: James 1:13–16 (The Origin of Temptation and the Four-Step Trap)

Word Study: The Greek word for "temptation" is *Peirasmos* (transliteration: pei-ras-mos). In the New Testament, this word has two meanings: the "trial" from God (to refine us), and the "temptation" from the devil (to cause us to stumble). It is the same word, but with completely opposite purposes. Here James states categorically: God will only test you; He will never tempt you to sin—"God cannot be tempted by evil, nor does He Himself tempt anyone."

Then where does temptation come from? Verse 14: "drawn away by his own desires and enticed." Here are two extremely vivid Greek verbs: "drawn away," *Exelkō* (transliteration: ex-el-ko), which means dragging prey by force out of its hiding cave; and "enticed," *Deleazō* (transliteration: de-le-a-zo), which means baiting a hook or setting a trap. James is using the language of hunters and fishermen: temptation is like an experienced hunter who first drags you out of your safe hiding place, and then hooks you with your favorite bait.

The Greek word for "desire" is *Epithymia* (transliteration: ep-i-thu-mi-a), which literally means "excessive craving." Please note, desire in itself is not sin—feeling hungry and wanting to eat, feeling thirsty and wanting to drink; these are God-given instincts. But when a desire swells to the point of "I must have it, I must have it right now, and I'll have it even if I must resort to sinful means," it becomes *Epithymia*, it becomes lust.

Exegesis and Spiritual Mechanism: James charts a "death assembly line" for us, consisting of four steps: **Desire → Deception → Disobedience → Death**.

Step one, Desire (*Epithymia*): An inordinate craving arises in the heart.
Step two, Deception: Temptation begins to "put on makeup," packaging sin as harmless or even wonderful. "Just this once, nobody will know," "Everybody does it"—these are the devil's lies; this is deception.
Step three, Disobedience: Desire "has conceived, it gives birth to sin"—the Greek word for "gives birth" is *Apokyeō* (transliteration: ap-o-ku-e-o), using the exact concept of a woman giving birth. Once sin is "fully matured," the action breaks forth; this is disobedience.
Step four, Death: "sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death." Notice, James says, "Do not be deceived"—many people assume the outcome of sin is "thrill" and "pleasure," but James says you are mistaken; the outcome is "death."

💡 Warren Wiersbe gave a wonderful analogy: desire is like the steam inside the boiler of a steam locomotive. Steam itself is energy, neutral; but if it is not kept on the right track and restrained, the entire train will derail and explode. Your desires are the same—running on God's track, they are driving power; breaking free from God's restraint, they become a catastrophe.

📜 Corresponding Scripture and Exegesis from Genesis

Corresponding Scripture (Gen. 3:1, 6): And he said to the woman, "Has God indeed said, 'You shall not eat of every tree of the garden'?" ... So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree desirable to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate. She also gave to her husband with her, and he ate.

Exegesis and Connection: Look, what played out in the Garden of Eden was precisely the four-step trap James describes. Step one, desire: "good for food, that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree desirable to make one wise"—a threefold craving was ignited. Step two, deception: the serpent said, "You will not surely die," packaging disobedience as "broadening one's horizons." Step three, disobedience: "she took of its fruit and ate." Step four, death: all humanity from then on lived under the shadow of sin and death. From the Garden of Eden until today, the devil's script has never changed; only the cast of actors changes from one generation to the next.

📜 Corresponding Scripture and Exegesis from Psalms

Corresponding Scripture (Ps. 141:3–4, excerpt): "Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; Keep watch over the door of my lips. Do not incline my heart to any evil thing, To practice wicked deeds With men who work iniquity."

Exegesis and Connection: David knew well that the first step of temptation occurs in the "heart"; therefore, the starting point of his prayer was not "keep my hands back," but "do not incline my heart to any evil thing." Overcoming temptation is not about forcefully suppressing external behavior, but crying out to God to keep guard right when desire first sprouts, before it has "conceived." This is precisely the practical exercise of "considering God's judgment"—slamming the brakes at step one, rather than waiting until step four to regret.

📜 Corresponding Scripture and Exegesis from Proverbs

Corresponding Scripture (Prov. 4:23): "Keep your heart with all diligence, For out of it spring the issues of life."
Corresponding Scripture (Prov. 7:21–23): The adulteress "With her enticing speech she caused him to yield, With her flattering lips she seduced him. Immediately he followed her, as an ox goes to the slaughter, Or as a fool to the correction of the stocks, Till an arrow struck through his liver. As a bird hastens to the snare, He did not know it would cost his life."

Exegesis and Connection: Proverbs 7 is the bloodiest live broadcast of the four-step trap: enticing speech (deception) → following her (disobedience) → arrow striking liver, bird into the snare (death). But chapter 4, verse 23 provides the way out: "Keep your heart with all diligence"—your heart is the master switch; if your heart is guarded, the very first step of the four-step trap cannot even start.

💡 Application in the Contemporary Chinese Context

Faced with pervasive temptation, "seeing clearly the four-step trap" serves as the first line of defense for every role:

Workplace professionals (dealing with the temptation of kickbacks and falsification): When someone implies, "Let's run this invoice off the books, everyone benefits," please see clearly: Step one is desire (wanting extra money), step two is deception ("Everyone does this; they'll never trace it to me"), step three is disobedience (once you sign, sin is born), and step four is death (when the truth comes to light, reputation and career are ruined). Consider God's judgment at step one, and do not take this deal.

Businesspeople (dealing with tax evasion and commercial fraud): Keeping double books or dual ("yin-yang") contracts may look "clever" in the short term, but in the long term, it is an assembly line of death. James says, "Do not be deceived"—you think you are gaining an advantage, but you are actually marching your own business to the execution ground with your own hands.

Students (handling temptations to cheat and pornography): The night before an exam, someone sends the answers into the group chat; late at night when you are alone, a pop-up appears on your phone with an illicit link. Remember the hunter’s two actions: *Exelkō* drags you out of your safe zone, and *Deleazō* hooks you with your favorite bait. There is only one countermeasure: run at the very first step—do not stop to have a "quick chat" with temptation.

In Sunday school, a child was once asked: "What do you do when temptation knocks at the door?" The child answered: "I let Jesus answer the door; He always wins." Brothers and sisters, this is the very essence of the First Rule—do not go to the door yourself, do not negotiate with temptation; see through the four-step trap, and let the Lord open the door at the very first step.

(28:00 - 48:00)

Seeing through the trap of temptation is not enough. The most formidable tactic of temptation is not to frighten you, but to "put on makeup"—packaging sin as a benefit, wrapping a trap as a gift. Therefore, the second rule is to learn to "meditate on God's goodness."

Please look with me at James 1:17: "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning." (Jas. 1:17)

📖 Scripture Exegesis 2: James 1:17 (God Is the Source of Goodness)

Original Language Analysis: Two Greek words in this verse are particularly beautiful: "gift" is *Dosis*, emphasizing "the act of giving"; "gift" (赏赐) is *Dōrēma*, emphasizing "the concrete gift itself." By using both words together, James shows us: God is not only a God who delights in giving, but every single gift He gives is in itself good.

"Father of lights" in Greek is *Patēr tōn Phōtōn*. In Scripture, light represents holiness, truth, and life. But what does temptation bring? It brings darkness, lies, and death. James draws a stark contrast here: temptation drags you into darkness, but God dwells in the light, sending down everything good from "above."

"With whom there is no variation or shadow of turning"—"variation" in Greek is *Parallagē*, meaning change or shifting; "shadow of turning" is *Aposkiasma*, referring to the shifting shadow on a sundial. James' point is this: even the celestial bodies in the heavens have shifting shadows and changes, but as for this Father of lights, His goodness never closes down, never turns away, and is never out of stock.

Exegesis and Spiritual Mechanism: What is the core lie of temptation? It is to make you doubt God's goodness. The very first sentence spoken by the serpent in the Garden of Eden was: "Has God indeed said...?" The underlying implication was: God is not being good enough to you; He is withholding good things from you. Today's temptations still use the exact same tactic: "Go ahead and sin; this is the real 'benefit'; God's way is too slow; taking a shortcut is the 'smart' move."

Yet James says: Do not be deceived. **All genuine goodness can come only from above.** Any "benefit" that requires you to sin to obtain it is a counterfeit. What is the hallmark of a counterfeit? It looks real at the moment, but over time it fades and rots. The mark of God's gift is that it is "perfect"—in the original Greek, *Teleion*, meaning complete, whole, and without harmful consequences.

Brothers and sisters, when temptation strikes, our first reaction is often to rely on our own flesh and cleverness—"I can handle this," "I'll just take one look," "I know my limits." Pastor Tong Liu reminds us: even your "sense of limits" can be of the flesh. Seeing through disguised benefits does not rely on having a sharper mind, but on returning before God's goodness and confessing, "I need the Holy Spirit." (Source: Silicon Valley River of Life Christian Church, *One Heart Devotions with Pastor Tong Liu: John 18:25–32*)

Pastor Zhuo tells us: that very sigh is taken over by the Holy Spirit Himself, transformed into powerful intercession brought before the Heavenly Father. 📜 Corresponding Genesis Passage and Exegesis

Corresponding Passage (Genesis 1:31): "Then God saw everything that He had made, and indeed it was very good." (Gen. 1:31)

Exegesis and Connection: This is God’s very first definition of "good" in Scripture: the source of all good is God's creation, not man's grasping. The serpent later deceived Eve by claiming that "eating it will make you like God," as though God were holding back good things from man; yet Genesis 1 had already proclaimed that everything God created was "very good"—He has never been a stingy God. Doubting God's goodness is the starting point of all temptation; being fully convinced of God's goodness is the starting point of all victory.

📜 Corresponding Psalms Passage and Exegesis

Corresponding Passage (Psalm 34:8): "Oh, taste and see that the LORD is good;" (Ps. 34:8)

Exegesis and Connection: The word David uses is "taste"—goodness is not mere theory; it can be tasted. Many people refuse to let go of the pleasures of sin because they have never truly tasted the goodness of the Lord. Those who have tasted it know: the pleasures of sin are like chewing sugarcane pulp, growing more flavorless the longer you chew; but the goodness of the Lord is like living water, quenching your thirst more deeply with every drink.

📜 Corresponding Proverbs Passage and Exegesis

Corresponding Passage (Proverbs 10:22): "The blessing of the LORD makes one rich, And He adds no sorrow with it." (Prov. 10:22)

Exegesis and Connection: This is the touchstone for testing the authenticity of any "benefit." The blessing God gives is characterized by "He adds no sorrow with it"; the "benefits" temptation offers feel exhilarating in the moment but bring panic afterward—fearing an audit, fearing exposure, fearing retribution. Any "benefit" that keeps you living on edge did not come down from the Father of lights.

💡 Application in China's Contemporary Reality

Businesspeople (Seeing through the disguise of "quick money"): Someone invites you to join an illegal Ponzi scheme or cook the books to evade taxes, promising that "your money will double in three months." Test it against Proverbs 10:22: Does making this money allow you to sleep peacefully at night? If it keeps you awake, it is not the blessing of the Lord. God's gifts never leave you living in anxiety and dread.

Full-time homemakers (Seeing through the disguise of "comparison"): On WeChat Moments, others show off luxury handbags, their kids' achievements, and elite school district homes. Bitterness creeps into your heart, and you feel compelled to push your husband and pressure your children to reach for those things. Please meditate on God's goodness: of the gifts in your home that come "from above"—your children's health, your husband's peace, a family gathered around simple meals—which of them could ever be bought with money? Counting your blessings is a homemaker's sharpest sword to overcome the temptation of comparison.

Working women (Seeing through the disguise of "compromise"): Your supervisor hints that if you cater to clients and flatter the right "connections," promotion and a raise will be yours. Please remember: any "benefit" that requires you to compromise your body or speak lies is a counterfeit. A daughter of the Father of lights does not obtain gifts by trading away her dignity. The door He opens for you, no one can shut; the door He does not open for you is a trap even if you force your way in.

The elderly (Seeing through health product scams and the temptation to control adult children): A salesperson calls you "Dad" or "Mom," claiming this mattress cures all diseases; or you might be tempted to use "Look how much I sacrificed for you" to manipulate your children into compliance. Meditate on God's goodness: your value does not lie in your children's submission, nor in the flattering words of a salesperson, but in the unchanging love of the Father of lights. His gifts are "perfect," requiring you neither to be swindled nor to exert control in exchange for them.

(48:00 - 58:00)

Seeing through the traps and unmasking the disguises still leaves one final step—the most critical step of all: having a new nature within. For the reason temptation triumphs time and again is that our old corrupt desires still reside inside us; to overcome them, a new life must take up residence within.

Please look with me at James 1:18: "Of His own will He brought us forth by the word of truth, that we might be a kind of firstfruits of His creatures." (Jas. 1:18)

📖 Scripture Exegesis 3: James 1:18 (The Birth of the New Life)

**Original Language Analysis:** In this verse, "brought us forth" is in Greek *Apekyēsen* (transliteration: a-pe-ku-e-sen). Please take note: this word shares the exact same root as "brings forth" (*Apokyeō*, transliteration: a-po-ku-e-o) in verse 15, "when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death"! James does this intentionally: lust "brings forth" death, yet God "brought us forth" by the word of truth—two instances of "childbirth," two vastly different outcomes. One brings forth death, while the other brings forth life.

"By the word of truth"—"the word of truth" in Greek is *Logos alētheias* (transliteration: lo-gos a-le-thei-as), meaning "the word of truth." How does this new life come about? It is not through ascetic discipline, nor through self-willed determination; it is God working a labor of "childbirth" within us through His word of truth.

"Firstfruits" in Greek is *Aparchē* (transliteration: a-par-che). In the Old Testament, the firstfruits were the first harvest to ripen, specially consecrated and offered to God (Num. 15:20–21). James is saying that we who have been brought forth by the word of truth are, among all God's creatures, that very harvest of firstfruits—the portion God values most, treasures most, and has set apart as holy.

**Exegesis and Spiritual Mechanism:** The previous two verses focused on what "not" to do—do not blame God, do not be deceived; this verse focuses on what "to" do—meditate on the fact that you already have a new nature within you. The formula for temptation is: old lust + external bait = stumbling. But God has given us a new formula: new life + the word of truth = victory.

Please observe how verse 18 begins: "Of His own will He brought us forth"—the source of this new life is God’s will, not your willpower. This is precisely why human resolutions cannot break addictions, and vows cannot bring transformation: the old lust was "brought forth" by birth, and it can only be overcome by another "birth"—by letting God bring forth a brand-new you within through the word of truth.

The very first aspect of this power he listed was "the power to overcome sin and lead people to repentance." Brothers and sisters, overcoming temptation does not come from "sounding eloquent in doctrine"; it relies on the power of the Holy Spirit actively working within us. This is precisely what James means by "brought us forth by the word of truth": when the word of truth enters the heart, it releases practical power to overcome sin. (Source: Silicon Valley River of Life Christian Church, *Devotions with Pastor Tong Liu: 1 Corinthians 4:17-21*)

He also emphasized that genuinely powerful preaching must bring the "biblical word" into the "specifics of daily life," speaking directly into people’s concrete circumstances rather than offering "panacea-style" platitudes. Brothers and sisters, this is precisely what being "firstfruits" means: we are not people who preach one thing and live another—God first births new life within us, and only then can we lead others into a place of victory. 📜 **Corresponding Passage in Ezekiel and Exegesis**

Corresponding Scripture (Ezek. 36:26-27): "I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you... I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes, and you will keep My judgments and do them."

Exegesis and Connection: This is the Old Testament counterpart to James 1:18. What God promises is not "to hand you a new set of rules," but "to give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you." Notice the cause-and-effect relationship in verse 27: God first places His Spirit within us, and that "causes" us to obey—the power to overcome temptation always follows this order: new life first, new behavior second. If this sequence is reversed, it degenerates into relying on the flesh for moral cultivation.

📜 **Corresponding Passage in Psalms and Exegesis**

Corresponding Scripture (Ps. 119:9, 11): "How can a young man cleanse his way? By taking heed according to Your word... Your word I have hidden in my heart, that I might not sin against You."

Exegesis and Connection: David provides us with the practical spiritual discipline that follows "being birthed into new life by the word of truth": "hide" God's word in your heart. In the original language, "hide" carries the sense of "treasuring up, storing away," like storing grain in a barn in preparation for famine. When temptation strikes, whether you have "provisions" stored in your heart determines whether you gain victory or stumble into defeat. Reading the Bible daily is not checking off a chore; it is stockpiling ammunition in your heart.

📜 **Corresponding Passage in Proverbs and Exegesis**

Corresponding Scripture (Prov. 28:13): "He who covers his sins will not prosper, but whoever confesses and forsakes them will have mercy."

Exegesis and Connection: The new life bears a distinctive hallmark: it no longer covers up. The instinct of the old lust is to hide—the very first thing Adam and Eve did after sinning was to look for fig leaves; the instinct of the new life is to confess—to confess to God, and to confess to trusted fellow believers. Many people sink deeper and deeper into temptation not because the temptation is overwhelmingly strong, but because they keep "covering it up." Confession is the very way the new life breathes.

💡 **Application to the Contemporary Context in China**

Students (Overcoming the Bondage of Smartphones and Habits): Staying up late scrolling through phones, addicted to gaming, unable to put the phone down even while knowing they won't be able to wake up the next morning. Please consider: you already have new life within you; you are not "helpless." Practical exercise: One hour before going to bed, place your phone in the living room, and tape Psalm 119:11 right above your bed—let the "word hidden in your heart" take charge of the "phone held in your hand."

Workplace Women (Overcoming Office Politics and Emotional Temptations): Talking behind people's backs, forming cliques, or dealing with ambiguous, flirtatious attention from married male colleagues. You are "firstfruits," set apart as holy. The office environment may be chaotic, but you must not compromise; others may spread gossip, but you will not pass it on. Emotionally, remember Proverbs 28:13: the moment a spark appears in your heart, confess it to God and confide in a sister in Christ; do not cover it up—covering up is the way of the old man, but confession is the way of the new man.

The Elderly (Overcoming Bitterness, Greed, and Judgmentalism): Feeling bitter when children do not visit often, harboring grudges when neighbors take advantage, or frequently criticizing the youth in the church. The promise of Ezekiel 36 is given to "you," regardless of age. Ask God every morning to grant you a new heart: count not the offenses of others today, but count only the grace of God. An elderly person with a new spirit in their heart is a blessing to the entire family, not a burden upon them.

(58:00 - 60:00)

Speaker:
Brothers and sisters, today we looked together at James chapter 1, verses 13 through 18, and three principles for overcoming temptation:

First, **Meditate on God's judgment**—discern the four-step trap of temptation: desire → deception → disobedience → death. Let Jesus answer the door at that very first step; He will always prevail.

Second, **Meditate on God's goodness**—unmask counterfeit blessings. Every good and perfect gift comes from above; any "benefit" that requires you to sin in exchange is an absolute fake.

Third, **Meditate on God's nature within you**—let the new life triumph over the old lust. Lust "brings forth" death, but God has "brought us forth" by the word of truth. You are the firstfruits; within you dwells the life of victory.

Temptation will not vanish simply because you believe in the Lord, but it no longer has the power to dictate your destiny. Because your destiny has already been written in James 1:18—you are a person brought forth by the word of truth.

Finally, let us bow our heads together in prayer:

"Dear Heavenly Father, thank You for illuminating our hearts today with Your word. We confess that we are often weak before temptation, often quick to shift the blame to You, and often prone to doubt Your goodness. Lord, forgive us.

From this day forward, we desire to learn to meditate on Your judgment, slamming on the brakes the moment desire begins; we desire to learn to meditate on Your goodness, no longer deceived by disguised benefits; and even more, we desire to meditate on the new nature You have already placed within us—that we are the firstfruits, brought forth by Your word of truth.

Holy Spirit, we ask You to take over our feeble prayers; when we know not what to say, make intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered. We pray that You would hide Your word in our hearts daily, that we may have the strength to say 'no' to temptation, and say 'yes' to You.

Thank You, Lord. In the victorious name of Jesus Christ we pray, Amen!"
