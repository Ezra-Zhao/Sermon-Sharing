## Content

How are we brought into relation with God? The highest point of the complete salvation is that God wants to bring us out of [Adam], so that we may be one with Him — not only obtaining union in eternal life, but also having its expression in this present life.

Brought into Christ (Adam; by faith (John)). 1. Grafted into Christ: "[You were] cut out of the olive tree which is wild by nature, and were grafted contrary to nature into a cultivated olive tree" (Rom. 11:24, NKJV). 2. Joined to Christ: "For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive" (1 Cor. 15:22, NKJV). 3. Brought into Christ: "Or do you not know that as many of us as were baptized into Christ Jesus [were baptized into His death]?" (Rom. 6:3, NKJV). In God's eyes there are only two men: the first man and the second man. The first man is the first Adam; he brought disobedience, [sin, and death; original text partially illegible]. The second man is Jesus Christ; He brought obedience, justification, and eternal life. "For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive" (1 Cor. 15:22, NKJV). [Salvation] here is to save us out of Adam, into Christ — grafted into Christ's life and brought under Christ's name (Romans).

Filled with the Holy Spirit (the flesh; by love (Rom.)). 1. Receiving the law of the Spirit: "For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free [from the law of sin and death]" (Rom. 8:2, NKJV). 2. Obeying the Spirit's moving: "Do not quench the Spirit" (1 Thess. 5:19, NKJV). 3. Submitting to the Spirit's leading: "Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh" (Gal. 5:16, NKJV). 4. Minding the things of the Spirit: "For those who live according to the Spirit [set their minds on] the things of the Spirit" (Rom. 8:5, NKJV). [Being] in Adam is objective; [being] in the flesh is subjective. The former is a matter of position; the latter is a matter of slavery (Romans). God not only wants us to be separated from Adam; God also wants to save us from the flesh. On the one hand God wants us to break with Adam (Joshua), and on the other hand He wants us to crucify the flesh (Galatians). For "if we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit" (Gal. 5:25, NKJV). Let the Spirit's moving replace our own ideas, and let the things of the Spirit come first. In this way, in the enmity and warfare of the lusts, we can overcome the flesh and live in the Holy Spirit. This is the salvation God wants us to enjoy today: causing us not to follow the flesh, but only to follow the Holy Spirit.

(Our daily duty is to manifest the power of the Holy Spirit, a power that operates through a life whose will is surrendered.) Hidden with God the Father (in man). 1. Hidden in God: "for you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God" (Col. 3:3, NKJV). 2. Entering into glory: "[bringing] many sons to glory" (Heb. 2:10, NKJV). 3. Made completely one: "[that they may be one, just as We are one:] I in them, and You in Me; that they may be made perfect in one" (John 17:22-23, NKJV). The final completion is the creature entering into the Creator. From the standpoint of past position, we are saved out of Adam into Christ; from the standpoint of present experience, we are saved out of the flesh into the Holy Spirit; from the standpoint of final completion, we are saved out of created man into the creating God. This mystery still awaits its future realization, but even now we have already experienced it in part on earth.

Homework questions: In God's eyes there are only two men — which two? What did Adam bring? What did Christ bring? How does man pass from Adam into Christ? What is the difference between being in Adam and being in the flesh? Since the Holy Spirit and the flesh are opposed, how can we overcome ourselves? Do you have the experience of the Holy Spirit's moving and leading? What is the final completion of salvation?
