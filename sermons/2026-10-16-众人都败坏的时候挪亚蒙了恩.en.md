## Content Summary (Approx. 100 words)

Genesis 6 portrays humanity before the Flood: wickedness was great, every intent of the thoughts of the heart was only evil continually, and the earth was filled with violence. God was grieved over the human beings He had made, yet right in the midst of this darkest picture He wrote a turning point—Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD. This sermon examines three things: when God looks at man, He looks first at what is thought within the heart; grace was not earned by Noah, yet it enabled him to walk with God when everyone else was corrupt; and those who have received grace must set their hands to prepare—the ark was built cubit by cubit through obedience before the rain ever fell.

## (00:00 - 08:00) Opening · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Peace be with you, brothers and sisters!

In the previous sermon, we finished reading the genealogy in Genesis 5. Walking through ten generations, it finally stopped at one name: Noah.

Today we turn to chapter 6. Frankly, the beginning of this chapter contains some of the heaviest verses in the entire Bible. How wicked must humanity become before the Bible renders such a verdict? Yet this chapter is also where grace is explicitly named for the first time—the phrase "found grace" appears for the very first time in this darkest of chapters.

The darkest background and the brightest word are placed together in the very same chapter. This is no coincidence; this is how the Gospel is written.

Let us first read the key passages:

"Then the LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. And the LORD was sorry that He had made man on the earth, and He was grieved in His heart." (Gen. 6:5-6)

"But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD." (Gen. 6:8)

"Noah was a just man, perfect in his generations. Noah walked with God." (Gen. 6:9)

"And God said to Noah, 'The end of all flesh has come before Me, for the earth is filled with violence through them; and behold, I will destroy them with the earth. Make yourself an ark of gopherwood; ... And behold, I Myself am bringing floodwaters on the earth, to destroy from under heaven all flesh in which is the breath of life; everything that is on the earth shall die. But I will establish My covenant with you; and you shall go into the ark—you, your sons, your wife, and your sons’ wives with you.'" (Gen. 6:13-14, 17-18, excerpted)

"Thus Noah did; according to all that God commanded him, so he did." (Gen. 6:22)

Today we will divide this into three sections:

First, 6:5-7, 11-12, the world as God saw it—wickedness was great, every intent of the thoughts of man's heart was only evil continually, the earth was filled with violence, and God was grieved in His heart;
Second, 6:8-10, Noah alone found grace—grace came first, and only then do we find the record of a righteous man, a blameless man, and one who walked with God;
Third, 6:13-22, the command of the ark and Noah's obedience—God clearly detailed the dimensions, the materials, the covenant, and the checklist for preparation, and Noah did according to all that God commanded.

Let us bow our heads in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. When humanity fell into utter wickedness, You were not silent. You were grieved, yet You also showed grace. We pray that today, through this lesson of Noah, You would illuminate the thoughts of our hearts, and teach us to be those who, having received grace, are willing to put our hands to work and prepare—to obey even before the rain begins to fall. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

## (08:00 - 24:00) Part 1: What God Looks at Is What Man Thinks Continually (6:5-7, 11-12)

### 1.1 "Every Intent of the Thoughts of His Heart Was Only Evil Continually": God Looks First at the Factory of the Heart

The judgment rendered upon that generation in chapter 6 falls first on the outside and then on the inside—though in reality, the order is reversed. The text first says: "Then the LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth" (Gen. 6:5). Wickedness was great speaks of the scale; then comes the cause: "and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually."

"Intent of the thoughts," in Hebrew *yetser* (יֵצֶר), refers to inclination, intention, that which is formed or fashioned in the heart, deriving from the root meaning "to form, shape, or fashion"[1]. Just as a potter shapes a vessel on the wheel, so man's heart fashions thoughts every single day. What God looks at here is not merely what man has done, but what the factory of the human heart is manufacturing all day long. "Continually" means every day, all the time; "only evil" means there is no other product.

External actions are the fruit; the thoughts within the heart are the root. In chapter 4, when God warned Cain, He warned him at the threshold of the door; by chapter 6, the hearts of an entire generation had become corrupted together, and fruit naturally littered the ground. The Lord Jesus later stated this line with utmost clarity: "For out of the heart proceed evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false witness, blasphemies" (Matt. 15:19). Reading Proverbs 4:23 again here carries even greater weight: "Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life."

Brothers and sisters, let us examine our own factory today: when your phone is not in hand, what does your mind automatically gravitate toward? When no one is watching, what scripts are being rehearsed in your heart? When God looked at Noah's generation, this is what He looked at first. This is what we too must examine first.

### 1.2 The Earth Was Filled with Violence: Sin Is Never Merely an Individual Matter

Verses 11 and 12 use a different term: "The earth also was corrupt before God, and the earth was filled with violence. So God looked upon the earth, and indeed it was corrupt; for all flesh had corrupted their way on the earth."

"Violence," in Hebrew *chamas* (חָמָס), signifies violence, cruelty, and unjust oppression[2]. This word appears repeatedly in the Prophets later on: the sign of a society's deepest corruption is not a lack of religious activity, but that people laying hands on one another has become the norm, oppression is treated as capability, and bloodshed is treated as a solution.

Please notice a detail in God's word: "So God looked upon the earth, and indeed it was corrupt." Corruption was not a report God heard second-hand; it was the conclusion of God looking upon it Himself. Man can polish his behavior for others to see, and hide his motives from human sight, but God beholds the whole picture. Those recurring words "and he died" throughout the genealogy of chapter 5 are the fruit of death; the corruption of chapter 6 is the rotting that precedes death. Sin first rots the heart, then rots behavior, and finally rots an entire generation.

### 1.3 "And the LORD Was Sorry... and He Was Grieved in His Heart": Before Judgment, There Are God's Tears

Verse 6 is the most surprising sentence in the entire chapter: "And the LORD was sorry that He had made man on the earth, and He was grieved in His heart."

"Was sorry," in Hebrew *nacham* (נָחַם), here refers to God grieving and sighing over man's corruption[3]—the very same root used when Lamech named Noah seeking "comfort" in the previous passage. This root appears on two opposite sides within a few verses: on one side, a father, Lamech, longs for comfort; on the other side, God grieves and sighs because of human corruption. Man's hope and God's grief meet before the Flood.

The English translations of this verse are worth comparing:
- KJV: "And it repented the LORD that he had made man on the earth, and it grieved him at his heart."
- NIV: "The LORD regretted that he had made human beings on the earth, and his heart was deeply troubled."
- NRSV: "And the LORD was sorry that he had made humankind on the earth, and it grieved him to his heart."

The three translations use different wording, but their common thread is that they all place the emphasis on the grief in God's heart. Numbers says, "God is not a man, that He should lie" (Num. 23:19); God does not change His character or His promises. The "repentance" / "being sorry" in Genesis 6 is the Bible using language human beings can comprehend to depict God's genuine grief when confronted with human corruption. The prophet Ezekiel later articulated this heart completely: "'As I live,' says the Lord GOD, 'I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn from his way and live'" (Ezek. 33:11).

So we must see the order clearly: the flood is real, judgment is real; but before judgment, there is grief first. God is not a cold-faced judge bent on destroying the world; He is first a Father whose heart is broken. Psalm 78:40 says of the Israelites, "How often they provoked Him in the wilderness, and grieved Him in the desert!"—grief is God's constant heart toward rebellious mankind.

To sum up Part One in a single sentence: **When God looks at a generation, He looks first at what is in the heart, and then at what is done on the earth; and His heart before judgment is grief. To preach the flood apart from this heart is to present God wrongly.**

## (24:00 - 42:00) Part Two: But Noah Found Grace in the Eyes of the Lord (6:8-10)

### 2.1 The Turning Point of the Entire Chapter Is Just One Sentence

Verses 5 through 7 are the news of doom; verses 11 through 13 are the proclamation of destruction. Tucked right in the middle, verse 8 is the pivot of the whole chapter:

"But Noah found grace in the eyes of the LORD." (Gen. 6:8)

"Grace," in Hebrew *chen* (חֵן), means grace, favor, being well-pleasing[4]. This is the very first time the word "grace" officially appears in the Bible. Where does it appear? Not in the Garden of Eden, not after Enoch walked with God for three hundred years—it appears when humanity has become corrupt to the extreme and God has already proclaimed judgment.

Even more important is the order. Scripture first says that Noah found grace, and only in the next verse does it say, "Noah was a just man, perfect in his generations. Noah walked with God" (Gen. 6:9). Grace first, then character; receiving grace first, then walking with God. It was not that Noah first succeeded in being a righteous man and only then did God grant him grace; rather, grace came to him first, and only then did he live out a different kind of life in that generation. Ephesians 2:8 brings this order into the New Testament: "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God."

Brothers and sisters, this truth is the foundation of the entire message today. Many people live out their faith completely backward: first striving desperately to prove they are good enough, and then waiting for God to show grace. The order of Genesis 6:8 is the exact opposite—while all others were corrupt, and while Noah was born into that very same corrupt generation, grace arrived first. Finding grace is not a certificate of merit; it is the starting point.

### 2.2 "Perfect in His Generations": Perfection Is Not Flawlessness, but Refusing to Go with the Flow

Verse 9 gives Noah three commendations: a just man, perfect, and one who walked with God.

"Perfect," in Hebrew *tamim* (תָּמִים), means complete, upright, without blemish; the same word is used of sacrificial animals that are entirely without defect[5]. Noah's "perfection" does not mean he never made a mistake—later in chapter 9 he would stumble. Perfection means his heart was undivided: not half for God and half for the world; not one way in front of people and another way behind their backs. "In his generations"—please pay attention to these three words—when everyone else was corrupt, his whole being was oriented toward God.

Hebrews 11:7 supplies the vital brushstroke to Noah's commendation: "By faith Noah, being divinely warned of things not yet seen, moved with godly fear, prepared an ark for the saving of his household, by which he condemned the world and became heir of the righteousness which is according to faith." In the eyes of the crowd, Noah was probably an eccentric misfit: building a ship on a sunny day, mocked by everyone, yet he kept building day after day. Those who walk with God in a corrupt generation are often first regarded as not fitting in. Fitting in does not matter; having the right direction is what matters.

Warren W. Wiersbe, in his commentary on Genesis when explaining Noah, reminds readers to pay attention to this biblical order of grace preceding character, and that Noah's righteousness was a righteousness wrought through obedience after having received grace. (Source: summary of relevant teaching from Warren W. Wiersbe's *The Bible Exposition Commentary: Genesis*, paraphrased and cited, not a direct quotation)

### 2.3 Three Sons: For the One Who Receives Grace, Grace Falls First on His Household

Verse 10: "And Noah begot three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth."

The genealogy in chapter 5 said the same thing, and here it is stated again. The post-flood world was to begin anew from this single household. Grace fell upon Noah, but it did not stop with him alone; it was to pass through him and fall upon his wife, his sons, and his sons' wives (Gen. 6:18). Joshua's later declaration, "But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Josh. 24:15), traces its source all the way back here: a person who has received grace first brings his own household into the covenant.

To sum up Part Two: **Noah's distinction was not that he was born into a good generation, but that in an evil generation he first received grace, and he did not let that grace stop with himself.**

Here we need to ignite the heart.

Brothers and sisters, the word "only" may very well fall upon you today.

In your office, in your industry, even in your family, the culture is just like that: words are spoken carelessly, books are cooked, people step on one another. You are the only one who wants to stay a little cleaner, a little more upright, a little more prayerful—and you feel like an outcast.

I want to tell you in the name of the Lord: being an outcast is the mark of grace. Noah was also the only one. Enoch was also one of the few who walked with God in his generation. Being "only" is not something to fear; what is truly fearful is selling away grace just to go with the flow, all so that you won't be the "only" one. Today, stand firm right where you are—while all around you are corrupt, there is still a word reserved for you in heaven: but you have found grace in the eyes of the Lord.

## (42:00 - 52:00) Part Three: Before the Rain Falls, Start Building—The Commandment of the Ark and Noah's Obedience (6:13-22)

### 3.1 God Makes His Word Plain: Materials, Dimensions, Structure—Not One Thing Is Left Vague

In verse 13, God delivers the judgment and the salvation together: "The end of all flesh has come before Me... and behold, I will destroy them with the earth. Make yourself an ark of gopherwood."

The moment the decree of judgment falls, the commandment of salvation immediately follows. God did not merely give Noah bad news; He gave him a blueprint: "And this is how you shall make it: The length of the ark shall be three hundred cubits, its width fifty cubits, and its height thirty cubits... You shall make it with lower, second, and third decks." (Gen. 6:15-16, excerpted) The material was also specified: gopherwood—a type of timber whose exact species has been debated throughout history; this manuscript follows the Chinese Union Version translation and avoids unverified claims[6]; it was to be covered inside and outside with pitch. "Pitch" in Hebrew is *kopher* (כֹּפֶר), meaning a covering, sharing the same root as the ransom that "covers sin"; see the notes for lexical details[7]—the ark was coated inside and out with a covering substance, and those who entered the ark were covered people. This imagery is a sermon in itself.

God never does things vaguely. What He gave Noah was not a vague feeling, but an actionable checklist: what wood, what size, how many decks, who enters, and what to eat (6:21, to gather all food that is eaten). Today, we should receive God's word in the same way: not merely being emotionally stirred after listening, but writing it down point by point, and living it out point by point.

### 3.2 "But I Will Establish My Covenant with You": In the Midst of Judgment, the Covenant Is Established First

Verse 18 marks the very first appearance of "covenant" in the Bible: "But I will establish My covenant with you; and you shall go into the ark—you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you."

"Covenant," in Hebrew *berit* (בְּרִית), means a solemn league or covenant[8]. Please notice the timing: the flood had not yet come, but the covenant was established first; the rain had not yet fallen, but the place for the household was already settled. God's covenant is not an afterthought or an emergency repair plan; it is the way of salvation laid out before judgment arrives. The covenant with Abraham in Genesis 15, the covenant in Genesis 17, the Sinai covenant in Exodus, all the way to the Lord Jesus lifting the cup at the Last Supper and saying, "For this is My blood of the new covenant" (Matt. 26:28)—this thread of covenant began to be drawn right here with Noah.

Psalm 25:14 says, "The secret of the LORD is with those who fear Him, and He will show them His covenant." This is precisely what Noah received: God revealed the future, revealed the covenant, and revealed the way of preparation beforehand to this man who walked with Him. Those who draw near to God are the first to know the movements of heaven—not because they are cleverer than others, but because they are close to God.

### 3.3 "According to All That God Commanded Him, So He Did": Obedience Has a Checklist

The conclusion of this chapter is the finest commendation in the whole chapter: "Thus Noah did; according to all that God commanded him, so he did." (Gen. 6:22)

"According to all"—these words embody the entirety of obedience. Not picking and choosing, not doing the easy parts first, not waiting until the storm clouds gather before taking action. Hebrews says that he was "moved with godly fear, prepared an ark" (Heb. 11:7)—godly fear was the reason Noah set his hands to work; preparation was what that fear looked like when it hit the ground. Second Peter also gives him a title: "Noah, one of eight people, a preacher of righteousness" (2 Pet. 2:5). During all those years of building the ark, he built while he preached: telling people on bright, sunny days that judgment was coming, that the covenant was already established, and that there was still time to enter the ark.

To sum up Part Three: **After grace came upon Noah, it did not stop at being an emotional stir; it turned into timber, dimensions, and the sound of hammering year after year. The one who has received grace is the one who sets his hands to work before the rain ever falls.**

## (52:00 - 60:00) Applications for Six Groups of People · Altar Call · Response Prayer

### Application 1: For Seekers Who Have Not Yet Believed in the Lord

Friend, today please listen first to God's heart, and only then listen to the judgment. God grieves over human corruption; before judgment, He first established a covenant, first prepared an ark, and first made His word plain.

The story of Noah is not meant to make you fear the waters; it is meant to let you see that God's order of doing things is to first prepare the way of salvation, and only then execute judgment. Today, this way of salvation has already been set before you—it is Jesus Christ. The door to enter the ark remains open until the rain falls. While the door is still open, come inside today.

### Application 2: For Husbands, Wives, Parents, and Children in Marriage and Family

Noah found grace, and that grace rested upon his entire household. The lesson for parents today is: first bring your home into the covenant.

You don't need to wait until the whole family is "ready" before beginning. Starting this week, establish a regular family prayer time, even if it is only ten minutes. Place this verse from Genesis 6:8 on the dining table, and memorize it together as a family: only those who find grace in the eyes of the Lord have the strength not to drift along with a corrupt culture. The world your children will face in the future will not be easier than Noah's generation; what you prepare for them today is their ark.

### Application 3: For Those in the Workplace and Business

The workplace is today's "own generation." A bad culture does not mean you may follow along with the evil. "Everyone is doing it" has never been an excuse in Genesis 6; rather, it was precisely the backdrop for judgment.

Here is a practical exercise for fellow workers: make an "ark checklist"—what are the things you already clearly know God has commanded, yet you have been putting off? An honest audit, a partnership you should refuse, an account that needs to be cleared up, a word of truth that must be spoken. Write them down one by one, set deadlines, and do according to all of them. Obedience is not abstract; it has a checklist and a schedule.

### Application 4: For New Believers

Friends new to the faith, learn from Noah the order that follows finding grace: first grace, then character; first receive, then build.

Do not try to trade performance for grace. You have already found grace; what you are to do now is, like Noah, live out that grace in obedience: one small thing God has commanded, do that one thing today. Bible reading, prayer, forgiveness, giving, returning to church—the ark was hammered together plank by plank, and the spiritual life is also built through obedience in one small thing after another.

### Application 5: For Leaders, Parents, and Those in Ministry

Leaders must learn two things from Noah: preaching righteousness and preparing the ark.

To offer only warnings without preparation is merely scaring people; to prepare without giving warning is a dereliction of duty. Noah preached while he built. What does the team you lead, your small group, or your children need you to prepare most today? Is it successors? Is it a contingency plan? Is it the foundation of faith for the next generation? Taking action before the rain begins to fall is a leader's most vital reverence.

### Application 6: For the Whole Church

The church is the ark of this generation. Our mission is not to stand on the shore commenting on how high the waters are; it is to keep the door open and welcome people in.

At the same time, the church must examine itself: have the thoughts and intents of our hearts also been assimilated by this generation? May we be a church that first finds grace, is then set apart, and is willing to take action and prepare—our programs may be simple, but the grace must be real; our scale may not be large, but the door must stay open.

### Ignition Moment

(Pause; slow the pace of speech)

Brothers and sisters, when Noah was building the ark, there was not a single rain cloud in the sky.

With every strike of his hammer, the neighbors could hear it, and they all laughed out loud. There was no applause, no understanding—only a command and a covenant-keeping God.

(Pause; speak with greater weight)

What is your ark? It is that very thing you know God wants you to do: returning to His presence, bringing your family into prayer, laying down that dishonest income, beginning to prepare the foundation of faith for the next generation.

It is today, not tomorrow. The rain will not wait until you feel ready before it falls. Grace has already arrived, the covenant has already been established, the command has already been made plain—all that is missing now is your first hammer strike. Take action today; even if you nail down just one plank of wood, heaven records it: according to all that God commanded him, so he did.

### Altar Call

I am making two calls:

**The First Call**: You know clearly in your heart that there is something God commanded long ago that you have been putting off—if you are willing today to delay no longer and go home to start preparing, please stand up, and we will pray for you together. Standing up is not a performance; it is claiming that first hammer strike before God.

**The Second Call**: You have not yet believed in the Lord, but today you heard that God first grieved, first covenanted, and first prepared the way of salvation. If you are willing to receive Jesus and enter into the salvation He has prepared for you, please raise your hand, and I want to pray for you. The door is still open; today is the day to enter the ark.

(Wait for response; pray for those standing and raising their hands)

### Concluding Response Prayer

Let us bow our heads together in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. When every intent of the thoughts of man's heart was only evil continually, and the earth was filled with violence, You were grieved in Your heart, yet You did not turn away and leave. Before judgment, You first made a covenant, first prepared an ark, and first caused grace to rest upon Noah.

Lord, we confess that our hearts are also frequently pulled by this generation; what we think and calculate may not always align with Your heart. We ask You to shine Your light into the factory of our hearts, tear down those corners that produce evil thoughts continually, and replace them with Your word.

We also thank You for Your grace. It is not that we were first good enough and then You showed grace; rather, Your grace arrived first, and only then did we learn to be just, blameless, and to walk with You. Please give us Noah's reverence and his hands and feet: that when we hear, we note it down; when we note it down, we schedule it; when we schedule it, we take action before the rain has even fallen.

May our families enter the covenant first, and may our church become the ark of this generation—with the door kept open, the light burning bright, welcoming people in one by one.

In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

## Chapter Endnotes

[1] yetser: Hebrew יֵצֶר (*yetser*), Strong's H3336, thought, inclination, that which is framed in the mind; related to the root for "to form, frame" (*yatsar*, H3335); definitions according to BDB and HALOT.

[2] chamas: Hebrew חָמָס (*chamas*), Strong's H2555, violence, wrong, injustice; see Gen. 6:11, 13.

[3] nacham: Hebrew נָחַם (*nacham*), Strong's H5162, to comfort, regret, grieve, or sigh; in Gen. 6:6 it refers to God being grieved over human corruption, sharing the same root as "comfort" in Gen. 5:29; its theological meaning is understood in balance with passages such as Num. 23:19 and Ezek. 33:11; Lu Zhenzhong's translation of this verse is referenced (his text is not directly quoted in this manuscript).

[4] chen: Hebrew חֵן (*chen*), Strong's H2580, grace, favor, finding favor in one's eyes; Gen. 6:8 is the first occurrence of this word in Genesis describing "found grace in the eyes of the Lord" (the scope of this statement is limited to the book of Genesis).

[5] tamim: Hebrew תָּמִים (*tamim*), Strong's H8549, blameless, without blemish, upright; see Gen. 6:9; also used of sacrificial animals requiring freedom from blemish.

[6] gopher (gopherwood): Hebrew גֹּפֶר (*gopher*), Strong's H1613, found only in Gen. 6:14; the exact tree species remains historically inconclusive; this manuscript adopts the Chinese Union Version rendering "gopherwood."

[7] kopher (pitch): Hebrew כֹּפֶר (*kopher*), Strong's H3724; the original text refers to the pitch-like substance used to coat the ark, translated as "pine resin" in the Chinese Union Version and "pitch" in English; closely related to the root for "cover" (*kaphar*, H3722), and also used in the sense of a ransom (e.g., related terminology in Ex. 30:12).

[8] berit: Hebrew בְּרִית (*berit*), Strong's H1285, covenant, alliance; Gen. 6:18, "I will establish My covenant with you," marks the first appearance of the covenant concept in Scripture.
