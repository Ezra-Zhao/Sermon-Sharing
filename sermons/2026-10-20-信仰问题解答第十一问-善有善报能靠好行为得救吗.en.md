# Faith Q&A Question 11: Good Is Rewarded with Good — That Is True, but Can a Person Be Saved by Good Works?

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «善有善报是真的，那靠好行为能得救吗？».

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 11
**Adaptation:** Guangyi Zhao (retold in his own preaching style; the core views belong to the original author)

---

## Scripture

> "Who 'will render to each one according to his deeds.'" (Rom. 2:6, NKJV)

> "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast." (Eph. 2:8–9, NKJV)

> "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." (Rom. 3:23, NKJV)

---

## I. Acknowledge This Half First: Retribution Is Real

The person asking asked two things, which in fact must be answered separately. The first: does God really render retribution according to men's deeds? Is it true that good is rewarded with good and evil with evil?

Pastor Tong's answer is unambiguous: **it is true.** God Himself has said that He will render to each one according to his deeds (Rom. 2:6); these words cannot be discounted. If a person's evildoing had no consequences and his well-doing made no difference, that would not be called love; it would be called the absence of righteousness. Acknowledge the retribution half first; there is no need to take it back on God's behalf.

The hard part is the second thing: if retribution is reckoned by deeds, then if I do more good deeds, can I earn salvation back? What need is there to believe in Jesus?

It is right here that Pastor Tong turns: retribution is real, **but being saved by works is false.** The two sentences hold at the same time; the key lies in the standard.

**Original-language analysis:** "Render" in Romans 2:6 is ***Apodidōmi* (ἀποδίδωμι, Strong G591)**, meaning to hand back in full what is due, to repay according to the account (per BDAG). When Paul uses this word, he is saying that God settles accounts accurately, not a fraction off. The problem arises precisely here: the more accurately the account is settled, the less man can stand — because what comes next is the question of by whose standard the account is settled.

## II. What Stops Everyone Is That the Standards Differ

Pastor Tong puts it very directly: **however well you do, it is men who see you as good; when God looks, it is not good enough.**

When people compare among themselves, they compare with one another. You are more decent than your neighbor, more diligent than your colleague; in the human row, you count as a good person, and that judgment is not wrong. But the standard of salvation is not your ranking in the human crowd; it is God's own glory. Romans 3:23 sets the target there: "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." Note, everyone falls short; not a single exception is left out.

Isaiah puts it even more unsparingly: "But we are all like an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are like filthy rags" (Isa. 64:6). Even our best righteousness, brought before God, is of this quality. That does not mean good works are worthless; it means they cannot get past the gate of salvation.

Pastor Tong's conclusion lands here: if man could be saved by his own goodness, Jesus would not have needed to come at all. **It is precisely because, however good man is, he is not good enough that Jesus came into the world and died for you and for me.** The cross itself is the evidence: for a matter that could be settled by oneself, God would not settle it with the life of His Son.

**Original-language analysis:** "Sin" in Greek is ***Hamartia* (ἁμαρτία, Strong G266)**; the verb *Hamartanō* (G264) originally carries the sense of missing the bullseye. Sin is not only having done a bad deed; it is the whole person missing the target he should have hit — and that target, Paul says plainly, is the glory of God (Rom. 3:23). Missing by a little is still missing. Comparing with one another on the shooting range who came closer to the bullseye is useless; what God requires is hitting the target. This is the root in the original text of "men see good, God sees not good enough."

## III. A Good Man Doing Good Is a Duty, Not Money for Buying Salvation

Then what do good works count as? Does believing in Jesus mean there is no need to do good? No, not that either.

Pastor Tong says that the many good people in the world do good as they ought. Why? **Because man was made in the image and likeness of God.** With the image of God upon him, inclining toward good is originally man's duty. A son's honoring his parents is a duty; he cannot use his honor as a bargaining chip with his parents: I have honored you all these years, so you owe me a sum. However well a duty is performed, it cannot turn into a transaction.

What is truly impossible is exchanging good works for salvation. Before God, however good I am, I am not qualified. Unqualified, the account cannot be paid — this is not a humble saying; it is the judgment Pastor Tong gives: **doing good is a duty; exchanging it for salvation is impossible.**

**Original-language analysis:** Ephesians 2:9 says salvation is "not of works"; "works" is ***Ergon* (ἔργον, Strong G2041)**, the portion a man earns by his labor. Set against it is "grace," ***Charis* (χάρις, Strong G5485)**, a favor freely given and undeserved (per BDAG). In Romans 6:23 Paul sets this pair of words even more clearly: "For the wages (*Opsōnion*, ὀψώνιον, G3800, originally a soldier's pay and provisions) of sin is death, but the gift (*Charisma*, χάρισμα, G5486) of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." Wages are earned; a gift is given. If salvation could be earned by works, it would become wages; Scripture insists on calling it a gift. Whichever word is chosen, the truth is there in it.

## IV. The More Saintly a Man, the Sooner He Says He Is a Sinner

There is one more layer of evidence: look at how men speak of themselves.

Pastor Tong names a string of names. Confucius said: "Give me a few more years, so that at fifty I may study the *Changes*, and I may be free of great faults." After a lifetime of study, a man nearing fifty hoped only to "be free of great faults"; he did not dare say he was without fault. The ancients also said: "Men are not sages; who can be without fault?" Job confessed his own iniquities; David said: "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me" (Ps. 51:5). Paul was even more direct, calling himself the "chief" among sinners (1 Tim. 1:15).

Notice this phenomenon: **the greater and holier a man, the sooner he admits that he is a sinner.** This is not politeness. The deeper a man's knowledge of himself, the more he knows how far he is from God's standard. It is the man of shallow knowledge who dares to thump his chest and say he is good enough.

Then Pastor Tong singles out the words of Jesus: "Which of you convicts Me of sin?" (John 8:46). In two thousand years, He is the only one who has dared to say this, and no one has been able to refute it. The saints all point out their own sins; He alone — no sin can be pointed out.

The distinction is established right here.

## V. A Religion's Founder Teaches; a Savior Saves

Pastor Tong finally gathers it up in a very short sentence, which I leave here in its original form: **He is a Savior, not a founder of a religion. A founder "teaches"; a Savior "saves."**

A founder tells you how you ought to conduct yourself; he points out the road to you, and you walk it yourself. If you cannot walk it, that is your problem. A Savior is different: He knows you cannot walk to the end — the standard is at God's glory, and you cannot hit it — so He comes personally, dies in your place, and saves you out of sin.

So, back to the original question: that good is rewarded with good is true; God will render to each one according to his deeds. That one can be saved by good works is false, because that goodness cannot pay the price of salvation in God's account. Keep doing good; that is duty. For salvation, one can only rely on grace, only believe in Jesus. These two things Pastor Tong distinguished very clearly, and we should not stir them together either.

---

## For Us Today

1. Acknowledge retribution: that God renders to each one according to his deeds is true; do not use grace to dilute righteousness
2. Aim the standard correctly: man's goodness comes from comparing with men; the target of salvation is the glory of God, and everyone falls short
3. Doing good is a duty, not currency: a man made in God's image ought to do good, but duty cannot be exchanged for salvation
4. Listen to how the saints speak of themselves: Confucius, Job, David, and Paul all admitted of themselves that they were sinners; Jesus alone could not be convicted by anyone — He is the Savior, not a founder of a religion
