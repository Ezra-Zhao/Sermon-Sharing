## (00:00 - 08:00) Opening · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Brothers and sisters, peace be with you all!

Yesterday we stopped at a very peculiar place. God questioned Adam, and He questioned Eve—asking a question, listening to an answer. When it came to the serpent, God did not ask a single question, but pronounced judgment directly: you shall go on your belly, and you shall eat dust all the days of your life.

Yet the sentencing was not finished. Today’s five verses are the second half of that judgment. And hidden right within this second half is the very first gospel proclamation in the entire Bible.

You did not mishear. The gospel does not start in Matthew; it does not start when the angels announced good tidings in Bethlehem. The first note of the gospel resounded in the Garden of Eden, at the very moment when humanity had just fallen and had not even had time to get back up. Moreover, it was not spoken to man, but to the serpent.

Please turn with me to Genesis chapter 3, verses 15 through 19. I will read:

"And I will put enmity  
Between you and the woman,  
And between your seed and her Seed;  
He shall bruise your head,  
And you shall bruise His heel." (Gen. 3:15)

To the woman He said:  
"I will greatly multiply your sorrow and your conception;  
In pain you shall bring forth children;  
Your desire shall be for your husband,  
And he shall rule over you." (Gen. 3:16)

Then to Adam He said, "Because you have heeded the voice of your wife, and have eaten from the tree of which I commanded you, saying, 'You shall not eat of it':  
'Cursed is the ground for your sake;  
In toil you shall eat of it  
All the days of your life.  
Both thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you,  
And you shall eat the herb of the field.  
In the sweat of your face you shall eat bread  
Till you return to the ground,  
For out of it you were taken;  
For dust you are,  
And to dust you shall return.'" (Gen. 3:17-19)

Five verses, three subjects. The serpent receives a promise-like sentence, the woman receives a proclamation of sorrow, and Adam receives an outcome of toil and dust. It sounds like entirely bad news.

Yet notice the order of hearing: God speaks first to the serpent, and the very first thing He says to the serpent is to announce that an offspring is coming who will bruise its head. That is to say, before humanity heard their own consequences, they first heard the end of their enemy. Grace spoke before judgment.

Today we will examine this in three parts:

First, 3:15: God Himself puts enmity between humanity and the serpent—this is not a curse upon humanity, but God Himself declaring war; the gospel is first and foremost a declaration of war;  
Second, 3:16-19: The consequences of sin fall upon reality, one by one—the pain of childbearing, the distortion of relationships, the thorns of the ground, and man’s return to dust; God offers no discounts;  
Third, how this head-bruising Seed comes, how He is wounded, and how He triumphs—from Bethlehem to the cross, and down to you and me today.

Let us first bow our heads in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. On the day humanity fell, You did not speak first of man's end; You spoke first of the serpent's end. We pray that You open our ears today, so that amidst the sound of curses we may hear the gospel, and in the proclamation of dust we may see the promise. Not one of Your words falls to the ground; the Seed who was to come has already come. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

## (08:00 - 25:00) Part One: God Himself Establishes Enmity—3:15 Is a Declaration of War

### 1.1 "And I Will Put" — It Is God Himself Who Establishes the Enmity

First, we must see clearly the opening words of verse 15: "And I will put."

This enmity between them was not initiated by man; it was not that the woman grew increasingly angry in hindsight, nor was it the natural evolution of history. It was established by God’s own mouth: I will put enmity between you and the woman.

The Hebrew word for "enmity" is *eivah*, meaning hostility or hatred. This word also appears in Numbers and Ezekiel to describe an irreconcilable, deadly opposition (cf. Num. 35:21-22; Ezek. 25:15).

Brothers and sisters, we must discern the weight of these words. After sinning, humanity was originally leaning toward the serpent—they listened to the serpent’s words, ate the fruit handed over by the serpent, and in a sense, humanity had just entered into an alliance with the serpent. The very first thing God does is tear down this alliance: I will not allow you to make peace; I will put enmity between you. From that point on, that slight pang of discontent toward sin in human hearts, that abhorrence of darkness, that residual yearning for God—all of it was placed there by God’s own hand.

So when you sit here today feeling uneasy about your sin and weary of living far from God, it is not because you are kinder than others; it is because Genesis 3:15 is still taking effect in you: God refuses to let you coexist in peace with the serpent.

### 1.2 The Unit of Warfare Is "Seed" Against "Seed"

God continues: Between your seed and her Seed, there will also be enmity.

The Hebrew word for "seed" is *zera*, which fundamentally means seed. The word "seed" traces a golden thread of promise throughout the entire Bible: God told Abraham that his seed would become a great nation; the Psalms say that a posterity shall serve Him (cf. Ps. 22:30); and when reaching the New Testament, Paul intentionally looks back to explain this line, saying of the Seed promised by God, as the Scriptures say: "He does not say, 'And to seeds,' as of many, but as of one, 'And to your Seed,' who is Christ" (Gal. 3:16).

Therefore, the battlefield of 3:15 begins as a long timeline: the seed of the serpent—all forces that oppose God, tempt humanity, and accuse the brethren; and the Seed of the woman—humanity passed down generation by generation from Eve. Yet this long line ultimately converges onto one Person. The Seed of the woman in the original text carries a singular trajectory; in the end, the One who crushes the serpent's head is not a crowd, but that solitary One.

Eve may not have understood this fully at the time. When she later gave birth to Cain, she said, "I have acquired a man from the Lord" (Gen. 4:1), with a tone carrying hope, as though waiting for that promise to be fulfilled. But Cain killed Abel. The path of promise was far longer than humans imagined. Yet God's timetable never follows human anxiety: it was not Cain, but the line of Seth going down generation by generation—Noah, Shem, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Judah, David. The seed lay buried in the soil for thousands of years, seemingly without movement, but when the fullness of time came, it broke through the earth.

### 1.3 The Same Verb, Two Outcomes: Bruising the Head and Bruising the Heel

The most vital part of this verse lies in the final two clauses:

"He shall bruise your head,  
And you shall bruise His heel."

The Hebrew word for "bruise" is *shuph*, meaning to strike, bruise, or crush. What is particularly remarkable is that both clauses use the exact same verb. The serpent strikes Him, and He strikes the serpent; the action is identical, but the locations are entirely different.

One is struck on the head, the other on the heel.

Brothers and sisters, a wound to the heel brings pain, bleeding, and limping, but it is not fatal. A blow to the head, however, is lethal and decisive. This verse does not evade the cost the Seed must pay—He does not win the battle standing at a distance; He will be bitten, He will bleed, He will suffer genuine wounds. Yet this verse also settles the outcome in advance: the serpent bruises merely the heel, but the Seed bruises the head.

There is a slight variation among English translations here that is well worth observing:
- KJV: "it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." Both use "bruise."
- NIV: "he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel." It translates the Seed's blow as "crush," while the serpent's blow is merely "strike."
- NRSV and the Chinese Union Version handle this similarly, both using the same word for "strike/bruise" in both places, relying on the distinction between the head and the heel to show the difference in severity.

The NIV’s use of "crush" is by no means an overstatement. Psalm 91 painted this very picture long ago: "You shall tread upon the lion and the cobra, The young lion and the serpent you shall trample underfoot" (Ps. 91:13). The Chinese Union Version uses "tread upon" and "trample underfoot"—planting one's foot upon the serpent, stepping right on its head. It turns out Genesis 3:15 is not an isolated statement; it is the seed of the entire biblical warfare narrative: the deliverance of the Exodus, the battles of David, the trampling in the Psalms, all the way to the ancient serpent being cast into the lake of fire in Revelation—all of it grew out of this single seed.

In his commentary on Genesis, Warren W. Wiersbe reminds readers to take note of the placement of this verse. His main point is that God’s sentence upon the serpent contains His promise to humanity; mankind’s hope was not announced only after the judgment had concluded, but was declared right in the middle of it. (Source: The general teaching of Warren W. Wiersbe, *The Bible Exposition Commentary*, on Genesis 3; this is a paraphrase, not a direct quote.)

To sum up Part 1 in a single sentence: **The first words humanity heard after sinning were not "It's all over," but "A Seed is coming." God declared the end to the enemy first, and in doing so, declared it to us as well.**

## (25:00 - 42:00) Part 2: The Consequences Fall One by One—3:16–19, God Offers No Discounts

Having heard the sentence upon the serpent, do not rush straight to the cross. These four intervening verses are directed to humanity. God gave mankind the gospel, but He also laid out the consequences before them one by one, sparing none.

### 2.1 To the Woman: Pain Enters the Two Most Intimate Realities (3:16)

God's words to the woman land on two realities: childbearing, and her husband.

"I will greatly multiply your sorrow and your conception; In pain you shall bring forth children."

The Hebrew word for "sorrow/pain" is *itstsavon*, meaning pain, hardship, or toil. This word will appear again shortly in the sentence upon Adam—for the woman, the sorrow falls upon childbearing; for the man, the toil falls upon labor. Once sin entered, it did not merely affect some peripheral corner; it touched the two most fundamental duties of human life: procreation and cultivation were both stained with pain.

"Your desire shall be for your husband, And he shall rule over you."

The Hebrew word for "desire" is *teshuqah*, an intense longing or turning toward. This word appears again in Genesis 4:7, where God says to Cain that sin’s "desire is for you, but you must rule over it." When you read these two passages together, the meaning becomes clear: sin’s desire for Cain was to master and control him; here, too, the relational picture shows control and the struggle to be controlled creeping into intimacy. What was originally "bone of my bones," what was originally equality, protection, and cherished love beside his rib, has now become a tug-of-war where one clings to grasp control and the other seeks to dominate.

Brothers and sisters, isn't the heartache in so many marriages today an echo of this very verse? The more one loves, the more one seeks to control; the more one is controlled, the more one longs to escape. Scripture did not write this as a command, giving husbands license to lord it over their wives; rather, it diagnosed the disease that entered relationships after sin took hold. A diagnosis is not a prescription. The prescription is found in the New Testament: husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her (Eph. 5:25)—the disease of domination must be cured with the medicine of self-sacrificing love. We spoke of this when we looked at 2:21–23, but let us hammer it home again today: the position beside the rib was God's original design; the tug-of-war between domination and grasping desire is a distortion born of the Fall.

Notice, however, that in 3:16 there is one thing God did not revoke: children were still to be born, and marriage was still to endure. The promised Seed was to come through this very line of childbearing marked by sorrow. God did not cancel the blessing because of the pain; He caused the blessing to move forward straight through the pain. Later, Eve was named by Adam "the mother of all living" (Gen. 3:20)—the sentence had just fallen, and Adam immediately responded to the promise in faith: within the judgment was death, yet within the naming was life. We will look closer at this divine order tomorrow when we study 3:20–24.

### 2.2 To Adam: Because You Heeded—The Trouble Began in the Ears (3:17)

When God spoke to Adam, the first thing He singled out was not his hands, but his ears:

"Because you have heeded the voice of your wife, and have eaten from the tree of which I commanded you, saying, 'You shall not eat of it'..." (Gen. 3:17)

Notice that God did not simply say, "Because you ate the fruit." He laid out the sequence: first came listening to the wrong voice, and only then came eating the wrong fruit. Yesterday we mentioned that the entryway for sin is often not the hand, but an ear that first listened to the wrong words. Today, this verse shows God Himself putting His seal upon that sequence.

We must also be clear here, lest anyone misunderstand: God was not rebuking Adam for listening to his wife speak. A wife's words ought to be heard; so much trouble in families arises precisely because the husband never listens. What God rebuked was that Adam had directly received a clear command—"Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat" (Gen. 2:16–17)—yet he allowed another voice to override what God had already spoken. Listening to what should be heard and guarding the boundaries that must be kept never contradict one another. Adam's failure was that when it came to boundaries, he compromised God's word.

### 2.3 The Ground Cursed: Thorns, Sweat, and Dust (3:17–19)

"Cursed is the ground for your sake; In toil you shall eat of it All the days of your life. Both thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you."

Man's work was not abolished; what changed was the nature of the work. Originally, the garden was to be tended and kept—a stewardship marked by delight. Now, what the earth produces on its own is thorns and thistles: you sow grain, but it sprouts thorns. If you want to eat the plants of the field, you must contend with an earth that is no longer compliant.

Proverbs long ago explained this reality to future generations: "In all labor there is profit, But idle chatter leads only to poverty" (Prov. 14:23). The toil is real, and the profit found in the toil is real too. We must hold both truths together: do not despise work because the ground has thorns, and do not entertain the fantasy that work will ever be free of them. "In the sweat of your face"—those are the very words of 3:19—"In the sweat of your face you shall eat bread." Brothers and sisters, when you come home from work each day thoroughly exhausted, when providing for your family costs you at every single turn, that is not your personal failure alone; this is the common condition all humanity carried out of the Garden of Eden. God did not promise here that man would escape toil; He promised that at the end of all toil, a Seed would come to bear something far heavier than toil itself.

The final sentence is the heaviest of the entire passage:

"Till you return to the ground, For out of it you were taken; For dust you are, And to dust you shall return." (Gen. 3:19)

The Hebrew word for "dust" is *aphar*. Recall Genesis 2:7, where the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life. Today's words essentially play that scene in reverse: if the breath is withdrawn, man returns to dust. The man's name, Adam, and the ground, *adamah*, are an inseparable pair in Hebrew. God was reminding Adam here, and reminding every one of us: where you came from and where your body will go are not matters for you to decide.

In Romans 8, Paul looked back at this scene, saying that the creation was subjected to futility, groaning and laboring with birth pangs until now; but he immediately added that the creation itself "also will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God" (Rom. 8:20–21). The curse upon the ground is not the end of the story; creation, too, is waiting for that Seed.

To conclude Part 2: **God offers no discounts. The sorrow of childbearing, the distortion in relationships, the thorns of the earth, and the finality of returning to dust—He stated them all plainly, one by one. But please remember the divine order: He declared the serpent's end before He declared man's consequences. Grace came first, judgment followed. That sequence itself is the gospel.**

## (42:00 - 52:00) Part 3: Heel Bruised, Head Crushed—How the Seed Triumphed

### 3.1 When the Fullness of the Time Had Come, Born of a Woman (Gal. 4:4)

Thousands of years passed, and that seed finally broke through the soil. Paul connected Genesis 3:15 with Bethlehem in a single sentence:

"But when the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those who were under the law, that we might receive the adoption as sons." (Gal. 4:4–5)

"Born of a woman"—these four words are easy to gloss over when we read them casually. Yet read in the light of Genesis 3:15, they are anything but ordinary: the One who would crush the serpent's head was purposefully designated by Scripture as "the Seed of the woman." Isaiah had foretold this sign long before: "Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a Son, and shall call His name Immanuel" (Isa. 7:14). The genealogy opening the Gospel of Matthew traces generation after generation, following this very line of the Seed, until it finally reaches Jesus, who was born of Mary (cf. Matt. 1:16).

Christmas, therefore, is not merely a holiday on the church calendar. Christmas is the maturity date of Genesis 3:15. What mankind incurred in Eden, God began to redeem at Bethlehem.

### 3.2 He First Suffered the Bruising of His Heel (Heb. 2:14; 1 John 3:8)

Yet the promise stated it plainly: victory would not come without suffering. The serpent would bruise His heel.

Hebrews explains this bruise thoroughly: "Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, He Himself likewise shared in the same, that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil" (Heb. 2:14).

Brothers and sisters, listen carefully to this order: the way to destroy the devil was not by bypassing death, but through death. He Himself shared in flesh and blood; He Himself entered into death. What was pierced on the cross were His hands and His feet—the bruise to the heel became a literal wound at Golgotha. When the serpent bit down, it was a real bite; the blood was real, the pain was real, and the death was real. The Lord Jesus did not merely pretend to be wounded; He was buried in the tomb.

John states the purpose of this battle even more plainly: "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8). To destroy—not to negotiate, not to coexist. The enmity established in 3:15 reached the cross, where the Son of God entered the field in person.

Then came the third day. The tomb was empty. The bruise to the heel did not become a fatal wound; death bit Him, but could not hold Him. In turn, the serpent's head received that fatal blow on the cross: the wages of sin were paid in full, and the record of accusations was wiped clean. In Paul's words, God, "having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them in it" (Col. 2:15).

This is why we say 3:15 is the Protoevangelium, the first gospel: in a single sentence, it foretells both the cross and the resurrection—He was to be bruised, which is the cross; yet what struck Him was only a bruise to the heel, while the serpent was struck on the head, which is the shadow of the resurrection written right into Genesis.

### 3.3 The Victory Is Already Settled, but the Church Is Still on the Road (Rom. 16:20; Rev. 12, 20)

The final step must land upon ourselves.

At the close of Romans, Paul writes a sentence patterned almost directly on 3:15: "And the God of peace will crush Satan under your feet shortly" (Rom. 16:20).

Please notice those four words: "under your feet." The One who bruises the serpent's head is Christ, yet the location where Satan is crushed, Paul writes, is under the church's feet. This does not mean we take Christ's place in winning the victory; rather, it means that the victory Christ has already won must be worked out step by step in the history of His body. The head has already been crushed, but the body is still on the battlefield. This is the church's position today—we do not fight *for* victory; we fight *from* victory.

Revelation brings this thread to its close: in chapter 12, the war between the woman and the great dragon—that serpent of old—rages all the way into heaven; in chapter 20, that serpent of old is bound, and finally cast into the lake of fire. The battlefield opened in Genesis 3 is closed once and for all in Revelation. From beginning to end, the Bible tells one single story: the words God spoke in the Garden of Eden, He fulfilled over thousands of years, sentence by sentence, without failing once.

To conclude part three: **The cross was not an emergency backup plan that God came up with on the fly. On the very day mankind ate of the fruit, God cast the shadow of the cross onto the ground of the Garden of Eden.**

## (52:00 - 60:00) Application for Six Groups of People · Altar Call · Response Prayer

### Application 1: For Seekers Who Have Not Yet Believed in the Lord

Friend, in today's passage, there is a sentence spoken directly to you: you are dust, and to dust you shall return.

These words are not pleasant to hear, but they are honest. Every one of us is heading in that direction; no one can bypass it. Only one question remains: before returning to dust, have you heard 3:15? There is an Offspring who took on your behalf the blow that should have fallen upon your head. He suffered a bruise to the heel so that you would never have to bear the crushing blow to the head. Are you willing to know Him today? This is not a matter of religious formalities; this is a matter of life and death.

### Application 2: For Husbands, Wives, and Parents in Marriage and Family

3:16 diagnoses the sickness of marriage: desire and rule, clutching and control. Today, here is a concrete assignment for husbands and wives: when you go home, ask your spouse this one question—"In our daily life, what is the one thing I do that makes you feel most controlled?" After asking, only listen; do not defend yourself.

Husbands, the standard of Eph. 5:25 is not domination, but self-sacrifice. Wives, the clutching hands also need to open and surrender into God's hands. When the position at the side is misaligned, the whole family hurts; when the position at the side is right, the home finally finds rest.

### Application 3: For Those Toiling and Sweating in the Workplace

When you push through traffic this morning, when you are still replying to emails late into the night, please remember 3:17-19: toil accompanied by thorns is the shared condition of all humanity, not God singling you out unfairly.

Yet when Christians work, there is something different: our sweat is not shed in vain. Paul says that your labor in the Lord is not in vain (cf. 1 Cor. 15:58). The thorns in the ground remain, but we are not working for the thorns; we are waiting for the Lord who wore the thorns upon His own head—the soldiers twisted a crown of thorns and put it on His head (cf. Matt. 27:29). The curse that sprang from the ground, He wore for us first. Your toil today—He understands; He understands it all.

### Application 4: For New Believers

Brothers and sisters who are new in the faith, you need to understand one thing early on: coming to faith in the Lord does not mean the battle is over; rather, it means the battle has just begun. 3:15 says there is enmity between seed and seed—once you stand on Christ's side, accusations, temptations, and tugs-of-war will all come knocking at your door.

Do not be afraid, however. Be clear about your position: the One who bruises the head is Christ. You do not win by your own strength; you stand inside the One who has already overcome. Daily Bible reading, prayer, and church gatherings are not religious duties; they are receiving rations, receiving orders, and returning to camp on the battlefield. When you are weak, go back to 3:15 and read it again: the outcome was announced by God long ago.

### Application 5: For Leaders, Parents, and Those Who Serve

Those who lead others must learn God's way in this passage: give the promise first, then address the consequences.

When dealing with those who have fallen, God did not start by announcing dust returning to dust; He first announced the serpent's end. How many parents, how many leaders, have the order completely backward: the moment trouble strikes, they lead with consequences, they lead with labeling, crushing the person right away. Learn God's order: first let them know where hope lies, and then honestly walk beside them to face the consequences. Discipline preceded by promise can actually be received; discipline that brings only judgment without promise will only drive people back into the bushes to hide.

### Application 6: For the Entire Church

The church is the community standing in Rom. 16:20: the God of peace will crush Satan under our feet shortly.

We are not a group of people waiting around to be beaten; we are followers of the Head-Crusher. Every time the church prays for the sick, every time it lifts up the fallen, every time it does good in the community, it is putting into practice the verdict of 3:15: the fatal blow to the head has already been delivered; we are merely carrying the news of this accomplished victory to the side of every person whose heel is still being bitten.

### Moment of Ignition

(Pause, speaking more slowly)

Brothers and sisters, I will ask you one final question today for you to ponder all week:

**Is the place where you are hurting most right now your heel?**

That matter that refuses to let you go, that voice accusing you, that strained relationship, that job where sweat flows with no end in sight—I tell you, the Bible's diagnosis of it is this: a bruise to the heel.

A bruise to the heel hurts—it truly hurts; it bleeds, and the bleeding is real. But a bruise to the heel has one defining feature—

(Pause, speaking with greater emphasis)

**It cannot kill you.**

Because that mortal blow to the head has already been taken by Someone on your behalf. The most the serpent can bite is your heel; your head is shielded by Christ, and your outcome was fully declared by God back in the third chapter of Genesis.

Right today, not tomorrow. Do not let that accusing voice declare your outcome any longer. Your outcome is not written in the serpent's mouth; your outcome is written in 3:15: the Seed of the woman shall bruise its head. He has already bruised it, it has already been defeated, and you are standing on the winning side.

### Altar Call

I am making two calls:

**First call**: You are being bitten on the heel by something right now—the accusation of sin, broken relationships, a chronic burden—and today you are willing to surrender this matter into the hands of the Head-crushing Lord, refusing to carry it on your own any longer. Please stand up, and we will pray for you together.

**Second call**: Friends who have not yet believed in the Lord. Dust returning to dust is our shared destination; but 3:15 gives you another way: that Offspring was wounded for you, so that through Him you may receive eternal life. If you are willing to receive Jesus Christ today, please raise your hand, and I want to pray for you.

(Waiting for response, praying for those standing and raising hands)

### Concluding Response Prayer

Let us bow our heads together in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. On the day mankind fell, You declared the serpent's end before You ever announced our consequences. Your grace outran judgment, and that is why we still dare to stand here today.

Lord, we confess that our days are filled with thorns and sweat, our relationships still suffer the pull of desire and domination, and our bodies must ultimately return to dust. We do not hide from these, we do not push them away; we lay them honestly before You.

Yet even more do we declare what You have already spoken: the Seed of the woman has come, He was born of a woman, He suffered the bruise to the heel on the cross, He rose from the dead, and the serpent's head has been thoroughly crushed. The God of peace will also crush Satan under our feet shortly.

Strengthen those who stood up today, Lord, and heal the wounds on their heels; personally receive the friends who raised their hands today, and bring them into the adoption as sons.

Throughout our lives, we stand in this very promise. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, the One who bruised the serpent's head, we pray, Amen."
