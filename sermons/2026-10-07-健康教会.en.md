---

Let me first ask you to think about a question: **In your mind, what does a healthy church look like?**

Some say that many people means health; some say that a good atmosphere in gatherings means health; some say that much giving and a large building mean health. None of these are wrong, but none of them are complete.

How does the Bible define the church? Ephesians chapter 1, verses 22-23:

> "And He put all things under His feet, and gave Him to be head over all things to the church, which is His body, the fullness of Him who fills all in all." (Eph. 1:22-23, NKJV)

Here "church" in Greek is **ἐκκλησία (ekklēsia)** (original ἐκκλησία, transliteration ekklēsia; Strong G1577; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "church, congregation"), meaning "the congregation called out." The church is not a building, not a set of programs, but a people called out by God from the world, belonging to Himself.

The word "fullness" in Greek is **πλήρωμα (plērōma)** (original πλήρωμα, transliteration plērōma; Strong G4138; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "fullness, fulfillment"), with the meaning of "fullness, completeness." The church is the body of Christ, the place where the fullness of Christ is manifested. A healthy church is a church in which the fullness of Christ is fully manifested.

The Lord Jesus personally promised:

> "And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it." (Matt. 16:18, NKJV)

Note: Here "Peter" (**πέτρος, petros**) (original πέτρος, transliteration Petros; Strong G4074; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "a small stone") is a small stone, while "rock" (**πέτρα, petra**) (original πέτρα, transliteration petra; Strong G4073; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "rock, bedrock") is a great rock — the foundation on which the church is built is the Christ whom Peter confessed ("You are the Christ, the Son of the living God"), not any person. The gates of Hades shall not prevail against her.

Schwarz points out in *Natural Church Development* (in general terms): The only prerequisite is a longing for God to manifest Himself more deeply and more broadly in the life of the church. No matter what the church's present condition is, as long as there is this longing, growth in both quality and quantity can be expected.

## I. Growth in Quantity Is the Natural Result of Growth in Quality

From 1996 to 2007, a study covering 70 countries and fifty thousand churches found that healthy churches reached an average growth rate of 51% over eleven years. On this basis Schwarz points out: **Growth in quantity is the natural result brought about by the church's pursuit of growth in quality.**

Paul says in 1 Corinthians chapter 3, verses 6-7:

> "I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the increase. So then neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase." (1 Cor. 3:6-7, NKJV)

The word "increase / grow" in Greek is **αὐξάνω (auxanō)** (original αὐξάνω, transliteration auxanō; Strong G837; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to increase, to grow"), which means natural growth — like a seed growing naturally in the soil. What we can do is plant and water, and let God give the increase. So the first responsibility for the church's growth does not lie in our methods, but in God; yet we have a responsibility to make the church "healthy," because healthy life naturally grows.

Then, what specifically does a healthy church look like? Schwarz proposed **eight quality characteristics**, which can be checked one by one:

1. **Empowering leadership** — Are your leaders raising people up and equipping people to take part in ministry, or holding everything in their own hands?
2. **Gift-oriented ministry** — Does the church arrange ministry posts according to each person's gifts, or is it "whoever is free goes up"?
3. **Passionate spirituality** — Is the spiritual life of brothers and sisters fervent, or routine?
4. **Functional structures** — Is the church's organizational structure helping growth, or creating internal friction?
5. **Inspiring worship** — Does Sunday worship allow people to meet God and be inspired?
6. **Holistic small groups** — Does small group life touch the real-life issues of group members, or does it stop at Bible study questions and answers?
7. **Need-oriented evangelism** — Does our evangelism respond to people's real needs?
8. **Loving relationships** — Is there real love among the congregation?

Schwarz also proposed **six growth principles**, which can be used to test every decision we make:

- **Interdependence:** What effect does this decision have on other dimensions?
- **Multiplication:** Does it bring "multiplication" or merely "addition"?
- **Energy transformation:** Are we good at transforming resources in our environment into blessing?
- **Sustainability:** Is there an inner mechanism to let it develop sustainably, rather than being a gust of wind?
- **Symbiosis:** Can it promote cooperation and bring overall fruitfulness?
- **Fruitfulness:** Does it produce visible fruit for the kingdom of God?

These eight characteristics and six principles are not a score sheet, but like a medical examination report — helping us honestly see the physical condition of the church.

## II. Holy Within and Kingly Without: The Nine Marks of a Healthy Church

A healthy body pays attention to being "holy within and kingly without": inside there is a healthy constitution, outside it can create value. The church is the same. The Nine Marks ministry has summarized **nine marks** of a healthy church, which can be understood as "three focuses, nine contents."

In the original preaching, **three focuses** were proposed: focus on evangelism and discipleship, focus on tolerance and social justice, focus on emotional health and spiritual capacity. These are the direction.

**The nine contents** are the foundation:

**1. Expository preaching.** Grasp the main point of a passage of Scripture, take the main point of the text as the core of the message, and apply it to people's lives today. "Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light." (Gen. 1:3, NKJV) As soon as God's word goes forth, there is creative power. Preaching is not speaking people's ideas; it is letting God's word speak for itself. (See Isa. 55:10-11; Acts 12:24; Heb. 4:12)

**2. Biblical theology.** Sound doctrine, a correct knowledge of God, convictions that come from the Bible. "Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth." (John 17:17, NKJV) The health of the church begins with the health of the mind. (1 Tim. 1:5)

**3. The gospel message.** The one holy God made us in His own image (Gen. 1:26-28); we sinned and fell, and were separated from God (Gen. 3; Rom. 3:23); God sent Jesus, who died on the cross to fulfill the law and rose again to quench God's wrath (John 1:14; Rom. 3:21-26; Acts 2:24); He calls us to repent and trust in Him alone (Acts 17:30; John 1:12; John 3:16). This is the whole gospel, and not one word of it can be missing.

**4. Conversion.** The part God does is to give life to the dead and cause the blind to see (Eph. 2:5; 2 Cor. 4:3-6); the part man does is to repent of sin and believe in Jesus (Mark 1:15; John 3:16). (Acts 2:38; Rom. 6:21-23)

**5. Evangelism.** Preach the whole gospel, call people to repent and trust in Christ, and honestly make clear the cost of following. (Matt. 28:19-20; Rom. 10:14-17; 1 Pet. 3:15-16)

**6. Church membership.** For a Christian to join a local church is a solemn commitment: to love the church, serve the church, and submit to the leadership of the church. (Matt. 16:16-19; 1 Cor. 5:12-13; 2 Cor. 2:6)

**7. Church discipline.** In the broad sense, it is every action that helps each member pursue holiness; in the narrow sense, it is the correction of sin within the community. (Matt. 18:15-17; 1 Cor. 5:1-13; Heb. 12:5-11) Love without discipline is indulgence.

**8. Discipleship.** A living Christian should be a continually growing Christian, helping one another in holiness and growing up together. "But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ." (2 Pet. 3:18, NKJV; see also Eph. 4:15; Phil. 4:9)

**9. Church leadership.** Every local church should be led by multiple godly and qualified elders, feeding, guiding, and protecting the flock. (1 Tim. 3:1-7; Titus 1:5-9; Acts 20:27-29)

James chapter 5, verse 16 says:

> "Confess your trespasses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The effective, fervent prayer of a righteous man avails much." (James 5:16, NKJV)

These nine marks are the content of being "holy within." Only with these does the church have the power to be "kingly without."

## III. Distinguishing Five Growth Models

Speaking of church growth, five models have appeared in history, and we must distinguish them:

- **The faithful model:** "I am a brick; wherever I am needed, there I will be moved." If a method does not come from the Bible, no matter how "successful" it is, we do not envy it.
- **The breakthrough model:** Revival meetings, spiritual formation conferences, praying for revival, preparing ourselves with all our might to welcome revival.
- **The exemplary model:** Lead by example, appreciate and encourage one another; run straight toward the goal, set goals firmly and complete goals.
- **The management model:** Bringing business management methods into the church. We must be alert: **When business techniques replace the place of the Holy Spirit, following patterns but not relying on prayer, the church is in danger.**
- **The principles model:** Looking for universally applicable, proven principles, putting first things first and adapting to local conditions. But we must avoid cultural traps — Western culture emphasizes competition, and its danger is domination; Eastern culture emphasizes harmony, and its danger is copying; Southern culture emphasizes solidarity, and its danger is conformity.

The Lord Jesus summed up the greatest commandment in one sentence:

> "You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your strength, and with all your mind, and your neighbor as yourself." (Luke 10:27, NKJV)

Every model must come back to this sentence to be tested: Does it make us love God more and love people more?

## IV. Application: Six Groups of People

**1. Brothers and sisters who have newly believed in the Lord:** Do not rush to "do many things"; first, in the eight characteristics, find your small group, find your gift, and take root in love.

**2. Servants who feel weary:** Check against this one item, "gift-oriented ministry" — are you holding on by force in a place that does not belong to you? Return to the gift God has given you, and only then will there be rest in ministry.

**3. Church leaders and coworkers:** Use the six growth principles to test every decision in your hands: Is this multiplication or addition? Can it be sustained?

**4. Those praying for the revival of the church:** Remember 1 Corinthians 3:6 — he who plants is nothing; only God gives the increase. Your prayer is watering.

**5. Seekers who do not yet have a stable church:** Use these nine marks to observe and to look for a church. A healthy church is worth entrusting yourself to.

**6. The next generation of the church:** Young people, the church is not only your parents' church; it is also your church. Find your small group, find your place.

## Conclusion

A healthy church is not a perfect church, but **a church continually growing up toward the fullness of Christ**. Growth in quality brings growth in quantity; this is God's promise, and it is also a fact verified by countless churches.

## Response Prayer

Dear heavenly Father, thank You for placing us in Your church. Lord, we confess that we often pursue numbers and pursue excitement, while neglecting the health of the body. Please forgive us.

Lord, we long for You to manifest Yourself more deeply and more broadly among us. Please raise up empowering leaders, kindle our fervent spirituality, and give us loving relationships. Let our pulpit preach Your word, let our gospel be whole and without lack, let our disciples continually grow.

We place the church in Your hands, because the church is Yours, and You said the gates of Hades shall not prevail against her. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
