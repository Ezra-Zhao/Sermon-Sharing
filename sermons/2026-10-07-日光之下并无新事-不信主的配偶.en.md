# Nothing New Under the Sun: The Unbelieving Spouse

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：不信主的配偶».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of having an unbelieving spouse

## Summary

Living with an unbelieving spouse is one of the deepest lessons many believers must learn. Does happiness depend on whether one's spouse is saved? Can grace become an excuse for divorce? How should one conduct oneself in the face of verbal attacks and control? Through three questions from real-life situations, this chapter returns to 1 Corinthians chapter 7: it is God who changes a spouse, not we ourselves; our duty is to pray earnestly, to bear witness by our lives, and, in peace, to be shaped by the Lord into His likeness.

## I. Happiness Does Not Depend on Whether Your Spouse Is Saved

First, listen to a question — words that are in the hearts of many brothers and sisters:

> It is so hard being with a non-believer. I am very unhappy. I feel that if I divorce, God will forgive me. Does God not want me to be happy? And would I not be able to serve Him better if I married a Christian?

Behind this question lies an assumption: my happiness depends on whether my spouse is a Christian. That assumption does not hold. God has allowed you to be in your present circumstances, and He always has His reasons. From now on, use the life given to you to bear witness to God's love. Your happiness does not depend on your husband's salvation, but on your relationship with God.

You must also recognize this: God's goal is not only to make you happy, but to expect you to grow into the likeness of Jesus. A person serves God not by momentary interest, but by a firm will — persevering steadfastly regardless of circumstances. Here marriage is not only a place to settle down; it is also the place where God shapes a person. Paul says:

> "But we all, with unveiled face, beholding as in a mirror the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as by the Spirit of the Lord." (2 Cor. 3:18)

"Being transformed into the same image" — this is precisely what God wants to do in a difficult marriage.

## II. Grace Cannot Become an Excuse for Sin

God hates divorce. God is merciful and willing to forgive, but His mercy and forgiveness cannot become an excuse for us to sin at will. In Romans, Paul puts it very heavily:

> "What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Certainly not! How shall we who died to sin live any longer in it?" (Rom. 6:1-2)

"I sinned, and God will forgive me" — this is not the language of faith; it treats grace as a license. Forgiveness is prepared for those who truly repent, not for those who plan sin in advance.

But Paul also gives a boundary: if the unbelieving party insists on leaving, let him leave. This is not the believing party actively tearing down the marriage; it is that when the other party is determined to leave, the believing party "is not under bondage" — God has called us to peace. In the next section we will look closely at this passage.

## III. Paul's Teaching: 1 Corinthians Chapter 7

The key Scripture of this chapter comes from Paul's teaching on marriages between believers and unbelievers (1 Cor. 7:12-16):

> "But to the rest I, not the Lord, say: If any brother has a wife who does not believe, and she is willing to live with him, let him not divorce her. And a woman who has a husband who does not believe, if he is willing to live with her, let her not divorce him. For the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife, and the unbelieving wife is sanctified by the husband; otherwise your children would be unclean, but now they are holy. But if the unbeliever departs, let him depart; a brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases, but God has called us to peace. For how do you know, O wife, whether you will save your husband? Or how do you know, O husband, whether you will save your wife?" (1 Cor. 7:12-16)

Here the Greek word for "is sanctified" is **hagiazō (ἁγιάζω)** (original text ἁγιάζω, transliterated hagiazō; Strong G37; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to make holy, to set apart"). Paul does not mean that the unbelieving spouse is automatically saved, but that this marriage, because of the believing party, is set apart as holy, and even the children are counted among the holy. The believing party at home is the witness God has placed there.

The two questions at the end of the passage leave hope for the believing party: "How do you know whether you will save your husband?" — you do not know. So do not give up first.

## IV. Mindset: Do Not Fix Your Eyes Only on What He Has Not Done

The second question was asked like this: If my husband is unwilling to change, what mindset should I have?

The response is: Do not focus your attention on the things your husband has not done, but on the positive aspects of what he does that can promote your relationship. Pray that your husband may respond to the work God is doing in his life.

It is important to recognize: your right mindset does not depend on other people's attitudes or behavior, but on your relationship with God. Your relationship with God is something others cannot affect. Hebrews makes this path very clear:

> "Therefore we also, since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight, and the sin which so easily ensnares us, and let us run with endurance the race that is set before us, looking unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith, who for the joy that was set before Him endured the cross, despising the shame, and has sat down at the right hand of the throne of God. For consider Him who endured such hostility from sinners against Himself, lest you become weary and discouraged in your souls." (Heb. 12:1-3)

When one's eyes are on Jesus, one is not easily weary and discouraged. When one's eyes are only on a spouse's shortcomings, discouragement is only a matter of time.

## V. The Two Become One Flesh: Neither Going Behind His Back, Nor Submitting to Abuse

The third question is more specific: The husband thinks his wife's faith is foolish and often attacks her faith verbally; he never considers her opinion in any important decision he makes. In such a situation, may she make some decisions on her own behind his back?

The response has two sides. First, the essence of marriage:

> "Therefore a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and they shall become one flesh." (Gen. 2:24)

According to the explanation in the source material, "one flesh" refers to an organic unity. Doing things behind your husband's back violates the essence of the marriage relationship. If the two have become one flesh, they cannot each go their own way and set up defenses against each other.

But the other side is equally clear: you need not submit to any form of abuse — physical, psychological, or verbal, all included. A husband's display of a controlling mentality is itself a form of abuse. Have you considered seeking counseling? Does your husband agree to counseling? If he does not agree, you might perhaps apply for a separation — living apart for a time and receiving counseling. Doing so might awaken him and cause him to cherish his relationship with you.

Holding fast to oneness does not equal enduring everything; pointing out abuse does not equal tearing down the marriage. The path in between is counseling and boundaries.

## VI. Steps to Resolution: Return to God the Sovereignty to Change Your Spouse

At the end, ask yourself honestly three questions:

First, do you realize that only God can change your spouse, and you cannot?

Second, do you realize that God gave you marriage in part to use marriage to change you?

Third, have you accepted this fact: God will use you to work in your spouse's life, but whether your spouse is saved is not your responsibility — he needs to bear responsibility himself and respond to God's call.

If you recognize these things, you will obtain an unexpected tranquility and peace:

> "Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Phil. 4:6-7)

For practice in daily life, the original manuscript especially marks out two passages. One is for memorization:

> "Let your speech always be with grace, seasoned with salt, that you may know how you ought to answer each one." (Col. 4:6)

One is for repeated reading — the chapter on love, 1 Corinthians chapter 13, verses 1 to 8. In this marriage, the verses most often needed are verses 4 to 7:

> "Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up; does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil; does not rejoice in iniquity, but rejoices in the truth; bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Love never fails." (1 Cor. 13:4-8a)

## Conclusion

The root cause in this chapter is exposed by the original manuscript in a single sentence: the wrong understanding is "Unless my spouse changes and becomes a Christian, I cannot be content"; the right understanding is "I will pray earnestly for my spouse to become a Christian, but regardless of the outcome, I am still content, because Christ who dwells in me is using this very experience to shape me into His likeness." Return the work of changing people to God, and keep for yourself the work of being changed — this is the most important division of labor on the road of walking with an unbelieving spouse.

## Prayer of Response

Lord, You have called us to peace. Please place in our hearts the peace that surpasses understanding, and guard our hearts and minds. The changing of our spouse is in Your hands; we will not snatch Your work. Only ask that You use this marriage to change us, so that we are transformed into the image of the Lord, from glory to glory. We pray earnestly for our spouse, and we entrust the outcome to You. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.

## Notes

1. This chapter is compiled from Q&A-style material in the 2019 lecture PPT (slides 158–166): the three case questions, the root cause, the steps to resolution, and the accompanying Scriptures all come from the original manuscript; this time only the structure has been rearranged and rewritten in prose form, with no new cases or testimonies added.
2. 1 Corinthians 7:14, "is sanctified": the Greek verb hagiazō (ἁγιάζω, Strong G37), here in the perfect passive tense, meaning that because of the believing party, the marriage and the children are set apart as holy; it does not mean the unbelieving party is automatically saved, and the main text expresses it with this measure.
3. The pastoral advice discussed in Section V — seeking counseling, and even temporarily separating while receiving counseling — is the advice given in the original manuscript to the questioner; in situations involving abuse, one should at the same time seek help from church pastors and professional counselors, and not bear it alone.
