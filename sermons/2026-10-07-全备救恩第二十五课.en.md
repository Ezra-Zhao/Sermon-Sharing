## Content

An analysis of salvation. What are the differences in being saved at different periods? If we make a semantic analysis of the complete salvation, you can discover how broad the broad sense of being saved is, and how specific the narrow sense of being saved is. Once this is explained, it should become especially clear. Please note that the analysis in this lesson refers to the nature of our having received grace and been saved. Past salvation is [one thing], present salvation is [another], and future salvation is [another]. In the broad sense, all of these are being saved; in the narrow sense, each has its own specific reference. Let us now make a brief analysis of them.

1. The salvation of the soul/spirit: "but [we are] of those who believe to the saving of the soul" (Heb. 10:39, NKJV). 2. Predestined salvation: "[God] did not appoint us to wrath, but to obtain salvation through our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Thess. 5:9, NKJV). 3. Chosen salvation: "But we are bound to give thanks to God always for you... because God from the beginning chose you for salvation" (2 Thess. 2:13, NKJV). The use of "being saved" here refers to the salvation God predestined from eternity, whereby through election our souls return to the Shepherd and Overseer, unto salvation. This does not refer to today's victory, nor to the future redemption. This is the narrow sense, with a specific reference.

1. Overcoming temptation: "He is able to aid those who are tempted" (Heb. 2:18, NKJV). 2. Overcoming suffering: "...with... the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ, [this] will turn out for my deliverance [salvation]" (Phil. 1:19, NKJV). 3. Overcoming evil: "And the Lord will deliver me from every evil work" (2 Tim. 4:18, NKJV). Present salvation, in the Bible's usage, in many places refers to overcoming. When, through the Lord who loves us, we overcome all tribulation and the sword, subjectively speaking it is truly victory; but objectively speaking it is also truly salvation, because this is not our own ability: "God [makes] us... triumph through our Lord Jesus Christ" — "thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Cor. 15:57, NKJV).

1. Waiting for redemption: "you were sealed... until the redemption of the purchased possession" (Eph. 4:30; 1:14, NKJV). 2. Longing for redemption: "even we ourselves groan within ourselves, eagerly waiting for the adoption, the redemption of our body" (Rom. 8:23, NKJV). 3. Boasting in redemption: "when you see these things happening... lift up your heads, because your redemption draws near" (Luke 21:28, NKJV). The redemption here is also salvation, but three points should be noted. First, it belongs to the future; it can only be realized when the Lord Jesus comes. Second, it concerns hope; the so-called hope of salvation refers to this. Third, it refers to the body, for the Bible clearly says it is "the redemption of our body" (Rom. 8:23, NKJV). When this day comes, we will enter into complete salvation. At that time, the complete salvation will be accomplished in us.

Homework questions: [Original text partially illegible.] What is the difference between the broad sense and the narrow sense [of being saved]? How does the nature of being saved differ in the past, present, and future? What are the three characteristics of past salvation? On what does today's victory rely? Relying on the Lord, what do we overcome? State the three characteristics of redemption. Are we now completely saved?
