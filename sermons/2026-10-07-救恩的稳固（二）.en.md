There is one thing that Christians need most in the midst of storms: certainty.

We often wonder: Will God's promises truly come to pass? Could my salvation be lost one day? The Apostle Paul gives us a resolute, decisive answer in 2 Corinthians 1:20: "For all the promises of God in Him are Yes, and in Him Amen, to the glory of God through us."

The word "Amen" in the original text means truly so, assuredly, yes. Paul also has an ingenious layer of meaning here: the Hebrew pronunciation of "faithfulness" is similar to the Greek "Amen"; the Lord Jesus Himself also calls Himself "the Amen" (Rev. 3:14). Therefore, when we say "Amen" to God, and the Lord says to us, "Most assuredly, I say to you," this is the proper duty of a Christian: to say "Amen" to God, and to speak the truth to man.

In this chapter, let us examine several key passages of Scripture together to see why salvation is secure.

## I. All the Promises of God in Christ Are "Yes"

"For all the promises of God in Him are Yes."

All of God's promises concerning His Son Jesus Christ have been fulfilled one by one. Because God is faithful, our response is to say "Amen" through Him, so that glory may be given to God.

The light of the Word brings a threefold reminder:

First, Christ is the Mediator through whom God grants grace to man (Heb. 7:22). Today we have already received Christ, and therefore we can practically enjoy all the promised blessings of God.

Second, if one has not touched Christ, he will doubt whether God's promises can be realized; only when one touches the reality of Christ will he feel how real God's promises are, and only then will he say "Amen" to God's promises from the depths of his being. The all-inclusive Christ is the reality, the guarantee, and the assurance of all God's promises.

Third, toward all of God's arrangements, the Lord Jesus said: "Even so, Father" (Matt. 11:26); and toward us, the Lord said: "Most assuredly, I say to you." Saying "Amen" to God and speaking the truth to man is our proper duty as Christians.

## II. He Will by No Means Cast You Out

"All that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out." (John 6:37)

To "cast" means to take away or remove; to "out" means to throw outside or drive out. "All that the Father gives Me" refers to those who are drawn by the Father (cf. v. 44).

This is the Lord's own spoken promise! Whoever comes before Him, He will by no means cast out. The Father's giving is the source of our believing in the Lord; the Son's keeping is the source of our being able to stand firm in faith to the end. Since salvation does not originate from ourselves, we can safely entrust ourselves to the Lord—for we may fail, but the Lord will never fail.

The Lord's power is our security, and the Lord's truth is our shield (Ps. 91:4). He said He will by no means cast you out; you will never find a reason for Him to cast you away!

## III. I Am the Door: Three Benefits of Entering

"I am the door. If anyone enters by Me, he will be saved, and will go in and out and find pasture." (John 10:9)

Literal translation of the original text: "I am the door; if anyone enters through Me, he shall be saved, and shall go in, and shall go out, and shall find pasture."

"The door" brings three benefits to the sheep: **will be saved**—delivered from danger; **and will go in and out**—receiving freedom; **and find pasture**—receiving satisfaction.

Notice the order in the original text: "in" first, then "out." The sheep must first come in to establish their relationship with the Shepherd, and then they can be led by the Shepherd to go out to the pasture and find all necessary supply.

Christ is the door. The Christian faith is not a set of dogmas, nor is it a church building, but a Savior, who is the Lord Jesus Christ. A door is for entering and exiting: if a person wants to receive salvation, he must enter inside the door, which means he must accept Christ by faith.

First "in," then "out": believers must first enter before God to have enjoyment, and then they can go out before men to bear witness. We must go within the veil to enjoy sweet fellowship with the Lord, and go outside the camp to bear the reproach He bore (cf. Heb. 13:11-13).

## IV. By One Offering, Perfected Forever

"For by one offering He has perfected forever those who are being sanctified." (Heb. 10:14)

Literal translation of the original text: "For by one offering He has perfected forever those who are sanctified."

"Are being sanctified" in the original text is in the perfect tense, indicating those who have already been sanctified; "perfected forever" refers to being completed unto eternity, never losing the sanctified position already obtained. Christ's work of offering is already finished; being offered once, it is effective forever, and there is no need for Him to offer sacrifice again.

By only "one" offering, Christ caused us to obtain the standing of sanctification "forever"; from now on, we can come boldly before God through Him at any time.

We are "those who are sanctified." Sanctification is not only our identity and position, but should also be our daily living expression—therefore we should continuously, day by day, rely on the Holy Spirit to sanctify our thoughts, affections, words, and deeds, living as saints worthy of the name.

## V. The Author of Eternal Salvation: Twelve Reasons

"And having been perfected, He became the author of eternal salvation to all who obey Him." (Heb. 5:9)

"Perfected" means accomplished, completed, fulfilled; "author" means cause, reason, source, originator.

"Having been perfected" does not mean that His attributes and moral character were originally imperfect and needed to become perfect after He learned obedience through suffering; rather, it means that having experienced human suffering, He became fully qualified to be the believers' High Priest (cf. Heb. 2:11, 18), able to sympathize with our human weaknesses.

"All who obey Him" refers to Christians, that is, those who believe and obey Christ; "eternal salvation" indicates that the salvation predestined by God for mankind is eternal, meaning that once saved, always saved, without any possibility of alteration; "author" means that this eternal salvation is not based on human conditions, but based on Christ Himself—He is the cause of our eternal salvation.

Some conclude from Hebrews 6:4-8 that the salvation received by Christians is not eternal, but subject to change and the possibility of being lost. However, since the Scriptures say "eternal salvation," salvation itself possesses absolute permanence, for the following reasons:

1. Since God saved us according to His own purpose (2 Tim. 1:9), and God's purpose is immutable (Heb. 6:17), our salvation will never be lost (John 6:39).
2. We are saved not because we chose God, but because God chose us (John 15:16), and the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable (Rom. 11:29); therefore, our salvation is eternally secure.
3. We are saved not because we loved God, but because God loved us (1 John 4:10), and nothing shall be able to separate us from the love of God (Rom. 8:35-39); therefore, He will save us to the uttermost.
4. We are saved not according to our works, but according to His grace (2 Tim. 1:9), and God's grace is abundant (Eph. 1:7-9), able to meet all our circumstances and needs.
5. We are saved in order to demonstrate God's righteousness (Rom. 3:26), and righteousness is the foundation of God's throne (Ps. 89:14); therefore, He cannot forsake us and thereby implicate Himself in unrighteousness.
6. We are saved because God made a new covenant with us (Heb. 8:8-13), and God's covenant cannot be altered (Ps. 89:34); therefore, our salvation is likewise unchangeable.
7. We are saved by the mighty power of God (Rom. 1:16), and God's power is supreme; no one can snatch us out of God's hand (John 10:29).
8. We are saved by receiving God's eternal life, so we shall never perish (John 10:28).
9. Our salvation is from God, with whom there is no variation (Jas. 1:17).
10. Christ's perfection is the author of our eternal salvation (Heb. 5:9).
11. No one can snatch us out of Christ's hand (John 10:28).
12. The Lord promised that the one who comes to Him He will by no means cast out (John 6:37).

Christ was originally "perfect" God, yet in order to serve us, He still had to go through the process of incarnation, suffering, and death to be perfected. How much more should we, who were originally imperfect, pursue all kinds of spiritual experiences so that we may become useful vessels in God's hands.

Man's perfection is to be united with God's will. Obedience is the only path for man to attain perfection, and the only proof that man is united with God's will. Without obedience, Christ could not have accomplished salvation; without obedience, we cannot enjoy salvation. Therefore, our obedience and the Lord Jesus' obedience are both vital elements of salvation.

If we desire to enjoy more of Christ's fullness and experience more of the reality of the Holy Spirit, the only way is to learn more lessons of obedience. The degree of our obedience to God determines the degree of our service to man.

The salvation we have received is an eternal salvation; the efficacy, outcome, and benefits of this salvation possess an eternal nature, unchanged by the passing of time.

## VI. Entering the Most Holy Place Once for All, Having Obtained Eternal Redemption

"Not with the blood of goats and calves, but with His own blood He entered the Most Holy Place once for all, having obtained eternal redemption." (Heb. 9:12)

Literal translation of the original text: "And not through the blood of goats and calves, but through His own blood, He entered the Most Holy Place once for all, having obtained eternal redemption."

"The blood of goats" was for the people (Lev. 16:15); "the blood of calves" was for the priests themselves (Lev. 16:11). "With His own blood"—"His own" refers to Jesus Christ, who is both the Priest and the sacrifice.

"Once for all" (emphatic tone) indicates that once was entirely sufficient, carrying the meaning of "accomplishing everything in a single stroke," whose efficacy lasts forever without any need to repeat the same action.

"Having obtained" carries two meanings in the original text: on one hand, accomplished or attained; on the other hand, manifested or presented. "Eternal redemption" refers to redemption that is perpetually effective. Although Christ's great work of redemption was accomplished on earth at the cross of Golgotha, the efficacy of eternal redemption was officially obtained when He appeared before God in heaven.

In the Old Testament, the blood of bulls and goats could only temporarily cover man's sins, but could not take them away (cf. Heb. 10:4); but in the New Testament, the blood of Jesus takes away the sin of the world (John 1:29).

We are redeemed with the precious blood of Christ (1 Pet. 1:18-19), and the cleansing efficacy of His blood is effective forever; whenever we confess our sins, the blood of Jesus cleanses us from all sin (1 John 1:7, 9).

## VII. Sanctified Through the Offering of the Body Once for All

"By that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all." (Heb. 10:10)

Literal translation of the original text: "By this will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once and for all."

"That will" is God's will that took no pleasure in sacrifices offered according to the Old Covenant manner. Here, "sanctified" does not refer to sanctification in daily experience, but positional sanctification—a status acceptable to God.

When we receive Christ, we receive God's will that Christ fulfilled for us; now, by this will, being renewed by the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:5), we are set apart as holy.

"Have been sanctified" (referring to positional sanctification) is not the ultimate end of salvation, but the necessary step of salvation, enabling us to enter boldly into the Holiest before God (cf. Heb. 10:19, 22) to have fellowship with the holy God.

"Sanctification" is the prerequisite for fellowship and communion with God. No wonder the Book of Hebrews mentions it repeatedly, showing the importance of sanctification. Positional sanctification was accomplished for us by the Lord; practical sanctification in daily life requires our diligent pursuit.

## Conclusion

Just as Christ's salvation is an eternal salvation, so our being saved is an eternal salvation.

He Himself became for us the author of eternal salvation, or we may say the basis of eternal salvation, the source of eternal salvation; it can also be understood as the means of eternal salvation, the ground of eternal salvation. What reason do you have left to doubt?

## Response Prayer

Dear Lord Jesus, thank You that Your promises in Christ are all "Yes." Thank You for Your own promise: "The one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out." Please grant that I may live each day in Your secure salvation: relying not on my feelings, nor on my performance, but solely on the eternal efficacy of Your one offering, and on Yourself as the author of my eternal salvation. Amen!
