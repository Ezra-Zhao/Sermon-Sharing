## Content

The object of salvation. What of us does God want to save? The so-called salvation refers to the accomplishment of the way of salvation. In this wonderful arrangement and process, we not only need to know the time of salvation; we should also understand the object that is saved. [Original text partially illegible regarding time.] Just as time includes [past, present, and future], so the object of salvation should also include our [whole person]. According to the Bible, the whole person of each of us consists of three parts: spirit, [soul], and body. For Scripture says: "May your whole spirit, soul, and body be preserved blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Thess. 5:23, NKJV). Therefore we know that what God wants to save of us takes our spirit, [soul], and body as the objects of salvation.

"The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves such as have a contrite spirit" (Ps. 34:18, NKJV). 1. The revival of the spirit: "...[He] made us alive together with Christ" (Eph. 2:5, NKJV). 2. The entering of the Spirit: "God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into your hearts" (Gal. 4:6, NKJV). 3. The rebirth of the spirit: "That which is born of the flesh [is flesh], and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit" (John 3:6, NKJV) (1 John). In the object of salvation, God first saves our spirit, so that our life, which was separated from God — the spirit that died toward God because of Adam's fall — may be revived. Then God gives us the Spirit of His Son, that is, the Spirit of Christ, so that we may have the life of God (2 Corinthians). The former is the revival of the spirit (Psalm), and the latter is the rebirth of the spirit (1 Peter).

Literal translation: "receiving the end of your faith — the salvation of your souls" (1 Pet. 1:9, NKJV). 1. The keeping of the mind: "[the peace of God] will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus" (Phil. 4:7, NKJV) (2 Corinthians). 2. The transfer of the affections: "I long for you all with the affection of Jesus Christ" (Phil. 1:8, NKJV). 3. The directing of the will: "for it is God who works in you both to will and to do [for His good pleasure]" (Phil. 2:13, NKJV). [The salvation of the spirit] pertains to the past; the salvation of the soul pertains to the present. This wonderful way of saving the soul explains how our God saves us out of our own mind, affections, and will. He not only cleanses all these; He is even more willing to keep all these. Not only so, God is also pleased to manage all these and to use all these, so that we set our mind on the things of God and, according to God's working, will and do, love what God loves, and hate what God hates (1 Timothy) (1 Thessalonians). "[Christ] will appear a second time... to those who eagerly wait for Him... for salvation" (Heb. 9:28, NKJV).

1. The resurrection of the body: "those who have fallen asleep in Christ" (1 Cor. 15, NKJV). 2. The change of the body: "We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed" (1 Cor. 15:51, NKJV). 3. The redemption of the body: "eagerly waiting for the adoption, the redemption of our body" (Rom. 8:23, NKJV). God not only saves our spirit and soul; He will also save our body. But this pertains to future salvation! When the Lord Jesus comes again, we who belong to Him — the saints who have already fallen asleep — will be resurrected in body; the saints who are still living will be changed in body, and together we will be caught up in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air. This is the redemption of our body: freed from the limitations of time and space, changed in form, like the Lord's own body.

Homework questions: Of which three parts does our whole person consist? In His complete salvation, which parts of us does God want to save? Which two steps does the so-called salvation of the spirit include? Is there a difference between the Spirit of the Son and the Spirit of Christ? What is there in man's soul? What is meant by the salvation of the soul? What is the salvation of the body?
