"which is the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth." (1 Tim. 3:15)

## I. God Does Not Just Want You to Believe in Him; He Calls You to Belong to Him

Even in the perfect, sinless Garden of Eden, God said, "It is not good that man should be alone." We were created to live in community, to interact with others, and to build families; none of us can fulfill God's purposes on our own.

The Bible records no lone-ranger saints or hermits isolated from other believers. The Bible says we are put together, joined together, built together, members of one another, co-heirs of God, fitted together, knit together, and will be caught up together in the future—you are no longer alone.

Although your relationship with Christ is personal, God never intended for it to be private. In God's family, you are connected with other believers, belonging to one another forever. The Bible says, "so we, being many, are one body in Christ, and individually members of one another."

Following Christ is not only about believing, but also about belonging; we are members of His body—which is the church.

C. S. Lewis believed that the word "membership" originated in Christianity, but it has now lost its original meaning. Stores give discounts to "members," and advertisers compile member lists for promotional use. In the church, church membership often deteriorates into merely having one's name added to a membership roll, with no demands or expectations.

For Paul, being a "member" of the church meant being a vital organ of a living body, an indispensable, inseparable part of the body of Christ.

We must recover and practice the true biblical meaning of membership. The church is a body, not a building; a living organism, not an organization.

For an organ of the body to function, it must be connected to the body; the same is true for you as part of the body of Christ. You were created for your specific assignment, but if you are not connected to a living local church, you will miss the second purpose of your life. When you interact with others, you will discover your life's role. The Bible tells us: each part of the body has its function; this body is the body of Christ, the chosen people. Each person finds their meaning and function in His body. What value would we have if we were like a severed finger or a chopped-off toe?

If an organ is separated from the body, it shrivels and dies; it can never exist on its own. It is the same with you. Disconnected from the lifeblood of a local church, your spiritual life will wither and disappear. That is why the first symptom of spiritual backsliding is infrequent church attendance and losing contact with other believers. When we no longer care about fellowship, everything else goes downhill as well.

We cannot belittle or ignore the importance of being members of God's family, because the church is God's plan on earth. Jesus said, "I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it." The church will never be destroyed; it will endure forever, outlasting the universe, and so will your role. Those who say "I don't need the church" are either arrogant or ignorant. The church is so important that the Lord Jesus was crucified for it: "...just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her."

The Bible calls the church "the bride of Christ" or "the body of Christ." I cannot imagine saying to Jesus, "I love You, but I don't like Your wife," or "I accept You, but I don't want Your body." Yet whenever we despise the church, disparage the church, or complain about the church, that is exactly what we are doing. God commands us to love the church, just as Jesus loved the church. The Bible says, "Love your spiritual family." Sadly, far too many Christians only use the church, but do not love the church.

## II. Your Fellowship

When the Bible mentions the church, except for a few instances where it refers to believers across all ages, it always refers to a visible, local community. In the New Testament, local communities practiced membership. The only Christians who were not members of any local church community were those who were disciplined by the church for serious, open sin, and were thus removed from fellowship by the church as a disciplinary measure.

The Bible says that a Christian who is not involved in a church is like an organ without a body, a sheep without a flock, or a child without a home—all of which are abnormal conditions. The Bible says: you and other believers belong together as members of God's family.

Today's culture emphasizes independent individualism, creating many spiritual orphans—so-called "bunny believers" who hop from one church to another like rabbits, without identity, without a sense of responsibility, and lacking commitment. Many believe that one can be a "good Christian" without joining (or even attending) a church, but God completely disagrees. The Bible provides many compelling reasons why you should commit to a church and actively participate in the local fellowship of believers.

## III. Why Do You Need a Church Family?

### 3.1 Proves You Are a True Christian

A church family proves that you are a true believer. Those who do not commit to any group of believers cannot claim to be followers of Christ. Jesus said, "By this all will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another." When people of diverse backgrounds, races, and social standings come together in love as God's large family, it is a powerful witness to the world. An individual alone is not the body of Christ; you need to be together with other believers to be the body of Christ.

### 3.2 A Church Family Keeps You from Being Self-Centered

The church is the classroom where we learn how to get along with others in God's family. It is a laboratory for learning unselfishness and compassion toward others. As a participating member, you learn here to care for others and to share experiences with them. "And if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; or if one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it." Only when we regularly interact with ordinary, imperfect believers can we learn true fellowship and experience the New Testament truth of being connected and supporting one another.

The fellowship that God teaches is our mutual commitment to one another, just as our commitment is to Jesus Christ. God expects us to lay down our lives for one another. Many Christians know John 3:16, but fail to notice 1 John 3:16: "By this we know love, because He laid down His life for us. And we also ought to lay down our lives for the brethren." What God expects is that you love believers with this sacrificial love, being willing to love others in the same way Jesus loved you.

### 3.3 A Church Family Helps You Grow Spiritually

You will never grow to maturity simply by attending worship services or being a passive spectator. Your spiritual life will grow only through active participation in the life of a local church. The Bible says: as each person in the church does their part according to their function and ability, helping one another, the church is built up in love and grows.

The New Testament uses the phrases "one another" and "each other" more than fifty times. God commands us to love one another, pray for one another, encourage one another, exhort one another, greet one another, serve one another, teach one another, accept one another, honor one another, bear one another's burdens, forgive one another, submit to one another, be faithful to one another, and so on. This is what the Bible calls membership! This is the "family responsibility" God expects you to fulfill in the local fellowship of believers! With whom will you fulfill this goal?

It is easier to live a holy life when no one interferes with your choices, but this is a false holiness that cannot withstand testing. Isolation breeds self-deception; without anyone challenging us, it is easy to think we are mature. True maturity can only be demonstrated in interpersonal relationships.

To grow, in addition to reading the Bible, we need other believers. Learning from others and mutual accountability will cause us to grow faster and stronger. When others share how God has taught them, we also learn from it and grow.

### 3.4 The Body of Christ Needs You

In God's family, He has given you a unique role; this is called your "ministry," and God has given you gifts to do it. "The Holy Spirit gives different abilities to each person for the common good of the whole church."

It is God who placed you in your local fellowship of believers so that you can discover, develop, and use your gifts. You may have other broader ministries, but those are ministries beyond your service in the local church. Jesus did not promise to build your ministry; He promised to build His church.

### 3.5 You Share in Christ's Universal Mission

When Jesus was on earth, God worked through His physical body; now God works through His spiritual body. The church is God's instrument in the world. Our loving one another is not merely to demonstrate God's love, but to bring this love together to every corner of the world; this is truly an extraordinary privilege given to us by God. As members of Christ's body, we are His hands, His feet, His eyes, and His heart; He works in the world through us. Each of us has a contribution to make. Paul tells us: He created us through Jesus Christ to join in His work, the good works that He prepared beforehand for us to do.

### 3.6 A Church Family Helps You Avoid Spiritual Backsliding

No one is immune to temptation. Under certain circumstances, both you and I would sin, which is why God gave each person the responsibility to help others stay on the right path. The Bible says, "but exhort one another daily, while it is called 'Today,' lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin." "Minding only one's own business" is not an attitude a Christian should have; we are called and commanded to be involved in one another's lives. If you know someone whose spiritual life is in peril, you have the responsibility to lead them back into fellowship. James tells us: if you know anyone who has wandered from the truth, do not leave him alone; go after him and bring him back.

Another benefit of the local church is that spiritual leaders provide spiritual protection. God gives shepherds the responsibility to lead, protect, defend, and care for the spiritual well-being of their flock. The Bible tells us, "for they watch out for your souls, as those who must give account." Satan loves wandering believers, enticing them away from God's family and spiritual leaders, because he knows such believers are defenseless and powerless to resist his schemes.

## IV. It Is All in the Church

In his book *The Purpose Driven Church*, the author of *The Purpose Driven Life* explained why being a member of a healthy church is vital to living a wholesome life. He said this helps readers understand how God, through His plan for the church, helps you fulfill the five purposes of your life. God established the church to meet your five greatest needs: a purpose to live for, partners to live with, principles to live by, a faith to live out, and power to live on. Nowhere else in the world can you find all five of these benefits at the same time.

The five purposes God gave to the church are identical to the five purposes He gave to you. Worship helps you focus on God; fellowship helps you face life's difficulties; discipleship strengthens your faith; serving helps you discover your talents; evangelism enables you to fulfill your mission. There is truly nothing in the world that compares to the church!

## V. Your Choice

The moment an infant is born, they naturally become a member of universal humanity. Yet an infant still needs to belong to a specific family, to be fed and cared for in order to grow healthily. It is the same with spiritual matters. When you are born again, you naturally become a part of God's family, but you also need to commit to a local church and become a member of that church.

The difference between attending a church and becoming a member lies in commitment. Attenders are spectators on the sidelines; members participate in ministry. Attenders are consumers; members are contributors. Attenders want to get benefits from the church without sharing responsibility; they are like a man and a woman who just want to live together without committing to marriage. Why is joining a local church so important? Because it proves that you are genuinely invested in your spiritual brothers and sisters, rather than just in theory. God wants you to love real people, not perfect people. You could spend your entire life looking for the perfect church, but you will never find it. You are called to love imperfect sinners, just as God does.

In the book of Acts, the Christians in Jerusalem placed great emphasis on their commitment to one another and were extremely faithful to fellowship. The Bible says, "And they continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in the breaking of bread, and in prayers." God expects the same of us today.

The Christian life is not only about commitment to Christ, but also about commitment to other believers. The Macedonian Christians understood this. Paul said, "they first gave themselves to the Lord, and then to us by the will of God." Once you become a child of God, joining a local church is the natural next step. You become a Christian by committing to Christ, and you become a church member by committing to a specific group of believers. The first decision brings salvation; the second decision brings fellowship.

## Reflection for This Chapter

**Point to Ponder**: I am not only called to believe in God, but also to belong to God.

**Verse to Remember**: "so we, being many, are one body in Christ, and individually members of one another." (Rom. 12:5)

**Question to Consider**: Does my level of involvement in my local church demonstrate that I love the church and am committed to God's family?

## Response Prayer

Dear Father, thank You for calling me to belong to You—not only to believe, but to belong. Please cause me to be deeply connected to the local church, to learn sacrificial love in real relationships, and to grow to maturity in mutual commitment. I am willing to be a contributor, not just a consumer; willing to love imperfect sinners, just as You have loved me. Amen!
