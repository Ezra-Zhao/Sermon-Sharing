# Nothing New Under the Sun: Dating

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：约会».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of dating

## Summary

The problems in the dating stage look like emotional problems, but underneath they are boundary problems. Some take "God's will" as a tool for pursuit, some use cohabitation in place of marriage, some cannot bear to leave an unsuitable relationship, some call premarital sex a "test drive." This chapter responds one by one with four real Q&As, then makes two things clear: Do not lose yourself in dating; and when it truly comes to the step of breaking up, how to break up honestly and cleanly.

## I. Key Scripture: Clean Conduct Comes from Keeping His Word

> "How can a young man cleanse his way? By taking heed according to Your word." (Ps. 119:9)

Dating is not a neutral matter. It concerns whether a person's conduct is clean, and whether two people will in the future be able to walk into marriage. The method the Psalms give is not complicated: keep God's word. The four questions below are all how God's word is to be kept in specific matters, one by one.

## II. Four Q&As

### (1) Someone Says, "It Is God Who Told Me to Date You" — What Should I Do

A person I am not interested in wants to date me, telling me, "It is God who told me to date you," "God told me to marry you"; what should I do? I do not want to disobey God's will.

The response is: Saying "no" does not mean you are disobeying God. If God wants you to date a particular person, He will certainly have told His will to both of you. If only one party says so, and the other party has no confirmation at all in his heart, then there is no need to be bound by these words. You may reply like this:

"Thank you, I feel very honored. I truly believe I should listen to God's will, but He has not told me so, so I cannot agree to you. God may be preparing to guide you slowly toward a person, but that person is not me. As you keep seeking, He will certainly lead you to find the person truly suitable for you."

This reply has three things: honesty — not using fondness as a door, nor using refusal as a knife; respect — not mocking the other party's feelings; and boundaries — returning "God's will" to God, not letting it become a tool for either party to exert pressure. The Bible says:

> "He who speaks truth declares righteousness, but a false witness, deceit." (Prov. 12:17)

### (2) My Cohabiting Boyfriend Says He Is Not Ready to Marry — What Should I Do

My cohabiting boyfriend says he loves me, but is not ready to marry yet. How can I make him summon the courage to marry?

First see through the logic: If your boyfriend wants to "have both the fish and the bear's paw" — to be satisfied sexually without bearing the responsibility of marriage — where would he still have the motivation to marry? Cohabitation is not the road to marriage; it precisely removes the motivation to walk toward marriage.

Do not have sexual relations before marriage, otherwise you will damage the essence of love. According to God's word, neither of you is now truly showing love to each other. What love looks like, the Bible writes very concretely:

> "Love suffers long and is kind; love does not envy; love does not parade itself, is not puffed up." (1 Cor. 13:4)

Therefore, begin now to truly express your love — beginning with having him move out. If he truly loves you, he will need you, not only sexually, and will be willing to wait until marriage. Waiting itself is evidence of love. God's original intention was never cohabitation as trial marriage either:

> "And the LORD God said, 'It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him.'" (Gen. 2:18)

What God prepared for loneliness is a spouse, marriage — not cohabitation without a covenant.

### (3) He Has Many Strengths, But I Always Feel Pressure — Should I Ignore It

For several years, I have been dating the person I love. He has many excellent qualities, but he is negative and has a strong possessiveness and desire to control. Should I ignore the pressure I feel and only pay attention to his outstanding points?

Take a metaphor: When you are shopping and take a fancy to a pair of shoes, if these shoes do not fit, will you still buy them? No matter what attracts you about these shoes, if you buy them, wearing them will not be comfortable. Likewise, if in dating you feel undue pressure, regard this as a warning to you — "Perhaps he is not suitable."

Pressure in the dating stage is a warning signal, not a test. Do not maintain an unhealthy dating relationship only for your own need to be in love and to enjoy the feeling of love; remaining silent about the other party's shortcomings will in the end hurt both people. Tell the other party your feelings honestly. Paul continues:

> "Does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, thinks no evil." (1 Cor. 13:5)

Look against these sentences: Possession and control seek one's own benefit; easily getting angry and bringing up old accounts are precisely the opposite of what this verse says. However many strengths there may be, if the character of love does not pass, in marriage it will only be harder.

### (4) He Says He Must First "Test Drive" — What Should I Do

My boyfriend thinks we must first determine whether we are also suited to each other sexually. He says he would not buy a car without test-driving it. I know premarital sex is wrong; what should I do?

The answer first lands on dignity: You are not a car owned by a person, but God's creation, worthy to be cherished and loved dearly. A person who compares a person to a car has already in his heart treated a person as a thing. When your boyfriend gets tired of you, he will kick you out of his life — the attitude toward things will sooner or later show itself.

Wait patiently for the person who respects your moral views. Your conduct of keeping yourself holy can truly please God. The Bible's attitude toward this line has no gray area:

> "Marriage is honorable among all, and the bed undefiled; but fornicators and adulterers God will judge." (Heb. 13:4)

In the Bible there is a negative example, worth every young person reading once. Amnon was infatuated with Tamar, and used a scheme to trick her into the bedroom (2 Sam. 13:10-11). Tamar refused him to his face:

> "Then Tamar said to him, 'No, my brother, do not force me, for no such thing should be done in Israel. Do not do this disgraceful thing! And I, where could I take my shame? And as for you, you would be like one of the fools in Israel. Now therefore, please speak to the king; for he will not withhold me from you.'" (2 Sam. 13:12-13)

Amnon would not listen, and forced her by strength. The next sentence in the Scripture punctures the so-called "only after testing will you know if you fit":

> "Then Amnon hated her exceedingly, so that the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. And Amnon said to her, 'Arise, be gone!'" (2 Sam. 13:15)

"Love" without a covenant, after obtaining, immediately becomes hatred. This is not an isolated case; this is a pattern. So before entering this chapter, the source material first places a sentence in front: Any romance not aimed at marriage is acting as a hooligan.

## III. Do Not Lose Yourself in Dating

There is another sentence that needs to be heard in advance. If a man in a dating relationship says like this: "Without her, I cannot live; I must be with her," this is equivalent to saying he has lost himself. If that woman suddenly died, what would happen to him? If his self is lost in her, then leaving her, he would not know who he is.

We should not lose ourselves because of a person. You can be united with another, but must still maintain your independent personality. You are an independent individual; in Christ you are a complete person.

Beneath this loss is the longing for love, self-worth, and security, used in the wrong place. The wrong understanding is: I feel I must find a suitable person to date, then marry, otherwise my needs will not be satisfied. The right understanding is: I hope to date and marry, but I entrust my future to God. God will not lead everyone into the hall of marriage, but He will satisfy our heart's needs for love, self-worth, and security. I will trust the Lord, let Him guide me, and satisfy my deepest needs.

> "And the LORD will guide you continually, and satisfy your soul in drought, and strengthen your bones; you shall be like a watered garden, and like a spring of water, whose waters do not fail." (Isa. 58:11)

The needs are real, but the source of satisfaction is not a certain person. When the source is found correctly, dating is two complete people choosing each other; when the source is found incorrectly, dating becomes a tug-of-war of mutual demanding.

## IV. When It Comes to the Step of Breaking Up, How to Break Up

Some relationships, walked honestly to the end, conclude that they cannot walk toward marriage. The Bible says:

> "A prudent man foresees evil and hides himself, but the simple pass on and are punished." (Prov. 27:12)

Seeing something wrong and still forcing forward, those hurt are often both parties. The words for breaking up may open like this: "I am becoming more and more clearly aware that it is difficult for us to walk toward marriage. We are developing in different directions, and I feel very uncomfortable about this."

The party proposing the breakup must keep several lines: Express honestly and directly, do not prolong the other party's pain; do not keep the word "reconciliation" on the lips, giving false hope; keep the breakup process as short as possible; if there is no intention of reconciliation, do not further explore the other party's thoughts; finally, pray for the other party.

The party being proposed to must also keep several lines: Listen seriously, do not beg, do not threaten; apologize where apology is due; do not talk about the other party anymore; forgive, then look forward. Paul's words fit precisely here:

> "Brethren, I do not count myself to have apprehended; but one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind and reaching forward to those things which are ahead, I press toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus." (Phil. 3:13-14)

Breaking up cleanly, both people can retain dignity, and also retain the road to begin anew.

## V. Practice

The memorization Scripture is the key Scripture of this chapter (Ps. 119:9). Read repeatedly Proverbs chapters 1 to 7 — those are the chapters the Bible wrote for young people about wisdom, about temptation, about boundaries; almost every pit in dating is written there in advance.

## Conclusion

This lesson on dating sums up in three sentences: God's will is not to be used as an excuse for pursuit; love takes marriage as its direction and waiting as its evidence; a person first becomes a complete person in God, then talks about union. There is nothing new under the sun; the temptations and pressures in dating today — Proverbs wrote them long ago, and 2 Samuel performed them long ago. Understanding them, one walks far fewer detours.
