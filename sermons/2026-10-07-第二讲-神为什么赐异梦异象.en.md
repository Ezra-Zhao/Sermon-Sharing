"Deep calls unto deep at the noise of Your waterfalls; All Your waves and billows have gone over me." (Ps. 42:7)

"Deep calls unto deep" — the depths of God call to our depths. God gives dreams and visions not to show us a spectacle, but so that His word and our lives answer one another.

If God speaks to you through a dream, what does He most want to tell you? Some say: to foretell the future. Some say: to point me to a clear path. Some say: to have me pray for a certain matter. All of these are right, but none is the whole picture. In this lecture, let us consider: Why does God give dreams and visions?

## I. The Basic Purpose: Communication Between God and Man

Brother Zhang Xun summarizes in the course that the most basic purpose of dreams and visions is **communication between God and man**, in five specific aspects:

**First, teaching and correction.** "But he who prophesies speaks edification and exhortation and comfort to men" (1 Cor. 14:3). God teaches us through visions, and He also corrects us. Job says that God "opens the ears of men, And seals their instruction" (Job 33:16) — sometimes we will not listen to reason, so God uses a dream to wake us up.

**Second, revelation and the indicating of a goal.** "Where there is no revelation, the people cast off restraint; But happy is he who keeps the law" (Prov. 29:18). The word "revelation" here (original Hebrew חָזוֹן, transliterated chazon; Strong H2377; according to *BDB Lexicon*, "vision, revelation") carries the meaning of "oracle, revelation" in the original language. A person, or a church, without direction from God will cast off restraint and drift with the current. God gives visions to give us direction.

**Third, warning.** God tests through dreams and visions whether our hearts are thirsty, and also reminds us: the day of the Lord's return is near. "He said to me: 'Do not seal the words of the prophecy of this book, for the time is at hand... And behold, I am coming quickly!'" (Rev. 22:10, 12). Visions of the last days always carry the cry of "prepare."

**Fourth, ministry.** This concerns the direction of the church, and the establishing and sending out of the fivefold ministry. Many missionaries' callings began with a vision.

**Fifth, the prophetic.** This concerns the last days, intercession, spiritual warfare, and prophecy. God lets us see part of His heart in advance so that we may participate in His plan — especially in intercession.

Do you see it? When God speaks, it is never to satisfy our curiosity. Behind every vision of His there is a purpose: to edify, to guide, to warn, to send.

## II. The Deeper Purpose: Preparing Us to Enter the Heavenly World of Eternal Life

Beyond communication, God has a deeper purpose: **to prepare us to enter the heavenly world of eternal life**. Brother Zhang Xun puts forward four "awarenesses":

**First, an awareness of the reality of the spiritual world.** Everything we serve in and everything in our lives must be measured from eternity. "For all flesh is as grass, And all the glory of man as the flower of the grass. The grass withers, And its flower falls away, But the word of the LORD endures forever" (1 Pet. 1:24-25). Dreams and visions often pull our short-sighted gaze up to the height of eternity in a single stroke. When you are anxious to the point of insomnia over gains and losses at work, a dream from God may let you see: in eternity, what truly matters?

**Second, an awareness of the brevity of life.** Do not come to this world in vain. When Jeremiah was still young, God said to him: "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you; Before you were born I sanctified you; I ordained you a prophet to the nations" (Jer. 1:5). Every person has a destiny given by God, and a vision is often God reminding us: return to your destiny. Do not exhaust your whole life on someone else's racetrack.

**Third, an awareness that God is Spirit.** "God is Spirit, and those who worship Him must worship in spirit and truth" (John 4:24). We are too accustomed to approaching God with the flesh, the body, and reason. Dreams and visions remind us: God is Spirit, and fellowship with Him must be in spirit. Worship is not only singing, and prayer is not only speaking; it is the fellowship of spirit with Spirit.

**Fourth, an awareness of God's power that transcends all.** Paul said that our gospel came to people "not in word only, but also in power, and in the Holy Spirit and in much assurance" (1 Thess. 1:5). When God manifests His great power in a vision, our faith is ignited, and we no longer preach the gospel by eloquence alone. This is how the early church turned the world upside down — not by debate, but by the power of the Holy Spirit.

These four awarenesses are in fact adjusting the coordinates of our lives: from earth to heaven, from the temporary to the eternal, from the flesh to the Holy Spirit.

## III. The Key to Interpretation: Interpretations Belong to God

Having understood the purpose, the next question is: **Having received a dream or vision, how do we interpret it?**

There is only one key, and Joseph spoke it long ago:

> "So Joseph said to them, 'Do not interpretations belong to God? Tell them to me, please.'" (Gen. 40:8, NKJV)

Interpretation is not by mental analysis, and not by a dream-interpretation book; it is by **asking the Holy Spirit**. Brother Zhang Xun gives several practical principles in the course:

First, dreams and visions are often symbolic and need the Holy Spirit to open them up. Do not interpret them literally and rigidly.

Second, when seeking an interpretation, you may ask for a verse of Scripture as confirmation. God's word is the most secure anchor.

Third, pay attention to the confirmations God gives through the people, matters, and things around you. When God speaks, there is often an "echo."

Fourth, **do not interpret dreams by empiricism**. The same symbol may have a completely different meaning for a different person. Last time water represented the Holy Spirit; this time it may represent something else. Every time, ask afresh.

Let us look at Paul — one of the people in the Bible who received the most visions. The book of Acts records eight key moments of guidance in his life:

1. On the road to Damascus he met Jesus, and his whole life was turned around (Acts 9:3-9);
2. The Lord instructed Ananias in a vision to go and lay hands on Paul, and Paul had also seen Ananias beforehand in a vision (Acts 9:10-12) — **God spoke on both sides at the same time, confirming one another**;
3. While praying in the temple he fell into a trance and received the calling to go to the Gentiles (Acts 22:17-21);
4. He was caught up to the third heaven and heard inexpressible words (2 Cor. 12:2);
5. At Troas he saw the Macedonian vision in the night — "Come over to Macedonia and help us," and from there the gospel entered Europe (Acts 16:9-10);
6. In Corinth the Lord strengthened Paul in a vision and promised His presence: "Do not be afraid, but speak, and do not keep silent" (Acts 18:9-10);
7. At night in Jerusalem the Lord strengthened Paul and sent him to testify in Rome (Acts 23:11);
8. In the storm an angel appeared: "Do not be afraid, Paul... God has granted you all those who sail with you" (Acts 27:23-24).

Please note: Paul never exalted these experiences. But these experiences were one of the reasons for his "much assurance." What we are to imitate is **not dreams and visions themselves, but Paul's intimate walk with God**. When we walk intimately with God to a certain point, God, who shows no partiality, will also surely manifest His presence to us.

## IV. Life Application: Six Kinds of Readers

**Those always asking, "Why does God speak to me?"**: The answer is in this lecture — to teach you, to guide you, to warn you, to send you. Ask about the purpose first, then ask about the meaning.

**Those who pursue experiences but neglect the purpose**: Stop and think: the last thing you received — did you carry it out afterward? A vision without obedience is only a dream.

**Those standing at a crossroads in life, needing guidance**: Ask God, and also pay attention to His guidance through dreams and visions. But remember: the right of interpretation belongs to God. Do not force your own interpretation; find mature coworkers to discern together.

**Leaders praying for the direction of the church**: "Where there is no revelation, the people cast off restraint." A church's vision is to be received in prayer, not discussed into existence in a conference room.

**Those with a burden of intercession**: God often lets intercessors see His heart first. When you see it, kneel down — a vision is an invitation letter to pray.

**Young people**: When Paul met the Lord on the road to Damascus, he was also a young man. Do not wait until your "spiritual life is deep" to seek God. God delights to speak to you while you are young.

## Conclusion

God gives dreams and visions not for us to watch a spectacle, but for us to hear Him and respond to Him. From teaching to warning, from direction to sending, behind every vision there is a Father's heart; from awareness of eternity to awareness of His great power, every vision is adjusting the coordinates of our lives.

## Response Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You that when You speak, You always have a purpose. Lord, we confess that sometimes we only want experiences and do not want obedience; we only want foretelling and do not want warning. Forgive us, we pray.

Lord, let us understand Your heart, we pray. When You speak to us through dreams and visions, grant us the interpretation as well, as You did for Joseph, for Daniel, and for Paul. Let us not interpret recklessly by the flesh, but only ask the Holy Spirit.

We are willing to imitate Paul — not imitating the experiences themselves, but imitating the intimate walk with You. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
