## Opening Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, as we come before You, we first quiet our hearts. Lord, each of us carries prayers that we have prayed for a very long time without yet seeing an answer. Some desires have stayed in our hearts for so long that we hardly dare mention them anymore. We ask that today, through Your Word, You would bring us back into Your heart. Not to teach us how to bargain with You, but to let us know afresh what kind of Father You are, and to understand what kind of life You created and redeemed us to live. Holy Spirit, please open our hearts Yourself and let Your Word abide in us. Remove discouragement, bitterness, and unbelief, and take away every notion that treats prayer as a mere formula. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.

Peace be with you, brothers and sisters. The theme of our message today is: *Praying from the Father's Heart*.

Please think honestly for a moment: Do you have a prayer you have been praying for three years, five years, or even ten years? You have prayed for your children, for your parents' health, for your marriage, for your job, or for a church and a city. In the beginning, you were full of faith. But as time went on, your words grew fewer and fewer, until only one question lingered in your heart: *Lord, are You really listening?*

Proverbs captures this heart condition with striking clarity: "Hope deferred makes the heart sick, / But when the desire comes, it is a tree of life" (Prov. 13:12). When hope is dragged out endlessly, a person's heart grows sick. But when what is longed for arrives, that answer does not merely resolve a single issue; like a tree of life, it imparts life to the whole person.

Before the Lord Jesus departed this world, He also spoke to His disciples about answered prayer. He said: "Until now you have asked nothing in My name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full" (John 16:24). Notice that Jesus never spoke of prayer as a religious obligation. He bound prayer to three things: asking the Father, receiving, and fullness of joy. Prayer is not a difficult puzzle God left for us from heaven; prayer is the Father's invitation to His children to enter His heart, to experience His faithfulness in His answers, and to experience joy returning afresh to our souls.

Today we will examine this theme from three perspectives: First, throughout Scripture, God weaves the story of answered prayer around the tree of life—we were originally created to bear fruit. Second, the root of prayer lies not in techniques, but in abiding in Christ, allowing the Father's heart to shape our hearts through His Word. Third, when answers are delayed, we do not compromise with disappointment; rather, we bring our questions clearly before the Lord, and let forgiveness clear the way for our prayers.

## Part 1: The Main Line of the Tree of Life—Man Was Originally Created to Bear Fruit

Scripture speaks of the tree of life from the very beginning. Genesis 2:9 says: "And out of the ground the LORD God made every tree grow that is pleasant to the sight and good for food. The tree of life was also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil."

In Hebrew, "the tree of life" is *ets ha-chayyim* (עֵץ הַחַיִּים). *Ets* means tree, and *chayyim* means life—in a plural form often used to convey an abundant, complete life. When God created man at the beginning, He did not place him in a barren world barely scraping by for survival. He placed man in a garden, surrounded by beautiful trees, with abundant food, and with the tree of life right in the midst of the garden. Man's original design was to receive life in God's presence, and then to live out and release this life.

Yet after man ate from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, the problem was not merely that a rule had been broken; man's relationship with God was fractured. Genesis 3:22–24 tells us that God drove man out of the Garden of Eden and placed cherubim with a flaming sword turning every way to guard the way to the tree of life. God guarded the tree of life not out of stinginess or an unwillingness to give good things to man. If man, in a state of sin and death, were to eat of the tree of life, that broken state would be frozen into eternity—and that would be the true tragedy. God's restraint was born of protection. Later, He sent His Son to deal with the problem of sin first, and then reopen the way of life to man.

By the time we come to Proverbs, the tree of life is no longer just a tree in a garden; it has become a posture of life. Proverbs 13:12 says: "Hope deferred makes the heart sick, / But when the desire comes, it is a tree of life." Here, "hope" in the original text conveys a long waiting, followed by a word meaning "drawn out, postponed." When hope is prolonged too long, the heart aches and loses strength, like a body falling ill. In the second half of the verse, "the desire," *ta'avah* (תַּאֲוָה) in Hebrew, refers to a genuine, deep longing of the heart. The Bible never tells us to pretend we have no desires. God knows people wait, and He knows waiting hurts. Even more, God tells us: when a desire aligned with His heart finally comes, it is not merely a wish fulfilled—it is like the fruit of the tree of life, bringing strength, direction, and life all over again.

Proverbs 15:4 says again: "A wholesome tongue is a tree of life, / But perverseness in it breaks the spirit." "Wholesome" in the original language carries the meaning of healing. In other words, a word spoken out of God's life can be like the fruit of the tree of life, bringing healing to someone. When someone is in despair, and you encourage them according to truth, pray for them, and tell them God has not given up on them, those are not mere pleasantries; your words can minister grace to their heart. Conversely, cynical, condemning, and cursing words break the spirit. Every day when we speak, we are actually making a choice: Will I hand people the fruit of the tree of life, or will I hand them bitterness?

Revelation 22:2 shows us the ultimate conclusion: "...on either side of the river, was the tree of life, which bore twelve fruits, each tree yielding its fruit every month. The leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations." The Bible begins in the Garden of Eden and concludes in the new heaven and new earth. In the beginning, man was separated from the tree of life because of sin; in the end, God's people come once again to the tree of life in His city. Moreover, this tree bears fruit continuously, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations.

Therefore, answered prayer is not an isolated spiritual technique. When a person is connected to God, and God's life is at work within him, what he asks of the Father is granted—and that is fruit. Psalm 1:3 describes the righteous person: "He shall be like a tree / Planted by the rivers of water, / That brings forth its fruit in its season, / Whose leaf also shall not wither." A tree does not need to anxiously command itself every day to bear fruit. What the tree must do is put its roots down by the water, receive the sunlight, and remain in its season. The Christian prayer life operates the same way: fruit is the result of connection, not of strained striving.

Brothers and sisters, you were not created merely to endure your days, maintain safety, and live unfruitful lives. You have been planted by God. God wants your family to be healed through your prayers, your workplace to be blessed through your integrity and intercession, and the people around you to stand back up because of the wholesome words from your mouth. Every answered prayer is a piece of fruit God produces in your life, reminding you: *I am not an orphan; I am a child connected to the Father. My life has an eternal purpose.*

Proverbs 3:18 says concerning wisdom: "She is a tree of life to those who take hold of her, / And happy are all who retain her." Proverbs 11:30 adds: "The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life." These two passages tell us that the tree of life is not a scene reserved only for the new heaven and new earth. Today, when someone walks in God's wisdom and follows the path of righteousness, their life becomes a place where others receive life. When you refuse deceit in your workplace, your colleagues may see for the first time that integrity can truly be lived out. When you refrain from hurling angry words at home, your children may taste peace through what you say. When you faithfully intercede in the church, someone who is weak may return before God because of your prayers. Fruit is not an ornament; inside the fruit are seeds that will continue to bring forth new life.

Ezekiel 47 speaks of the river flowing from the temple: wherever the river flows, there is life; on both banks grow all kinds of trees, bearing new fruit every month, and their leaves are for healing. The tree of life in Revelation does not appear out of nowhere; it echoes the picture the prophet saw: God's presence flows out from the sanctuary, bringing life to the city, to all nations. Today, the church is called to live out this picture in advance. If our prayers only ask for our own problems to be solved, there is certainly nothing wrong with that—a father is willing to hear his children's needs; but God wants to take our prayers further: to pray for our families, to pray for our coworkers, to pray for our city, to pray for those who do not yet know Him. The more the scope of our prayer expands from the Father's heart, the more our lives become like a tree planted by the river, its fruit for people to eat, its leaves for healing, its shade for people to rest.

Be careful, too—the fruit spoken of in the Bible is not an achievement displayed for people to see. Some people look very busy on the outside, yet in their hearts they are disconnected from God; some people quietly care for their families, work faithfully, and continually intercede—they may seem unremarkable, yet in God's season they bear abundant fruit. The Lord values connection and faithfulness, not the size of the stage. You need only take root, only abide in Him, only live and pray according to His word. The season of fruit is determined by God, and the glory of the fruit belongs to God as well.

In John 15:2, the Lord Jesus also mentions something that many people dislike, yet is vitally important: "...every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit." (John 15:2). After bearing fruit, there is pruning. When God takes something away from our lives, it is not necessarily punishment; it may be to concentrate the nutrients so that the next season's fruit will be even better. A failure, a closed door, a season forced to slow down—on the surface they look like loss, but in God's hands they may be a pruning. When we pray for fruit, we must also be willing to accept the Vinedresser's hand. Do not only ask God to change your circumstances without asking Him to prune you; many times, before the circumstances change, what God deals with first is our pride, impatience, greed, and unbelief. Only those who are willing to be pruned can hold greater fruit.

## Part Two: The Root of Prayer—Abiding in Christ, Letting the Father's Heart Become Our Heart

John 15 is where the Lord Jesus speaks about the vine and the branches. Only when the branch is connected to the vine will it bear fruit. Verse 7 says: "If you abide in Me, and My words abide in you, you will ask what you desire, and it shall be done for you." (John 15:7).

"Abide" in the original Greek is *menō*, which means not just dropping by occasionally, but staying, remaining, and continually dwelling. For a person to be in Christ is not merely spending two hours at church on Sunday, but allowing the Lord's word to continually dwell in their heart throughout the week: letting God speak during morning Bible reading, letting His word take charge when facing decisions at work, and surrendering one's heart to His examination when reflecting on the day at night. The condition for answered prayer is not how stirred our emotions are, but whether we abide in Him and whether His words abide in us.

Verse 16 continues: "You did not choose Me, but I chose you and appointed you that you should go and bear fruit, and that your fruit should remain, that whatever you ask the Father in My name He may give you." (John 15:16). First comes election, then appointment; first relationship, then fruit; first the identity of children, then prayer in the Lord's name. Praying in Jesus' name is not tacking an incantation onto the end of a prayer; it is standing in the salvation Jesus has accomplished, asking the Father according to His character, His will, and His renown. If our prayer contradicts Jesus' heart, saying "in His name" with our lips is meaningless; if our prayer springs from His word, God is delighted to fulfill it, because it is already the shared desire of the Father and the Son.

How, then, are human desires shaped? Psalm 37:4 says: "Delight yourself also in the Lord, And He shall give you the desires of your heart." (Ps. 37:4). This verse does not mean that as long as I joyfully worship once, I can shove my wish list to God. Its order is crucial: delight yourself first in the Lord; first let God Himself become your greatest joy. When a person truly delights in God, the desires of their heart will gradually change. Previously, they only sought their own comfort; later, their heart began to ache for the salvation of others. Previously, they asked God to remove every difficulty; later, they asked God to display His glory in the midst of difficulty. Previously, they prayed for position and income; later, they still asked for provision, but even more, they asked to be salt and light in that position. God does not destroy their desires; He redeems, purifies, and elevates them.

John 14:13 says: "And whatever you ask in My name, that I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son." (John 14:13). The ultimate purpose of answered prayer is that the Father may be glorified in the Son. When a child is healed of sickness, the Father is glorified; when a company is provided for with integrity, the Father is glorified; when a prodigal returns home, the Father is glorified; when a church prays in revival, the Father is glorified. The angels have never tasted what it is like for a sinner to be forgiven, nor have they experienced the process of a broken person being made a child of God anew, petitioning the Father, and having the Father personally respond. Yet we live in this grace every single day. Do not treat this privilege lightly.

Praying from the Father's heart does not mean giving up your own desires or pretending not to care about anything; it means coming before the Father, laying out your desires, letting His word align them, His love cleanse them, and His will elevate them. Such prayer is both authentic and submissive, both bold and orderly. We can say to God: "Father, this is the desire of my heart; I do not hide it. Yet I desire even more that what You want may come to pass, because Your heart sees more accurately than my vision, Your timing is more beautiful than my urgency, and the fruit You give me will endure forever."

When prayer is answered like this, joy is not merely the relief of getting something done, but the satisfaction of seeing the Father listen, seeing His will accomplished, and seeing oneself co-laboring with Him. Therefore Jesus said: "Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full." (John 16:24). "Full" in the original language conveys the sense of being filled up and completed. God's answer fills a place that was once vacant, bringing the believer's heart into a conviction: the Father knows me, the Father hears me, the Father is at work.

When the Lord Jesus taught the disciples to pray, He also gave us an order: first address God as Father; first pray that His name be hallowed, His kingdom come, and His will be done on earth as it is in heaven; and only then ask for daily bread, the forgiveness of our debts, and deliverance from the evil one. This order is not telling us to hide our own needs. On the contrary, precisely because He is our Father, we can boldly ask for our daily needs. But before we open our mouths to list our needs, we are first brought into His heart: who He is, what He is going to do, and what His will is. Praying like this over time aligns the human heart. We will no longer treat God as a tool to accomplish our personal plans, but will bring our plans before Him, willing to be revised and led by Him.

Hannah in the Old Testament gives us a very real example. For years she had no children, and she was grieved in spirit, weeping bitterly in prayer before the Lord. She did not deny her desire, nor did she pretend to ask for nothing just to appear spiritual. She poured out her soul and anguish before God, asking Him for a son, and made a vow to dedicate the child to the Lord all the days of his life. Later she said: "For this child I prayed, and the Lord has granted me my petition which I asked of Him." (1 Sam. 1:27). Notice that Hannah's desire was elevated in prayer. Initially, she asked for a child to comfort her own grief; in the end, she was willing to offer the child she received to God, allowing him to become an instrument of blessing for Israel. She did not lose her joy because of giving him up; the beginning of 1 Samuel 2 is precisely her prayer of joyful praise. True answered prayer does not merely allow a person to receive what they desired; it also makes them willing to let the grace they received continue flowing to others.

Romans 8:26–27 tells us that we do not know what we should pray for as we ought, but the Spirit Himself makes intercession for us with groanings which cannot be uttered, and the Spirit makes intercession for the saints according to the will of God. (Rom. 8:26-27). Many people assume that praying from the Father's heart is some lofty spiritual realm, and that because they are so weak and chaotic, they are far too distant from it. But that is not true. The Holy Spirit dwells within believers, and He helps us. He understands the pain in our hearts that cannot be put into words; He makes up for our faltering prayers; when we do not know what to ask, He intercedes according to God's will. What you need to do is not package your prayers to look pretty, but genuinely come before the Father, lingering, listening, and obeying in the help of the Holy Spirit. Prayer is not an exam; prayer is children coming home to talk with their Father.

Praying from the Father's heart has another mark: you will begin to pray for others. In his suffering, Job prayed for his friends, and the Scripture says: "And the Lord restored Job’s losses when he prayed for his friends." (Job 42:10). Those friends had once wounded Job with misplaced words, yet he was able to intercede for them. When a person fixates only on their own needs, their prayer easily becomes more and more bitter the more they pray; but someone who is willing to stand in the gap for others will find that the Father's heart has widened within them. In the church, the most powerful prayers often do not come from those who shout the loudest for themselves, but from those who quietly take note when they hear of someone's trouble, and faithfully intercede for them by name week after week. They bear fruit where no one sees, and their prayers uphold the entire church.

## Part Three: When the Answer Is Delayed—Bring the Problem Before the Lord, Let Forgiveness Clear the Way

Mark 9 records that a father brought his demon-possessed child to the disciples, but they could not cast it out. The father came before Jesus and spoke the words that lie deep in the hearts of so many who pray: "Lord, I believe; help my unbelief!" After Jesus cast out the unclean spirit, the disciples asked Him privately, "Why could we not cast it out?" Jesus said, "This kind can come out by nothing but prayer and fasting" (Mark 9:29).

In this passage, what I value most is one action of the disciples: they asked Jesus privately why. After failure, it is very easy for people to concoct an explanation: Maybe God just does not want to do it; maybe this problem will never change; maybe our generation can only be like this. The disciples did not sit in disappointment making up excuses; they took Jesus aside and pressed in with an earnest question. Brothers and sisters, when prayer has not yet been answered, do not rush to package your disappointment as theology. Bring the question before the Lord: "Lord, where is the problem? Do I need to keep waiting, or do I need to confess and repent? Do I need to fast, or do I need to go and make peace with someone? Do I need to pay a price and take action, or do I need to learn to rest and cease my own striving?"

Not every delay has only a single cause, and God's sovereignty and mysteries are not something we can fully explain. Some sicknesses are not healed immediately; some doors do not open according to our timetable; we do not crush those in pain with simplistic words. But we have one certain lesson: do not let an unanswered prayer become a reason for growing distant from God. After the disciples failed, they did not leave Jesus; instead, they drew closer to Him to ask. True faith is not about never having questions, but about still coming to the Lord when you do.

In Mark 11, Jesus spoke about the prayer of faith: "Whatever things you ask when you pray, believe that you receive them, and you will have them" (Mark 11:24). The verse that immediately follows in verse 25 is crucial: "And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive him, that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses." After speaking of great faith in prayer, Jesus did not pivot to an unrelated topic. Forgiveness is an integral part of the prayer life. When a person asks God to make a way on the one hand, while locking someone tightly in the prison of their heart on the other, their own heart is locked up as well.

The original word for "forgive" is *aphiēmi*, which carries the meaning of releasing, letting go, and unbinding. Forgiveness does not mean the other person did nothing wrong; nor is it forcing the wounded person to restore an intimate relationship immediately; much less is it letting wrongdoers evade the responsibilities they ought to bear. Forgiveness means taking judgment and retaliation out of my own hands and returning them to God. It means I no longer allow that person to occupy my heart day after day; I release myself from bitterness first. Many prayers are stuck not because God cannot hear, but because our hearts are crammed full of old debts, leaving no room to receive new grace.

If effective prayer is likened to an advancing spear, forgiveness is its very tip. This is not because forgiveness earns grace from God, but because an unforgiving heart is disconnected from the Father's heart; whereas when a person is willing to forgive, their heart is first set free by God, and their prayer returns once again to the relationship of a child with their Father. Brothers and sisters, if the Holy Spirit reminds you today of a certain person, a certain phrase, or an event from years ago, do not delay. First confess your bitterness before God, and ask the Lord to give you strength to release that person step by step, and let your own heart be released as well.

When we pray like this, breakthrough does not merely refer to a sudden change in external circumstances. True breakthrough often happens within first: the discouraged regain hope, the bitter are once again able to bless, and those who hid from God become willing once more to draw near to the Father. Before the outer door opens, the inner lock is unlocked first; before the matter is fully resolved, the heart has already returned to the Father's embrace. This is the deepest fruit of prayer.

In Luke 18, the Lord Jesus spoke a parable "that men always ought to pray and not lose heart." The widow kept coming before the unjust judge pleading for justice, and even such a judge finally granted her request because of her persistence. How much more, then, will our heavenly Father avenge His own elect who cry out day and night to Him? Jesus told this parable precisely to address the discouragement of those who pray. Discouragement takes many forms: our words grow shorter, our hearts grow harder, gradually we cannot even summon enthusiasm for the Lord's Day, and finally we simply stop mentioning the matter altogether, as if having no expectations means we will not be hurt again. But having no expectations is not peace; having no expectations is the heart surrendering first. The Lord calls us back before Him, time and again, with genuine desires and with a submissive heart.

There is another kind of prayer: the church praying together. In Acts 12, Peter was kept in prison, but constant prayer was offered to God for him by the church. The doors were opened, and Peter returned to the house of Mark. When he knocked on the door, the very people inside who were praying could hardly believe it. Our faith is sometimes pitifully small; those who pray do not even dare to recognize the very thing they asked for when it arrives. Yet God did not refrain from working because their faith was small. Brothers and sisters, do not shoulder alone the things you cannot pray through on your own. Bring your needs to your small group, bring them to the prayer meeting, and let the church join you in asking. Much fruit is not borne by one person alone; it is borne by the whole body remaining connected together to the vine.

When you are in the midst of waiting, let me give you four questions you can bring before the Lord. First, has my desire been aligned by Your Word? Am I seeking Your will, or am I merely asking You to fulfill my own wishes? Second, is there unaddressed sin, bitterness, or unforgiveness within me? Am I willing to let the Lord cleanse me first? Third, is there an action I ought to take in this matter? Am I praying on the one hand, while evading my responsibilities in reality on the other? Fourth, am I willing, even when I do not see fruit immediately, to remain in You, not lose heart, and continue to love You, love others, and live faithfully? When you finish asking these four questions, you may still not know God's timing, but your heart will be realigned with Him. This is the most important breakthrough in the waiting.

Daniel 10 offers great comfort to those who wait. Daniel humbled his heart and prayed for twenty-one days to understand the vision, and when the angel arrived, he told him: from the very first day he set his heart to understand and to humble himself, his words were heard. The answer did not begin on the twenty-first day; it was already on its way on the very first day, only he could not see it. Brothers and sisters, delay does not equal denial; silence does not mean God is absent. The prayer of your very first day on your knees has already been heard in heaven. During those days we cannot see, God may be preparing people, preparing circumstances, and preparing our own hearts. Do not judge the progress of heaven's work by the speed visible to your eyes. Simply continue to abide in Him, continue to pray according to His heart, and wait for His season.

## Living Out This Sermon

First, to those striving in the workplace: As you pray for promotions, projects, visas, and team reorganizations, do not merely ask God, "Can You give this to me?" First ask the Father's heart: "Lord, what kind of person do You want me to become in this role?" When facing unfairness at work, first bring your anger into prayer, asking God to give you wisdom and courage; communicate where communication is needed, take responsibility where it is yours to bear, and forgive before the Lord where forgiveness is called for. Your office can also become a place where the fruit of the tree of life is borne: an honest word, a refusal to go along with the crowd, an intercession for a colleague—these are all fruit.

Second, to those in business and entrepreneurship: As you pray for orders, cash flow, and partners, God cares about your provision, and He also cares about the way you receive that provision. Do not walk into gray areas just to obtain an "answer," and do not treat clients and employees as mere tools to achieve your goals. Lay your accounts bare before God, ask honestly, and work diligently; in the days of waiting, do not grab recklessly at opportunities, nor exploit others out of anxiety. A business sustained by God in integrity will bear fruit far longer than a scale propped up by worldly schemes.

Third, to our senior brothers and sisters: You have prayed for many years for your children, grandchildren, and your own health, and when some hopes are delayed, sorrow inevitably creeps into your heart. Today the Lord says to you: do not let delay steal your closeness with Him. Your life experience and prayers are treasures in your family. You can become intercessors for your children and grandchildren, blessing them with gentle words and telling them of the faithfulness of God you have experienced throughout your life. Reaching an older age does not mean the season of bearing fruit has ended; a tree planted by the rivers of water still brings forth its fruit in its season, and its leaf does not wither.

Fourth, to students and young people: As you pray for exams, further education, your first job, and your future direction, first surrender your anxiety, and do not let your grades define your worth. While preparing diligently, ask the Lord to place His will—what is truly suited for you—into your heart. Desires change, standards change, but the Father's heart never changes. If a door is temporarily shut, do not immediately conclude that God does not love you; bring the question before the Lord and ask Him what He wants you to learn and where He wants you to adjust. Learning to abide in Him in your youth will spare you many detours throughout your entire life.

Fifth, to sisters caring for the family at home full-time: The things you do each day are repetitive and trivial, with rarely any applause, and in your heart you may wonder: "Do my prayers and sacrifices bear any fruit?" Yes, they do. When you pray for your children by the dining table, keep watch for your family in the dead of night, and preserve the atmosphere of a home with gentle words—these are the fruit of the tree of life. Do not look down on the altar of the home. First let your own heart find rest in the Lord, and then go care for others; you do not prove your worth by exhausting yourself—you are a beloved daughter of God.

Sixth, to women bearing multiple pressures in the workplace. You may simultaneously face job performance, family responsibilities, and external expectations, feeling utterly exhausted inside. Today, please first lay down the burden of "I must please everyone," and return to the Father's heart to reaffirm your identity: you are chosen, loved, and appointed to bear fruit. Prayer is not completing one more task; it is pouring out your heart genuinely to your Father. When facing hurt and injustice, first release them before the Lord, not letting bitterness shape your judgment; at the same time, establish healthy boundaries according to truth and wisdom, and live courageously.

## Summary and Response Prayer

Brothers and sisters, today's message can be summarized in three sentences. First, the tree of life runs from Genesis to Revelation: God desires His children to be connected to Him and continuously bear fruit; answered prayer is fruit. Second, the root of prayer is abiding in Christ. Let His words abide in us, our desires be shaped by the Father's heart, and as we ask in the Lord's name, the Father is glorified in the Son, and our joy is made full. Third, when the answer is delayed, do not let disappointment do theology for you. Bring the issue privately before the Lord to ask and discern, learning how to pray, fast, repent, act, or rest; when you stand praying, if you remember that anyone has offended you, ask the Lord to help you forgive, so that your heart may first be set free.

After hearing the Word, we must also take it home. This week, I invite you to do three specific things. First, find a quiet time to write down the one desire you have prayed about the longest, bring it before the Lord, and pray for it once again according to the Father's will: surrender the desire to Him, and surrender yourself to Him as well. Second, take the initiative to reach out to a brother or sister, tell them that you are willing to intercede for one of their needs this week, and actually do it. Third, if the Holy Spirit reminds you of something that requires forgiveness, do not merely stop at feeling moved; take the first step this week: perhaps a prayer, perhaps a sincere conversation, or perhaps first entrusting that person into God's hands. Fruit is not produced simply by being moved when hearing a sermon; fruit is gradually borne through obedience.

Now please stand, and let us pray together. You can bring before the Lord a desire that you have prayed about for a long time and have almost felt like giving up on. Do not rush to set terms with God; first, quietly say to Him: Father, I surrender this desire to You. Please mold me and guide me according to Your will, and let me abide in You.

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You for bringing us back into Your house through Jesus Christ. Lord, we confess that so often, when our hope is deferred, our hearts grow sorrowful; when our prayers are not immediately answered, we begin to doubt Your love, and even harbor bitterness and old grievances in our hearts. Please forgive us and heal us. Today, we desire to abide afresh in Christ and let Your words abide in us; may Your heart become our heart, and may what pleases You become what we ask. Lord, show us any unforgiveness within us, grant us the strength to release others, and set us free as well. For those prayers that are still waiting, we will not give up, nor will we barge ahead in our own strength; teach us how to pray, how to act, and how to rest. We believe that You are a Father who hears prayer; the fruit You give will remain, and the joy You impart will fill us. In the victorious name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
