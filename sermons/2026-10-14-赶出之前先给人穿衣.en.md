## (00:00 - 08:00) Introduction · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Peace be with you, brothers and sisters!

Over these past few days, we have been walking through Genesis chapter 3. God asks questions, and man shifts the blame; God pronounces judgment, and the serpent hears its doom first, followed by man hearing his consequences. Yesterday we stopped at the very last phrase of verse 19: "For dust you are, and to dust you shall return."

Logically speaking, once the story gets here, the next sentence should be driving them out. The sentence has been pronounced; drive man out, shut the gate, end of story.

Yet that is precisely not what Scripture records. Sandwiched between the sentencing and the expulsion are two verses. In one verse, Adam speaks; in the other, God works with His own hands. Under the declaration of dust, man speaks a word overflowing with faith; before driving man out of the garden, God first performs an act overflowing with mercy.

These two verses are the first two verses of today's passage. Please turn with me to Genesis 3:20-24. I will read:

"And Adam called his wife’s name Eve, because she was the mother of all living." (Gen. 3:20)

"Also for Adam and his wife the Lord God made tunics of skin, and clothed them." (Gen. 3:21)

"Then the Lord God said, 'Behold, the man has become like one of Us, to know good and evil. And now, lest he put out his hand and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live forever'—therefore the Lord God sent him out of the garden of Eden to till the ground from which he was taken. So He drove out the man; and He placed cherubim at the east of the garden of Eden, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to guard the way to the tree of life." (Gen. 3:22-24)

Five verses, three matters: Adam names his wife, God makes clothes for humanity, and man is driven out of the garden.

The sequence must not be read backward. First the naming, then the clothing, and only then the expulsion. First comes the response of faith, first comes the covering of grace, and only then comes the execution of judgment. Even on the way to driving man out, God moves with such divine order.

Today we will examine this in three parts:

First, 3:20: Just after the declaration of dust hits the ground, Adam first speaks a word concerning life—naming her Eve is fallen man’s very first response of faith to the promise;
Second, 3:21: God makes tunics of skin with His own hands—man uses leaves to hide his shame, but God uses skins to cover him; between the leaves and the skins lies the price of a life;
Third, 3:22-24: Man is driven out—on the surface, this closed door is judgment, but inside it hides mercy: God would not allow man to live forever in a state of sin, and He preserved the way to the tree of life for the future.

Let us first bow our heads in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. When You pronounced judgment, You did not leave out mercy; when You drove man out, You did not forget first to clothe him. We pray that today You would help us see this order clearly: how You treated fallen man then is how You still treat us today. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

## (08:00 - 24:00) Part 1: After the Dust, Life Is Spoken First—Adam Names Eve (3:20)

### 1.1 Where This Statement Appears Matters More Than the Statement Itself

First, look at its position.

Verse 3:20 immediately follows 3:19. What does verse 19 say? "In the sweat of your face you shall eat bread till you return to the ground... For dust you are, and to dust you shall return." The sentence of death has just been uttered, without the slightest buffer.

In verse 20, Adam opens his mouth. He does not plead for mercy, he does not shift blame again, nor does he bargain with God. He does one thing: he names his wife.

Brothers and sisters, naming in Genesis is never a trivial matter. God called the light Day, and the dry land He called Earth; Adam named the livestock, exercising his authority of dominion. Now, under judgment, the name Adam gives his wife amounts to the very first tone-setting statement he utters in this new circumstance.

What name does he give her? Eve.

He could easily have named her according to the immediate circumstance before his eyes. Calling her "Bitterness," "Tears," or "Paradise Lost" would all have suited the atmosphere of that moment. Verse 3:16 had just spoken of the pain of conception and the pain of childbearing; going by feelings, any of those names would have rolled off the tongue more naturally than Eve.

Yet he said: Eve, because she was the mother of all living.

### 1.2 The Name Eve Looks Directly to 3:15

The Hebrew for "Eve" is *Chavvah* (חַוָּה), which exegetical tradition largely links to "life": Hebrew words for "living" and "life" sound similar to it. The explanatory phrase beside it in the Chinese Union Version puts it plainly—because she was the mother of all living. "All living" means every living person. Before a single child was ever born, Adam gave her the name "the mother of all living."

On what basis could he do this? On the basis of 3:15.

God had just declared that the Seed of the woman would bruise the serpent's head. Where would this Seed come from? From this woman, born generation after generation. God did not give this promise to anyone else; the cord of redemption was tied to this very wife who had just fallen alongside him. Adam understood. Therefore, his way of responding was not writing a confession; it was giving his wife a new name: "You are not the end of the story; you are the mother of all living. God spoke the word of death, and I have believed the word of life."

Proverbs 18:21 says: "Death and life are in the power of the tongue." This statement found its very first case study back in the Garden of Eden. From the exact same mouth, in 3:12 came blame-shifting words, calling his wife "the woman whom You gave to be with me"; in 3:20 came words of faith, calling that same wife "the mother of all living." Whether a person can turn back after falling often depends on this single step: is your mouth tuned first by judgment, or is it tuned first by the promise?

In his commentary on Genesis, Warren W. Wiersbe reminds readers to notice the element of faith in Adam's naming, to the effect that: Adam, having heard the sentence of death, still believed God's promise concerning the Seed, and therefore the name he gave his wife pointed toward life, not toward death. (Source: general teaching concept from Warren W. Wiersbe’s *Bible Exposition Commentary* on Genesis 3; paraphrased, not a direct quote.)

### 1.3 Eve Herself Later Responded in Kind

Follow this thread one chapter further. In Genesis 4:1, Eve bore Cain and said, "I have acquired a man from the Lord." Later, when she bore Seth, she said, "God has appointed another seed for me instead of Abel" (Gen. 4:25).

You see, this woman did not remain stuck in the sorrow of 3:16. With each child she bore, she aligned herself anew with the promise. She may not have understood everything fully, and the Seed she was waiting for was certainly not Cain—Cain later murdered Abel, but that is a later story. Yet her direction was right: the sorrow was real, but the promise was real too, and she chose to live her life keeping her eyes fixed on the promise.

To summarize this first part in one sentence: **Judgment is spoken by God, but whether to believe it requires man to open his own mouth. Under the declaration of dust, Adam spoke life first—this is the very first step for fallen man to stand back up.**

## (24:00 - 42:00) Part 2: What Leaves Cannot Cover, God Covers with Skins—God Clothed Them with His Own Hands (3:21)

### 2.1 First Look Back at the Garment Man Made for Himself

Before discussing the tunics of skin, we must first recall the first garment man made for himself.

In Genesis 3:7, the eyes of both of them were opened, and they knew that they were naked, "and they sewed fig leaves together and made themselves coverings."

Notice two words. "Sewed from leaves"—the original meaning leans toward an apron or loincloth, covering only a section, unable to cover the whole body. More crucial is the material: leaves. Once leaves are plucked, they begin to wither; no matter how carefully you sew them, within a few days they grow brittle and crumble. Man's first instinct after sinning is to use whatever material at hand is cheapest and quickest to cover up his shame for the moment.

For thousands of years, humanity has upgraded this many times, but the material has never changed. Becoming busier at work—leaves; maintaining a dignified public face—leaves; curating an impressive life on social media—still leaves. They can hide things from the eyes of others, but they cannot hide them from your own heart, much less from the eyes of God. The moment a person quiets down, they know: the person beneath those leaves has not changed.

In Psalm 32, David lays this matter bare: when he kept silent, his bones grew old; he who confesses and forsakes his sins will have mercy (cf. Ps. 32:3-5; Prov. 28:13). The biggest problem with leaves is not that they are flimsy, but that they deceive a person into thinking that once they are covered, there is no need to take another step forward.

### 2.2 The Garment God Made: Three Distinct Differences

By 3:21, the garment maker changes.

"Also for Adam and his wife the Lord God made tunics of skin, and clothed them."

First, the Maker is different. It is made by God’s own hands. The sinners did not ask for it; God stepped in and did it Himself. Throughout the entire Bible, there are very few things God crafted with His own hands: He formed man of the dust of the ground, He fashioned the woman from the rib, and now He makes clothes for fallen mankind. The relationship that man wrecked, God repeatedly restores with His own hands.

Second, the material is different. Not leaves, but skins.

The Hebrew word for "skin" is *or* (עוֹר), meaning skin or hide. Brothers and sisters, read this sentence slowly: Where did the skins come from? The Bible does not explicitly describe the process, and we must not add drama on Scripture's behalf. But one thing is glaringly obvious: when leaves are plucked, the tree stays alive; when skins are made into garments, a life must first have ceased to exist. The cost of covering shame fell upon another life for the very first time. As Adam and Eve wore this garment, it served as a daily reminder: your covering was not casually picked from the garden; it came at a cost.

Later scriptures explain this step by step. In Exodus, priests had to put on holy garments to enter the sanctuary, and the tabernacle tent required coverings of skins; only through the shed blood of sacrificial beasts could human sin be covered. Coming to the New Testament, John the Baptist pointed to Jesus and said: "Behold! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29). The tunics of skin were the shadow; the Lamb is the reality. The very first garment God made in the Garden of Eden was already a dress rehearsal for the way of the cross: covering comes at a cost, and that cost is not paid by the sinner.

As an aside, there is a minor detail in the original language that commentators often bring up: in Hebrew, "skin" (*or*, עוֹר) and "light" (*or*, אוֹר) are pronounced the same, though spelled differently. In the beginning, God said, "Let there be light," and man was created in God's image, bearing glorious light; after the fall, the "light" of glory was gone, and the "skin" to cover the body was put on. The pronunciation is the same, but the condition has completely changed. The Bible does not explicitly say this is a pun, so we can take it as a thought-provoking reminder: that glory which man lost will one day, piece by piece, be put back on in Christ.

Third, the style is different. What God made here is in the original text called *kethoneth* (כְּתֹנֶת), a long tunic reaching from the shoulders down to the knees. Later on, this very word is used for the tunic of many colors that Joseph wore and the tunics worn by the priests. What man stitched for himself were aprons; what God made for man were tunics. Man covers shame only to the point where he can get by; God covers to the point where man's dignity is restored. An apron is a makeshift measure; a tunic is true dignity. God did not send them out of the garden wrapped in leaves; He had them dress like human beings, walking out with dignity.

### 2.3 From Tunics of Skin to Putting on Christ

Isaiah 61:10 connects this thread with crystal clarity; the prophet says that God "has clothed me with the garments of salvation, He has covered me with the robe of righteousness." In Eden, it was skins; under Isaiah's pen, it is salvation and righteousness. The material continually upgrades, yet the method never changes: the clothes are all prepared by God, and man's part is to put them on.

Paul makes the fullest declaration in Galatians 3:27: "For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ."

Put on Christ. It is not about sewing your own leaves more tightly, nor about hiding your past a little better. It is your entire being enveloped by Christ, so that when God looks at you, He first sees this garment of His Son. The prodigal son's father did the exact same thing: when the son returned home in rags, the very first command the father gave was not to take a bath first, nor to reflect on his wrongdoing, but: "Bring out the best robe and put it on him" (Luke 15:22). Only when the robe was on was he welcomed back into the household to sit at the banquet.

To conclude this second part: **Leaves are man's excuse to himself; skins are God's covering for man. That matter which you have tried to cover for so many years and failed, God already told you in Genesis 3: lay down the leaves, and let Him clothe you.**

## (42:00 - 52:00) Part Three: The Closed Door Is Also a Preserved Way—Driven Out of Eden (3:22-24)

### 3.1 "Has Become Like One of Us" and the Unfinished Sentence

In 3:22, God said something that sounds very peculiar:

"Behold, the man has become like one of Us, to know good and evil. And now, lest he put out his hand and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live forever—"

Let us first explain "to know good and evil." This was the very thing the serpent had promised: in the day you eat of it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil (cf. Gen. 3:5). Now God Himself acknowledges that man indeed knows good and evil. Yet this "knowing" is an entirely different matter from God's knowing. God knows good and evil standing above good and evil, doing only good without a trace of evil; man knows good and evil by first plunging into evil, buying that knowledge at the cost of shame and torment. The same word: one is the authority of the King, the other is the scar of a wound.

The renderings in English translations here are worth looking at:
- KJV: "Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil."
- NIV: "The man has now become like one of us, knowing good and evil."
- NRSV is close to the Chinese Union Version; both directly state that man has become like God, knowing good and evil, and then turn to the concern: "lest he reach out his hand and take also from the tree of life."

The Lu Zhenzhong Version follows the same sentence order as the Chinese Union Version in this passage, first stating man's new situation and then the reason God prevents man from eating of the tree of life again; citations in this manuscript follow the Chinese Union Version.

### 3.2 Being Driven Out: A Severe Act Wrapped in Mercy

Next comes the weightiest sentence in the entire chapter: lest he eat of the tree of life and live forever.

Brothers and sisters, this sentence must be heard in reverse to be understood. Living forever was originally a good thing; but living forever in a state of sin would be a sheer catastrophe. It would mean that man would remain trapped in shame, in sorrow, in the twisted torment of ruling and desiring, in the toil of the sweat of the brow—never able to get out, without even the exit of death. If sin were locked in by eternal life, there would never again be any possibility of redemption for mankind.

Therefore, God barred the way to this tree. He would rather let man experience death, so that the Seed of 3:15 could blaze a trail out of death, than let man turn the fallen state into eternity.

Then come the actions. Verse 23 uses "sent... out," and verse 24 uses "drove out." The Hebrew word for "drove out" is *garash* (גָּרַשׁ), meaning to expel or drive out, carrying more weight than "sent out." God's words become increasingly solemn: first He sent him out to till the ground from which he was taken, and then He formally drove him out, stationing a guard. A person cannot eat the forbidden fruit on one hand and continue living in the original place pretending nothing happened on the other. The judgment is real, and the tears are real—let us not downplay this. The Bible does not describe that backward glance Adam cast over the garden, but we all understand it.

Yet please notice a detail: before he was driven out, he was wearing the garment God had made for him (3:21 precedes 3:24). When the judgment was executed, grace had already been put on his body. What God drove out was the sinner's sin; God did not sweep this human being out like trash. Man walked out wearing a covering, bearing a promise, and having received the name "the mother of all living."

### 3.3 What the Cherubim Guarded Was a Way Destined to Be Reopened

In the final verse, He placed cherubim and a flaming sword which turned every way, "to guard the way to the tree of life."

The Hebrew word for "guard" is *shamar* (שָׁמַר). Does this word sound familiar? In Genesis 2:15, God put Adam in the Garden of Eden to "tend and keep" it. It is the very same word. Adam failed to keep the garden and failed to keep the commandment; now God commissions cherubim to guard what he failed to keep. The object guarded is precisely stated: not guarding the main gates of Eden, but guarding "the way to the tree of life." The gate may change, but there is only one way, and this way God Himself sealed, waiting for His own appointed time to reopen it.

Cherubim will reappear later in the Bible. In Exodus, on the two ends of the mercy seat upon the ark of the covenant, there were cherubim of gold, their wings overshadowing the mercy seat, where God met with man (cf. Ex. 25:18-22). The veil of the temple was embroidered with cherubim. It turns out that the cherubim guarding the way to the tree of life later stood upon the mercy seat: the one who barred mankind became the one who overshadowed the ark and testified to the forgiveness of sins. Barring and bestowing grace are the two hands of the very same God.

The New Testament speaks the final word on this. Hebrews 10 says that we have boldness to enter the Holiest by the blood of Jesus, by "a new and living way" which He consecrated for us, through the veil (cf. Heb. 10:19-20). The veil was embroidered with cherubim, and it was this very veil that was torn. When the Lord Jesus breathed His last, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom (cf. Matt. 27:51). The way that had been sealed for thousands of years was torn open from above—not broken into by man, but opened from on high by God.

In the last chapter of Revelation, the tree of life is back, on both sides of the river, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations; "Blessed are those who wash their robes, that they may have the right to the tree of life, and may enter through the gates into the city" (Rev. 22:14). Notice, it is still clothing. In Genesis 3:21 God clothed man with tunics of skin; in Revelation 22:14 man washes his robes and enters the city. Those who were driven out of the Garden of Eden ultimately walk back through the gates wearing cleansed robes. What God closed, God Himself opened; those whom God drove out, God Himself welcomed back.

To wrap up the third part: **Being driven out of the Garden of Eden is not the end of the story; it is God setting man on a longer road home. During those years when the door was shut, not for a single day did He stop preparing to open it.**

## (52:00 - 60:00) Applications for Six Groups of People · The Call · Closing Prayer

### Application 1: For Seekers Who Have Not Yet Believed in the Lord

Friend, today’s passage offers you a very practical reminder: do not seek longevity in a state of sin; seek first a change in the nature of your life.

Everyone wants to live long. But Genesis 3:22 tells us that if the problem within is not resolved, living longer only stretches out the suffering that much further. The purpose of Jesus’ coming was not to prolong the old life, but to give man a new life. Today, if you are willing, do not talk first about how long you will live; talk first about this garment: Will you wear the covering God has prepared?

### Application 2: For Spouses and Parents in Marriage and Family

Genesis 3:20 leaves a lesson for married couples: whatever "name" you give your family members sets the tone for your household.

In Adam's hardest moment, he did not call his wife a failure or a troublemaker; he called her the mother of all living. When you go back home, how do you address your spouse and your children? Do you name them according to their most recent mistake, or do you name them according to God's promise upon them? The names on parents' lips will grow into the identity in children's hearts. Anyone can speak words of judgment, but words of faith must be practiced at home first.

### Application 3: For Those in the Workplace and in Labor

The first thing Adam did after being driven out of the garden was to till the ground from which he was taken. The garden was gone, but the work remained; ease was gone, but the duty remained.

Brothers and sisters, do not think your life is ruined just because you have left the "garden"—left favorable circumstances or left a familiar position. What God sent Adam out to was the very ground he was most familiar with. The work in your hands is the first field God gives you after being driven out. Those who go out to till the ground wearing His covering may still sweat just the same, but in their hearts they have assurance.

### Application 4: For New Believers

New believers need to distinguish between two things: leaves and skins.

After coming to faith, it is very easy for a person to keep living the spiritual life using leaves: perform a little better, speak a little more spiritually, hide it first when a problem arises. God does not want your leaves. He wants you to live every day in the garment you have already put on—putting on Christ was done once for all on the day of your baptism, and it is also reclaimed every morning in prayer. When you stumble, do not rush to weave leaves; come back first to that garment, confess to Him, and His covering is still there.

### Application 5: For Leaders, Parents, and Those Who Serve

Those who lead people must learn the way God sent man out: clear boundaries, with covering coming first.

God did not cancel the expulsion because of mercy, nor did He take away the clothing because of the expulsion. Today, when the church deals with issues or when parents discipline children, it is easiest to swing between two extremes: only boundaries without covering, driving someone out and making them leave naked; or only covering without boundaries, mixing leaves and skins together and dealing with nothing at all. God’s order is: first provide the garment, then enforce the boundary; and after enforcing the boundary, the way is still kept open for him. People raised this way will know both the gravity of sin and that they can still return home.

### Application 6: For the Whole Church

The church consists of those standing on this side of the torn veil. Every week when we gather, break bread, and pray, we are walking on that reopened way.

Yet do not forget that outside the city, there are still many people standing before closed gates, knowing only that they were driven out, not knowing that the way has been opened. The church exists to bring the news of Revelation 22 into their midst ahead of time: the way to the tree of life has not been abolished; it is reopened in Christ. Furthermore, those who enter the city through the gates have their clothes washed clean; not a single one needs to enter wrapped in leaves.

### The Spark Moment

(Pause, slowing down tone)

Brothers and sisters, I want you to remember this order today—just three steps:

**First naming, then clothing, then sending out.**

On the day you were at your lowest and most humiliated, God first listened to you speak words of faith, first clothed you with covering with His own hands, and only then let you face the consequences. This order has not changed for thousands of years. In dealing with you today, this is still His order.

(Pause, with increased emphasis)

Therefore, it is today, not tomorrow. Put down the leaves in your hands.

How long have you been covering yourself? Aren't you tired of covering? No matter how tightly leaves are woven, the wind blows them apart; but the clothing made by God gives you dignity the moment you put it on. Stop sewing for yourself. Let Him clothe you, let His Son become your covering, and let that way, which was shut for thousands of years, be opened again starting with you today.

### The Call

I want to give two calls:

**The first call**: You have been using leaves to cover up a certain matter—covering it from your family, from the church, and from yourself. Today, if you are willing to lay down the leaves and let God personally cover you, please stand up, and we will pray for you together.

**The second call**: For friends who have not yet believed in the Lord. You have been standing outside the garden long enough. The gate was not shut to keep you out forever; it has been reopened for you in Christ. Today, if you are willing to receive Jesus Christ and put on the robe of righteousness He has prepared, please raise your hand, and I want to pray for you.

(Wait for response; pray for those who stand up and raise their hands)

### Closing Response Prayer

Let us bow our heads and pray together:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. Before man was driven out of the garden, You first heard Adam's naming of faith, and with Your own hands You made clothing for man. Your judgment is real, but Your mercy runs one step ahead of judgment.

Lord, we confess that after living all these years, we have gathered handful after handful of leaves. We cover our shame, cover our failures, cover those things we dare not bring to light, covering until we ourselves are exhausted. Today we lay our leaves at Your feet. We ask that according to what You did in the Garden of Eden, You would clothe us with Your own hands in the covering You have prepared, which is the righteousness of Your Son, Jesus Christ.

We also thank You that the way You once guarded has been reopened on the cross. The veil is torn, the tree of life remains, and the city gates are open. We pray that You would lead those who stood today to walk this road, and lead the friends who raised their hands today to enter through this gate.

From now on, we will no longer name our family members according to their mistakes; we will speak according to promise. We will no longer go out wrapped in leaves; we will put on Christ and till the ground You have entrusted to us.

In the name of the Lord who made a way for us, Jesus Christ, we pray. Amen."
