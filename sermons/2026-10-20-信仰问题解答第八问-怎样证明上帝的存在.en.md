# Faith Q&A Question 8: How Can the Existence of God Be Proved?

## Scripture

> "For since the creation of the world His invisible attributes are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even His eternal power and Godhead, so that they are without excuse." (Rom. 1:20, NKJV)

> "The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament shows His handiwork." (Ps. 19:1, NKJV)

> "No one has seen God at any time. The only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, He has declared Him." (John 1:18, NKJV)

---

## I. Pastor Tong First Turns the Question Around: God Does Not Need to Be Proved

Someone asks: how do you prove that God exists?

Pastor Tong's first sentence is unexpected: in his apologetics and theology, **God does not need to be proved.**

The reasoning is not convoluted: if God were small enough to be contained by the evidence you produce and to be seen in full by your eyes, then He would be smaller than your field of vision. And could something smaller than your field of vision still be God?

So the order must not be reversed. It is not that man first assembles a complete set of evidence and only then does God count as existing; it is that God is so great that the frame of "evidence" simply cannot contain Him. **What needs to be proved is usually smaller than the one proving; God happens to be greater than all proof.**

## II. Invisible — Precisely Because He Is God

Between the Creator and the created there is an essential boundary: God created matter, so God cannot Himself be matter.

Material things can be seen, touched, and measured. God is not matter, so He cannot be seen — **a God who cannot be seen is the God who is God.** If a "god" could be set out on a table for people to look at and test, he would at most be a somewhat larger created thing.

Scripture itself speaks this way: God is "invisible" (Rom. 1:20), yet also "clearly seen" — not seeing Him in Himself does not mean knowing nothing about Him.

Moreover, invisible things are not necessarily unreal. Uprightness, righteousness, democracy, independence — which of these can be seen with the eyes? Visible things, in fact, are often the most superficial. A person who will only believe what his eyes can see has a remarkably narrow life.

When God came into the world as a man, what people saw was the body of Jesus, the form of a man; but His words and deeds and the miracles He performed truly manifested the image of God. The invisible God, through the incarnation of the Word, let men "see."

**Original-language analysis:** John 1:18 says the only begotten Son "has declared" God; the Greek is ***Exēgēsato* (ἐξηγήσατο)**, meaning to explain, to interpret, to bring out so that people understand — the theological word "exegesis" comes from it. God is not argued into existence by us; it is God who, through Christ, explains Himself to us.

Romans 1:20 also uses two words for what God manifests of Himself through created things: first, His **eternal power**, in Greek ***Aïdios Dynamis* (ἀΐδιος δύναμις)** — *aïdios* being the power that is "eternal, without beginning or end"; and second, His **Godhead**, ***Theiotēs* (θειότης)**. Man cannot see God's being in itself, yet from created things he can clearly recognize these two things, and Paul says this is what it means to be "without excuse."

## III. The Crucial Distinction: Proof Is Man Taking the Initiative; Manifestation Is God Taking the Initiative

This is the bone of the whole piece, and Pastor Tong draws it very clearly:

**Proof** is man taking the initiative — man stands on high, treats his own reason as a court, and summons God to stand trial.

**Manifestation** is God taking the initiative — God stands in His own place and sets His glory, wisdom, and power in the created world for man to see.

Faith takes the second road. God actively reveals Himself, so His eternal power and Godhead are seen with perfect clarity in the created world (Rom. 1:20). Man's responsibility is not to "produce" a god, but, in the face of the manifestation already set before his eyes, to be willing or unwilling to open his eyes and to submit.

**Original-language analysis:** Psalm 19:1 says "The heavens declare the glory of God"; "declare" in Hebrew is ***Mesapperim* (מְסַפְּרִים)**, from ***Saphar* (סָפַר)**, which carries the sense of counting, recounting in detail, telling item by item. The heavens do not vaguely "hint" at God's existence; like someone calling out numbers, they count God's glory out to man item by item. When man does not hear, it is usually not that the heavens have not spoken; it is that man has not been listening.

## IV. Where Is the Manifestation? Start with Your Own Nose

Pastor Tong's example is very down-to-earth: **our whole body is a sign of God's manifesting His existence.** He singles out the nose and speaks of four of its functions:

First, **it breathes without ceasing**. Decade after decade, no holidays, no downtime for maintenance; when you are asleep it is still at work.

Second, **a natural air-conditioner**. In a hot, dry wilderness, the air inhaled is dry and hot; by rights the lungs should not be able to stand it, yet after passing through the regulation of the nose, it goes in cool; in bitterly cold places, the air is cold enough to sting, yet after being warmed by the nose, it arrives in the lungs warm.

Third, **it regulates temperature and humidity**. The cold it warms, the hot it cools, the dry it moistens — all the calibration is inside, and man himself does not have to worry about any of it.

Fourth, **the sense of smell**. Whether the person sitting next to you today has bathed, your nose knows with one sniff — this power of discrimination is used every day, and nobody finds it remarkable.

Pastor Tong's conclusion: **there is nothing man-made that can replace this one small nose that God created.** For the parts of a man-made machine, everyone admits there is a designer; yet when it comes to the body, which is far more intricate than any machine, people turn around and say it came about by chance — that turn simply cannot be made.

And then there is the distance in the heavens: the distance between the earth and the sun is set with such precision — a little nearer and we burn to death; a little farther and we freeze to death. Pastor Tong asks: who arranged this?

So in the end he states it plainly: **if you say God does not exist, the faith you need is greater than the faith I need to believe that God exists.** Unbelief is also a kind of "belief" — believing that all this precision happened by itself; that faith, honestly speaking, is not small.

## V. The Things of the Spirit, Science Also Cannot Explain

Go one layer deeper: the things of the spirit are even more manifest.

A great spirit writes something, and it can influence tens of thousands of people and remain effective across hundreds of years. Pastor Tong asks: how does science explain this? A laboratory can measure weight, but it cannot weigh significance; it can dissect the brain, but it cannot dissect the heart that moves people.

So faith does not stop at argument. Pastor Tong's closing words are very pastoral: **at the very moment today when you are willing to obey, God will speak some very wonderful words to you.** The last leg of manifestation is not in the laboratory; it is in obedience — you are willing to open your heart, and His word comes in. This sentence connects exactly with what Question 1 said: "first resolve to obey, and only then will you know."

---

## For Us Today

1. God does not need man to prove Him: if He were small enough to be contained by evidence, He would not be God
2. Take the road of manifestation, not the road of proof — God has already actively set His eternal power and Godhead in created things; man's responsibility is to open his eyes, not to manufacture a god
3. Begin by looking at your own body: the four functions of one nose are enough to keep a person thinking for a long time
4. Argument, at its final step, must land on obedience — at the moment you are willing to obey, God speaks to you personally
