# Nothing New Under the Sun: Single Parents

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：单身父母».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of single parents

**Summary:** Raising a child alone — what is tired is not only the body, but also that sentence in the heart, "I can only rely on myself." This chapter points out that the source of a single parent's security is the relationship with Jesus: God is the perfect provider of the family, and also the faithful Father of the children. Facing a rebellious child, the office of discipline cannot be laid down; facing anger, depression, fear, and loneliness, renew the mind with God's truth. Single mothers and single fathers — God personally remembers them both.

The key Scripture is Isaiah 41:10:

> "Fear not, for I am with you; be not dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you, yes, I will help you, I will uphold you with My righteous right hand." (Isa. 41:10)

These words were spoken to people who are alone and without help. Three "I wills" — I will strengthen, I will help, I will uphold — are not vague comfort; they are God's personal promise. Everything spoken of in this chapter comes from here.

## I. The Source of Security: God Is the Provider, and Also the Father of the Children

The material records a wrong understanding common among single parents: "Without a spouse to help me physically and emotionally, I cannot feel secure; I must do everything well for myself and my children; I can only trust myself."

This understanding sounds strong, but in fact it presses the weight of the whole family onto one person's shoulders. The right understanding is: As a single parent who believes in Christ, the relationship with Jesus is the source of security. God is the perfect provider of this family, and also the faithful Father of the children. Provision is not produced by one person holding on by sheer effort; it is received from God. Jeremiah says: "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the LORD, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope." (Jer. 29:11) Second Corinthians also says: "Not that we are sufficient of ourselves to think of anything as being from ourselves, but our sufficiency is from God." (2 Cor. 3:5) Admitting "not of ourselves" is, on the contrary, the beginning of strength.

Isaiah chapter 30 also gives a picture of waiting: "Therefore the LORD will wait, that He may be gracious to you; and therefore He will be exalted, that He may have mercy on you. For the LORD is a God of justice; blessed are all those who wait for Him." (Isa. 30:18) It also says: "Your ears shall hear a word behind you, saying, 'This is the way, walk in it,' whenever you turn to the right hand or whenever you turn to the left." (Isa. 30:21) When you cannot go on, you do not need to find a way out yourself; first wait, first listen to that voice behind you pointing out the right way.

## II. Do Not Give Up Discipline: Be Responsible for Your Office, Not for Your Child's Choices

The material records a real question. A mother said: My son is in his teens, very rebellious, always causing trouble. He does not listen to discipline, and thinks I nag. His father left two years ago; should I give up disciplining him?

The answer is decisive: No. Even if the child always makes wrong choices, as a parent you have the responsibility to tell him what is right. You do not need to be responsible for your child's wrong decisions, but you must be responsible for your own office as a parent. If you do not teach your child what is right, who else would be willing to do so?

This distinction is extremely important for single parents. The child's choices are the child's; your office is yours. If you have fulfilled your office and the child still goes his own way, that is not your failure; if you give up your office, that is the true loss of ground. Proverbs says: "The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to turn one away from the snares of death." (Prov. 13:14) The instruction in the parents' mouth is the fountain for the child to turn away from the snares of death — the words in the mouth of a single parent carry no less weight than in a two-parent family.

The material also reminds us to meet the child's needs, especially in the child's emotional health and sense of security: let the child know that even though one party has left, he is still loved; the parents' separation is not the child's responsibility. When this knot in the child's heart is untied, he can then be able to receive Jesus and turn to God.

## III. The Root of Pain and Loneliness: Renew the Mind with the Truth

The material speaks very thoroughly about the root cause of a single parent's anger, depression, fear, and loneliness: the root lies in wrong understanding. Before we knew Christ, and realized that only He can satisfy all our needs, our pursuit of satisfying our own needs was all built on wrong understanding. When a person renews his mind with God's truth, he can face the higher challenges God has given him.

Paul says: "And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what is that good and acceptable and perfect will of God." (Rom. 12:2) The word "renewing" in Greek is **anakainōsis (ἀνακαίνωσις)** (original text ἀνακαίνωσις, transliterated anakainōsis; Strong G342; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "renewal, renovation"). Renewal is not exchanging one emotion for another; it is exchanging one set of thoughts for another. When the thoughts change, the way one looks at the child, at the circumstances, and at oneself changes with them.

Connected with this path are several Scriptures worth reading repeatedly: "For in Him we live and move and have our being" (Acts 17:28); "As His divine power has given to us all things that pertain to life and godliness" (2 Pet. 1:3); He has also "given to us exceedingly great and precious promises, that through these you may be partakers of the divine nature, having escaped the corruption that is in the world through lust" (2 Pet. 1:4). The material especially recommends reading repeatedly Psalm 145:8-20: The LORD is gracious and full of compassion, slow to anger and great in mercy; He upholds all who fall and raises up all who are bowed down; He is near to all who call upon Him in truth, He hears their cry and saves them. In days of holding up a home alone, this psalm is worth reading aloud.

## IV. For Single Mothers, and Also for Single Fathers

At the end, the material leaves memorization Scriptures separately for single mothers and single fathers, both very personal.

For single mothers, it is Psalm 10:14: "But You have seen, for You observe trouble and grief, to requite it by Your hand. The helpless commits himself to You; You are the helper of the fatherless." (Ps. 10:14) Being helpless is not being forgotten by God; it is precisely the kind of person God especially cares for in the Bible.

For single fathers, it is Isaiah 49:15-16: "Can a woman forget her nursing child, and not have compassion on the son of her womb? Surely they may forget, yet I will not forget you. See, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands; your walls are continually before Me." (Isa. 49:15-16) Fathers also get tired, and also feel forgotten by life. God says, I have inscribed you on the palms of My hands; when I lift My hand I can see you.

## Conclusion

The burden single parents carry is real, and God's promise is also real. Do not lay down the office of discipline, for it is the fountain of life for the child; do not build the foundation of security on yourself, for that burden you cannot carry. God is the provider, the Father of the children, and the One who inscribes you on His palms.

## Prayer of Response

Lord, holding up a home alone, I am truly tired, and truly afraid. Thank You for promising that You will strengthen me, You will help me, You will uphold me with Your righteous right hand. Please be the Father of my children and the provider of my home; give me strength to fulfill the office of a parent, and renew my mind, so that I no longer rely only on myself. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I pray, Amen.
