"For he who speaks in a tongue does not speak to men but to God, for no one understands him; however, in the spirit he speaks mysteries." (1 Cor. 14:2)

When you pray, do you have many wandering thoughts? Many people honestly say: Many. The moment I kneel down, the whole to-do list surges up; the moment I quiet down, yesterday's words come back. We want to draw near to God with concentration, but our minds will not obey.

In the Bible there is an answer: praying in tongues — "the prayer of the spirit."

## I. The Biblical Basis

Tongues were not invented by any person; they are a gift clearly recorded in the Bible. Let us first look at several key passages:

> "For he who speaks in a tongue does not speak to men but to God, for no one understands him; however, in the spirit he speaks mysteries." (1 Cor. 14:2, NKJV)

"Tongue" in Greek is **glōssa (γλῶσσα)** (original Greek γλῶσσα, transliterated glōssa; Strong G1100; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "tongue, language"), whose basic meaning is "tongue, language." Speaking in a tongue is speaking directly to God, speaking all kinds of mysteries in the spirit — even oneself does not understand, but God understands.

> "He who speaks in a tongue edifies himself, but he who prophesies edifies the church." (1 Cor. 14:4, NKJV)

The primary function of tongues is to **edify oneself** — to build up the spiritual man within us.

> "For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, but my understanding is unfruitful." (1 Cor. 14:14, NKJV)

This is the source of the name "the prayer of the spirit": **my spirit prays, but my understanding is unfruitful**. When the understanding does not stir up trouble, wandering thoughts cannot come in, and one's attention naturally concentrates on God.

Paul also set the example personally:

> "I thank my God I speak with tongues more than you all." (1 Cor. 14:18, NKJV)

The apostle who spoke in tongues the most was precisely Paul, the one most concerned with order and most grounded in the truth. This in itself is the best balance.

The book of Jude says:

> "But you, beloved, building yourselves up on your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Spirit, keep yourselves in the love of God." (Jude 20-21, NKJV)

"Praying in the Holy Spirit" — praying in tongues is exactly this kind of prayer.

The precedents in the Bible are also clear: On the day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit descended, and the one hundred and twenty disciples "were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance" (Acts 2:4); when the household of Cornelius believed in the Lord, the Holy Spirit fell upon all those who heard the word, "for they heard them speak with tongues and magnify God" (Acts 10:46); the twelve disciples at Ephesus received the Holy Spirit and "spoke with tongues and prophesied" (Acts 19:6). **When the Holy Spirit descends, tongues often accompany Him** — this is a pattern Luke records three times; it is not a human theory, it is the Bible's record.

## II. The Fourfold Function of Praying in Tongues

In the course, Brother Zhang Xun summarizes four functions of praying in tongues:

**First, it is "the prayer of the spirit."** The understanding is unfruitful and is not disturbed by wandering thoughts, helping our attention to concentrate on God. That opening question — what to do about wandering thoughts in prayer? Praying in tongues is one of God's answers: let the spirit move first, and the soul follows.

**Second, it edifies oneself.** It makes the inner spiritual man strong. Just as the body needs exercise, the spiritual man also needs exercise; praying in tongues is the spiritual man's "gymnasium."

**Third, it releases the anointing.** Powerful praying in tongues releases the anointing, so that the spiritual man receives anointing. The more one practices, the stronger one becomes, and the more sensitive all the spiritual senses become. This is why Lecture Four's "spiritual perception" and this lecture are connected — praying in tongues is the fuel of sensitive perception.

**Fourth, it opens up prophetic experiences.** As the quality and quantity of the practice of praying in tongues increase, the frequency, breadth, and depth of prophetic experiences generally increase as well. Paul is the best example: he spoke in tongues more than all, and his visionary experiences were also the most — Damascus, Macedonia, Corinth, the third heaven. This is no coincidence.

These four functions form a chain: the prayer of the spirit → the spiritual man made strong → the anointing released → perception made sensitive → prophetic experiences opened up. The starting point is simply opening one's mouth to pray in tongues.

## III. Actual Use and Balance

**Actual use** — the course gives very concrete suggestions:

Begin by praying in tongues **softly**. Why softly? Because the emphasis is not on the volume of the voice, but on the heart turning toward God. "My understanding is unfruitful" — do not think about miscellaneous matters; simply turn toward Him. You may accompany it with worship music, or meditate on a verse of Scripture.

**Alternate between the understanding and tongues in prayer**: first use tongues to enter God's presence, then use the understanding to pray, to inquire, and to take notes. Paul said: "I will pray with the spirit, and I will also pray with the understanding. I will sing with the spirit, and I will also sing with the understanding" (1 Cor. 14:15, NKJV). Spirit and understanding are not an either-or choice; they alternate and work together. A practical rhythm is: tongues for five minutes → quiet waiting for one minute → pray out with the understanding the thoughts received → tongues again for five minutes. Alternating back and forth, as natural as breathing.

The key reminder: **Thirst simply for God Himself, not for visions or experiences.** Tongues are the boat, not the shore; a means, not the end. If one day you find yourself loving tongues more than loving God, stop, and come back before the cross to refocus.

**Balance** (these points are guardrails and must be held):

First, tongues are one of the gifts; they are **not a measure of spiritual life**. Jesus is the standard. Do not use them to judge brothers and sisters who do not have this experience.

Second, when speaking in tongues in a public meeting, there must be interpretation; "Let all things be done decently and in order" (1 Cor. 14:40). In personal devotion one may be free; in a public setting one must follow order.

Third, neither exalt nor despise. Paul's example is: use them much in private, with restraint in public.

## IV. Life Application: Six Kinds of Readers

**Brothers and sisters who have never spoken in tongues**: Do not feel inferior, and do not be anxious. This is a gift of the Holy Spirit; He distributes to each one as He wills. You may pray and ask, but do not treat it as the assurance of salvation.

**Those who have the gift of tongues but seldom use it**: Has your "gymnasium" membership card expired? Begin with ten minutes a day, and let the spiritual man within grow strong again.

**Those who misunderstand tongues and resist them in their hearts**: Go back to the Bible. Paul spoke in tongues more than all — you may not agree with a certain way of practicing, but do not deny a gift clearly recorded in the Bible.

**Those with many wandering thoughts in prayer who cannot quiet down**: Try this: first pray softly in tongues for a few minutes, let the spirit quiet down first, then pray with the understanding. Many people discover that the wandering thoughts really do decrease.

**Those thirsting for more sensitive spiritual perception**: Praying in tongues is the fuel. Sensitive perception is not obtained by begging; it is obtained by practice — and the starting point of practice is often praying in tongues.

**Those who lead meetings and lead worship**: In public settings, be sure to follow order: if there are tongues, there must be interpretation; do not let the meeting fall into disorder. Your restraint is protection for the flock.

## Conclusion

Praying in tongues is God's gift to us: it lets our spirit speak directly to Him, undisturbed by wandering thoughts; it lets our spiritual man be edified day by day; it lets the anointing be released and perception be sensitive. Neither exalting it nor despising it, according to the Bible and in order, simply in order to draw nearer to Him.

## Response Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, thank You for giving the Holy Spirit, and for giving the gift of tongues as well. Lord, we confess that sometimes we misunderstand it, sometimes we neglect it, and sometimes we exalt it above Yourself. Forgive us, we pray.

Lord, we are willing to practice according to Your word: praying in the Holy Spirit, keeping ourselves in Your love. Make our spirits strong, our hearts quiet, and our perception sensitive, so that we may know You more deeply.

We thirst simply for You Yourself, not for experiences themselves. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen.
