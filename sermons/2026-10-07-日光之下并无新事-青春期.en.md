# Nothing New Under the Sun: Adolescence

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：青春期».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of adolescence

## Summary

The problems of adolescence, on the surface, are the child's behavior; underneath, reliance has been placed on the wrong object. Young people regard "obtaining others' acceptance" as more important than doing the right thing, and also want to live according to their own truth and their own way, and problems come one after another. This chapter first gives young people one sentence and parents one sentence, then uses two young kings as a contrast: Absalom let hatred accumulate in his heart for two years and walked onto the road of revenge and rebellion; Josiah at sixteen put God in the first place in his life and resolutely resisted the trend of idolatry. The same age, two outcomes; the difference lies in whom one relies on.

## I. Words for Young People: Flee, and Pursue

Paul said to young Timothy:

> "Flee also youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart." (2 Tim. 2:22)

Here "lusts" in Greek is **epithymia (ἐπιθυμία)** (original text ἐπιθυμία, transliterated epithymia; Strong G1939; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "desire, longing"). The action the Scripture gives is twofold, one negative, one positive: flee youthful lusts, pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace. And pursuit is not running alone; it is running together "with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart." The victory or defeat of adolescence is half in what people one flees from, and the other half in what people one draws near to. Reading Proverbs repeatedly is the fixed work the source material gives to children.

## II. Words for Parents: Teach Diligently, Regardless of Occasion

The Scripture on the parents' side is in Deuteronomy:

> "And these words which I command you today shall be in your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up." (Deut. 6:6-7)

The order is first to be in the heart, then to teach the children. Words that are not in the parents' hearts cannot be spoken by their mouths; occasional preaching cannot last long either. The occasions for teaching listed in the Scripture are four: when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up. That is to say, faith education is not a program once a week; it is talk in daily life.

Another part of the parents' work is to transform Scripture into prayer. The source material designates parents to read repeatedly Ephesians 3:16-19, and turn this passage into prayer for their children:

> "That He would grant you, according to the riches of His glory, to be strengthened with might through His Spirit in the inner man, that Christ may dwell in your hearts through faith; that you, being rooted and grounded in love, may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the width and length and depth and height — to know the love of Christ which passes knowledge; that you may be filled with all the fullness of God." (Eph. 3:16-19)

Strength of the inner man, Christ dwelling in the heart, love rooted and grounded — these three are precisely what young people lack most and can least fake. Parents may not be able to out-talk an adolescent child, but they can out-pray.

## III. Negative Example: Absalom

Second Samuel chapter 13 records a family tragedy. Absalom's half-brother defiled his sister, causing Absalom's heart to be filled with fury. In the following two years, Absalom let hatred accumulate in his heart, and as a result he step by step walked onto the road of revenge and rebellion.

In this matter there are two failures. One is in Absalom himself: Hatred had no outlet, so it fermented in the heart; in two years' time, it simmered an elder brother in a victim's family into a murderer and a rebel. The other is in his father David: Because King David took no measures to punish the evil deed, Absalom avenged himself and killed his half-brother.

The reminder for parents is hidden here: When a child is wronged and evil appears in the home, for parents to pretend not to see is not called tolerance; it is called dereliction of duty. When justice is late in arriving, the child will seek it himself, and the child's way of seeking justice is often greater unrighteousness. The problems of adolescence sometimes do not begin with the child; they begin with the parents' silence.

## IV. Positive Example: Josiah

Second Chronicles chapter 34 is another young man. Because his father was assassinated, Josiah at eight years old became the sixteenth king of Judah. Josiah, unlike his ungodly father and grandfather, "did what was right in the sight of the LORD" (2 Chron. 34:2). At sixteen, he began to put God in the first place in his life and sincerely pursued the Lord. Out of obedience to God's authority, Josiah resolutely resisted the sins in his nation's culture and abolished the trend of idolatry that had prevailed for two generations.

Sixteen — precisely what is called adolescence today. Josiah's environment was no better than that of today's young people: what his father and grandfather left was two generations of idolatry. He did not wait for the environment to become better, nor wait until he was older, but at sixteen set the direction.

Although Josiah unfortunately was mortally wounded on the battlefield and died at the prime age of thirty-nine, for a long time he has been regarded as the greatest king after King David (2 Kings 23:25). Age has never been the problem; whom one puts in first place is the problem.

## V. Root: Wrong Reliance

The cause of young people's problems, the source material sums up in the concept of wrong reliance.

The wrong understanding thinks like this: What is important is not doing the right thing, but obtaining others' acceptance. I want to obey my own truth and live according to my own way.

Peers' acceptance became the highest standard; one's own feelings became truth itself. With these two as reliance, a person is pushed along by the crowd and led along by lusts; Absalom's road was walked out in this way — he cared too much that his own justice be sought by himself.

The right understanding changes the reliance: I hope to be accepted by others, but I hope even more to become a person who dares to refuse others' improper demands. Christ lives in me; when I live from His truth and according to His way, my life will be satisfied. The Lord Jesus personally said:

> "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.'" (John 14:6)

Truth is not "my own truth"; truth is a Person. Shifting reliance from the crowd and lusts to Christ and His truth solves more than half of the greatest problem of adolescence.

## VI. Practice

The practice left by the source material is very concrete, divided into the children's side and the parents' side.

Young people: Memorize 2 Timothy 2:22; read Proverbs repeatedly.

Parents: Memorize Deuteronomy 6:6-7; read repeatedly Ephesians 3:16-19, and transform this Scripture into prayer, praying sentence by sentence for the strength of the child's inner man, for Christ to dwell in the heart, and for love to be rooted and grounded.

## Conclusion

Adolescence is not a disaster to be endured through; it is an opportunity to set direction. Absalom and Josiah stood at the same crossroads; one let hatred accumulate, the other at sixteen put God in first place. Which road the child walks — the parents' teaching and prayer have real weight in it. There is nothing new under the sun; today's adolescence has all been written in the Bible — and the way out has all been written too.
