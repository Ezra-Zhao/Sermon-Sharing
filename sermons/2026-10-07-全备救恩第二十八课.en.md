## Content

Hindrances to salvation. [Original text partially illegible.] Can the order be disrupted? Because of God's revelation, we have come to know the order God arranged in salvation; because of the manifestation of facts, we have also seen man's and Satan's disruption of this order. This is not a blatant attack; it is an understated camouflage. Although it differs by a hair's breadth, the result errs by a thousand miles. In actual experience, how great is the disruption to God's plan caused by man's weakness and the devil's work?

God's plan: first being, then resembling; being and also resembling (2 Tim.). 1. Believing at once is [being]: "[These are written] that you may know that you have eternal life" (1 John 5:13, NKJV). 2. Being, and one ought to resemble: "since [we are] called, walk worthy of the calling" (cf. Eph. 4:1, NKJV). 3. First being, then resembling: "Christ will be magnified in my body... for to me, to live is Christ" (Phil. 1:20-21, NKJV). In God's arrangement of salvation, He wants us, through faith, to be united with Him and obtain His eternal life, and thus become His sons. The expression of [being], even if it is not [complete], cannot cancel [the being itself]. [Being] is accomplished at once upon believing; [resembling] must be gradually completed. Therefore God's arrangement is first being, then resembling. When we have the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ, and can all say, "to me, to live is Christ," then we are those who both are and resemble.

Man's weakness: emphasizing being and neglecting resembling; being but not resembling (James). 1. Not being, yet resembling: "[certain men] spy out our liberty which we have in Christ Jesus" (Gal. 2:4, NKJV). 2. Resembling but not being: "false prophets... who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravenous wolves" (Matt. 7:15, NKJV). 3. Being but not resembling: "It is actually reported that there is sexual immorality among you" (1 Cor. 5:1, NKJV). 4. Emphasizing being and neglecting resembling: "Awake to righteousness, and do not sin; for some do not have the knowledge of God" (1 Cor. 15:34, NKJV). When man's weakness is compared with God's plan, we see that there are false brethren, and we also see that there are false prophets. Besides these — who neither are nor resemble, or who resemble yet are not — there are also some believers who, though they are, do not resemble; or who emphasize only [being] and seek only eternal life, disregarding glory; asking only about being saved, disregarding reward; paying attention to life, neglecting living. Although these people have received grace, they are also people who fall short.

The devil's method: destroying being and seeking resembling; resembling but not being. 1. A fabricated resemblance: "presumptuously [calling] on the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, '[We command you to come out!]'" (cf. Acts 19:13, NKJV). 2. A pretended resemblance: [original reference incomplete]. 3. A resemblance in works: "when the grain had sprouted..., then the tares also appeared" (Matt. 13:26, NKJV). The enemy's method is to confuse God's plan. God wants being and also resembling; it wants resembling but not being. For example, it sows tares among the wheat, so that before the grain sprouts you cannot easily distinguish them. It also disguises itself as an angel of light, so that you cannot easily recognize it. But those who belong to God are not ignorant of its schemes.

Homework questions: What is the procedure God arranged for salvation? How can one reach the point of [being and resembling]? In God's arrangement, what is man's weakness? In the church are there [being-but-not-resembling] Christians? [Are there resembling-but-not-being] Christians? What are the weaknesses of [being-but-not-resembling] Christians? What is the devil's method here?
