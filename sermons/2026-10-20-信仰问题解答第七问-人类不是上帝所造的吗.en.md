# Faith Q&A Question 7: Was Mankind Not Created by God?

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «人类不是上帝所造的吗？».

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 7
**Adaptation:** Guangyi Zhao (retold in his own preaching style; the core views belong to the original author)

---

## Scripture

> "So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them." (Gen. 1:27, NKJV)

> "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living being." (Gen. 2:7, NKJV)

---

## I. Trace It Back One Level First: Where Did You Come From?

Someone says: man was not created by God. Pastor Tong does not argue with him; he first asks the simplest question: **then where did man come from?**

"Born of his mother." Good; where did mother come from? "Mother's mother." Go on up: where did mother's mother come from? Tracing level by level, the trouble arrives: **you always have to trace back to a very first man. Where did the first man come from?**

This is not quibbling. It is a question that cannot be circumvented: the chain can be very long, but the chain must have a head.

## II. Trace It Back to the Monkeys, and You Still Do Not Reach the Bottom

"Then man came from monkeys." Good; Pastor Tong asks on: where did monkeys come from? It is the same sentence — **where did the first monkey come from?**

At this point, the other side often says: "I don't know; go ask Darwin."

Pastor Tong says Darwin could not answer it either. In 1859, Darwin wrote the world-shaking *Origin of Species*, which describes how species change and how they diverge. But **where the first life came from, he gave no account at all**. The end of that book says instead that life was originally "breathed" into a few forms, or even a single form — and the revised editions state explicitly that it was "breathed into by the Creator." After going around a huge circle, the source still comes back to God.

In one sentence: **evolution speaks of what happens "after life already exists"; the source of life itself it did not explain, and cannot explain.**

## III. Life Cannot Arise by Itself out of Non-Life

In the nineteenth century, the French scientist Louis Pasteur proved one thing with his swan-neck flask experiment: **life cannot arise by itself out of non-living things.** However long broth sits, if it does not come into contact with germ-laden air, life will not grow in it by itself. So-called "spontaneous generation" was overturned by experiment.

So Pastor Tong's conclusion is: **God is the source of life.** A person who does not accept the Bible simply has no way to explain the question of "where the first life came from."

(A small note: the original manuscript recorded this scientist's name as "Pascal"; by historical fact it should be Pasteur — Pascal lived in the seventeenth century, and the dates do not fit. The name is corrected here according to historical fact; the argument is unchanged.)

## IV. Scripture's Answer: Dust, Plus God's Breath

Genesis gives two words, which are precisely the two sides of man:

**Original-language analysis one:** The "created" in Genesis 1:27 is, in Hebrew, ***Bara'* (בָּרָא)**. In Scripture, the subject of this word is always only One — God. Man can make, can manufacture, can assemble; only *bara'* he cannot do. **"Creation" is an action unique to God.** In this verse *bara'* is used three times in a row, precisely so that you will hear clearly that "man was created by God."

**Original-language analysis two:** Genesis 2:7 changes the word: ***Yatsar* (יָצַר)**, the potter's "forming" of a clay vessel. God used the dust of the ground and, like a potter, formed man into shape — this side speaks of man's lowliness: our material is nothing but dust.

Then comes the decisive step: God "breathed into his nostrils the breath of life." **"Breath of life" in Hebrew is *Nishmat Chayyim* (נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים), literally "the breath of life."** To the man formed of dust, with the breath God Himself breathed in, man "became a living being."

So what is man? **Dust + the breath of God.** Speak only of dust, and man becomes high-grade mud with no dignity; speak only of the spirit-breath, and man floats in midair and does not acknowledge his own finitude. Put the two words together, and you have the whole man.

---

## For Us Today

1. "Where did man come from" is not a child's question — pressed to the end, the first cause cannot be evaded; there must be a source
2. The theory of evolution explains change; it does not explain the source; keep the two things separate, and the discussion will not get muddled
3. Man is dust, and also dust into which God has breathed — his lowliness is real, and his bearing the image of God is also real
