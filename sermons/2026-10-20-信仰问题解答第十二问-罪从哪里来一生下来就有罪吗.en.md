# Faith Q&A Question 12: Where Does Sin Come From? Do We Have Sin from the Moment We Are Born?

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 12

## Scripture

> "Therefore, just as through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and thus death spread to all men, because all sinned." (Rom. 5:12, NKJV)

> "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me." (Ps. 51:5, NKJV)

> "For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, that each one may receive the things done in the body, according to what he has done, whether good or bad." (2 Cor. 5:10, NKJV)

---

## I. Divide the Question First: Where the Sinful Nature Comes From, and by What Judgment Is Reckoned

This question sounds simple, but two matters are actually wrapped inside it, and without dividing them it cannot be answered clearly.

One is: I never consulted with Adam; why do I bear sin from the moment I am born? From where was this sin transmitted to me?

The other is: if sin was transmitted, then when God judges me, does He reckon me by Adam's account, or by my own account?

Pastor Tong's answer touches both matters, and the order is very clear: first the source of the sinful nature, then the principle of judgment. The answer about the source leaves a man no room to shift the blame, and the principle of judgment also forbids him to blame others — both ends must be acknowledged.

## II. We Are Born in the Stream of Adam's Life

Pastor Tong's first sentence lands on the source: **we are born in the stream of Adam's life, and Adam is the representative of the whole human race.**

These words are not pleasant to hear, but they cannot be evaded. No one applied to come into this world himself, and no one was created separately, detached from mankind as a whole. The moment we are born, we are connected to Adam's line — our bloodline comes from him, and the disposition of our life also comes from him. When the representative sinned, all who are in him have a share in his fall. So Paul says that through one man sin entered the world, and death spread to all men (Rom. 5:12). It is not that each person becomes an Adam all over again; it is that in that one act of Adam's, the whole human race was present.

**Original-language analysis:** Romans 5:14 says Adam is "a type of Him who was to come"; "type" is ***Typos* (τύπος, Strong G5179)**, whose basic meaning is a die, a pattern, the first form struck out, according to which those after it are shaped (per BDAG). In using this word, Paul sets Adam and Christ as a pair: Adam is the first die, and those in him all bear sin and death; Christ is the second die, and those in Him obtain righteousness and life. Romans 5:19 states this pair of relations most directly: "For as by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so also by one Man's obedience many will be made righteous." In which representative you stand matters more than what kind of person you think you are.

David's words have the same meaning, only stated more personally: "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me" (Ps. 51:5).

**Original-language analysis:** This verse uses two Hebrew words. "Iniquity" is ***'Avon* (עָוֹן, Strong H5771)**, denoting what is bent, the inward corrupted state; "sin" is ***Ḥet'* (חֵטְא, Strong H2399)**, denoting concrete indebtedness and transgression (per BDB). David is not saying his mother committed some sin, nor pushing the responsibility onto his parents; he is tracing back to the deepest place of his own life, back to the moment of birth, admitting that this corruption was not picked up later — he was born with it. Only when a man confesses his sin in this way has he confessed it down to the root.

So the first question has its answer: **to have a sinful nature from the moment of birth is true.** Not because an infant has done some evil deed, but because he is born in Adam and his nature is already bent. Ephesians says we were "by nature children of wrath" (Eph. 2:3); "by nature" is said precisely of the nature itself.

**Original-language analysis:** The "by nature" in "by nature children of wrath" is, in Greek, ***Physei* (φύσει, Strong G5449, the dative of *Physis*)**, meaning according to the inborn nature, so by essence — not put on, and not learned (per BDAG). If the sinful nature were only something the environment taught badly, a change of environment would fix it; Scripture insists on calling it *Physis*, a matter within the nature, which is why nothing short of the new birth will do — when the nature itself has a problem, mending behavior is useless; the life must be exchanged.

## III. But God Does Not Judge You by Adam's Account

Having said this, Pastor Tong immediately adds a second sentence, and this sentence must not be missed: **God does not judge us because we have Adam's sinful nature; God will judge us according to what we ourselves have done, whether good or bad.**

This sentence preserves righteousness. The sinful nature is inherited, but judgment is according to what each person himself has done. No one, standing before the judgment seat, can say "my sinning is all Adam's fault" — Adam explains why you have the inclination to sin; he cannot explain that particular reaching out of your hand on that occasion. The inclination was transmitted by him; the choice was made by you. The words of 2 Corinthians 5:10 stand here: "that each one may receive the things done in the body, according to what he has done, whether good or bad." The things done in the body — no one can substitute for anyone else.

These two sentences must be heard together: hear only the first, and man will think God unfair, punishing descendants for an ancestor's sin; hear only the second, and man will think himself a blank sheet of paper and blame all bad deeds on the environment. Pastor Tong says both: the sinful nature has a source, and responsibility lies with the individual. This is Scripture's balance.

## IV. If an Infant Dies, Never Having Heard the Gospel, Can He Be Saved?

Here Pastor Tong gives a very definite answer, and I pass on his judgment as he gave it: if an infant or a small child dies, and he himself has committed no sin, **he can be saved. Because Christ died for him on the cross, He automatically saves him out of original sin.**

Note his reason: it is not that the infant is sinless — it was just explained that the infant likewise bears original sin; nor is it that the infant gets to heaven by innocence — innocence cannot be exchanged for salvation, and that point was made thoroughly in the previous question. His reason is only one: the cross of Christ. The infant has himself committed no actual sin, and has not rejected the gospel; the salvation Christ accomplished for him comes directly to him and saves him out of original sin.

For the adult, this answer works entirely differently. Pastor Tong says immediately after: **an adult who, having moral awareness and moral responsibility, commits sin must undergo God's righteous judgment.** An adult has the opportunity to hear the gospel, has the freedom to refuse, and has sins he has actually committed himself. He cannot use the infant's situation to excuse himself, saying "I couldn't control it either" — you have moral awareness, you knew, and you still did it; that account is entered under your name.

The difference between the infant and the adult is not that God plays favorites; it is that one has not yet exercised his own will, and the other has repeatedly used his own will to choose sin. For the former, the death of Christ covers directly; for the latter, he must come personally before the cross to confess, repent, and believe. There is no second road.

## V. So the Answer Is Two "Yes"es

Back to the original question: where does sin come from? It comes from Adam — he is the representative of mankind; we are born in his stream, and the sinful nature thus comes to every person.

Do we have sin from the moment we are born? As to the sinful nature, yes; as to judgment, what God reckons is the ledger of what you yourself have done. Only with these two sentences together is Pastor Tong's answer complete.

And the true landing point of this piece is not Adam; it is Christ. Adam is the first *Typos*, bringing sin and death to all; Christ is the One who was to come after, bringing righteousness and life to all who are in Him (Rom. 5:14, 19). The question was never "how did I come to be born in Adam" — that is already a fact; the question is: are you willing today to come out of Adam and enter into Christ?

---

## For Us Today

1. Acknowledge the sinful nature to the bottom: it is not that the environment ruined me first; I was born in Adam and my nature already bears sin — David traced back into his mother's womb to confess it; we too should not confess only the few surface items
2. Do not push responsibility outward: Adam explains the inclination; he does not bear the choice in your place; judgment is according to what each person has done in the body, and no one can use original sin as a shield
3. Receive the comfort about infants steadily: an infant who dies can be saved, relying not on innocence but on the cross of Christ — when comforting people, say it this way, and do not get the foundation wrong
4. Adults, do not wait any longer: having moral awareness and the opportunity to hear the gospel, confess and repent today, and do not apply the infant's situation to yourself
