### II. The Promised Son

[Scripture] (Gen. 13:15) (Gen. 17:8)

1. God preached the gospel (Gal. 3:8) (Gen. 22:15–18) (Gen. 12:1–3)
2. Waiting and looking (Gal. 3:19) (Gal. 3:23–24)
3. Already fulfilled (Matt. 1:1) (Gal. 3:25–26)

God was the first to preach the gospel. He promised Abraham that he should know that through him all nations would be blessed. This One who was born according to the promise has come (Gal. 4:23) — Jesus of Nazareth. Not only do we look to Him; Abraham himself also rejoiced to see His day (John 8:56).

### III. The Descendant of David

1. The promise of the King (2 Sam. 7:12–14) (Jer. 23:5) (Ps. 132:11)
2. The birth of the King (Luke 1:31–33) (Luke 2:10–11)
3. The testimony of the King (John 18:36–37) (2 Tim. 2:8)

The Lord Jesus is not only the son of Abraham in fulfillment, but also the Son of David in fulfillment (Matt. 1:1). Not only did Abraham look for His day; David also called Him Lord (Matt. 22:42–43). The former points out that He is the source of salvation (Heb. 5:9); the latter explains that He is the Prince of Peace (Isa. 9:6–7). Thank God — what He promised, He Himself has fulfilled.

### The Covenant of Grace

According to the "plan of salvation," the covenant that God made with us sinners in Christ is entirely God's infinite love and free grace; therefore it is called the "Covenant of Grace," in distinction and contrast to the "Covenant of Works" in Adam. In the "Covenant of Works," the success or failure of the covenant's law depended on Adam's own choice and action; however, in the "Covenant of Grace," God Himself prepared for sinners the Lord Jesus Christ as Mediator, to be our ransom, so that through the Holy Spirit we might be born again and receive the status of God's children (1 Tim. 2:5–6; Rom. 8:14–17).

**1. The two parties who made the covenant.** God is the initiator of the "Covenant of Grace"; He established the content of this covenant and determined the other party's relationship with God. Sinners who are saved by grace in Christ are the other party of this covenant, receiving the blessings that come from this covenant. The "Covenant of Grace" can be viewed in its meaning from two aspects: "purpose" and "means."

**(1) "Fellowship of life" — the purpose of the covenant.** The purpose of this covenant itself is to bring about fellowship of life between God and man, so that sinners can be saved by faith, become God's children again, and enjoy God's presence. This holy covenant of "God with man," carried out through the operation of the Holy Spirit, has been realized in the history of redemption; God's grace in Christ brings spiritual results: man's real, living faith lays hold of God's promise, man enjoys and experiences the blessing of the exchange of life, and will receive the complete fulfillment of God's promise.

According to this purpose, the "Covenant of Grace" may be defined as: the gracious covenant that God made with us saved sinners in Christ, in which God gives us His presence and all the blessings of salvation, and we receive by faith His salvation and all His grace and lovingkindness (Gen. 18:18–19; Deut. 7:9; 2 Chron. 6:14; Ps. 25:10–14; 103:17–18; John 1:12–13; 3:16–18; 2 Pet. 1:3–4).

**(2) "A legal holy covenant" — the means of the covenant.** This covenant is a holy covenant established to reach the spiritual purpose stated above, as the means of realizing "God with man." As a legal covenant, the one who breaks the covenant receives the covenant's curse and punishment — such as Ishmael, Esau, the wicked sons of Eli, the rebellious Israelites who fell in the wilderness, and Judas who betrayed the Lord. This also shows that although they received the sign of the gracious covenant and entered into the legal relationship of the covenant, they did not obtain the substantive blessing of fellowship with God and the exchange of life. God's people who keep the covenant and trust in God receive the well-being of life guaranteed in the gracious covenant. In this broader sense, the "Covenant of Grace" may be defined as: the legal holy covenant that God made with believers and their descendants (Gen. 6:18; 12:1–3; 17:7; Heb. 11:7–10; Acts 2:38–39; 16:13–15, 30–34; Rom. 9:4). In short, the "Covenant of Grace," in its inward substance, is "God with us, fellowship of life"; in its outward form, it is "a legal holy covenant, a rule of life."

**2. The theme and characteristics of the covenant.** The "Covenant of Grace" is the gracious covenant of "God with man (Immanuel)"; its theme is "God will be our God, and we will be God's people" (Ex. 6:7; Jer. 31:33; 32:38–40; Ezek. 34:23–25, 30–31; 36:25–28; Heb. 8:10; 2 Cor. 6:16–18; Rev. 21:3). This covenant has four characteristics: (1) It is a covenant "of grace," the result and manifestation of God's grace toward sinners; from beginning to end it is God's grace. (2) It is an "everlasting" covenant, a covenant that cannot be broken or abolished (Rom. 9:6–8; 11:29); although man sometimes is unfaithful and breaks the covenant, God is forever faithful and reliable (2 Tim. 2:13). (3) It is a covenant with a "substantive scope": the promises of the covenant are realized only on those who are saved by grace (Matt. 7:13–14; 22:14; Acts 13:48). (4) It is a "consistent" covenant: although the outward manner of its administration differs in different ages, in its basic substance it is the same, and its basic promises are no different (Gen. 17:7; Gal. 3:29); the gospel is the same (Acts 4:12; Gal. 3:8), the requirement of faith is the same (Rom. 4:16, 23–24; Gal. 3:6–7), and the Mediator is the same — all must be saved by trusting in Christ (Heb. 13:8; Gal. 3:16; John 14:6; 1 Tim. 2:5).

**3. The manifestation of the "Covenant of Grace" in the history of redemption.** The process of the manifestation of the "Covenant of Grace" in the different ages of redemption history is as follows: (1) The "first gospel": after Adam and Eve sinned and fell, God promised that "the Seed of the woman shall bruise the serpent's head" (Gen. 3:15); this means the Messiah would come and break the power of the evil one. (2) The "Covenant with Noah" caused Noah's family to be saved through the ark, and the earth to remain and no longer receive the punishment of the flood; this common grace guaranteed the stability of sinners' living environment, which was necessary for the realization of the "Covenant of Grace." (3) The "Abrahamic Covenant" is the formal proclamation of this gracious covenant, the special arrangement of the Old Testament age, directed to Abraham and his descendants; "faith" is this covenant's necessary requirement, and "circumcision" is this covenant's sacrament, as the seal of "justification by faith." (4) The "Mosaic Covenant" was established at Mount Sinai; it is basically the same as the "Abrahamic Covenant," directed to the nation of Israel. The law was given as a rule of life, its purpose being to make man know sin and humbly repent (Rom. 3:20); its function is that of a tutor, to bring men to Christ (Gal. 3:24). On the basis of the original "circumcision," the "Passover meal" became the second sacrament, as the seal of "redemption by the blood of the Lamb." (5) The "Davidic Covenant" shows that the Messiah the Savior is the descendant of David, and God's kingdom has no end. (6) The "New Covenant" is what was promised in Jeremiah 31:31–34 and Ezekiel 36:25–28, and testified to in Hebrews 8:6–13 as the "better covenant"; it is basically consistent with the covenants above (Rom. 4; Gal. 3), and further expands the scope of the former covenants, becoming a universal gracious covenant reaching all mankind. This new covenant has great glory; it is a ministry that gives life, justifies, endures, and is spiritual (2 Cor. 3:6–11). "Baptism" replaced circumcision, and the "Holy Communion (the Lord's Supper)" replaced the Passover meal, becoming the sacraments of the "New Covenant." The "New Covenant," established by "the precious blood of the Lord Christ," is the highest manifestation and final realization of the "Covenant of Grace" — namely, "Immanuel: God with man in Christ."
