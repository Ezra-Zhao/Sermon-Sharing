# Faith Q&A Question 13: Before the Archangel Fell, Where Did Evil Come From?

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «天使长堕落之前，罪恶从哪里来？».

**Original work:** Rev. Stephen Tong, *Faith Q&A*, Question 13
**Adaptation:** Guangyi Zhao (retold in his own preaching style; the core views belong to the original author)

---

## Scripture

> "I form the light and create darkness, I make peace and create calamity; I, the LORD, do all these things." (Isa. 45:7, NKJV)

> "When he speaks a lie, he speaks from his own resources, for he is a liar and the father of it." (John 8:44, NKJV)

> "God is light and in Him is no darkness at all." (1 John 1:5, NKJV)

> "'How you are fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!... For you have said in your heart: "I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God... I will be like the Most High."'" (Isa. 14:12–14, NKJV, excerpted)

---

## I. Change One Word First: Evil "Arose"; It Did Not "Pre-Exist"

This question is sharply put: the archangel wanted to be equal with God and fell, becoming the devil — but before he fell, where did evil come from? Did God create evil first, and only then did evil have a chance to come out?

Pastor Tong begins by seizing on a single word in the question. He says: your saying "evil arose" is right; adding a "there is" in front of it — "there is an arising of evil" — is not right. The meaning differs greatly: **evil arose; evil did not pre-exist.**

To pre-exist is to have been there all along, waiting. To arise is not to have been there originally and to appear only later. God did not create evil, and evil is not another eternal source standing alongside God. If evil pre-existed, there would be two sources in the universe, one good and one evil, and God would no longer be the sole source. Scripture does not take that road. God is light and in Him is no darkness at all (1 John 1:5) — darkness is not a work He created; darkness is the state that appears after leaving the light.

So the first answer stands: **in tracing the source of evil, you cannot trace it to God.** You can only trace it to the step where the creature himself deviated.

## II. The "Evil" of Isaiah 45:7 Is Not the Evil You Are Thinking Of

Someone will immediately bring out Isaiah 45: some English versions translate that verse as if "God creates good and also creates evil" — does Scripture itself say God creates evil?

Pastor Tong's answer is: look at the word first; do not draw the conclusion first. One word has several senses; read one sense wrong, and the whole sentence is read backwards. He divides the word "evil" as he explains it: there is the sense of calamity and judgment, there is the sense of transgression, and there is the sense of the morally corrupted sinful nature; they cannot be stirred into one lump. The Chinese Union Version translates this verse very carefully; it does not render it as God's creating moral evil, but translates: "I make peace and create calamity."

**Original-language analysis:** The "calamity" in the second half of Isaiah 45:7 is, in Hebrew, ***Ra'* (רָע, Strong H7451)**. The range of this word is very wide; it can denote badness, harm, disaster, calamity; which sense to take depends on the context (per BDB, HALOT). The structure of this verse is a neat parallelism: light against darkness, peace against *Ra'*. "Peace" is ***Shalom* (שָׁלוֹם, Strong H7965)**, denoting wholeness, peace, welfare; what stands opposite it is naturally the reverse of welfare — calamity and judgment, not "the moral sinful nature." To read *Ra'* as a sinful nature God personally created is to read the word too narrowly and to break the parallelism.

What this verse truly speaks of is sovereignty: whether peace, or calamity and judgment, all are under God's governance; nothing runs outside of Him. It does not say: sin is God's work. That sovereignty is God's, and that God is the author of sin, are two entirely different things; never confuse them.

## III. God Did Not Create Satan, and He Did Not Create Evil Either

In the Reformation period, this question was already argued over. Pastor Tong mentions that at that time some accused Lutheran and Calvinist theology of leading, if its logic is followed through, to a very frightening conclusion: that God became the creator of Satan and of sin. The Reformers did not accept that conclusion, and the *Augsburg Confession* made a response and a defense on this very point.

Pastor Tong says that in fact this was never our meaning. The evidence is a sentence of Jesus Himself, which he calls "very beautifully and very correctly" said: that evil one, "when he speaks a lie, he speaks from his own resources" (John 8:44).

**Original-language analysis:** "From his own resources" is, in Greek, ***Ek tōn idiōn* (ἐκ τῶν ἰδίων)**, meaning out of what is his own, what belongs to himself; "his own" is ***Idios* (ἴδιος, Strong G2398)**, denoting what belongs to oneself, private (per BDAG). Jesus did not say that Satan's lying came out of God's arrangement or God's design, nor that God put the lie into him; He said the lie came out of Satan himself. The source is in himself, not in God.

So Pastor Tong draws a very crisp conclusion: **God did not create Satan, and God did not create evil.** What God created was angels; after the angel fell, he became Satan — this identity of "Satan" is not the finished product at creation; it is the result after the fall. Likewise, evil is not a created thing either; it is an ontological deficiency that the creature himself produced: because of disobedience, moral corruption was produced, and that is how evil arose.

Hear this precisely: it is not that God created a thing called "evil" and stuffed it into the world; it is that a created being, originally good, deviated from the position where he ought to be; deficiency appeared, and corruption followed.

## IV. 2 + 5 = 3: Error Did Not Originally Exist; It Existed Only When Arranged Wrongly

Here Pastor Tong gives an especially memorable illustration, which I pass on according to his meaning.

Set the five symbols 2, 3, 5, +, = on the table: which symbol is wrong? Not one of them is wrong. The numerals are not wrong, and the signs are not wrong either.

But if you arrange them as 2 + 5 = 3, error appears at once. Error did not originally exist; how did it suddenly come to exist? Because in arranging them, you used your freedom wrongly — you have the freedom to arrange, and you used that freedom on the wrong order, and error was produced.

Arrange them again as 3 + 2 = 5, and the error is gone again. Why? Pastor Tong says: because freedom has returned to the order where it ought to be.

He gathers the illustration into one theological sentence: **when freedom submits under God's sovereignty, it produces righteousness; when freedom does not submit under God's sovereignty, it produces unrighteousness. Because God is righteousness itself; what departs from righteousness is unrighteousness.**

The archangel's fall must be seen within this sentence:

**Original-language analysis:** Isaiah 14 depicts the words in the heart of that fallen one — "I will be like the Most High." "The Most High" is ***'Elyon* (עֶלְיוֹן, Strong H5945)**, denoting the Most High, a title of God; the verb in "I will... be like" is ***Dāmāh* (דָּמָה, Strong H1819)**, meaning to make oneself resemble, to reckon as equal (per BDB). His sin was not in wanting to shine — 14:12 calls him ***Hēlēl* (הֵילֵל, Strong H1966)**, whose basic meaning is a shining one, a bright star; at creation he was originally one who shines. His sin lay in this: a created shining one, unwilling to remain in the creature's position and shine there, insisted on making himself equal with the Most High who made him. The light was not wrong; what was wrong was the heart that wanted to seize the position. Freedom was not taken away; freedom was used in reverse.

So do not ask "why did God not stop the first step." Acknowledge first: the first step was taken by the creature himself, taken toward being equal with God, taken out of God's order — and sin arose in that very "stepping out."

## V. Freedom Is a Good Thing; Precisely Because It Is Good, It Is Dangerous

One last question cannot be evaded: why did God give the archangel freedom? Without freedom, would he not have been unable to fall?

Pastor Tong's answer: **because freedom is the foundation of morality.** Without freedom there is no true obedience, no true love, and no morality to speak of. An existence that cannot say "no," saying "yes" a thousand times, is not obedience; that is just a program running.

Is freedom good? Good. Is freedom dangerous? Very dangerous. Pastor Tong gives, in passing, an example from daily life: the faster a car runs, the faster business gets done — that is the benefit; but the higher the speed, the greater the danger too. Do you want a car that is both good and completely free of danger? There is one — a car without an engine. It will never speed and hit anyone; it also cannot go anywhere.

In God's giving freedom to angels and to men, there is hidden a blessing that carries crisis with it. Pastor Tong closes very steadily: **blessing is produced through being with God; crisis is produced through being separated from God.**

The same freedom, with God and submitting under His sovereignty, bears the fruit of righteousness; departing from God and replacing sovereignty with autonomy, what it bears is unrighteousness. The problem was never in freedom itself; it is in where freedom is used.

## VI. So, Where Does Evil Come From, After All?

Back to that first question: before the archangel fell, where did evil come from?

The answer has three layers, and missing any one layer makes it incomplete:

First, evil did not pre-exist, and it was not created by God. God is light, with no darkness at all; Isaiah 45:7 speaks of God's sovereignty in governing peace and calamity and judgment, not of God's manufacturing moral evil.

Second, evil arose when a created being misused freedom. The archangel was originally a shining one, yet his heart wanted to be equal with the Most High; lies and corruption came out of himself, not out of God. The error is not in the symbols; it is in the arrangement; sin is not in being created itself; it is in freedom's departing from God's order.

Third, freedom itself is the good gift God gave; it is the foundation of morality. God did not, in order to save trouble, create a company of beings who cannot say "no." Such a world would have no sin — and also no love, no obedience, no righteousness — nothing at all, only a machine that cannot make mistakes and also cannot glorify God.

Man's later story is in fact the same line: Adam's step, too, was freedom departing from order. The difference is that for the archangel's story, Scripture gives no road back; in man's story, God prepared salvation. Knowing where sin came from is not for pushing responsibility onto the archangel; it is precisely for seeing clearly: every time I use freedom to depart from God, the same danger is in it; and every time I return into His order, there is true righteousness and peace.

---

## For Us Today

1. Do not push sin onto God: evil was not created by God, nor did it pre-exist; in suffering you may ask about God's sovereignty, but do not fasten the authorship of moral evil onto God
2. In reading Scripture, check the word meanings first: the *Ra'* of Isaiah 45:7 must be read by its context as calamity and judgment; do not use one translation of one word to overturn the whole Bible's testimony to God's holiness
3. Be on guard against the "I will be equal" heart: the fall did not begin with committing one great evil deed; it began with refusing to remain in the creature's position and insisting on being one's own master
4. Use freedom in the right place: freedom submitting under God's sovereignty produces righteousness, freedom departing from God produces unrighteousness; every concrete choice today is rearranging that equation
