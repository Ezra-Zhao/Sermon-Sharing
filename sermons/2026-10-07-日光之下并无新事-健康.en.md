# Nothing New Under the Sun: Health

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：健康».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of health

## Summary

The apostle John prayed for his friend, that he may prosper in all things and be in health, just as his soul prospers — and this is also God's heart for us. This chapter looks at health from the Bible: the body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, a merry heart does good like medicine, and Jesus has already paid the price for healing; it also looks at man's days from the Bible's records of longevity. The landing point is self-discipline: rest reasonably, be temperate in eating and drinking, guard your heart, present your body, and glorify God in your body.

## I. God's Will Is for You to Be in Health

The key Scripture of this chapter is the apostle John's greeting to his friend Gaius:

> "Beloved, I pray that you may prosper in all things and be in health, just as your soul prospers." (3 John 1:2)

"Be in health" in Greek is **hygiainō (ὑγιαίνω)** (original text ὑγιαίνω, transliterated hygiainō; Strong G5198; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to be in bodily health, free from disease"). John places bodily health alongside the prospering of the soul as a wish — the good of the body is not outside of faith.

When the Lord Jesus was on earth, this was seen most clearly. A man with leprosy came and worshiped Him, saying, "Lord, if You are willing, You can make me clean." His question was not whether Jesus could, but whether Jesus was willing. Jesus' answer was:

> "Then Jesus put out His hand and touched him, saying, 'I am willing; be cleansed.' Immediately his leprosy was cleansed." (Matt. 8:3)

"I am willing" — these two words fully express God's heart toward the sick. In Exodus, God also personally proclaimed His name:

> "If you diligently heed the voice of the LORD your God and do what is right in His sight, give ear to His commandments and keep all His statutes, I will put none of the diseases on you which I have brought on the Egyptians. For I am the LORD who heals you." (Exod. 15:26)

## II. The Body Is the Temple of the Holy Spirit

Why should we take care of the body? Paul gave two reasons:

> "Or do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own? For you were bought at a price; therefore glorify God in your body and in your spirit, which are God's." (1 Cor. 6:19-20)

The body is a temple, and it was bought at a price — taking care of the body is not simply cherishing oneself; it is glorifying God in the body. The standard for managing life, as cited in the original manuscript, is this sentence:

> "Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God." (1 Cor. 10:31)

If even eating and drinking are so, how much more the use of the whole body.

## III. A Merry Heart Does Good Like Medicine

Health is not only on the bodily side. Proverbs says:

> "A merry heart does good, like medicine, but a broken spirit dries the bones." (Prov. 17:22)

Managing one's emotions is taking care of the body; sorrow left in the heart dries the bones. Proverbs chapter 4 tells this path more completely — God's words themselves are medicine:

> "My son, give attention to my words; incline your ear to my sayings. Do not let them depart from your eyes; keep them in the midst of your heart; for they are life to those who find them, and health to all their flesh. Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it spring the issues of life." (Prov. 4:20-23)

Here "health to all their flesh" — "health" in Hebrew is **marpēʾ (מַרְפֵּא)** (original text מַרְפֵּא, transliterated marpēʾ; Strong H4832; according to the *BDB Lexicon*, "healing, medicine, health") — God's words kept in the heart are medicine that heals the whole body. Keep your heart above all keeping. The source of health is in the heart; only when the heart is guarded does the body have a root.

## IV. Jesus Has Already Paid the Price for Healing

Where is the confidence of believers when they pray for the body? At the cross. Isaiah prophesied centuries earlier:

> "But He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; the chastisement for our peace was upon Him, and by His stripes we are healed." (Isa. 53:5)

The Gospel of Matthew matches this prophecy to Jesus' healing work:

> "That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Isaiah the prophet, saying: 'He Himself took our infirmities and bore our sicknesses.'" (Matt. 8:17)

Peter confirms it again in the New Testament:

> "Who Himself bore our sins in His own body on the tree, that we, having died to sins, might live for righteousness — by whose stripes you were healed." (1 Pet. 2:24)

The psalmist had long counted God's grace in this way:

> "Who forgives all your iniquities, who heals all your diseases, who redeems your life from destruction, who crowns you with lovingkindness and tender mercies." (Ps. 103:3-4)

And this Lord who healed people yesterday has not changed today:

> "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, today, and forever." (Heb. 13:8)

## V. Prayer and Declaration: With a Heart of Bold Confidence

The original manuscript gave several specific reminders on prayer. First, pray with bold confidence:

> "Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us. And if we know that He hears us, whatever we ask, we know that we have the petitions that we have asked of Him." (1 John 5:14-15)

Second, timidity is not from God:

> "For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind." (2 Tim. 1:7)

Third, be careful with the words of your mouth, and make positive declarations. The Bible says a person can be snared by his own words — "You are snared by the words of your mouth; you are taken by the words of your mouth" (Prov. 6:2). The words often spoken by the mouth will shape a person's view of his own body. When Jesus spoke of faith in prayer, He said:

> "Therefore I say to you, whatever things you ask when you pray, believe that you receive them, and you will have them." (Mark 11:24)

Fourth, ask others to lay hands on you and pray for you. The Lord promised that signs would follow those who believe, and one sentence among them is precisely: "They will lay hands on the sick, and they will recover." (Mark 16:18) Praying in one accord and supporting one another is a practice the original manuscript emphasizes again and again.

## VI. Longevity in the Bible

The original manuscript used considerable space to study longevity. First, look at the boundary God set for man's days:

> "And the LORD said, 'My Spirit shall not strive with man forever, for he is indeed flesh; yet his days shall be one hundred and twenty years.'" (Gen. 6:3)

Moses, looking back on his life, said:

> "The days of our lives are seventy years; and if by reason of strength they are eighty years, yet their boast is only labor and sorrow; for it is soon cut off, and we fly away." (Ps. 90:10)

People before the Flood lived far longer than today. According to Genesis chapter 5, the original manuscript lists: Adam lived a total of nine hundred and thirty years, Seth nine hundred and twelve years, Enosh nine hundred and five years, Cainan nine hundred and ten years, Mahalalel eight hundred and ninety-five years, Jared nine hundred and sixty-two years, Methuselah nine hundred and sixty-nine years, Lamech seven hundred and seventy-seven years, Noah nine hundred and fifty years, Shem six hundred years. The original manuscript also mentions that Genesis mentions seven people whose lifespans exceeded nine hundred years — Adam, Seth, Enosh, Cainan, Jared, Methuselah, and Noah, all of whom were born before God sent the great Flood; the Bible also mentions at least twenty-five other people who lived much longer than modern people. In addition, the original manuscript also cited the Sumerian King List as a comparison: after kingship descended from heaven, Alulim ruled for twenty-eight thousand eight hundred years, Alalgar ruled for thirty-six thousand years, the two together ruled for sixty-four thousand eight hundred years — the ancient world's memory of primeval longevity does not appear only in the Bible.

For modern longevity records, the original manuscript recorded two people: On April 1, 2015, the world's oldest person, Misao Okawa of Japan, died of heart failure in her hometown at the age of 117; she was born on March 5, 1898, and just one month before her death had celebrated her 117th birthday, saying that 117 years of time did not seem as long as it might look; before her, the Guinness record was Jeanne Calment of France, who died in August 1997 at the age of 122.

On longevity, the original manuscript also gave two biblical promises. One is connected with honoring parents:

> "'Honor your father and mother,' which is the first commandment with promise: 'that it may be well with you and you may live long on the earth.'" (Eph. 6:2-3)

> "Honor your father and your mother, that your days may be long upon the land which the LORD your God is giving you." (Exod. 20:12)

One is connected with walking in God's way. God said to Solomon:

> "And if you walk in My ways, to keep My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then I will lengthen your days." (1 Kings 3:14)

The landing point of the original manuscript's discussion of longevity is the restorative capacity God placed in the human body: Scientists exploring the structure of the human body have discovered that the body has an astonishing capacity for self-renewal and repair. People born before the Flood were closer to perfection, so they lived much longer than we do; precisely because of sin and imperfect body and mind, human beings fall ill, grow old, and die. And the Creator has never changed His will — He sent His Son Jesus Christ to pay the ransom, preparing restoration and eternal life for man. Paul says: "For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ all shall be made alive." (1 Cor. 15:22)

## VII. Self-Discipline: The Foundation of Health

The last sentence of the key-points manuscript is: Self-discipline is the foundation of health. The original manuscript lands self-discipline in several concrete matters.

**Reasonable rest.** How did the Lord Jesus Himself arrange rest for His disciples?

> "And He said to them, 'Come aside by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while.' For there were many coming and going, and they did not even have time to eat." (Mark 6:31)

Even the Lord led His disciples aside to a deserted place to rest a while; rest is not laziness — it is the rhythm with which God made man.

**Temperance in eating and drinking.** Proverbs' warning against indulgence is very direct:

> "Do not mix with winebibbers, or with gluttonous eaters of meat; for the drunkard and the glutton will come to poverty, and drowsiness will clothe a man with rags." (Prov. 23:20-21)

First Corinthians furthermore lists drunkenness among those who will not inherit the kingdom of God (1 Cor. 6:9-10). Temperance is not mistreating the body; it is precisely taking care of the body.

**Presenting the body.** The endpoint of Paul's exhortation is to present the body as a sacrifice to God:

> "I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that you present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service." (Rom. 12:1)

**Meditating on His word day and night.** The words of Joshua, which the original manuscript placed under "cognition and self-discipline":

> "This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate in it day and night, that you may observe to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success." (Josh. 1:8)

The body presented, the mind filled with His words, eating and drinking with temperance, rest with rhythm — self-discipline does not rely on a burst of ruthlessness; it is returning the whole person's life to God's order.

## Conclusion

This lesson on health begins with a greeting in 3 John and ends with an exhortation in Romans: God wishes us to be in health, just as our soul prospers; the way we respond to Him is to care for this body as the temple of the Holy Spirit and to present it as a living sacrifice. The price for healing, Jesus has already paid; the duty of care is in our own hands. Leaving neither aside is the complete biblical view of health.

## Prayer of Response

Lord, thank You that You wish us to prosper in all things and be in health, just as our soul prospers. Our body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, bought by You at a price; teach us to guard our hearts, to live joyfully, to be temperate in eating and drinking, to rest in due measure, to present our bodies as a living sacrifice, and to glorify You in our bodies. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.

1. This chapter is compiled from the 2019 lecture PPT (slides 247–279). The "cases" section of the original manuscript contained only four phrases — "the body's self-healing ability, meeting the right person, the right thing happening, the effect of self-discipline" — without expansion into prose; this chapter is composed from the four application points of the key-points manuscript and the original manuscript's Scripture materials, and among the four phrases only "the effect of self-discipline" is expanded elsewhere in the original manuscript (see Section VII); the rest were not forced together. Items listed in the original manuscript's "industry and cognition" section, such as CAR-T therapy, RNA therapy, anti-aging therapy, and 3D bioprinting, were listed in title form only, without expanded text, and were not adopted in this chapter.
2. The modern figures in the longevity section (Misao Okawa, 117 years old; Jeanne Calment, 122 years old), the ages of the pre-Flood figures, and the numbers from the Sumerian King List are all recorded as in the original manuscript, without alteration; the ages of the Genesis figures can be read against the original text of Genesis chapters 5 and 11.
3. "Be in health" in 3 John 1:2, in Greek hygiainō (ὑγιαίνω, G5198), is also used in the New Testament for "sound doctrine" (e.g., 1 Tim. 1:10); body and mind share one word in the original text.
