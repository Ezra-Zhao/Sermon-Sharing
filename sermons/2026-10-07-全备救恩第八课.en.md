## Content

Having read through the Scriptures above, we should clearly know that God has not the slightest intention of making man keep the law. God gave the law to man only to use the law to expose man's sin and make man know himself. Because man has fallen into the knowledge of good and evil, he supposes that God wants man to keep the law. And because man does not know himself, he wants by his own strength to keep the law. Little does he know that man himself has no strength to keep the law, and that God also has no intention of making man keep the law. God wants man, through the law, to know his own corruption and inability, and to take refuge in and receive His grace. (Gal. 3:21–25) (Gal. 4:4–5)

**Law and Grace:**

1. Source: the law was added halfway (Rom. 5:20); grace existed from the beginning (2 Tim. 1:9).
2. Status: the law has the status of a bondwoman (Gal. 4:23–24); grace has the status of the freewoman (Gal. 4:23–24).
3. Identity: under the law, a slave (Gal. 4:24); under grace, a son (Gal. 4:28).
4. Requirement: the law says "do" (Gal. 3:10–12); grace says "believe" (Rom. 4:4–5).
5. Toward "righteousness": the law demands righteousness from man (Rom. 2:13); grace adds righteousness to man (Rom. 3:21–22).
6. Toward "sin": the law condemns man's sin (Rom. 3:19); grace forgives man's sin (Rom. 4:6–8).
7. Toward "life and death": the law makes man die (2 Cor. 3:6); grace makes man live (Eph. 2:4–6).
8. Toward "the curse": the law puts man under a curse (Gal. 3:10); grace redeems man out of the curse (Gal. 3:13).
9. Toward "the mouth": the law stops every mouth (Rom. 3:19b); grace opens the mouth to praise (Heb. 13:15).
10. Far and near: the law makes man far from God (Eph. 2:12); grace makes man near to God (Eph. 2:13).
11. Toward "God": the law makes man fear God (Ex. 20:18); grace makes man love God (Matt. 22:37).
12. Toward "enemies": the law makes man hate his enemy (Matt. 5:43); grace makes man love his enemies (Matt. 5:44).
13. Toward "the rebellious son": the law stones the rebellious son to death (Deut. 21:18–21); grace forgives the rebellious son (Luke 15:20–24).
14. Toward "the adulterous woman": the law stones her to death (John 8:5); grace does not condemn her (John 8:10–11).

### II. The Problem of Life

1. Separated from God (Eph. 4:18) (Jer. 2:13)
2. Fallen into slavery (Gal. 4:7–8) (Gal. 4:24–25)
3. Empty and limited (Ps. 39:5–6) (James 4:14)
4. Fallen and lowly (Rom. 1:21–23) (Eph. 2:2–3)
5. Self-conceited and self-righteous (Rom. 9:30—10:4) (2 Tim. 3:1–6)

Mankind's created-yet-fallen life has become an empty, limited, mortal life. Scripture says: "…flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God; nor does corruption inherit incorruption" (1 Cor. 15:50). This already explains our substance and proves that we have no capital to enter eternal life. If God is to save us, He must give us another life — His own eternal life — so that what is mortal may become immortal, and what is corruptible may become incorruptible (1 Cor. 15:53). Otherwise, we have no capital to enter God's eternity! For only eternal life can enter eternity; only God's life can enjoy God's glory.

### III. The Problem of Sin

1. All under sin (Rom. 3:9) (Rom. 7:14)
2. All shut up in sin (Gal. 3:22–23) (Rom. 3:19–20)
3. All have sinned (Rom. 3:23) (Ps. 130:3–4) (1 Kings 8:46)

Not only do we have no capital to enter eternal glory; in reality we also have no qualification to enter eternal glory. In our substance, we cannot inherit the kingdom of God; in our character, we also cannot inherit the kingdom of God. "Do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God?" (1 Cor. 6:9). Before Adam sinned, for God to save him, solving only the problem of life would have been enough. After Adam sinned, for God to save us, He needs not only to solve the problem of life; He needs even more to solve the problem of sin.

### IV. The Problem of the Enemy

1. The power of darkness (Col. 1:13) (2 Cor. 4:4) (Luke 22:53)
2. The ruling prince (Eph. 2:2) (Heb. 2:14) (Eph. 6:12)
3. The ruler of this world (John 12:31) (John 14:30) (John 16:11)

The one spoken of here is "…the enemy, the devil" (Matt. 13:39). He is the ruler of darkness; he is the prince of the air; he is God's enemy; he is the ruler of this world! For the whole world lies under his power, and man is no exception. If God is to save us, besides the problems of life and sin, He also needs to solve for us the problem of the enemy, and rescue us out from under his power. (Acts 26:18)

### Homework Questions

1. What is the difference between the fundamental problems of life and the problems to be solved for salvation?
2. What is our whole life like?
3. Even if we had no sin, could we be saved? Why?
4. What is our character like?
5. If Adam had not sinned, in what respect would he have needed God to save him?
6. And after Adam sinned?
7. How does our Lord solve the problem of the enemy for us?
