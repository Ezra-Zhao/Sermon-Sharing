## Content

The eternity of salvation. Is being saved eternal? If our salvation has been confirmed, then our salvation is already secure. [What] is secure and will not be shaken — such salvation is of course eternal, for temporary security cannot be counted as security. Therefore we firmly believe that salvation is eternal! If God is the eternal God, His salvation must be eternal salvation. He saved us once, and He has saved us forever. Let us now look again at what the Bible says.

God's will does not change (Hebrews). "[He] predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, according to the good pleasure of His will" (Eph. 1:5, NKJV). Our salvation originates from God's good-pleasure will before the creation of the world. In His omniscient divinity, because He took pleasure in us, He predestined us to obtain the adoption as sons through Jesus Christ. God's will is an eternal will, and this salvation is also an eternal salvation; God's will is a will that cannot be changed, and this salvation is also a salvation that cannot be changed.

[God's wisdom] is profound and rich (Romans). "For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable" (Rom. 11:29, NKJV). The grace God gave in eternity [past] is what God, with all wisdom and prudence, abundantly lavished on us. Since God arranged salvation according to His wisdom, His gifts and calling are certainly without regret. He knows us more than we know ourselves; He will never say, "I saved the wrong person."

God's lovingkindness is forever and ever (Jeremiah). "I will have mercy on you [with everlasting kindness]... says the LORD, your Redeemer" (Isa. 54:8, NKJV). As God's eternal life does not change, God's eternal lovingkindness will not change; as God's eternal lovingkindness does not change, God's eternal salvation will not change either. Although sometimes in our experience it seems as though God hides His face from us and seems not to love us, this is only like a river which at a bend seems to change direction; in fact, if you can climb high and look far, you can see that it is always advancing toward its final destination — the ocean. "Having loved His own who were in the world, He loved them to the end" (John 13:1, NKJV).

God's righteousness is given to those who believe (Romans). "[The gospel is] the power of God to salvation for everyone who believes, for in it the righteousness of God is revealed" (Rom. 1:16-17, NKJV). Salvation not only manifests God's love; salvation also manifests God's righteousness. Since He brought us here through creation, He cannot fail to take us there through redemption; since He bought us back through Christ, He will never sell us again; since He laid our sins on Christ, He will never again condemn those who are in Christ.

God's power is able to do all things (Genesis). "My Father, who has given them to Me, is greater than all; and no one is able to snatch them out of My Father's hand" (John 10:29, NKJV). God's power is the power that created all things, and God is the God who is greater than all. His hands made the earth; His fingers made the heavens. He places us who have received grace in His hand, keeps us, and cherishes us — is this not safe enough? Is this not eternal enough?

Homework questions: God is the eternal God — what about God's salvation? He saved us once — then what? God's will cannot be changed — what about God's salvation? What God has given is according to His wisdom — do you think He will regret it? Will God's lovingkindness change? Will God's keeping fail? Can man snatch us out of God's hand?

[Placed before The Complete Salvation] The main theme of the whole Bible is salvation. This salvation is the complete way of salvation God prepared for man. Jesus is the center of the universe, the center of history, the center of the church, the center of the believer, and even more the center of the Bible. The whole Bible takes Jesus as its center and the cross as its theme. The whole Old Testament, without exception, takes the cross as its destination; the whole New Testament, without exception, originates from the cross. In every book and every verse of the whole Bible, without exception, one sees the form of Jesus; in every book and every chapter, if one has not seen Jesus, one has not read the Bible.

Brothers and sisters, you must know: man's greatest lack is salvation; the great pain in man's soul is the need for salvation; God's greatest lovingkindness is preparing salvation for man; the sole main theme of the whole Bible is Christ's wonderful salvation. Everything the whole Bible discusses is nothing other than explaining how urgently man needs salvation, and the salvation God accomplished in Jesus Christ through His [incarnation, death, resurrection, ascension, and] return, and how man, through repentance and faith, can obtain this salvation. The Christian's sole hope and purpose is also nothing other than to experience this salvation in himself and to preach this salvation. "How shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation?" (Heb. 2:3, NKJV). "Therefore we must give the more earnest heed to the things we have heard, lest we drift away. For if the word spoken through angels proved steadfast, and every transgression and disobedience received a just reward, how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation, which at the first began to be spoken by the Lord, and was confirmed to us by those who heard Him, God also bearing witness both with signs and wonders, with various miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit, according to His own will?" (Heb. 2:1-4, NKJV). [Original text partially illegible.] "[You] have given me [a shield]... Your right hand has held me up, Your gentleness has made me great" (Ps. 18:35, NKJV). "We will rejoice in your salvation, and in the name of our God we will set up our banners! May the LORD fulfill all your petitions" (Ps. 20:5, NKJV).

[Original text partially illegible.] The Bible clearly reveals: God works all things according to His own will and plan (Eph. 1). Therefore God's saving of man is according to God's predestined will and plan; it is not that after man's fall He improvised an idea and thought up a remedy. In other words, God's plan of salvation is in God's eternal will; it was arranged and determined before the foundation of the world, before the ages (Eph. 1; 2 Tim. 1).

The foundation of the Bible: In the Gospels, the Lord Jesus Christ often testified that He was sent by the Father into the world, and His mission was to complete the plan of salvation, fulfill the promises of salvation, and save the lost people (John 5). John 17, Ephesians 1, and Romans 8 all explain in detail that this plan of salvation is the plan and work of the triune God: the Father's good pleasure predestines and arranges; the Son becomes the Mediator of salvation, obeys the Father's will, receives the promises given by the Father, completes the redemption of the cross, and is one with God's people; the Holy Spirit applies this great grace of redemption in the life situations of the people. The "plan of salvation" God prepared for us is so rich and wonderful — truly "eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor have entered into the heart of man" (1 Cor. 2:9, NKJV).

The Bible emphasizes three main points of this "plan of salvation":

The plan of salvation was predestined by God the Father in eternity: The Son, the Lord Jesus, according to the Father's determinate counsel and foreknowledge, became flesh and came into the world, was delivered over to men, and died on the cross; this is achieving what the Father's will predestined must happen (Acts 2). The center of this eternal plan of salvation is the redemption of the Lord Christ's precious blood; this was determined before the foundation of the world, but was manifested in these last times for us (1 Pet. 1); our names were written in the Lamb's Book of Life before the foundation of the world (Rev. 13), which manifests the eternity of the "plan of salvation."

The plan of salvation is God's sovereign will; God had no obligation to save sinners (God does not deliver angels — Heb. 2). This is entirely the Father's good pleasure; there is no external factor compelling God to save mankind. "God saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to His own purpose and grace" (2 Tim. 1:9, NKJV); our being saved is not because of ourselves; it is entirely by God's grace (Eph. 2); God, "[because of] His great love with which He loved us," "predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, according to the good pleasure of His will" (Eph. 1:5, NKJV).

The purpose of the plan of salvation is to make us holy: God the Father chose us in Christ to be holy and without blame before Him (Eph. 1); He who called us is holy, so we also must be holy; God called us out of darkness into His marvelous light (1 Pet. 1; 2:9); therefore we are a holy people together called by heaven — Christians are called ["saints"; see the openings of Paul's epistles]. Our Christian life, from the starting point of [salvation] to its end point, cannot be separated from [holiness]; the final purpose of our redemption and becoming holy is to restore God's image, to become the likeness of the Lord Jesus Christ, so that the grace of God's glory may be praised (Rom. 8; 2 Cor. 3).

In sum, the plan of salvation includes every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places, namely the inheritance obtained in the Lord Christ (Eph. 1); it includes all things predestined by God's good pleasure — all things work together for good to those who are called (Rom. 8); all things in heaven and on earth are to be gathered together in one in Christ according to the arranged plan of salvation (Eph. 1), making Christ the head over all things to the church (Eph. 1).

Christ as Mediator: The Mediator of the plan of salvation is the Son, the Lord Jesus Christ. Christ is the last Adam, the head and representative of the covenant (Rom. 5; 1 Cor. 15). Psalm 2 speaks of the Son's great work of redemption and the promises of salvation; Psalm 40 discusses Christ's willingness to follow the Father's good pleasure and come into the world as a sin offering. The Son is even more the guarantee and executor of the plan of salvation (Heb. 7). He took the sinner's place, bore our guilt and punishment, satisfied the righteous requirements of the law, and gave us new life. Therefore, as the last Adam, He became a life-giving spirit, giving men release and freedom (1 Cor. 15; 2 Cor. 3). The plan of salvation, for the Lord Christ, is a "covenant of works," in which He satisfied the requirements of the original "covenant of works" between God and man. He succeeded where the first Adam failed: by His one righteous act and His one obedience, we are justified and obtain life (Rom. 5). The plan of salvation, for us, is the eternal foundation of the "covenant of grace": we obtain grace freely, are redeemed in Christ, our transgressions are forgiven, and we inherit the rich glory Christ obtained for us. "In Christ" is the center of the whole "plan of salvation" (Eph. 1; 1 Tim. 1): God's children are in Christ, one with the Lord, and become new creations (2 Cor. 5).

Determinations and promises: The content-determinations of the "plan of salvation": The Father sent the Son to become flesh and come into the world, taking human nature and identifying with man, yet without sin (Gal. 4); the Son was born under the law, bore the punishment sinners deserved, and gave Himself for us, so that we should not perish but have eternal life (Ps. 40); through the renewing of the Holy Spirit, the Son will apply the grace of redemption He accomplished to us, causing us to devote our whole person to live for the Lord (Titus 3; Rom. 14). The promise-arrangements of the plan of redemption: The Father promised the Son to prepare a body for Him (Heb. 10); to anoint Him with the Holy Spirit (Isa. 42, 61); to be with Him and support Him in His work (Isa. 42; Luke 22); to raise Him from the dead and exalt Him to the Father's right hand (Ps. 16); to cause Him to send the Holy Spirit the Comforter to come, build the church, and teach us (John 14); to save and keep us to the end (John 6; 1 John 5); and to give Him innumerable descendants (Ps. 22, 72; Isa. 53).

The Complete Salvation: The content of the thirty-one lessons of "The Complete Salvation" has now been studied completely. This set of series courses mainly causes people to know what kind of person they themselves are, to know the Father's lovingkindness, to know Christ's salvation, and to know the Holy Spirit's work. Through systematic study, it brings people into salvation. Salvation is the essence of the Christian faith; salvation is the way of life by which sinners go to God and attain the union of heaven and man; salvation is based on God's giving and man's drawing. The main theme of the whole Bible is salvation — the complete way of salvation God prepared for all mankind. Taking Jesus as the center and the cross as the theme.

To preach the message of salvation, there must be a clear sequence; this is the consistent sequence the whole Bible reveals to us. On this point, this book "The Complete Salvation" speaks very clearly. "The Complete Salvation" has thirty-one lessons in total, and we divide it into seven parts to look at it: Lessons 1 to 4: man's problem; Lessons 5 to 8: the Father's salvation (preparing salvation); Lesson 9: Christ's redemption (the solution to the problem); Lessons 10 to 16: the Holy Spirit applying salvation (the essentials of being saved); Lessons 17 to 25: the completeness of salvation — every lesson expounds from the three aspects of spirit, [soul], and body; Lessons 26 to 28: the order of God's saving; Lessons 29 to 31: the permanence/security of salvation.

Looking at these seven part-titles together, the outline emerges. Please look again at the seven steps divided above: First, the problem of the people of the world ([lessons 1-4]), the Father has lovingkindness ([lessons 5-8]), Jesus has the way of salvation ([lesson 9]), the Holy Spirit has His moving, sinners have their response ([lessons 10-16]). Salvation is complete (including the three parts of spirit, soul, and body) ([lessons 17-25]; these nine lessons, every lesson, speak from these three aspects). The reason God's salvation is complete is that the complete God (Father, [Son], Holy Spirit) wants to save our whole person (spirit, [soul, and body]). According to the Bible, our whole person is divided into the three parts of spirit, [soul], and body. As great as the scope of our being created is, so great is the scope of our fall; as great as the scope of our fall is, so great is the scope of our needing to be saved; and so great is the price of suffering and death Christ paid for us! Therefore, the scope of our sinners receiving God's salvation should also be that great. Moreover, God's saving work is according to order ([lessons 26-28]), and Satan also uses every trick to disrupt God's order of saving. The last three lessons discuss that the effect of salvation is secure and eternal.

It is hoped that through the above sectional summary of "The Complete Salvation," we may, in our examination and study, clearly see the outline of this course at a glance and obtain illumination from it — not only experiencing God's salvation ourselves (clearly born again and saved), but also helping more people know and truly obtain God's complete salvation. Behind every lesson there are seven homework questions. Except for those concerning personal subjective experience, which must be answered truthfully according to actual experience, the other answers are all in the lesson text and must be [found] from the lesson text.

Study answers — The illumination of salvation. What is the basic problem of life? Which problems can mankind not solve by itself? Answer: There are three problems in total that man cannot solve: (1) The problem of sin: born with sin (Psalm); evil by nature (Genesis); corrupt to the uttermost (Jeremiah); cannot be improved (Jeremiah); all are sinners (Romans). (2) The problem of death: the origin of death (Romans); the spread of death (Romans); the reign of death (Romans); the universality of death (1 Corinthians); the appointment of death (Hebrews). (3) The problem of Satan: it rules the world; it deceives the whole world (Revelation); it controls men's hearts (Ephesians).

Why did we originally pay no attention to these problems, and why do we now feel they are urgent? Answer: We originally paid no attention to these problems. Now, because of the illumination of the gospel, we see that our life is walking in darkness, not knowing where we are going — truly like "a blind man riding a blind horse, at midnight approaching a deep pool": great calamity is before us, yet we are unaware. Moreover, we see that the problems of sin, [death], and Satan are problems mankind itself cannot solve; unless God bestows grace, we cannot be saved. Therefore we feel they are urgent.

Are you a sinner? Why? [Answer:] I am a sinner, because Scripture records: "I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin my mother conceived me" (Ps. 51:5, NKJV). Is it because one commits sins that one is a sinner, or because one is a sinner that one will certainly commit sins? [Answer:] Mankind's ancestor Adam sinned, fell, and became a sinner; and we, because sin is an innate inheritance — a heredity of life — are born sinners, beyond repair and unable to be improved. [Answer:] Because one is a sinner, one will certainly commit sins, because Scripture records of man: born with sin (Psalm); evil by nature (Genesis); corrupt to the uttermost (Jeremiah); cannot be improved; all are sinners (Romans). These Scriptures are the verdict on the sin of all mankind.

Where did death come from? If Adam had not sinned, would he have died? [Answer:] Death came from sin, because "the wages of sin is death" (Rom. 6:23, NKJV); "when desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, brings forth death" (James 1:15, NKJV). Therefore, if Adam had not sinned, he would not have died.

Is Satan a synonym for sin? Or is he still a living thing? Answer: Satan is not a synonym for sin; he is the root of sin. He is still a living thing. Scripture says: "the great dragon... that serpent of old, called the Devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world" (Rev. 12:9, NKJV); "[the spirit] who now works in the sons of disobedience" (Eph. 2:2, NKJV). Besides opposing God, what does he do in this world? [Answer:] Besides opposing God, he rules the world (John); he deceives the whole world (Revelation); he controls men's hearts (Ephesians). Fallen mankind, because Satan has become the ruler of this world, has fallen under his authority — controlled, deceived, benighted for a whole lifetime, until death.
