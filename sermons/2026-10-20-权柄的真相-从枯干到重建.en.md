# The Truth About Authority: From Withering to Rebuilding

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «权柄的真相——从枯干到重建».

Passion Week 2026 | Scripture: Mark 11:12–26

> Author: Guangyi Zhao

[Introduction: Peter's Astonishment and Our Anxiety]

Peace be with you, brothers and sisters. Today is the second day of the fast.

Today we read Mark 11:21, where Peter exclaimed, "Rabbi, look! The fig tree which You cursed has withered away."

What was Peter astonished by? He was astonished by the efficiency. It was just spoken the day before, and by the next day it had "withered away from the roots." Isn't this just like us today? In this era of big data and fast-paced living, we pursue "instant feedback." We want to make money fast, become famous fast, and even in our prayers we hope God gives immediate results. While Peter was shocked by this absolute authority upon seeing the tree, the Lord Jesus wants to use this tree to tell us: true authority is not for destruction, but to distinguish between the genuine and the counterfeit, and to reclaim God's sovereignty from amidst falsehood!

I. Authority Brings Judgment: Exposing the False Filters of an Age of "Involution"

(Scripture: Mark 11:12–14)

[Verse-by-Verse Exegesis: Mark 11:12–14]

Verse 12: Now the next day, when they had come out from Bethany, He was hungry.

Exegesis: The "hunger" here demonstrates Jesus' complete humanity. But its deeper meaning is that the Messiah, as Judge, is looking for the offering (fruit) that belongs to Him.

Verse 13: And seeing from afar a fig tree having leaves, He went to see if perhaps He would find something on it. When He came to it, He found nothing but leaves, for it was not the season for figs.

Exegesis: This is a crucial difficulty. Since "it was not the season for figs," why was Jesus angry? In Palestine, when a fig tree puts forth leaves, it usually first produces an early fruit called "taksh" (Hebrew פַּגִּים, transliterated *paggim*; Strong's H6291; according to *Strong's Concordance*, "unripe figs"; in the text, *taksh* is an Arabic loanword). If there are leaves but no early fruit, it indicates that the tree is diseased and will not bear mature fruit later either.

This tree perfectly illustrated the temple of that time: lush on the outside (having leaves / having sacrifices), but withered on the inside (no fruit / no righteousness). This is a vivid portrait of today's "Moments-feed piety"—it looks beautiful, but when the Lord draws near, it is all filters.

Verse 14: In response Jesus said to it, "Let no one eat fruit from you ever again." And His disciples heard it.

Exegesis: Jesus exercised here the authority of the "Creator." His word is judgment.

Jesus was hungry, and He went to the tree looking for fruit.

In contemporary Chinese society, what we lack least of all is "leaves." Look at social media today, everywhere are exquisite filters: internet celebrity personas, the curated idyllic lives on Moments, and magnificent slide decks in the workplace. Everyone is desperately striving to project an image of "I am very successful" and "I am very happy."

In the church, if we only have check-ins for fasting and flowery spiritual jargon, but lack the fruit of transformed lives, that too is merely "leaves."

Why are young people today "lying flat"? Because they have seen right through those "empty promises" that have only leaves but no fruit! When Jesus cursed the fig tree, He was declaring war on all false prosperity.

Application: Brothers and sisters, the Lord requires us during this fast to face our honest selves. When the Lord comes into your life at an unexpected time, can He find the fruit of love, integrity, and self-control? Or will He find only a pile of pretty excuses? A tree with only leaves will inevitably wither under the Lord's authority.

II. Authority Brings Cleansing: Overturning the "Algorithms" and "Calculations" in Our Hearts

(Scripture: Mark 11:15–18)

Verse 15: Then Jesus went into the temple and began to drive out those who bought and sold in the temple, and overturned the tables of the money changers and the seats of those who sold doves.

Exegesis: The outer court of the temple was originally reserved as a place for the Gentiles to pray, but now it was crowded with vendor stalls. "Exchanging money" was the commercialization of sacred worship, and "selling doves" was the exploitation of the poor.

Connecting this to contemporary "utilitarianism": Have we also turned faith into an "exchange of interests"? What Jesus overturned was not only tables, but also the "success-peddling algorithms" in our hearts.

Verse 17: Then He taught, saying to them, "Is it not written, 'My house shall be called a house of prayer for all nations'? But you have made it a 'den of thieves.' "

Exegesis: Jesus quoted Isa. 56:7 and Jer. 7:11. A "den of thieves" refers not only to stealing, but even more to a hideout from judgment. The religious leaders thought that as long as they offered sacrifices in the temple, their evil deeds on the outside could be covered up.

Fasting is not a safe haven for evading life's responsibilities. If we oppress our colleagues on the outside and are callous toward our families, but come back to church acting very spiritual, then this place becomes our "den of thieves." The Lord's sovereignty requires of us that our lives and our faith must be in complete alignment, inside and out.

Next, Jesus entered the temple. He overturned the tables.

Why? Because the temple was full of "business mindsets."

Consider the prevailing social mindset today: everything is about monetization, everything is about return on investment. Big data algorithms trap people in desires and anxiety. And we have actually brought this kind of "algorithm" into the temple:

"Lord, I paid my tithe this year, so You must ensure my company doesn't lay me off."

"Lord, my child is taking the *Gaokao*, so I'll fast a few more days, and You must boost his score."

This kind of transactionalism is not faith; this is turning the temple into a "den of thieves"! You treat God as a service agency and treat prayer as an exchange of resources.

Application: Jesus would not allow anyone to carry wares through the temple. In this fast, we need to clear out the "vessels of anxiety" and the "tables of comparison" from our hearts. He wants to restore the temple's sole function—prayer. Prayer is not making demands, but looking up; it is not an exchange, but an entrustment.

III. Authority Brings Reversal, Faith, and Forgiveness: Uprooting Faith and Forgiveness

(Scripture: Mark 11:20–26)

[Verse-by-Verse Exegesis: Mark 11:20–26]

Verses 20–21: Now in the morning, as they passed by, they saw the fig tree dried up from the roots. And Peter, remembering, said to Him, "Rabbi, look! The fig tree which You cursed has withered away."

Exegesis: "Dried up from the roots" symbolizes the thoroughness of the judgment. Peter's astonishment revealed that his faith still lingered at the stage of "watching miracles," without grasping the essence of "authority."

Verses 22–23: So Jesus answered and said to them, "Have faith in God. For assuredly, I say to you, whoever says to this mountain, 'Be removed and be cast into the sea,' and does not doubt in his heart, but believes that those things he says will be done, he will have whatever he says."

Exegesis: "This mountain" refers not only to difficulties, but in that context more likely to the "Temple Mount." Jesus was prophesying: if the temple lost its function of prayer, it too would be removed. Faith is not for seeking wealth, but for allowing God's will to be accomplished without hindrance.

Verse 25: "And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive him, that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses."

Exegesis: This is an astonishing turning point! After speaking of authority and faith, Jesus immediately turned to forgiveness. For authority without forgiveness is violence, and faith without forgiveness is pride.

Society today is full of hostility and cyberbullying. The true authority of a Christian lies in having the power through the Lord to forgive when facing injustice. For a person who refuses to forgive still has an unclean heart-temple, and God's power cannot flow through him.

Returning to verse 21: Peter saw that the tree was "dried up from the roots."

What does the "root" represent? It represents the unbelief, greed, and fear at the deepest level of life.

Society today is filled with uncertainty. The pressure of unemployment, anxiety over elderly care, and fractured relationships—these grow like roots of bitterness in our hearts. Jesus answered Peter in verse 22: "Have faith in God."

Reject falsehood: prune away those pretenses that are merely leaves.

Clear out calculations: overturn those utilitarian tables in your heart.

Proclaim Faith: By the authority of the Lord, let the roots of anxiety wither.

Live Out Forgiveness: Let the house of prayer for all nations flow with the love of Christ.

Practical Application:

When you face injustice in the workplace, when you face online hostility, and when you feel suffocated in this fiercely competitive environment, Jesus says: You have authority!

This authority is not for cursing others, but through faith, letting the "roots of anxiety" and the "roots of hatred" wither away in your life. Jesus goes on to say: forgive. In this era where everyone strives and bickers, forgiveness is the highest form of authority. Striking back is instinct, but forgiving is sovereignty that comes from God!

[Conclusion: Reclaiming Spiritual Sovereignty in Fasting]

Brothers and sisters, on this second day of fasting, your stomach may be protesting, and your body may feel weak. Yet this is precisely the moment for you to reclaim sovereignty.

Please close your eyes and imagine Jesus walking into the temple of your heart. Which table will He overturn? Is it the table called "Worry"? Or is it the table called "Calculation"?

Finally, let us declare together:

Lord! Rebuild the altar of prayer in this place!

Let our lives no longer be empty shells with leaves only, but lives bearing the abundant fruit of the Spirit.

Lord! In Your name we declare that those habits of the old self that have entangled us for years, those roots of anxiety, shall today wither away from their very roots!

In this turbulent age, let us look not at the circumstances, but only at the Savior who has all authority!

May Your authority begin from our hearts and rebuild this era!

Amen!

Lord! Perform surgery in our hearts. Those "roots of the old self" that have entangled us for so long—today, in the name of the Lord, we declare: be withered from the roots! May Your authority manifest the power of death and resurrection in those of us who are willing to pay the price of fasting!

Amen!
