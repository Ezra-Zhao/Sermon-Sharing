## Main Text

### Introduction (00:00–05:00)

[Fact] Deuteronomy 12 seals the place of worship; chapter 13 at once sets up the gatekeeper of revelation. In the numbering of the companion manuscript's base text, this chapter has eighteen numbered slots, sixteen with text and two empty numbers (13 and 18; for the alignment see the header); in the KJV the chapter has eighteen verses, all with text, the material of the two CUNPSS empty numbers standing in KJV 13:13 and 13:18 respectively. The whole chapter is the promulgation of three cases in succession, and the cases are of one shape: each has an enticer, each has a word of enticement, each has a command of disposal — the prophet or the dreamer (vv. 1–5), the intimate friend or kin (vv. 6–11), and the worthless men of a city (CUNPSS vv. 12–17; KJV vv. 12–18). The word of enticement is of one form in all three cases: in the first case, "Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them;" (v. 2); in the second, "Let us go and serve other gods, which thou hast not known, thou, nor thy fathers;" (v. 6); in the third, "Let us go and serve other gods, which ye have not known;" (KJV v. 13, in the second half of CUNPSS v. 12). The outer circle of the three cases moves one step farther out each time — first the prophet standing among you, then the wife of your bosom, last a city one hears of; and the distance of the three cases moves one step nearer each time — from the public sign, to the secret whisper, to the temper of a whole city. In the legal portion of chapters 12–26, this chapter stands second: chapter 12 fixes the "where" of worship, chapter 13 fixes the "by what standard" of revelation — once the place is established, the next thing to be guarded is the source of the words. One further fact of arrangement is registered: in the arrangement of CUNPSS, this chapter follows immediately upon 12:32, "What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it." — the seal of worship (12:32) and the gatekeeping of revelation (chapter 13) stand end to end on the pages of the base text; this verse is numbered 13:1 in the Masoretic text, and its readings as threshold and as seal have been set out side by side in the Discussion layer of the Deuteronomy 12 manuscript; here only the arrangement is registered, and the adjudication is not repeated.

[Argument] This is an inference: the three cases are arranged by the strength of their credential, and also by the difficulty of refusing them. The prophet has a fulfilled sign as his credential, the intimate has flesh and blood as his, the whole city has the agreement of the multitude as its. The harder the credential, the nearer the relation, the greater the number, the harder it is to say no. Moses sets the three hardest gates second in the legal portion, because he knows: when Israel enters the land, the first thing it will meet is not Canaan's sword but Canaan's words. This is an inference.

[Summary] The governing sentence of the chapter: the standard of discernment lies not in fulfilment but in direction (vv. 2–3); the order of loyalty lies not in nearness but in the covenant (vv. 6–8); the procedure for settling a case lies not in rumour but in enquiry (CUNPSS vv. 12–14; KJV vv. 12–14); the end of judgment lies not in plunder but in mercy (CUNPSS vv. 16–17; KJV vv. 16–18).

### Part One: And the Sign or the Wonder Come to Pass (13:1–5; 05:00–16:00)

[Fact] Verse 1, the opening of the case: "If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder," Three items are registered: first, two classes of person — the prophet (nāḇîʾ H5030) and the dreamer of dreams (ḥōlēm, from ḥālam H2492): the one a public office, the other a channel of the night; the self-styled bearer of revelation either stands in an office or rests on a dream. Second, two things as credential — a sign (ʾôt H226) and a wonder (môpēt H4159): "giveth" — he gives them out to be seen. Third, this verse does not ask whether they are true or false; it first records his "giving" — the test of truth and falsehood is in verses 2 and 3.

[Fact] Verse 2, the cutting edge of the whole chapter: "And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them;" Three items are registered: first, the word of enticement opens in the first person plural — "Let us go": the enticer does not stand opposite you commanding; he comes over to your side inviting. Second, the object toward which men are led is defined as gods "which thou hast not known" — not knowing them is the identity mark of the other gods in this chapter: no exodus, no covenant, no name by which to call them. Third, "come to pass" — the original yāḇôʾ says only that the sign or the wonder "comes, happens," which the base text renders "come to pass, be fulfilled": the text itself grants that a fulfilment can be real (for the singular/plural variant, see register (iii) in the header).

[Fact] Verse 3, the criterion falls: "Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams: for the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul." Three items are registered: first, the object of the prohibition is "the words" — you shall not hearken to his words; what is to be tested is not his sign but his word. Second, the reason is stated in so many words: "for the LORD your God proveth you" (mənasseh, the piel of nāsâ H5254) — this chapter supplies its own explanation: a sign that comes to pass is, in God's hand, a proving. Third, the content of the proving is love: "whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul" stands over against the Shema command of 6:5 — the two words lēḇāḇ (H3824) and nepeš (H5315) in the same order: the examination room of discernment sets the Shema's examination question. One further turn of phrase is registered: "to know whether ye…" — the purpose clause of the proving takes God "knowing" as its predicate; this form is also found at Genesis 22:12, "now I know that thou fearest God" (registered as a report, not checked word for word): the end of the proving, in both places, is "knowing" — not that God gains new information, but that man's love is made manifest in the proving, and known.

[Fact] Verse 4, the six positive verbs: "Ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and keep his commandments, and obey his voice, and ye shall serve him, and cleave unto him." Registered item by item: walk after (in the original, "walk behind him"), fear, keep (šāmar H8107), obey his voice (šāmaʿ H8085), serve (ʿāḇaḏ H5647), and cleave unto him (ûḇô tiḏbāqûn, dāḇaq H1692 — to stick fast, to cling). Set against verse 3: what verse 3 forbids hearing is "the words of that prophet," what verse 4 commands hearing is "his voice" — the two "words" stand positive against negative; in the end, the study of discernment comes down to whose words one hears.

[Fact] Verse 5, the disposal: "And that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams, shall be put to death; because he hath spoken to turn you away from the LORD your God, which brought you out of the land of Egypt, and redeemed you out of the house of bondage, to thrust thee out of the way which the LORD thy God commanded thee to walk in. So shalt thou put the evil away from the midst of thee." Four items are registered: first, the offence lies in words — "he hath spoken to turn you away" (dibber-sārâ; the word-form sārâ is registered, its number pending verification): the object of the rebellion is the God who brought you out of Egypt. Second, the intent is "to thrust thee out of the way which the LORD thy God commanded thee to walk in" (ləhaḏḏîḥăkā, the hiphil of nāḏaḥ H5080) — the direction of the thrusting is "out": out of the way already commanded. Third, the exodus formula in this verse begins in the plural and ends in the singular (see register (xii) in the header): "which brought you out of the land of Egypt, and redeemed you out of the house of bondage" set against "the way which the LORD thy God commanded thee to walk in" — the collective history of redemption lands upon the individual's way. Fourth, the closing formula, "So shalt thou put the evil away from the midst of thee" (ûḇiʿartā hārāʿ miqqirbekā, bāʿar H1197), occurs once in this chapter; the formula occurs again at 17:7, and the two are set out side by side in Part Five (on the KJV wording there, "from among you," see Endnote 10).

[Argument] This is an inference: this part sets up the first principle of discernment — fulfilment is not the seal of truth; direction is. A sign can come to pass while the word is rebellion; the design of the proving lies exactly here: if a word that leads men to other gods never came to pass, discernment would need no love, only a memory. This part also makes plain that the examination question of discernment is not a question of knowledge but a question of love — "to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul": what God would know is not whether the people can recognise the procedure, but to which side their heart cleaves. The "cleave unto him" (dāḇaq) at the end of verse 4 and the "there shall cleave nought of the cursed thing to thine hand" (the same dāḇaq) at KJV verse 17 (CUNPSS 17) stand head against tail: the heart is to cleave to God, the hand is not to cleave to the devoted thing — one and the same verb marks out the two poles of the whole chapter. This is an inference.

[New Testament Reading] Three echoes are registered (all registered as reports, not checked word for word): Matthew 24, speaking of the last days, records that false Christs and false prophets shall arise and show great signs and wonders — the combination of signs and wonders with false prophets is of the same form in the New Testament scene as in this part; 1 John 4:1 charges believers to try the spirits whether they are of God — "trying" joins the proving motif of verse 3 of this part; 1 Corinthians 5:13, "put away from among yourselves that wicked person," is the echo in the New Testament of the evil-removing formula of verse 5. Registered only, not read back: what this part promulgates is a case in the law, and it does not prophesy the manner of its execution in the church.

[Scholarly and Historical Discussion] Whence comes a sign that comes to pass? Two readings are set out side by side, without adjudication: (a) the proving reading — this chapter supplies it itself: the fulfilment lies within God's design of proving; the coming true of the sign is permitted by God, its purpose being to prove out the people's love; (b) the empty-fulfilment reading — the so-called fulfilment is either the working of the system of the other gods or the misrecognition of the beholders; this chapter does not explain the source, it only judges the direction. The common point of the two readings is already in the text: whatever the source, a word that leads to other gods is in no case to be heard; the point of divergence — the question of source — this chapter does not answer, and this manuscript does not adjudicate it.

[Summary] The governing sentence of Part One: a sign will come to pass, but a word must be tested by its direction; the examination room of discernment is the ear, and its question is love with all the heart and with all the soul.

### Part Two: Thy Friend, Which Is as Thine Own Soul (13:6–11; 16:00–27:00)

[Fact] Verse 6, the roll of enticers in the second case: "If thy brother, the son of thy mother, or thy son, or thy daughter, or the wife of thy bosom, or thy friend, which is as thine own soul, entice thee secretly, saying, Let us go and serve other gods, which thou hast not known, thou, nor thy fathers;" Four items are registered: first, four groups in the roll — thy brother (in the original "thy mother's son"; see register (vi) in the header), thy son or thy daughter, the wife of thy bosom, and thy friend which is as thine own soul (kənap̄šəkā — as thine own life): the roll runs from blood kin to the one at your breast, and then to the friend named by your own life, the circle closing in layer upon layer upon the person. Second, the manner is "secretly" (bassēter) — the second case has no sign, only a whisper; the enticing (yəsîṯəkā, the hiphil of sût H5496) uses a different word from the thrusting (nāḏaḥ) of the first case: the prophet pulls in public, the intimate pushes in secret. Third, the word of enticement is of the same form as in the first case, with only "thou, nor thy fathers" added — the "not known" of the other gods here takes in the fathers as well: your house for generations has not known them. Fourth, the person switches at this verse to the singular (see register (xi) in the header): the first case is promulgated to "you," the second to "thee" — the whisper of an intimate is heard one person at a time.

[Fact] Verse 7, the quotation closes: "Namely, of the gods of the people which are round about you, nigh unto thee, or far off from thee, from the one end of the earth even unto the other end of the earth;" Two items are registered: first, the origin of the other gods is only a distribution — of the peoples round about you, nigh unto thee or far off from thee, from the one end of the earth to the other end of the earth; the fuller the origin is written, the emptier their provenance: they have a distribution, and no history. Second, "from the one end of the earth even unto the other end of the earth" — all the gods of the whole earth added together, not one of them is on the roll of the exodus.

[Fact] Verse 8, the fivefold negative: "Thou shalt not consent unto him, nor hearken unto him; neither shall thine eye pity him, neither shalt thou spare, neither shalt thou conceal him:" Registered item by item: thou shalt not consent (lōʾ tōʾḇeh, ʾāḇâ H14 — thou shalt not be willing), nor hearken (šāmaʿ), thine eye shall not pity (ḥûs H2347 — the pitying of the eye), thou shalt not spare (ḥāmal H2550 — the pitying of the heart), thou shalt not conceal (the piel of kāśâ H3680 — thou shalt not cover over). The five run from the will to the act: not willing, not hearing, the eye not softening, the heart not pitying, the hand not covering — in dealing with an intimate, a man must first pass his own gate: ear, eye, heart, and hand, sealed one by one (for the punctuation, see register (vii) in the header).

[Fact] Verse 9, the disposal: "But thou shalt surely kill him; thine hand shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterwards the hand of all the people." Two items are registered: first, the command carries an intensified form (hārōg tahargennû — "kill, thou shalt surely kill him," which the base text renders "thou shalt surely kill him"), with no spare word left for evasion. Second, "thine hand shall be first upon him" — the first to lay a hand is the man who heard the whisper; its relation to "the hands of the witnesses shall be first" at 17:7 is set out side by side in Part Five, and only registered here. One further item on the subject of the execution: "and afterwards the hand of all the people" — those who lay hands are not professional executioners but "all the people" (kol-hāʿām) entire: the removing of evil in this chapter is an act of the whole people, not a private vengeance; the one whose hand is first leads, and all the people follow — the order itself is the guarantee of the case's openness.

[Fact] Verse 10, the manner of execution and the ground of the offence: "And thou shalt stone him with stones, that he die; because he hath sought to thrust thee away from the LORD thy God, which brought thee out of the land of Egypt, from the house of bondage." Two items are registered: first, stoning is stated in so many words only here in this chapter (ûsəqaltô ḇāʾāḇānîm, sāqal H5619, ʾeḇen H68) — the disposal clause of the second case is fuller than that of the first: both the manner and the ground are present. Second, the ground is still the exodus formula, in the singular throughout (see register (xii) in the header): "he hath sought to thrust thee away" (nāḏaḥ, its second occurrence) — the offence as fixed rests on the "sought": even if the enticement does not succeed, its offence is already constituted, because what was attacked is the covenant itself.

[Fact] Verse 11, the close of the whole case and its purpose: "And all Israel shall hear, and fear, and shall do no more any such wickedness as this is among you." Two items are registered: first, "shall hear, and fear" (yārēʾ H3372) — the audience of the punishment is all Israel: one man is punished, and all the people are taught. Further, the "wickedness" of this verse (hārāʿ, rāʿ H7451) is the same word as "the evil" of the evil-removing formula at verse 5: at verse 5 the evil is put away, at verse 11 the evil is done no more — the evil-removing formula and the purpose of deterrence are fastened head to tail upon one and the same word. Second, in the BHS this verse (Masoretic 13:12) ends with the paragraph marker samekh (registered from direct checking of the verse page): between the case of the intimate and the case of the city, the text itself leaves a pause.

[Argument] This is an inference: the sharpness of the second case lies in its not permitting a man to change the criterion out of family affection. What the fivefold negative seals is not love for the intimate, but family affection's veto over the law: the eye may ache, the hand may not cover; the heart may pity, the mouth may not consent. The order is still the order of the covenant — first the redemption of the exodus (verse 10), then the wife of the bosom; the grace of redemption comes first, and only then does the order of relations stand firm. Again, "thine hand shall be first" lays the heaviest step upon the nearest person: the enticed man executes with his own hand, which is as much as to declare with his own hand — the whisper I heard, I have judged it myself first. This is an inference.

[New Testament Reading] No direct New Testament citation of this part can be registered. One echo is registered (registered as a report, not checked word for word): Matthew 10:37 speaks of him who loves father or mother, son or daughter, more than the Lord, as not worthy to be His disciple — the question of the order of family affection and loyalty is raised again in the New Testament in the language of discipleship. The echo is registered only; it is not made the interpretation of this part, and still less are this part's legal disposals rewritten by the New Testament's ethic of discipleship.

[Scholarly and Historical Discussion] First, the subject of "thine hand shall be first," two readings set out side by side without adjudication: (a) the enticed-man reading — the hand is that of the man enticed himself: he heard with his own ears and underwent the enticement in his own person, and his identification of it is here; (b) the accuser reading — in the procedure of the case, the one whose hand is first is the witness after the charge is established, of the same form as the witnesses whose hands are first at 17:7. The two readings are set out side by side, and their relation to chapter 17 is placed together in Part Five. Second, that the manner of execution is not stated in so many words at verse 9 but is so stated at verse 10, two readings set out side by side without adjudication: (a) the joint reading — the "kill" of verse 9 is the stoning of verse 10, one case, one clause; (b) the layered reading — verse 9 is the decision of disposal, verse 10 the supplement of the manner, the clauses standing at different levels. The two readings are set out side by side; this manuscript does not adjudicate.

[Summary] The governing sentence of Part Two: the enticement comes from the bosom, and the criterion is not changed from the bosom; the fivefold negative seals ear, eye, heart, and hand — the order of the covenant comes before the order of nearness.

### Part Three: Heard of by Report — Enquire First (CUNPSS 13:12–14 = KJV 13:12–14; 27:00–37:00)

[Fact] CUNPSS verse 12, the case of the third matter — a verse formed by the fusion of two Masoretic verses (for the numbering register, see the header); in the KJV the material stands in two verses, 12 and 13: "If thou shalt hear say in one of thy cities, which the LORD thy God hath given thee to dwell there, saying," "Certain men, the children of Belial, are gone out from among you, and have withdrawn the inhabitants of their city, saying, Let us go and serve other gods, which ye have not known;" Four items are registered: first, the entrance of the case is "hear say" — the third case begins from a report: no office of a prophet, no whisper of an intimate, only a single "hear say." Second, the enticers are called "Certain men, the children of Belial" (bənê-ḇəliyyaʿal, beliyyaʿal H1100 — sons of worthlessness): the first case names an office (prophet), the second a relation (brother, wife, friend), the third a character (worthless men) — the manner of naming slides all the way from status to verdict. Third, those thrust away are "the inhabitants of their city" entire (nāḏaḥ, its third occurrence) — the scale of the enticement has by now become a city. Fourth, the word of enticement appears for the third time, its subject now "ye": speaking to a city, the plural is used. One further item of the definition is registered: "one of thy cities, which the LORD thy God hath given thee to dwell there" — the cities are "given," given for men "to dwell there"; the severity of the third case begins by first recording grace: the city to be enquired into and disposed of is, in its very existence, first a gift, and only then a case.

[Fact] CUNPSS verse 13, an empty number. One fact is registered: in the base text this slot has a number and no text (for the evidence, see the header and Endnote 1) — what the reader finds as 13:13 in other versions is exactly the children-of-Belial sentence in the second half of verse 12 of this manuscript; in the KJV, by contrast, 13:13 is not an empty number but that very sentence standing as a verse in its own right. In reading this chapter, first recognise the verse numbers, then read the text.

[Fact] Verse 14 (the same number in CUNPSS and in the KJV), the procedure: "Then shalt thou enquire, and make search, and ask diligently; and, behold, if it be truth, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought among you;" Registered item by item: first, three verbs of enquiry stand side by side — enquire (dāraš H1875), make search (ḥāqar H2713), ask diligently (šāʾal H7592, with hêtêḇ, the hiphil of yāṭaḇ H3190 — thoroughly, well): three verbs and one adverb — the density of the enquiry is written into the syntax. Second, the two words that fix the standard of a settled case — "truth" (ʾĕmet H571; the KJV reads the noun, "if it be truth") and "the thing certain" (nākôn haddāḇār, kûn H3559 — the thing established, made certain): a report must travel as far as "truth" and "certain" before it may travel to the next verse. Third, the name of the offence is "such abomination" (hattôʿēḇâ hazzōʾt, tôʿēḇâ H8441) — registered over against the words of abomination at 12:31, "every abomination to the LORD, which he hateth." One further item, the object of the enquiry, is registered: what the three verbs enquire into is "the thing" (haddāḇār) — first the truth or falsehood of the thing is settled, and only then the persons who did it; the order of the procedure is first the facts, then the persons, and the clause keeps these two steps clearly apart.

[Argument] This is an inference: the heaviest punishment in this chapter is matched with the finest enquiry. Before a whole city is disposed of, a procedure is first set up: hearing is not the same as knowing; knowing must pass the three gates of enquiring, searching, and asking, and must land upon the two words "truth" and "certain." The place of rumour in this part is nailed down: it is fit only to start an investigation, not to start a punishment. The severity of the law and the care of the law run together in the same clause — unsparing toward the offence, unslack toward the facts; the heavier the case, the more diligently one must ask. This is an inference.

[New Testament Reading] No direct New Testament citation of this part can be registered. One echo is registered (registered as a report, not checked word for word): 1 Thessalonians 5:21, "prove all things; hold fast that which is good" — the command to prove joins the enquiry motif of this part. The echo is registered only, not read back.

[Scholarly and Historical Discussion] The relation between the enquiry formula at 13:14 and that at 17:4 is set out side by side only, without adjudication: the configuration of verbs in the original differs between the two places — at verse 14 of this chapter (Masoretic 13:15) all three words stand in full (dāraš, ḥāqar, šāʾal, with hêtêḇ), while at 17:4 only the one word dāraš remains, with hêtêḇ, and the base texts' renderings accordingly differ (this chapter, "enquire, and make search, and ask diligently"; 17:4 in CUNPSS, "enquire diligently," and in the KJV, "enquired diligently"); the closing sentence is of one form in both places ("if it be truth, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought among you" / at 17:4, "it be true, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought in Israel" — on the KJV's truth/true difference between the two closings, see Endnote 8). Whether the difference of configuration is a difference of redactional layer or of the weight of the case, scholarship handles it variously; this manuscript registers only the textual fact and does not adjudicate. The relation of the two chapters' cases and procedures as a whole is placed together in Part Five.

[Summary] The governing sentence of Part Three: the third case begins from a single sentence of report, yet must pass a threefold enquiry before a case may be settled; before the heavy punishment, there is first a fine enquiry.

### Part Four: A Heap for Ever — Cleave Nought to Thine Hand (CUNPSS 13:15–17 = KJV 13:15–18; 37:00–49:00)

[Fact] Verse 15 (the same number in both base texts), the disposal after the case is settled: "Thou shalt surely smite the inhabitants of that city with the edge of the sword, destroying it utterly, and all that is therein, and the cattle thereof, with the edge of the sword." Three items are registered: first, "smite… destroying utterly" is an intensified form (hakkēh takkeh with haḥărēm, nāḵâ H5221, ḥāram H2763) — upon a city of Israel, the vocabulary of ḥērem is used. Second, the scope is the whole city: the inhabitants, all that is therein, and the cattle (bəhēmâ H929); the sword (ḥereḇ H2719) occurs twice in this verse (both times in the KJV phrase "with the edge of the sword"). Third, the threshold of the execution stands in the verse before: the disposal of this verse has as its premise the "truth" and "certain" of verse 14, and the order of the clauses may not be read backwards. One further contrast is registered: in chapter 12, herds and flocks are things brought to the place, to be rejoiced in together with men (12:6–7, 12:17–18, registered from the surrounding context); in this chapter, the cattle of a city that breaks covenant are destroyed with the city — the fate of the cattle turns with the covenant-fate of the city; the contrast is registered, and no explanation is extended from it.

[Fact] Verse 16, the disposal of the property: "And thou shalt gather all the spoil of it into the midst of the street thereof, and shalt burn with fire the city, and all the spoil thereof every whit, for the LORD thy God: and it shall be an heap for ever; it shall not be built again." Four items are registered: first, the spoil (šālal H7998) is first "gathered" (tiqboṣ, qāḇaṣ H6908) into the midst of the street (rĕḥōḇ H7339) — heaped up in public, burned in public: the enticement was done openly among the inhabitants, and the disposal of the property is likewise done openly in the city's public space; to carry it off hidden is exactly the "cleaving to the hand" forbidden in the next verse. Second, the character of the burning: the original reads kālîl laYHWH (kālîl H3632 — a whole-burnt offering), the whole city together with its spoil made one whole-burnt offering before the LORD (śārap H8313); the base text renders it "burn with fire… for the LORD thy God," without directly translating "whole-burnt offering," and the 2010 version's "burn wholly to the LORD" is registered beside it. Third, "an heap for ever" (tēl ʿôlām, tēl H8510), "it shall not be built again" (bānâ H1129) — the judgment is to leave a permanent memorial: the heap itself is the inscription. Fourth, a contrast with chapter 12 is registered: chapter 12 sets the habitation of His name, and men are to "go thither"; the heap of this chapter is such that men are to "not build it again" — one place is chosen for a name to be set, one place is sentenced for a name to remain; the two ways of a name's remaining are set out side by side (for the punctuation, see register (x) in the header).

[Fact] CUNPSS verse 17, the close of the whole chapter, formed by the fusion of two Masoretic verses — in the KJV its material stands in two verses, 17 and 18 (for the register, see (i) in the header): "And there shall cleave nought of the cursed thing to thine hand: that the LORD may turn from the fierceness of his anger, and shew thee mercy, and have compassion upon thee, and multiply thee, as he hath sworn unto thy fathers;" "When thou shalt hearken to the voice of the LORD thy God, to keep all his commandments which I command thee this day, to do that which is right in the eyes of the LORD thy God." Five items are registered: first, "the cursed thing" is haḥērem (ḥērem H2764) — the verb of verse 15 has by now become a noun: of what is sentenced to destruction, not even "nought" — not a whit (məʾûmâ; the word-form is registered, its number pending verification) — may cleave (dāḇaq, the same root as the "cleave unto him" of verse 4, in the reverse direction: the heart cleaves to God, the hand does not cleave to the thing). Second, the condition stands in the middle (in the word order of the base text; see register (i) in the header): "hearken to the voice… to keep all his commandments… to do that which is right in the eyes of the LORD thy God" — "right in the eyes" (hayyāšār bəʿênê, yāšār H3477) is registered over against the axis of the eyes at 12:8, 25, 28: chapter 12 asks in whose eyes a thing is right; the final verse of chapter 13 gives the same answer. Third, the promise in three layers: He will turn from the fierceness of His anger (yāšûḇ mēḥărôn ʾappô, šûḇ H7725, ḥārôn H2740, ʾap H639), show mercy and have compassion (shew thee mercy — raḥămîm given; have compassion — riḥam; rāḥam H7355, raḥămîm H7356), and multiply thee (hirbəkā, rāḇâ H7235); and the root of the promise is "as he hath sworn unto thy fathers" (nišbaʿ, šāḇaʿ H7650). Fourth, "all his commandments which I command thee this day" (ṣāwâ H6680) — "this day" occurs once in this chapter (at KJV 18, in the second half of CUNPSS 17), and is registered over against the "this day" motif of the whole book. Fifth, the empty number CUNPSS 18: the quotation marks of the address close at the end of CUNPSS 17 (see register (ii) in the header) and the number 18 is left empty — in the KJV there is no empty number here: KJV 18 carries the condition sentence itself. The textual shape of this chapter is closed at this point in both base texts, each as it stands.

[Argument] This is an inference: the hardest discipline in the whole chapter falls upon the property: having won, you may take nothing. The rule of ḥērem cuts judgment off from plunder — if the man who executes judgment cleaves even a whit away from the ruins, the judgment changes its nature: the man who removed evil for God would become a man who used God to enrich himself. So the condition follows hard upon "cleave nought to thine hand": to hearken, to keep, to do what is right, and not to let the hand cleave, are two faces of one and the same thing. And further, the whole chapter closes on mercy — turning, showing mercy, having compassion, multiplying: after the severity of the three cases, the last words are mercy and the oath. Judgment in this chapter is never the end point; the end point is the God who swore to the fathers, and who will still multiply His people. This is an inference.

[New Testament Reading] No direct New Testament citation of this part can be registered. One echo is registered (registered as a report, not checked word for word): Joshua 7 records that Achan trespassed in the accursed thing, taking of the devoted thing and hiding it in his tent — the narrative echo of "the thing cleaving to the hand," registered at the narrative layer of the whole canon. Registered only; it is not made the interpretation of this part.

[Scholarly and Historical Discussion] The character of the ḥērem of a whole city, two readings set out side by side without adjudication: (a) the covenant-law reading — this clause is a sanction within the covenant: when a city of Israel breaks covenant, the sword turns inward; it is the self-cleansing of the covenant community, its vocabulary of the same form as the ḥērem of outward war but its direction reversed; (b) the code reading — this clause belongs to the boundary-setting writing of the legal code; in the Bible's historical narrative no one instance is found recorded as executed expressly by this chapter's procedure (enquiry, whole city, burning of the city, never to be built again); its function is to set the boundary, not to record events. The two readings are set out side by side; this manuscript does not adjudicate. One further textual fact is registered for reference: in the battle of Gibeah in Judges 20, the object is a city of Israel and the offence is monstrous, yet the narrative does not cite this chapter's clause — citing and not citing are registered only, without adjudication.

[Summary] The governing sentence of Part Four: a city may be burned, but its things may not cleave; judgment is executed to the uttermost, and yet what closes it is the promise of turning, compassion, and the oath.

### Part Five, Set Out Side by Side: The Individual Case of Deuteronomy 17 and the Test of Fulfilment of Deuteronomy 18 (49:00–57:00)

[Fact] The relevant clauses of chapters 17 and 18 beside this chapter are registered item by item, set out side by side only, their relation not adjudicated. A. Deuteronomy 17:2–7 (five verses — 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 — checked directly on the verse pages): 17:2, "If there be found among you, within any of thy gates which the LORD thy God giveth thee, man or woman, that hath wrought wickedness in the sight of the LORD thy God, in transgressing his covenant," — an individual case: its object is one person (man or woman), and its definition of the offence is "in transgressing his covenant" (bərît H1285); 17:3 records in its course that he has gone and served other gods and worshipped them — the sun, the moon, and the host of heaven (registered as a report, not quoted verse by verse); 17:4, "And it be told thee, and thou hast heard of it, and enquired diligently, and, behold, it be true, and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought in Israel:" — the enquiry formula, read against verse 14 of this chapter (the difference of verb configuration already registered in the Discussion layer of Part Three); 17:5, "Then shalt thou bring forth that man or that woman, which have committed that wicked thing, unto thy gates, even that man or that woman, and shalt stone them with stones, till they die." — the manner is stoning, and the place is at the gates; the second case of this chapter has stoning without naming the place; 17:6, "At the mouth of two witnesses, or three witnesses, shall he that is worthy of death be put to death; but at the mouth of one witness he shall not be put to death." — the rule of the number of witnesses (ʿēḏ H5707), of which this chapter has no such clause; 17:7, "The hands of the witnesses shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterward the hands of all the people. So thou shalt put the evil away from among you." — the subject of the "first hand" there is the witnesses, here at 13:9 the man who heard the whisper (for the two readings of the subject, see the Discussion layer of Part Two); the evil-removing formula there closes an individual case, here at 13:5 it closes the case of the prophet (on the difference of the KJV wording between the two places — "from the midst of thee" and "from among you" — see Endnote 10). One further contrast of the definitions of the offence is registered: for one and the same class of offence, the three places define it each differently — 13:5, "he hath spoken to turn you away"; 17:2, "in transgressing his covenant"; 18:20, presuming "to speak a word in my name, which I have not commanded him to speak": rebellion in speech, transgression of the covenant, presumption — the three words are set out side by side, registered only, not adjudicated. B. Deuteronomy 18:20–22 (two verses — 20 and 22 — checked directly on the verse pages): 18:20, "But the prophet, which shall presume to speak a word in my name, which I have not commanded him to speak, or that shall speak in the name of other gods, even that prophet shall die." — the offence of the false prophet in two forms: presuming to speak in the Lord's name a word He has not commanded (the motif of "presume," zāḏôn H2087), and speaking in the name of other gods; the offence of the prophet in the first case of this chapter, by contrast, is leading men to serve other gods. 18:21 sets the question in its course: if thou say in thine heart, How shall we know the word which the LORD hath not spoken? (registered as a report, not quoted verse by verse); 18:22, "When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him." — the test is fulfilment: if it follows not and comes not to pass, it is not what the LORD has spoken; the close is "thou shalt not be afraid of him" (lōʾ tāgûr, gûr H1481).

[Argument] This is an inference (stating only what the side-by-side setting shows, and not adjudicating the relation of the three): reading the three places side by side, the discernment of this book shows three points of force — chapter 13 bears on direction: if a word leads a man to other gods, it is not heard even if it comes to pass; chapter 18 bears on fulfilment: if a word spoken in the Lord's name does not come to pass, it is presumption; chapter 17 bears on procedure: an individual case must be enquired into, must have witnesses, and the witnesses' hands must be first. Each of the three places sets up one test: the test of direction, the test of fulfilment, the test of procedure. This manuscript registers only the textual fact that the three tests stand together; which comes first and which after, which governs and which assists, is left to the side-by-side setting and is not adjudicated here. This is an inference.

[New Testament Reading] One echo is registered (registered as a report, not checked word for word): Matthew 18:16 cites the rule of "the mouth of two or three witnesses" (its source at 17:6) for the church's procedure of winning a brother — the point at which chapter 17's rule of witnesses is cited in the New Testament falls here; the three cases of this chapter themselves are not found cited verse by verse in the New Testament. Registered only, not read back.

[Scholarly and Historical Discussion] The handling of the relation of the three chapters, three readings set out side by side without adjudication: (a) the division-of-labour reading — the three chapters each govern one domain: chapter 13 governs the offence of enticement (prophet, intimate, whole city), chapter 17 the judicial procedure for an individual's idolatry, chapter 18 the test of truth and falsehood for the prophetic office; the three supplement each other, and there is no conflict to speak of; (b) the progressive reading — chapter 13 sets up the substance of offence and punishment, chapter 17 supplements the details of procedure (witnesses, the place outside the gates), chapter 18 supplements the test for the case of the prophet; the later chapter is the working rule of the earlier; (c) the strata reading — the three chapters come from different strata of the legal compilations, each a case in its own right, adjacent in arrangement without needing to serve as each other's footnote; they are to be read chapter by chapter, not merged into one clause. The three readings are set out side by side; this manuscript does not adjudicate. Further, as to the relation of the "two tests" of chapter 13 and chapter 18 — the test of direction and the test of fulfilment — scholarship has its own special discussions, some arguing harmonisation, some holding the tension; these too are set out side by side here, without adjudication.

[Summary] The governing sentence of Part Five: the three chapters side by side — test direction, test fulfilment, test procedure — this manuscript only sets the three measures side by side upon the table; it does not merge them for the reader.

### Conclusion (57:00–60:00)

[Fact] Looking back over the whole chapter: a sign that comes to pass must not be heard (vv. 1–5), the whisper of an intimate must not be followed (vv. 6–11), the report of a whole city must first be enquired into (CUNPSS vv. 12–14; KJV vv. 12–14), the devoted thing must not cleave to the hand, and the close is on mercy (CUNPSS vv. 15–17; KJV vv. 15–18); the empty numbers in the Chinese base text stand at 13 and 18, its KJV counterpart verses being verses with text (KJV 13, the children-of-Belial verse, and KJV 18, the hearken sentence); the chapters set out side by side are chapters 17 and 18. One stroke on the word-root in retrospect: dāḇaq occurs twice, forming a ring head to tail — at verse 4, "cleave unto him," the heart cleaving to God, and at KJV verse 17, "there shall cleave nought of the cursed thing to thine hand," the hand not cleaving to the devoted thing; the discernment of the whole chapter is gathered up upon the positive and negative faces of this one verb — and the KJV letter carries the ring in the one English word "cleave" in both places.

[Argument] This is an inference: Deuteronomy 13 gathers the lesson of discernment into three sentences — a word must be tested by its direction, affection must not change the criterion, a case must pass through enquiry. Its severity is as striking as its care: toward a word that leads to other gods it yields not an inch; toward fixing the guilt of a city it asks diligently. And the last thing the whole chapter leaves the reader is not the heap but mercy: turning, showing mercy, having compassion, multiplying. When Moses finished speaking this chapter east of the Jordan, Israel had not yet crossed the river — the map of discernment was put into their hands before the promised land. This is an inference.

[Summary] The governing sentence of the conclusion: from the sign that comes to pass to the hand that cleaves to nothing — let the ear guard the direction, the hand guard the boundary, and the heart guard love with all the heart and with all the soul.

## Devotional Excerpt (for daily devotional use)

**Scripture (Deuteronomy 13:4, KJV)**: "Ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and keep his commandments, and obey his voice, and ye shall serve him, and cleave unto him."

**Reflection**: Chapter 13 first sets down a rule of discernment: even though a sign or a wonder come to pass, the word that leads a man to other gods must not be heard; and only then does it give the six positive movements — walk after Him, fear Him, keep His commandments, obey His voice, serve Him, and cleave unto Him. Discernment is never only a saying of No: the ear closes to one voice in order to obey another voice; the hand does not cleave to the devoted thing because the heart is to cleave to God Himself. Many voices speak to you today — some carrying the credential of fulfilment, some carrying a near relation, some carrying the momentum of the multitude. Ask first: toward whom does this word lead me? If it leads toward the God who brought me out of Egypt and redeemed me, then obey it, keep it, and cleave to Him; if it leads toward gods I have not known, do not hear it, do not follow it, do not shield it. To love Him with all the heart and with all the soul is the examination question of all discernment — and also its answer.

**Prayer**: Lord, You prove us, to know whether we love You with all our heart and with all our soul. Give me, I pray, ears that discern: not blindly following what comes to pass, not bending to nearness, not following the voice of the multitude; Your voice alone I would obey, keep, and cleave to. Make my heart cleave unto You, as You have commanded; and keep my hands clean, cleaving to not a whit of the devoted thing. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen.
