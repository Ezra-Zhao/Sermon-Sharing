"Now may the God of peace who brought up our Lord Jesus from the dead, that great Shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant." (Heb. 13:20)

Why is salvation secure? Because God placed salvation within a "covenant," and it is an "everlasting covenant"—a covenant established with Christ's own blood that will never lose its efficacy. God has also added a double guarantee to this security: He established a covenant for us on earth, and He intercedes for us in heaven.

In this chapter, we look at the security of salvation from two perspectives: the Lord's New Covenant, and the Lord's intercession.

## I. The Lord's New Covenant—An Everlasting Covenant

### 1. Through the Blood of the Everlasting Covenant

"Now may the God of peace who brought up our Lord Jesus from the dead, that great Shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant." (Heb. 13:20)

Literal translation of the original text: "Now may the God of peace, the One who brought up from the dead that great Shepherd of the sheep, our Lord Jesus, through the blood of the eternal covenant."

"Brought up" means to lead up and bring back again. God is the source of peace; apart from God, humanity has no peace.

"The blood of the everlasting covenant" refers to the New Covenant established by the blood of the Lord Jesus (cf. Matt. 28:28; Luke 22:20), which is effective forever, because His precious blood has accomplished eternal redemption (cf. Heb. 9:12); therefore, the New Covenant is also called an eternal covenant, and it will never fail. "That great Shepherd of the sheep" means that the Lord Jesus Himself feeds and shepherds us so that we may have a more abundant life (cf. John 10:10).

"The God who brought up our Lord Jesus from the dead"—"from the dead" means that though He died, He is now alive and lives forever and ever (Rev. 1:18).

The peace God gives is true peace, unaffected by any external circumstances; though we have tribulation in the world, in the Lord we have peace (cf. John 16:33).

The blood and death of the Lord Jesus resolved the problem of human sin, qualifying us to be God's vessels for His use; yet we still must rely on the Lord Jesus' resurrection and His role as the great Shepherd of the sheep to receive the power of life and the supply of life, thereby fulfilling the purpose God determined for us before the ages.

### 2. This Is My Blood of the Covenant

"For this is My blood of the new covenant, which is shed for many for the remission of sins." (Matt. 26:28)

"Covenant" refers to a formal agreement or a testament. According to Jewish idiom, the phrase "many" means "all people" (cf. Matt. 20:28; Isa. 53:12).

The Lord's body was broken for us, and His precious blood was poured out for us, becoming the pledge establishing the New Covenant—Christ crucified is the foundation of all the New Covenant blessings.

When believers break bread and drink the cup, they proclaim the Lord's death; we do this in remembrance of Him (cf. 1 Cor. 11:24-26).

### 3. The Blood of Sprinkling That Speaks Better Things

"To Jesus the Mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling that speaks better things than that of Abel." (Heb. 12:24)

Literal translation of the original text: "And to Jesus the Mediator of the new covenant, and to the blood of sprinkling, (which) speaks better than that of Abel."

"Jesus the Mediator of the new covenant" refers to the slain Lamb eternally standing as our guarantee before God in heaven (cf. Heb. 7:24-25; Rev. 5:6); "the blood of sprinkling" refers to the blood shed by Jesus, which has the efficacy of eternal redemption. Here, Jesus and His blood are paired together specifically to emphasize that both eternally "speak on our behalf" before God.

"This blood speaks better things than that of Abel": Abel's blood had a voice crying out to God from the ground, asking God in His righteousness to avenge him and demand recompense for the sin of the one who shed his blood (Gen. 4:8-11). The blood of the Lord Jesus has a voice asking God to treat humanity with grace and forgive the sin of shedding His blood. His blood does not accuse us; therefore, what this blood speaks is better.

### 4. Writing the Law on the Heart

"For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, says the LORD: I will put My laws in their mind and write them on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they shall be My people." (Heb. 8:10)

Literal translation of the original text: "Because after those days, says the Lord, I will make this covenant with the house of Israel: I will put My laws into their minds and write them upon their hearts; and I will be their God, and they will be My people."

"Make" means to place, arrange, or appoint; "mind" refers to intention, mindset, or intellect.

"The house of Israel" here represents the entirety of God's chosen people, the twelve tribes. "I will put My laws in their mind and write them on their hearts"—"My laws" refers to the law of Christ (Gal. 6:2), which is distinct from the Law of Moses; the former is of life, while the latter is of the letter; the former emerges in response to circumstances with ever-adapting wisdom, while the latter is rigid, archaic, and incapable of flexibly meeting needs.

The word "mind" in the original text carries the connotation of enabling one to gain "understanding"; "heart" refers to inner intent, for out of the heart spring the issues of life (Prov. 4:23). "Mind" combined with "heart" governs the entire inner life of a person.

"I will be their God, and they shall be My people"—this statement contains rich meaning: (1) no longer repeating the failures of the Old Covenant; (2) establishing a brand-new mutual relationship based on the new law; (3) this new law is the law of the Kingdom of God; (4) God Himself will enable human beings to fulfill the demands of the law.

The greatest difference between the New Covenant and the Old Covenant law is that one is inside man, while the other is outside man. The external law only binds man's hands and feet, but cannot inspire man's heart; the internal law causes man to obey willingly, such that disobedience brings unhappiness.

The Old Covenant failed because the covenant and man were two separate entities; the New Covenant is one where the covenant and man become one, and therefore it never fails.

The Spirit of the living God writes the law upon human hearts (cf. 2 Cor. 3:3), enabling man to delight in doing God's will, just as the Son of God, Jesus, did (Ps. 40:8). When God writes the law upon our "hearts," we are able to respond spontaneously to the desire God has revealed.

The law God writes upon our hearts is the "law of the Spirit of life" within us (Rom. 8:2), and the function of this law requires that each of us walk according to and set our minds on the Spirit (Rom. 8:5-6), while allowing life to mature quickly, so that its power may be fully realized.

God's desire is to have a people set apart from the world, communing and fellowshipping with Him alone, to be His people; then, God will be their God, supplying all their needs (2 Cor. 6:16-18).

### 5. Their Sins and Lawless Deeds I Will Remember No More

The Lord has made a new covenant with us, saying: "For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their lawless deeds I will remember no more." (Heb. 8:12)

Literal translation of the original text: "For I will be merciful toward their unrighteousness, and their sins I will remember no more."

"Be merciful" means to reconcile, to accept with favor; "unrighteousness" means unjust, wicked; "sins" means missing the mark, failures.

"Unrighteousness" is plural in the original text, referring to deeds considered unjust in God's eyes. "Their sins... I will remember no more"—"remember" includes active retribution, carrying the sense of judgment here; "sins" is plural in the original text, so it refers not to the inner sinful nature, but to outward sinful deeds.

Unrighteousness and sins are the greatest obstacles to fellowship between God and man; therefore, if we want to have fellowship with God, the sin problem must be resolved (1 John 1:7).

All the sins a believer committed before being saved were forgiven by God at the moment of believing in the Lord Jesus (Col. 2:13); if one sins again after being saved, one must still confess sins and apply the Lord's precious blood (1 John 1:9; 2:1).

### 6. If We Are Faithless, He Remains Faithful

"If we are faithless, He remains faithful; He cannot deny Himself." (2 Tim. 2:13)

"Faithless" means not believing, having no faith; "deny" means to disown or repudiate.

"If we are faithless, He remains faithful"—"if" indicates no matter what our condition may be; "faithless" has two interpretations: one refers to showing unfaithfulness toward Him, and the other refers to abandoning the faith; "faithful" means He is faithful to His own word.

"He cannot deny Himself"—means that the Lord's faithfulness is based on His own nature; it does not change based on whether human beings are faithful or not; He can never deny who He is.

God's faithfulness is built upon God Himself. God Himself is the solid rock of God's faithfulness. The reason God's faithfulness can stand steadfast and unchangeable is that God Himself never changes.

Often, because of our immaturity and weakness, we fail and fall under the temptations of our circumstances, and we may even fear coming before the Lord again. This is truly a misguided mindset! For the Lord personally told us that having loved us, He loves us to the end (John 13:1). No matter to what depth we may fall, we can boldly return to His presence, and He will surely forgive us and receive us.

## II. The Lord's Intercession—Already Heard

### 1. Father, I Thank You That You Have Heard Me

"Then they took away the stone from the place where the dead man was lying. And Jesus lifted up His eyes and said, 'Father, I thank You that You have heard Me.'" (John 11:41)

The "stone" blocking the entrance of the tomb symbolizes man's natural religious notions, manifesting in this chapter as various kinds of "human opinions"; unless they are rolled away, one cannot experience the power of the Lord's resurrection.

"Then they took away the stone"—man must express obedience to the Lord through actions of faith; if we can believe and obey the Lord's word, His mighty power will be revealed.

"I thank You that You have heard Me"—before we even pray, God already knows our needs (cf. Ps. 65:2-4); before we have spoken, God has already heard.

"And I know that You always hear Me, but because of the people who are standing by I said this, that they may believe that You sent Me." (John 11:42)

Our prayers before the public are not only offered to God, but are also spoken for people to hear, with the purpose that others may be encouraged and believe in the Lord all the more.

### 2. Who Is He Who Condemns?

"Who is he who condemns? It is Christ who died, and furthermore is also risen, who is even at the right hand of God, who also makes intercession for us." (Rom. 8:34)

Perhaps we think that even our justification is questionable, let alone our glorification! But regarding our standing before God, apart from God, only Christ Jesus is qualified to judge us. And since He already died for our offenses and was raised for our justification (Rom. 4:25), He certainly raises no objection to our being justified; moreover, He is now at the right hand of God as the High Priest, ever living to make intercession for us, so He is surely able to save us to the uttermost (Heb. 7:24-25), bringing us to the point of glorification.

Not only did the Lord Jesus die for us to save us, but after His resurrection and ascension, He continues to intercede and plead for us in heaven—oh, how complete His love is, and how thorough His salvation is!

The High Priest's intercession for us at the right hand of the Most High is the wellspring of victory in the spiritual warfare of us believers.

### 3. He Is Able to Save to the Uttermost

"Therefore He is also able to save to the uttermost those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them." (Heb. 7:25)

Literal translation of the original text: "Therefore, He is able to completely save those who come to God through Him, since He is always living to intercede for them."

"To the uttermost" means completely, absolutely, forever; "always" means continually, at all times.

"He is also able to save to the uttermost"—"to the uttermost" carries the sense of completeness and permanence; "save to the uttermost" means saving thoroughly, entirely, perfectly, saving to the extreme limit, and saving forever.

"Since He always lives to make intercession for them"—"for them" means on their behalf; "make intercession" means to speak or pray for them. The whole sentence here means that Christ acts as our High Priest before God in heaven, and His primary ministry is to intercede with God on our behalf, so that we may obtain salvation in every circumstance and condition.

By oneself, man cannot come before God, but the Lord Jesus said, "I am the way" (John 14:6); He is our only way to God, so through Him we can come before God.

That Christ is a priest forever indicates that He will accomplish the work in us by the power of an endless life. Because He lives forever, He is able to save to the uttermost.

## Conclusion

Our Lord is the ever-living Lord, so His salvation is also an eternal salvation.

Just as He is willing to love us to the end, so also He is able to save us to the uttermost. For He not only grants us protection on earth, but He also provides intercession for us in heaven. Consider this: what security could ever be more steadfast than these?

## Responsive Prayer

Dear Father, thank You for establishing a covenant with us through the blood of the everlasting covenant, and thank You, Lord Jesus, for being the great Shepherd of the sheep, rising from the dead, and ever living to intercede for us. Please cause me to firmly believe: even if we are faithless, You remain faithful, for You cannot deny Yourself. I securely place my salvation into Your hands, because not only are You able to love me to the end, but You are also able to save me to the uttermost. Amen!
