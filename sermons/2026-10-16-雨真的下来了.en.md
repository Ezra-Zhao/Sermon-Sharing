## Summary (approx. 100 words)

Genesis chapter 7 records that in the six-hundredth year of Noah's life, God commanded him to bring his whole household and living creatures into the ark, and after seven days the floodwaters overflowed. The ark is not decorative fiction; it is the arrival of that day after years of obedience. This sermon examines three things: entering the ark is the response of those who have received grace, not an escape; the distinction between clean and unclean reminds us that preparation must be according to God's word, not our own convenience; and the door was shut by God—man's responsibility is to go in, and God's responsibility is to shut the door. Judgment and salvation arrive on the very same day.

## (00:00 - 08:00) Introduction · Scripture Reading · Prayer

Brothers and sisters, peace be with you all!

In our last sermon, we stopped at the very last verse of Genesis chapter 6: "Thus Noah did; according to all that God commanded him, so he did." (Gen. 6:22)

The ark was built. Planks of wood were nailed into place one by one, pitch was applied inside and out, and the three decks were fully laid out. Yet the sky was still clear, and the ground was still dry. What is often hardest for people to endure is not the season of hands-on building, but the season after the work is done and the outcome is not yet seen.

Today we turn to chapter 7. In this chapter, the rain really came down.

Let us first read the essential passages:

"Then the Lord said to Noah, 'Come into the ark, you and all your household, because I have seen that you are righteous before Me in this generation.'" (Gen. 7:1)

"'For after seven more days I will cause it to rain on the earth forty days and forty nights, and I will destroy from the face of the earth all living things that I have made.'" (Gen. 7:4)

"And Noah did according to all that the Lord commanded him." (Gen. 7:5)

"In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, on that day all the fountains of the great deep were broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened. And the rain was on the earth forty days and forty nights." (Gen. 7:11-12)

"And the Lord shut him in." (Gen. 7:16)

"Now the flood was on the earth forty days. The waters increased and lifted up the ark, and it rose high above the earth." (Gen. 7:17)

Today we will examine this in three parts:

First, 7:1-10, the command to enter the ark—God said enter, and Noah entered; waiting for seven days was not waiting in vain;
Second, 7:11-16, the rain comes down and the door is shut—the great deep is broken open, the windows of heaven are opened, and God Himself shuts the door;
Third, 7:17-24, the waters rise—the judgment was real, and the ark was real; what was lifted up by the waters was the fruit of many years of obedience.

Let us first bow our heads in prayer:

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. Your word is not empty words; sooner or later, the rain will come just as You said. We pray that You teach us today not to panic only when the rain falls, but to be willing to listen, willing to prepare, and willing to enter the ark while the skies are still clear. We pray in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, Amen."

## (08:00 - 24:00) Part One: God Said to Enter the Ark, and Noah Entered (7:1-10)

### 1.1 "Come into the ark, you and all your household": Salvation is never one person hiding away

In the very first sentence of chapter 7, God does not speak of the rain first; He speaks of people first: "Come into the ark, you and all your household." (Gen. 7:1)

As we said in the last sermon, when grace fell upon Noah, it did not stop with him alone. Coming to this chapter, this reality is stated even more concretely: the whole family entered together. Wife, sons, and daughters-in-law—an entire generation of a family was brought into the very same ark.

When the Bible speaks of salvation, it often speaks of it household by household. Joshua said, "as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." (Josh. 24:15) In the Book of Acts, Paul said to the Philippian jailer, "Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household." (Acts 16:31) This does not mean that one person can substitute for the faith of his family members; it means that the heart of someone who has received grace must hold his entire family. When Noah entered the ark, he did not find a safe place just to hide himself away; he led his whole family together to listen to God's word.

Brothers and sisters, examine yourselves today: when you prepare your ark, do you care only for your own peace, or does your whole household have a place in God's word? Are prayer, Bible reading, and Lord's Day arrangements in your home strictly your own solitary business, or are you willing to take pains to bring your family members in one by one? The door of the ark is open to the whole household.

### 1.2 "You shall take with you seven each of every clean animal": Preparation must be according to God's word, not convenience

In verses 2 and 3, God spells out the numbers of the living creatures in great detail: "You shall take with you seven each of every clean animal, a male and his female; two each of animals that are unclean, a male and his female; also seven each of birds of the air, male and female, to keep the species alive on the face of all the earth." (Gen. 7:2-3, excerpt)

"Clean," in Hebrew *tahor* (טָהוֹר), means clean, pure[1]; "unclean" is *tame* (טָמֵא), unclean[2]. The Levitical purity laws would not be formally promulgated until Mount Sinai, yet here in Genesis the concept of distinction appears beforehand: entering the ark was not a matter of grabbing whatever came along; it was preparing according to the distinction God had already revealed.

The numbers were also different. More clean animals were brought, because after the flood they would be needed for sacrifices and for reproduction; only one pair of unclean animals was brought, in order to preserve the species. God's preparation is never merely approximate; it factors in even sacrifices and preservation of species.

When commenting on Noah entering the ark in his Genesis commentary, Warren W. Wiersbe reminds readers to notice that Noah's obedience was specific obedience; even the numbers and kinds were strictly in accordance with what was commanded, without compromise. (Cf. Warren W. Wiersbe, *The Bible Exposition Commentary: Genesis*)

Obedience is not verbal agreement; it is the willingness to do according to God's word even in the details.

### 1.3 "After seven more days": The final seven days are the most testing of all

Verse 4: "'For after seven more days I will cause it to rain on the earth forty days and forty nights.'"

Seven days is a familiar number in Genesis. In chapter 1, God created in six days and rested on the seventh; here, after seven more days, judgment will arrive. What was Noah doing during these seven days? The text gives only one sentence: "And Noah did according to all that the Lord commanded him." (Gen. 7:5) No arguing, no bargaining.

Bringing people and living creatures into the ark pair by pair took time; food and drink had to be arranged, and the family had to be settled. Seven days were not given for hesitating; they were given to complete the final steps of obedience.

Later, when the Lord Jesus spoke of the days of Noah, He said: "But as the days of Noah were, so also will the coming of the Son of Man be. For as in the days before the flood, they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noah entered the ark, and did not know until the flood came and took them all away." (Matt. 24:37-39) The word "as" or "customarily" pierces the heart most deeply. It was not that the people of that generation did not know Noah was building the ark; it was that they carried on with life as usual, treating the warning as mere wind in their ears as usual.

Reading the words of Psalm 95:7-8 in light of these seven days is especially fitting: "Today, if you will hear His voice: 'Do not harden your hearts.'" "Today" was those seven days for Noah; "today" is also the day we hear this sermon.

Let us close this first part in one sentence: **Entering the ark is not about panic, but about moving the moment you hear; the test of the final seven days is whether you are still willing to carry through to the end what you have already heard.**

## (24:00 - 42:00) Part Two: The Deep Breaks Open, the Windows of Heaven Open, and God Shuts the Door (7:11-16)

### 2.1 The date is recorded in such detail because this day was real

Verse 11 sets down the date in plain clarity: "In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, the seventeenth day of the month, on that day."

When the Bible records redemptive history, it does not use vague phrases like "once upon a time." It records the year, the month, and the day. The flood is not a symbolic allegorical tale; written in the Scriptures, it is a dated event. Later, the Apostle Peter also treated the flood as real history when discussing judgment and salvation (cf. 2 Pet. 2:5; 3:5-7).

Brothers and sisters, faith is not a mere feeling. God has His timetable when He acts, and when the day arrives, His word is fulfilled. Noah waited a very long time, so long that perhaps even he could not count how many clear days had passed; yet when God's day arrived, it did not miss by even a single day.

### 2.2 "All the fountains of the great deep were broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened": Water came from above and below together

The picture in verse 11 is a pincer attack from above and below: "all the fountains of the great deep were broken up, and the windows of heaven were opened."

"The great deep," in Hebrew *tehom* (特霍姆), the deep abyss, deep waters[3], is the very word used in Genesis 1:2: "darkness was on the face of the deep." In creation, God divided the waters and set their boundaries; in the flood, those boundaries were temporarily removed, and the waters returned over the earth. In the language of Genesis, judgment is often depicted as the reversal of creation: God does not summon new weapons; He merely gives man over to departing from His order, and the waters submerge the earth once again.

Proverbs 8:27-29 describes Wisdom when the earth was first established, when God "assigned to the sea its limit, so that the waters would not transgress His command." Boundaries are grace. In days of peace and ease, people often resent boundaries as nuisances; only when the waters breach those boundaries do they realize that boundaries were their protection all along.

Let us press this point home.

Brothers and sisters, do not wait until all the boundaries are broken before thinking of turning back. Noah was not a man frantically searching for timber in the middle of a downpour; he was a man who built the ark under clear blue skies. Today—not tomorrow—confess the sins that need confessing today, reconcile with those you need to reconcile with today, enter the ark you ought to enter today. The rain will not wait until you are ready to fall, but God's word has already reached your ears today. Those who hear first have the first opportunity to prepare. This is not meant to scare you; this is grace speaking ahead of judgment.

### 2.3 "And the Lord Shut Him In": Man's Duty Is to Enter; God's Part Is to Shut

The final clause of verse 16 is the most comforting sentence in the entire chapter: "and the Lord shut him in."

Noah did not bolt the door from the inside; God shut it. Noah's responsibility was to go in, to do according to all that was commanded; shutting the door was beyond his power, so God did it Himself.

This picture finds an echo in the New Testament. The Lord Jesus said, "I am the door. If anyone enters by Me, he will be saved" (John 10:9). He also said that of all whom the Father has given Him He should lose nothing (cf. John 6:39). Man's entering the door is a response; God's preservation is grace. Inside the ark, Noah heard the sound of the rain and the roaring waters outside; what he relied on was not how tightly he had barred the door, but that the One who shut the door was the Lord.

We must clearly distinguish human obedience from divine preservation: man does what man ought to do, and God does what only God can do. We must neither confuse them nor shirk our part.

The statement that "the Lord shut him in" shows that the initiative of salvation belongs to God; Noah's obedience was a response after receiving grace, not a bargaining chip.

To sum up this second part in one sentence: **The moment the door was shut, the division became a reality: those who entered the ark were personally shut in and preserved by God, while those outside, no matter how much they carried on as usual, could never return to clear skies. This separation is not something for man to boast in; it depends on which side you are standing when the word of God arrives.**

## (42:00 - 52:00) Part Three: The Waters Increased, and the Ark Rose Above the Earth (7:17-24)

### 3.1 The Same Waters: Destruction and Salvation Occurring Simultaneously

"Now the flood was on the earth forty days. The waters increased and lifted up the ark, and it rose high above the earth." (Gen. 7:17)

Please notice this action: the waters lifted up the ark. The very same waters brought destruction to those on the earth, but bore up those inside the ark. The ark had no engine, no rudder; it was carried along by the waters. Everything Noah could do was already done; as for the rest, it was God's hand underneath the waters upholding it.

First Peter 3:20-21 links the flood with baptism, pointing out that in those days few—that is, eight souls—were saved through water, and stating that there is also an antitype which now saves us—baptism, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ. This sermon will not elaborate on the theology of baptism, but will capture just one essential point: God's way of saving man, from the flood to the New Testament, has always been that He Himself prepares it, He Himself preserves it, and man enters into it by faith and obedience.

### 3.2 "All Flesh Died That Moved on the Earth": The Word of Judgment Must Be Spoken Honestly

The words in verses 21 through 23 are very heavy: "And all flesh died that moved on the earth: birds and cattle and beasts and every creeping thing that creeps on the earth, and every man." (Gen. 7:21)

Preaching this passage cannot be glossed over lightly, nor turned into sensational curiosity. Scripture records judgment so that people may know that the end of sin is real. Romans 6:23 says, "For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." The first half and the second half must be proclaimed together: if you only preach the gift without the wages, people will treat sin lightly; if you only preach the wages without the gift, people will fall into despair. In this chapter of the flood, both are laid out before us: the waters were real, and the ark was also real.

### 3.3 "Only Noah and Those Who Were with Him in the Ark Remained Alive": What Remained Was the Seed of a New World

The end of verse 23: "Only Noah and those who were with him in the ark remained alive."

The people on earth are not described as utterly vanishing only for the story to end. God preserved one family, and preserved the seed of living creatures, so that after the flood there would be a new beginning. This follows the exact same logic as the genealogy in chapter 5: death is stated with unvarnished honesty, yet God always preserves a line so that the promise can continue onward.

Psalm 46 is a psalm in the midst of great waters: "Therefore we will not fear, even though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea; though its waters roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with its swelling." (Ps. 46:2-3) It was not that those inside the ark could not hear the sound of the waters; rather, amid the sound of the waters, they knew they were enclosed in God's hands.

To sum up this third part in one sentence: **When the waters rose, everything normally built on the earth was swept away, and only the one ark prepared according to the word of God was lifted up by the waters. Judgment reveals what can truly stand: not that which is highest, but that which is founded upon God's word.**

## (52:00 - 58:00) Application: Six Categories of People

Today's chapter speaks to six categories of people:

**To seekers:** You may have heard the gospel many times, always thinking there is still time. The days of Noah remind us that "there is still time" does not last forever. The door of the ark is still open today—the Lord Jesus is that door. Do not wait until the rain falls to look for the door; take seriously today the word you have already heard: trust Him, and come in.

**To new believers:** Having entered the ark, you will find that the rain still roars outside, and it is not comfortable inside either. Do not think that everything immediately settles into quiet peace after you believe in the Lord. Noah stayed in the ark for a very long time. What matters is that you are already inside, and the One who shut the door is God. Ground yourself solidly in the fundamentals—reading Scripture, prayer, and the Lord's Day. Do not doubt whether you entered the wrong place the moment you hear the sound of rushing waters.

**To brothers and sisters who have walked with the Lord for many years:** How many years have you been learning this lesson of building an ark in fair weather? Do not let years of obedience become mere inertia. Ask yourself: is the ark in your home still intact? Are there cracks in the pitch—those sins and strained relationships you have known about for so long, yet kept putting off dealing with? Go and mend them today; do not wait until the waters rise.

**To parents:** God commanded Noah to bring his whole household into the ark. Beyond academics and daily living, have you prepared for your children a path into the ark? You cannot believe on behalf of your child, but you must point out the door to them, establishing in your home the habit of praying together and worshipping together. The rain your children will face in the future will be no less fierce than yours; what you lead them into today will set the direction of their entire life.

**To those striving in the workplace:** Your environment may daily consist of people carrying on as usual—eating, drinking, marrying, and treating God's word with casual disregard. Do not be conformed to the routine of this world. While everyone else carried on as usual, Noah prepared according to God's word; that is what set him apart. In matters of honesty, moral boundaries, and finances, take your stand upon God's word first; do not wait until disaster strikes to suddenly remember the ark.

**To those currently in suffering:** You may feel that the waters have already risen to your neck. The picture of the ark tells you: being surrounded by waters does not mean you have been abandoned by God. Noah had no rudder; he could only depend on God to sustain him. Today, there may be only one thing left for you to do: stay in the ark, and do not jump out. Hold fast to a verse of Scripture, hold fast to the hands of brothers and sisters, hold fast to the Lord's Day and do not let go—God's hand is beneath the waters, and He has not let go.

## (58:00 - 60:00) Conclusion · Response Prayer

Let us review today's three key points:

First, when God said enter, Noah entered—the test of the final seven days was to carry through to the end the word he had heard;
Second, it was God who shut the door—man's part is to obey and enter, while God's part is to personally preserve;
Third, the waters lifted up the ark—judgment reveals that what is prepared according to God's word will stand.

Let us now bow our heads and respond to today's word in prayer.

"Heavenly Father, we thank You. The rain will truly fall, and Your word will truly come to pass. Thank You that before judgment arrived, You prepared the ark beforehand, kept the door open beforehand, and made Your word known to us beforehand. Forgive us for our days of repeated delays and carrying on as usual. Today, not tomorrow, we are willing to hear, willing to confess, and willing to enter. We pray that You would shut us and our entire households into Your preservation; when the waters rise, let us know that it is Your hand beneath us holding us up. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen."

Amen.
