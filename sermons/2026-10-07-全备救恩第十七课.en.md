## Content

The time of salvation. When are we saved? In the complete salvation, all of time is included. From the standpoint of God's will, His plan of salvation extends from before the creation of the world until eternity. From the standpoint of our experience, it also includes three segments: past, present, and future. For the Bible clearly tells us: "who delivered us from so great a death, and does deliver us; in whom we trust that He will still deliver us" (2 Cor. 1:10, NKJV). This verse, by its nature, refers to present salvation, but grammatically it explains the three segments of past, present, and future.

Past salvation. "He delivered us from so great a death." "He has delivered us from the power of darkness" (Col. 1:13, NKJV) (2 Tim.). "He who believes in Him who sent Me... has passed from death into life" (John 5:24, NKJV). The Scriptures here all use past grammar and tone to explain that a person who believes in Jesus is already saved. At the very moment of believing, the Lord saved him. Therefore he should clearly know that the Lord once saved him, that he is already saved! He has already been delivered from death, Satan, and condemnation! He does not need to hope to obtain it again; he has already obtained it.

Present salvation. 1. Saved now: "and does deliver us." 2. Delivered from evil: "Do not lead us into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one" (Matt. 6:13, NKJV). 3. Delivered from the lion's mouth: "And I was delivered out of the mouth of the lion" (2 Tim. 4:17, NKJV). A person who has a share in past salvation still needs present salvation. [This] refers not only to the past in [time], but also to the past in [nature]; it is the same now. The characteristic of past salvation pertains to life; the characteristic of present salvation pertains to living and survival. All who have received grace, when in their present circumstances they are saved out of deep waters and fiery trials and a hail of bullets, are experiencing present salvation. For example, when a believer is saved by God from the danger of death, grammatically it seems to be past salvation, but by nature it is still present salvation.

Future salvation. 1. He will still save: "we trust that He will still deliver us." 2. Hope of being saved: "For we were saved in this hope" (Rom. 8:24, NKJV). 3. Appearing to save: He will appear a second time "for salvation" to those who eagerly wait for Him (Heb. 9:28, NKJV). The so-called future salvation also has a specific reference. It refers not only to the grammatical future, but also to the future in nature. This future salvation refers specifically to the Lord Jesus appearing a second time to save the saints. This includes the resurrection of those who have fallen asleep and the transformation of those who are awake, to meet the Lord together in the air (Romans) (1 John).

Homework questions: [Original text partially illegible.] In our experience, which three segments does [salvation] include? How do we know that in the experience of salvation there are these three segments? From the standpoint of past salvation, when were we saved? What did God do in past salvation? What does the so-called present salvation mean in its use regarding time? What does the so-called present salvation mean in its use regarding nature? To what specifically does future salvation refer?
