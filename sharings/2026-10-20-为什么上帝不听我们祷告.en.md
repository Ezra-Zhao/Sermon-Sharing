"You ask and do not receive, because you ask amiss, that you may spend it on your pleasures." (James 4:3)

Almost every Christian has been through a moment like this: you kneel down, you pray urgently, you pray with tears, you even fast and pray — but heaven seems to be made of bronze and the earth of iron, and God does not say a word.

And so a question rises in our hearts, one we hardly dare to say out loud: **Why does God not hear our prayers?**

Let us first set one truth firmly in place: the problem is not with God's ears. Isaiah 59:1 says, "Behold, the LORD's hand is not shortened, that it cannot save; nor His ear heavy, that it cannot hear." God can hear. The problem has never been on God's side; it is on our side.

The Bible does not avoid this question. On the contrary, it speaks very clearly about why prayer is hindered. In this chapter, we will look at four common reasons why prayer is hindered, and at what kind of prayer God does hear.

## I. Sin Causes God to Hide His Face and Not Listen

Isaiah 59:2 goes right on to say, "But your iniquities have separated you from your God; and your sins have hidden His face from you, so that He will not hear."

This is the most direct word in the Bible on the subject: sin is a wall between prayer and God. It is not that God does not want to listen; it is that sin causes Him to "hide His face."

Psalm 66:18 puts it in even more personal terms: "If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear." Notice those words, "regard in my heart" — this is not about stumbling in a moment of weakness, but about holding on to a sin in your heart, unwilling to let it go, clutching the sin with one hand while asking God for blessing with the other. God says: I will not hear.

Proverbs 28:9 even says, "One who turns away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer is an abomination." A person who ordinarily turns his ear away from God's word, and only thinks of praying when trouble comes — God regards that kind of prayer as an abomination.

This has everything to do with God's holiness. First Corinthians 3:16–17 says, "Do you not know that you are the temple of God and that the Spirit of God dwells in you? If anyone defiles the temple of God, God will destroy him. For the temple of God is holy, which temple you are." The church is holy and must not be treated lightly; our bodies and our hearts are likewise the temple of the Holy Spirit. A holy God cannot turn a blind eye to sin.

So the first thing to do before prayer is not to list out our wishes, but to be quiet and examine ourselves: is there a sin in my heart that I "regard" — that I cherish? Is there something I know is wrong but refuse to let go of? Confession is not a formality; it is tearing down the wall, so that prayer can go up again.

## II. Asking Amiss: Treating God Like an ATM

James 4:3 is our key Scripture: "You ask and do not receive, because you ask amiss, that you may spend it on your pleasures."

What does it mean to "ask amiss"? It means the motive is wrong. What you are asking for may not be bad in itself, but the motive behind the asking is "that you may spend it on your pleasures" — to use God as a tool for satisfying your own enjoyment, your pride, and your desires.

It is not that God will not give; it is that He looks at the motive. People look at the outward appearance, but God looks at the heart. If God gave success to someone who asks for it in order to show off, or wealth to someone who asks for it to satisfy selfish desire, it would actually harm that person.

So how should we ask? First John 5:14 gives us the standard: "Now this is the confidence that we have in Him, that if we ask anything according to His will, He hears us."

There is one condition for prayer that is heard: **ask according to His will**. Not "God, do it my way," but "God, do it Your way." The Lord Jesus Himself set the example: in the Garden of Gethsemane, He said, "not as I will, but as You will" (Matt. 26:39). If even the Son of God prayed this way, how much more should we?

So here is the second examination: is this prayer of mine according to God's will, or according to my own selfish desire? If God answered it right now, would it truly be a good thing for my life?

## III. Double-Minded: Believing and Doubting at the Same Time

James 1:6–7 says, "But let him ask in faith, with no doubting, for he who doubts is like a wave of the sea driven and tossed by the wind. For let not that man suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord."

This is the third hindrance: while you pray, you seem to believe, but as soon as you stand up you start to worry, to calculate, and to look for a way out on your own. On the one hand you ask God to make a way; on the other hand you run around trying to work things out yourself. On the one hand you say, "I have surrendered it"; on the other hand you cannot sleep all night.

God does not ask us to pretend we have no difficulties. He asks us to trust Him "with no doubting." Doubt is not a moment of weakness; it is continually wavering between "Can God really do it?" and "I will do it myself," like a wave of the sea driven and tossed by the wind. Such a person should "not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord" — those are heavy words.

Faith is not a feeling; it is a decision: I decide to believe God's promise, I decide to wait for His timing, and I decide not to reach out and grab with my own hands. When you ask in this way, God hears.

## IV. Pride and Broken Relationships Hinder Prayer

James 4:6 says, "God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble."

God Himself "resists" the proud person. When a proud person prays, it is not that God has not heard; He is standing opposite, blocking the way. That is why humility is an indispensable character for prayer that is heard — in the list of qualities of a good shepherd, "prayer" and "humility" go together.

Pride also breaks relationships, and broken relationships directly hinder prayer. The Bible gives two very concrete examples:

**Unforgiveness.** In Mark 11:25, the Lord Jesus said, "And whenever you stand praying, if you have anything against anyone, forgive him, that your Father in heaven may also forgive you your trespasses." To pray while holding resentment in your heart is to ask God to forgive you on the one hand, while refusing to forgive another person on the other. God says: go and forgive first, then come and pray.

**Disharmony between husband and wife.** First Peter 3:7 says, "Husbands, likewise, dwell with them with understanding, giving honor to the wife, as to the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life, that your prayers may not be hindered." Even when there is a problem in the relationship between husband and wife, it can cause our prayers to be "hindered." The family is the smallest church, and harmony in the home and openness in prayer are connected to each other.

Even more serious is forming factions and divisions in the church. Titus 3:10–11 says, "Reject a divisive man after the first and second admonition, knowing that such a person is warped and sinning, being self-condemned." Most people think that driving someone out of the church is an unloving thing to do, but the Bible says that tolerating a person who divides the church and allowing him to remain in the church is to profane God's holy church — this is something God utterly detests. Pride, division, and slander and defamation are not merely "interpersonal problems"; they directly offend God, and they directly hinder the prayers of the whole church.

So here is the fourth examination: is there pride in me? Is there someone I refuse to forgive? Are my relationships with my family and with my brothers and sisters broken?

## V. What Does the Prayer That God Hears Look Like?

After speaking of four hindrances, let us finally look at the positive example: what kind of prayer will God surely hear?

Look at the church in Antioch. Acts chapter 11 records that the disciples who were scattered by the persecution that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Antioch and preached the Lord Jesus to the Greeks as well, and "a great number believed and turned to the Lord" (Acts 11:21). Then in chapter 13, the Bible records a crucial moment: "As they ministered to the Lord and fasted, the Holy Spirit said, 'Now separate to Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.' Then, having fasted and prayed, and laid hands on them, they sent them away." (Acts 13:2–3)

The prayers of the church in Antioch had several characteristics: **ministering to the Lord, fasting, being filled with the Holy Spirit, and praying for the mission**. They did not ask for their own pleasures, but for the kingdom of God; they did not proudly issue orders, but humbly waited for the Holy Spirit to speak. And what was the result? God Himself spoke and sent out Paul and Barnabas, and from then on the gospel spread to the ends of the earth!

In John 15:7, the Lord Jesus gave us a promise: "If you abide in Me, and My words abide in you, you will ask what you desire, and it shall be done for you." Notice the order: first "abide in Me," first let "My words abide in you," and only then "you will ask what you desire" — a person who always abides in the Lord and is filled with God's word will naturally desire what is in line with God's heart, and what he asks will be done for him.

First John 3:22 also says, "And whatever we ask we receive from Him, because we keep His commandments and do those things that are pleasing in His sight." Keeping His commandments and doing what pleases Him — this is the foundation in our lives for prayer that is heard.

## Life Application: Four Self-Checks Before Prayer

Next time, before you kneel down to pray, first examine yourself with these four questions. Each question corresponds to one of the hindrances above:

**First, is there a sin in my heart that I "regard" — that I cherish?** Is there something I know is wrong but am unwilling to let go of? Confess first, and tear down the wall.

**Second, is my motive pure?** Is this prayer according to God's will, or according to my own selfish desire? If God answered it right now, would it truly be a blessing for my life?

**Third, do I truly believe, or am I asking while doubting?** Am I praying on the one hand and grabbing with my own hands on the other? Make a decision to trust "with no doubting."

**Fourth, are my relationships clear?** Is there someone I refuse to forgive? Are my relationships with my family, my spouse, and my brothers and sisters broken? Go and be reconciled first, then come and pray.

These four questions are not meant to set a threshold for prayer; they are meant to help us move the hindrances out of the way. God wants to hear our prayers even more than we do — "Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you" (Matt. 7:7). He has promised, and He will surely fulfill it; the hindrance has never been on His side.

## Conclusion

Why does God not hear our prayers? The Bible's answer is not "God no longer loves you," but four hindrances that can be moved away: sin causes God to hide His face, asking amiss means the motive is not right, being double-minded means wavering back and forth, and pride and broken relationships stand in the way.

The good news is this: for every hindrance there is a way out. Confess, and the wall falls down; turn back and align with God's will, and asking amiss becomes asking rightly; decide to trust, and doubt becomes faith; be humble and forgive, and relationships become clear, and prayer becomes clear as well.

God's ear has never been heavy. He has always been listening, waiting for us to move the hindrances away, so that He can pour down the blessings He has already prepared.

## Responsive Prayer (Added by the Editor)

Dear heavenly Father, thank You that Your arm has never been shortened and Your ear has never been heavy. It is our sin, our asking amiss, our doubting, and our pride that have hindered our prayers.

Lord, we come before You now and first confess the sins we "regard" in our hearts — those things You know we are unwilling to let go of. Please forgive us, and please wash us clean by Your precious blood. Please search our motives, and turn our asking amiss, "that we may spend it on our pleasures," into prayer "according to Your will." Please strengthen our faith, so that we wait for You "with no doubting." Please also give us humility, remove the pride within us, and help us to forgive those who have offended us and to restore broken relationships.

Lord, we are willing to abide in You and to let Your words abide in us, so that what we ask may be in line with Your heart and may be heard by You. We give thanks in prayer, in the name of Jesus, Amen!
