# Nothing New Under the Sun: Premarital Counseling

> **Translation status:** Initial translation, pending review by a native-speaking Christian reviewer.
> **Note:** Translated from the Chinese sermon manuscript «日光之下并无新事：婚前辅导».
> **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao

- **Series:** Nothing New Under the Sun (Eccl. 1:9)
- **Preacher:** Guangyi Zhao (2019, compiled and edited)
- **Theme:** The patterns, pitfalls, and way out in the area of premarital counseling

**Summary:** Marriage is a lifelong choice; the more work done before marriage, the fewer tears shed after marriage. This chapter first lays the foundation of marriage: in Christ, a person is already complete, not relying on marriage to complete himself; then it proposes eight yellow cards in preparing for marriage, each compared with a biblical figure; finally it answers several of the most common confusions before marriage: Can faithfulness be guaranteed, what to do when parents object, how to change poor communication, and whether one can still stop after the invitations have been sent. The conclusion is only one sentence: It is never too late to do the right thing.

The key Scripture is Ephesians 5:25:

> "Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her." (Eph. 5:25)

Here "love" in Greek is **agapaō (ἀγαπάω)** (original text ἀγαπάω, transliterated agapaō; Strong G25; according to *Thayer's Lexicon*, "to love, to prize"). Christ loved the church not merely in words, but "gave Himself for her." Everything discussed in premarital counseling must finally return to this standard: Is the love in this relationship of the taking type, or of the self-giving type?

## I. First Lay the Foundation Right: Completeness Is in Christ, Not in Marriage

The material first corrects a wrong understanding at the root: "Only in marriage can I find love, self-worth, and security, making me feel that I am complete."

The right understanding is: Whether married or not, as a Christian, in Christ I am absolutely complete. Colossians says: "For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily; and you are complete in Him, who is the head of all principality and power." (Col. 2:9-10) Fullness is in Christ, not at the wedding. If God wants me to marry, His enduring love can enable me to love selflessly the spouse He has given. The order cannot be reversed: first be complete in Christ, then go with completeness to love another; using marriage to fill one's own emptiness will sooner or later drag marriage down.

Only when the foundation is laid right can one talk about doing the work. The material puts it heavily: How many couples hastily chose a spouse, married in a flash, and now live year after year in regret. In order to lay a solid foundation for marriage, before being joined to another person, please learn as deeply as possible about yourself, your future spouse, and God's will for marriage. Also ask first: What is the motive for marrying? "Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the hearts." (Prov. 21:2) It also says: "The wisdom of the prudent is to understand his way, but the folly of fools is deceit." (Prov. 14:8) This gate of motive must be passed honestly before God.

Ephesians 5:21-33 is the memorization Scripture designated in the material, worth reading through verse by verse before marriage: submitting to one another in the fear of Christ; the husband is head of the wife, as also Christ is head of the church; the husband loves his wife, just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her, and sanctifies and cleanses her with the washing of water by the word, that she might be holy; "For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh" (Eph. 5:31); Paul says this is a great mystery, concerning Christ and the church. Philippians 1:9-10 is the prayer for this relationship: "And this I pray, that your love may abound still more and more in knowledge and all discernment, that you may approve the things that are excellent, that you may be sincere and without offense till the day of Christ." Love must grow, and discernment must also grow; if there is only love without discernment, premarital counseling loses its meaning.

## II. Eight Yellow Cards in Preparing for Marriage

A yellow card is a warning, not a condemnation. The material lists eight yellow cards, each paired with a biblical figure. When you see a yellow card, do not force your way through; stop first and talk it through clearly.

**First, parents' objection.** The example is Esau (Gen. chapters 25-26). Proverbs says: "My son, hear the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother." (Prov. 1:8) It also says: "A foolish son is a grief to his father, and bitterness to her who bore him." (Prov. 17:25) The reasons for parents' objection may not all be right, but parents' concerns must be listened to seriously — the next section will discuss this in detail.

**Second, financial irresponsibility.** The example is Ananias and Sapphira (Acts chapter 5). The Lord Jesus said: "He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much; and he who is unjust in what is least is unjust also in much." (Luke 16:10) Small matters of money are the most honest: undisclosed debts, unrestrained spending, unkept promises are all marriage landmines.

**Third, excessive anger.** The example is Samson (Judg. chapters 14, 15). Proverbs warns: "Make no friendship with an angry man, and with a furious man do not go." (Prov. 22:24) If you have been frightened by anger while dating, after marriage it will only be more frequent, not less.

**Fourth, inability to distinguish priorities.** The example is Nabal and Abigail (1 Sam. chapter 25). Proverbs says: "Go from the presence of a foolish man, when you do not perceive in him the lips of knowledge." (Prov. 14:7) A person who is confused on great matters and shrewd on small matters cannot hold up a home.

**Fifth, a pagan woman.** The example is Solomon (1 Kings chapter 11). The Scripture says: "For the LORD had said to the children of Israel, 'You shall not intermarry with them, nor they with you. Surely they will turn away your hearts after their gods.' Solomon clung to these in love." (1 Kings 11:2) Even Solomon's wisdom could not withstand the pull brought by difference in faith, how much less we.

**Sixth, lack of upright character.** The material takes Samson and Delilah as the example. Proverbs says: "The integrity of the upright will guide them, but the perversity of the unfaithful will destroy them." (Prov. 11:3) Problems of character can be seen before marriage; it is only that people in love often pretend not to see them.

**Seventh, a marriage based on interests.** The example is David and Michal (1 Sam. chapter 18). Isaiah says: "'Woe to the rebellious children,' says the LORD, 'who take counsel, but not of Me, and who devise plans, but not of My Spirit, that they may add sin to sin.'" (Isa. 30:1) A marriage united for conditions, status, and interests — its covenant from the beginning is not of God.

**Eighth, no romantic love.** The example is Jacob and Leah (Gen. chapters 29, 30). Isaiah 5:18-19 warns those who draw iniquity with cords of vanity. A marriage without love, forced together, is irresponsible to both parties; love cannot be faked, nor can it be rushed.

## III. Four Common Premarital Questions

**First, how can I be completely certain that the person I marry will always be faithful to me?** The answer is: You cannot be one hundred percent certain that anyone will always be faithful to you, but you can devote yourself completely to the marriage, and choose to be completely faithful to your spouse. This is the requirement God puts forward in His word. Malachi says: "Because the LORD has been witness between you and the wife of your youth, with whom you have dealt treacherously; yet she is your companion and your wife by covenant." (Mal. 2:14) It also says God made man, "And did He not make them one, having a remnant of the Spirit? And why one? He seeks godly offspring. Therefore take heed to your spirit, and let none deal treacherously with the wife of his youth" (Mal. 2:15). Faithfulness does not come by guarantee; it comes by covenant and a watchful heart.

**Second, I agreed to a marriage against my own will; if God does not want it, would He not stop it?** The answer is: No. When you know God's will, God will not stop you from exercising your free will. How can you know God's will? If you have made the right decision, God will give you peace; otherwise, your heart will have no peace. Philippians says: "And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Phil. 4:7) Since God has not given you His peace, whether to obey His will depends entirely on yourself. Pushing responsibility onto God's "not stopping" is self-deception.

**Third, my fiancé is not good at expressing feelings; how can I help him open his heart?** The answer is: If you cannot establish a good way of communication before marriage, then after marriage you may one day wake up and discover you have nothing in common. Therefore before marriage, seek advice from older, mature couples, and ask for help from a church that can provide premarital counseling; list the topics you need to discuss and communicate about, and talk through each one thoroughly. You can also ask married couples: What questions do they wish they had discussed before marriage? Proverbs says: "The heart of the prudent acquires knowledge, and the ear of the wise seeks knowledge." (Prov. 18:15) Being willing to ask for teaching and to speak openly completes half the lesson of communication. The material also proposes an angle for evaluation: How to evaluate our love for the other party, and how to evaluate the other party's love for you, must both return to Philippians 2:1-2 — being like-minded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind.

**Fourth, my parents do not believe in the Lord, yet they object to my marrying this person; what should I do?** The material reminds: God may intervene in your decision through non-Christian authority figures, even intervening in your decisions after adulthood. The concrete method is: Ask what your parents are truly worried about; do not argue blindly, but repeat what they said as a reply — "You are saying..." — then ask what advice your parents have. The best way to let unbelieving parents see your Christian faith is to make requests politely and listen seriously to counsel. Many parents know their children very well; they can be aware of why such a marriage would have problems. The Scripture is still that one sentence: "My son, hear the instruction of your father, and do not forsake the law of your mother." (Prov. 1:8)

## IV. Even If the Invitations Have Been Sent, You Can Stop; Even If the Wedding Is Simple, It Must Be Solemn

The hardest question is: The invitations have already been sent, the wedding plans are all made, many gifts have been received, yet I want to break the engagement; is there reason to do so?

The material's answer is worth reading word by word: It is never too late to do the right thing. No matter what has been said or done, please do not do the wrong thing. Before marriage you are still unmarried, and anything before the marriage covenant is established is not something that must be obeyed. Even if you are walking down the wedding aisle and suddenly understand in your heart that what you are about to do is wrong, then please stop. Regardless of whether others understand, if this marriage is wrong for you, then it is wrong for everyone involved, including your fiancé or fiancée. You can tell the other party: Concerning this marriage, God has not given me peace in my heart. Please forgive me; I decided too late, all the way to the wedding planning stage. I always thought my heart would change, but it did not. I know you cannot yet see this clearly, but if stopping is the right choice for me, then it should also be the right choice for you. If we marry now, we will both regret it in the future. In the long run, doing the right thing can bring peace in our hearts to both of us. Isaiah says: "The work of righteousness will be peace, and the effect of righteousness, quietness and assurance forever." (Isa. 32:17) Face is for a moment; marriage is for a lifetime — this account must be calculated clearly.

Opposite this question is the other extreme: Is it not good to skip the wedding and simply travel to marry? The material acknowledges that traveling to marry saves money and effort; of this there is no doubt. But a wedding is a sacred ceremony held before people who love and care for you: when the two of you read your wedding vows, you are making a solemn promise before people who support you in keeping the covenant for a lifetime. Genesis says: "Come now, let us make a covenant, you and I, and let it be a witness between you and me." (Gen. 31:44) A wedding does not have to be grand or luxurious, but the witness cannot be omitted. Proverbs also reminds: "Do you see a man hasty in his words? There is more hope for a fool than for him." (Prov. 29:20) In the great matter of marriage, haste is not permitted.

## Conclusion

The whole purpose of premarital counseling is to resolve problems before the covenant. The foundation is laid in Christ, the eight yellow cards are checked one by one, whether there is peace is faced honestly, and the topics that should be talked about are talked through one by one. Marriage is joined together by God: "So then, they are no longer two but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let not man separate." (Matt. 19:6) Precisely because it cannot be separated, one must do the work fully before entering it.

## Prayer of Response

Lord, thank You for first showing me clearly the picture of marriage: as Christ loved the church, so a husband should love his wife. Before I enter marriage, please let me first obtain completeness in Christ, and not use marriage to fill my own emptiness; give me an honest heart to face the yellow cards, and give me courage to stop when there is no peace. In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ I pray, Amen.
