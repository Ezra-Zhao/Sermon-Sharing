"In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was without form, and void; and darkness was on the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters. Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light." (Gen. 1:1-3)

These three verses are the foundation of the entire Bible—the very first sentence that opens the Scriptures.

Have you ever thought: Why does the Bible not begin with human stories, not begin with the law, not begin with poetry, but begin with "creation"?

Because before God answers any of your questions, He first wants to answer the three deepest questions in your heart: Where did I come from? Why is my life such a mess? Is there still light in the darkness?

These three verses are God's answers to these three questions. This chapter will examine them in three parts: first, the God of the beginning—the source of all things; second, the Spirit in the darkness—hovering over the chaos; third, the light of the Word—God said, "Let there be light."

## I. The God of the Beginning—The Source of All Things

### 1. "In the Beginning": The Very Starting Point of Time

Let us look first at the opening word: "In the beginning."

The Hebrew word for "in the beginning" is **Bereshit** (original בְּרֵאשִׁית, lemma רֵאשִׁית, transliterated *re'shiyth*; Strong's H7225; according to *BDB*, "beginning, chief"). This word does not simply mean "a very long time ago"; it refers to the very starting point of time itself—before time ever began to "tick," God was already there.

Notice this order: it is not "in the beginning, time came into being, and then God appeared"; rather, "in the beginning, God was already there, and only then did time begin from Him." God is not inside time; time is inside God!

The Hebrew word for "God," *Elohim* (original אֱלֹהִים, transliterated *Elohim*; Strong's H430; according to *BDB*, "God"), is a plural noun paired with a singular verb, which many commentators view as a foreshadowing of the truth of the Trinity; the Hebrew word for "created," *Bara* (original בָּרָא, transliterated *bara'*; Strong's H1254; according to *BDB*, "to create, shape"), always has God as its subject in the Old Testament—man can manufacture and build, but only God can create something out of nothing.

### 2. Four Golden Passages and Original Language Confirmations

**(1) The Word in the beginning is the Creator**

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through Him, and without Him nothing was made that was made." (John 1:1-3)

The opening phrase "In the beginning" in the Gospel of John is in Greek **En archē** (original ἐν ἀρχῇ, lemma ἀρχή, transliterated *archē*; Strong's G746; according to *Strong's*, "beginning, ruler"), which uses the very same word as the "in the beginning" in the Greek translation of Genesis (the Septuagint)! John deliberately echoed Genesis 1:1—he wants to tell us: the very God who created the heavens and the earth in the beginning is Jesus Christ incarnate!

The very One who created you later entered personally into the world He made and went to the cross for you. The Creator and the Redeemer are one and the same!

**(2) All things consist in Him**

"For by Him all things were created that are in heaven and that are on earth... All things were created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist." (Col. 1:16-17)

Notice "in Him all things consist"—it is not merely that He created them "in the beginning" and then left them alone; rather, every single moment they are upheld by Him! That your heart is still beating today, the earth is still rotating, and the sun has not burned out is not because of inertia; it is because Christ is upholding all things at this very moment!

**(3) The things which are seen were made of things which are not seen**

"By faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that the things which are seen were not made of things which are visible." (Heb. 11:3)

Scientists can tell you how the universe works, but only faith can tell you why the universe exists. The things which are seen were made from the unseen Word of God—this is *Bara*, creating out of nothing!

**(4) He alone is the Creator**

"You alone are the LORD; You have made heaven, The heaven of heavens, with all their host, The earth and everything on it, The seas and all that is in them, And You preserve them all. The host of heaven worships You." (Neh. 9:6)

"You alone are"—there is only one Creator! How many people today worship created things as God: worshipping money, worshipping houses, worshipping their own abilities. The Book of Nehemiah reminds us: no matter how great a created thing may be, it is still created; only the One who created is worthy of worship!

### 3. A Biblical Example: Job Encounters the Creator in the Whirlwind

In the Old Testament, there was a man named Job. Overnight, he lost his children, his possessions, and his health. He sat in ashes, scraping himself with a potsherd. His three friends took turns "comforting" him, but in reality they condemned him: "You must have sinned for God to treat you like this!"

Job refused to accept this; he wanted to argue with God: "O God, come forth and reason with me!"

God truly did come—not with answers, but with questions! Out of the whirlwind, God asked Job more than seventy questions in succession, the very first of which was:

"Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Tell Me, if you have understanding." (Job 38:4)

"Can you hunt the prey for the lion?" "Do you know the time when the wild mountain goats bear young?" "Did you give wings to the ostrich that flap joyfully?"

Job could not answer a single one. Finally Job said, "Behold, I am vile; What shall I answer You? I lay my hand over my mouth." (Job 40:4)

God did not first explain Job's suffering; God first revealed Himself as the Creator. When a person truly sees the One who "in the beginning created the heavens and the earth," human questions cease to be questions—because the one asking the questions is too small, and the One answering the questions is too great!

### 4. Pastoral References

Warren Wiersbe once taught along these lines: he called Genesis 1:1 "the foundation of the entire Bible"—all the redemption, judgment, and promises that follow in the Bible are built upon this foundation that "God is the Creator"; once the foundation is shaken, the entire building totters.

Pastor Stephen Tong once emphasized in apologetic lectures on creationism to this effect: God is self-existent and eternal; He does not need to be created. All created things bear witness to a Creator—"For since the creation of the world His invisible attributes are clearly seen... being understood by the things that are made" (Rom. 1:20).

The classic hymn "How Great Thou Art" sings:

> "O Lord my God, when I in awesome wonder,
> Consider all the worlds Thy hands have made;
> I see the stars, I hear the rolling thunder,
> Thy power throughout the universe displayed!
>
> Then sings my soul, my Savior God, to Thee,
> 'How great Thou art, how great Thou art!'
> Then sings my soul, my Savior God, to Thee,
> 'How great Thou art, how great Thou art!'"

## II. The Spirit in the Darkness—Hovering Over the Chaos

### 1. Tohu va-Bohu: The Bible Does Not Avoid Chaos

Verse 2: "The earth was without form, and void; and darkness was on the face of the deep."

"Without form, and void" (*Tohu va-Bohu*) (original תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ, transliterated *tohu va-bohu*; Strong H8414 + H922; according to *BDB*, "formlessness and emptiness, desolation") depicts waste and confusion; "deep" (*Tehom*) (original תְּהוֹם, transliterated *tehom*; Strong H8415; according to *BDB*, "deep, sea abyss") refers to a bottomless abyss—shrouded in darkness, representing chaos, death, and separation from God.

Be honest: have you ever had a *Tohu va-Bohu* moment in your life? Your plans were completely derailed, your relationships were completely severed, your health broke down, and ahead was total darkness with no path in sight. The Bible does not shy away from this condition—in the very first chapter and the second verse of the Bible, it acknowledges that the world was initially a mass of chaos!

However, verse 2 still has a second half, which is the turning point of the entire verse: "And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters."

### 2. Ruach and Merachephet: The Hovering of a Mother Eagle Brooding over Her Young

The Hebrew word for "Spirit" is **Ruach** (original רוּחַ, transliterated *ruach*; Strong H7307; according to *BDB*, "spirit, wind, breath"), a word with three meanings: spirit, wind, and breath. The Hebrew word for "hovering" (or "moved") is **Merachephet** (original מְרַחֶפֶת, root רָחַף, transliterated *rachaph*; Strong H7363; according to *BDB*, "hover, brood")—this usage of "hovering/brooding" appears only twice in the Old Testament, the other instance being in Deuteronomy 32:11:

"As an eagle stirs up its nest,  
Hovers over its young,  
Spreading out its wings, taking them up,  
Carrying them on its wings."

The very same word! A mother eagle fluttering her wings over her young—that picture is *Merachephet*. The Spirit of God was over the chaotic, dark waters, not looking on from afar, but like a mother eagle brooding over her young, swooping down, fluttering her wings, incubating with her body heat, and shielding with her wings!

The Lu Zhenzhong version translates this sentence most beautifully: "The Spirit of God was brooding warmly over the waters"—"brooding warmly," like a mother hen sheltering her chicks with her wings to give them warmth.

The English translations are also worth comparing:
- KJV: "And the Spirit of God **moved upon** the face of the waters."
- NIV: "and the Spirit of God was **hovering over** the waters."
- NRSV: "while **a wind from God swept** over the face of the waters." (NRSV translates *Ruach* as "a wind from God"—spirit, wind, and breath are originally the same word!)

### 3. Four Golden Scriptures and Original Language Word Studies

**(1) Chaos is not the destination; it is the starting point**

"For thus says the LORD,  
Who created the heavens,  
Who is God,  
Who formed the earth and made it,  
Who has established it,  
Who did not create it in vain,  
Who formed it to be inhabited." (Isa. 45:18)

The Hebrew word for "in vain" (or "desolate") is precisely **Tohu**! Isaiah says: God created the earth not for it to remain forever in a state of *Tohu*—chaos is not the destination; it is the starting point! God's heart is to turn chaos into a home "to be inhabited."

The *Tohu* in your life is not the end either! God allows chaos to exist so that He may work upon it; His intention is to build your ruins into a home!

**(2) When Ruach moves, dry bones become an army**

"Again He said to me, 'Prophesy to these bones, and say to them, "O dry bones, hear the word of the LORD! Thus says the Lord GOD to these bones: 'Surely I will cause breath to enter into you, and you shall live.'"' ... and breath came into them, and they lived, and stood upon their feet, an exceedingly great army." (Ezek. 37:4–5, 10, excerpted)

The Hebrew word for "breath" here is again **Ruach**! What Ezekiel saw in the valley of dry bones is the exact same picture as Genesis 1:2: when the *Ruach* moves, death turns into life, and chaos turns into an army!

Ezekiel's valley of dry bones is the human version of *Tohu va-Bohu*—a valley full of bones, and "they were very dry." But God says: "I will cause *Ruach* to enter!" Over that "very dry" valley in your life, the Spirit of God will hover today as well!

**(3) The light of creation and the light of regeneration are the same light**

"For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness, who has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ." (2 Cor. 4:6)

Paul directly brings Genesis 1:3 into our hearts—the way God spoke to the chaotic universe in the beginning is the way He speaks into your heart today. The light of creation and the light of regeneration are the very same light!

**(4) The destination of creation is provision**

"O LORD, how manifold are Your works!  
In wisdom You have made them all.  
The earth is full of Your possessions." (Ps. 104:24)

Psalm 104 is the longest "creation psalm" in the entire Bible. After reviewing creation, the psalmist's conclusion is not simply "God is so powerful," but "The earth is full of Your possessions"—the destination of creation is provision! God turned chaos into abundance in order to sustain those He loves.

### 4. Biblical Example: Ezekiel's Valley of Dry Bones

Ezekiel's vision is a "re-enactment" of Genesis 1:2, and it is worth dwelling on for a moment.

God brought Ezekiel into the midst of a valley, and "there were very many in the open valley; and indeed they were very dry." God asked: "Son of man, can these bones live?" Ezekiel did not dare to answer presumptuously: "O Lord GOD, You know."

God said: "Prophesy to these bones." So Ezekiel prophesied. First came a noise, bones came together, bone to bone, sinews and flesh came upon them, and skin covered them over—yet "there was no breath in them." Doesn't this resemble the earth in Genesis 1:2? It had form, but it was still dead.

Then God said: "Prophesy to the breath, prophesy, son of man, and say to the breath, 'Thus says the Lord GOD: "Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe on these slain, that they may live."'" As soon as Ezekiel prophesied, *Ruach* entered the bones, and they lived, and stood upon their feet, an exceedingly great army!

The sequence is crucial: first comes the movement of *Ruach*, and only then does life appear. Your striving, your planning, and your tears cannot bring dry bones to life; only the moving of the Spirit of God can transform *Tohu va-Bohu* into an exceedingly great army!

### 5. Pastoral Insights

Pastor Tong Liu of River of Life Christian Church in Silicon Valley once taught to the effect: Do not wait until you have cleaned up your life before coming to God—the Spirit of God specializes in hovering over "formlessness and emptiness"; the more you feel that you are a complete mess, the more that is the very place where God wants to work. Prayer is not handing a "finished product" to God for inspection, but handing a "ruin" over to God for reconstruction.

The classic hymn "This Is My Father's World" sings:

> "This is my Father's world, and to my listening ears all nature sings, and round me rings the music of the spheres. This is my Father's world: I rest me in the thought of rocks and trees, of skies and seas; His hand the wonders wrought."

## III. The Light of the Word—God Said, "Let There Be Light"

### 1. Wayyomer: God's Mode of Creation Is Speaking

Verse 3: "Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light."

"God said" (*Wayyomer*) (original Hebrew וַיֹּאמֶר, root אָמַר, transliterated *amar*; Strong's H559; according to the *BDB Lexicon*, "to say, declare") appears ten times in Genesis 1—God's method of creation is speaking! "Light" (*'Or*) (original Hebrew אוֹר, transliterated *or*; Strong's H216; according to *Strong's Dictionary*, "light") was created on the first day, yet the sun, moon, and stars were not created until the fourth day: the source of light is not the sun, but God Himself!

### 2. Four Golden Scriptures and Original Word Analysis

**(1) He spoke, and it was done**

"By the word of the Lord the heavens were made, and all the host of them by the breath of His mouth... For He spoke, and it was done; He commanded, and it stood fast." (Ps. 33:6, 9)

This is the entire Bible's most precise commentary on Genesis 1:3: God's word is the guarantee of accomplishment!

Between human words and deeds lie countless mountains and rivers—what is said may not be done; between God's word and reality, the distance is zero—once spoken, it is finished!

**(2) Wisdom as a master craftsman**

"The Lord possessed me at the beginning of His way, before His works of old... Then I was beside Him as a master craftsman; and I was daily His delight, rejoicing always before Him." (Prov. 8:22, 30)

Proverbs tells us that when God created, Wisdom was beside Him "as a master craftsman"—participating like a chief engineer! Therefore, this world was not made haphazardly; it was designed according to wisdom: the coding of DNA, the orbits of galaxies, the cycle of the seasons—everywhere is the handiwork of wisdom. In rebuilding your life, God will not act haphazardly either; He works according to wisdom!

**(3) Darkness cannot stop the light**

"And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it... That was the true Light which gives light to every man coming into the world." (John 1:5, 9)

Notice that "the darkness did not comprehend it"—darkness cannot stop the light; it can only fail to receive it. The moment light appears, the only destiny for darkness is to flee! You do not need to sweep clean the darkness in your heart first; just let the light shine in!

**(4) God saw that the light was good**

"Then God said, 'Let there be light'; and there was light. And God saw the light, that it was good; and God divided the light from the darkness." (Gen. 1:3-4)

"God saw the light, that it was good"—this is the first occurrence of "good" in the Bible. God has a standard of evaluation for His creation: good! When God looks at you, it is also "good"—"So God created man in His own image" (Gen. 1:27); you were created according to the standard of "good"!

### 3. Biblical Examples: Jesus Calming the Storm and Lazarus Coming Forth

The authority of God's "He spoke, and it was done" was fully manifested in Jesus.

Once, Jesus and the disciples were on the Sea of Galilee when a sudden storm arose, and "the waves beat into the boat, so that it was already filling." Terrified, the disciples woke the sleeping Jesus: "Teacher, do You not care that we are perishing?"

Jesus awoke, "rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, 'Peace, be still!' And the wind ceased and there was a great calm." (Mark 4:39)

Jesus did not scoop water with His hands, nor did He organize the disciples to row—He spoke only a single sentence! He spoke, and it was done; He commanded to cease, and it ceased. This was the God of Genesis 1:3 reenacting His power on the Sea of Galilee!

Another time, Lazarus had been dead for four days and already "stinketh." Jesus stood before the tomb and cried with a loud voice, "Lazarus, come forth!" (John 11:43)—and the dead man came out, bound hand and foot with graveclothes, and his face was wrapped with a cloth!

Death is humanity's greatest *Tohu va-Bohu*, yet even death obeys His word!

### 4. Pastoral Reference

A pastor once taught along these lines: God has also given the pattern of this authority—"He spoke, and it was done"—to His children. The declarations of our mouths must align with God's heart; we are not to declare our own desires, but to declare the words God has already spoken. When we declare God's word, light shines into the darkness.

## IV. Life Application: "From Chaos to Light" in Six Scenarios

Genesis 1:1-3 is not merely the story of the "beginning"; it is the story of today. How God worked in the beginning is how He still works today. Now let us apply these three verses to the lives of six groups of people:

**Those in darkness and chaos.** If you are currently in *Tohu va-Bohu*—depression, despair, feeling stuck in life, everything pitch-black ahead—please remember: the Spirit of God specializes in hovering over places like this. Your chaos is not evidence of God's absence; it is the very site where God is at work. Every day this week, declare to yourself once: "The Spirit of God is hovering over my chaos."

**Parents and teenagers.** Your child is rebellious, addicted, closes the bedroom door and will not speak to you—does your home feel like *Tohu va-Bohu*? Do not merely try to "straighten things out" in the flesh; learn from the mother eagle, *Merachephet*—brooding and hovering. Every day before entering your child's room, pray for your child for 5 minutes first; let the Spirit of God move first before you open your mouth.

**Husbands and wives.** Darkness in marriage often begins with "not speaking." The lesson of Genesis 1:3 for spouses is: open your mouth to speak words of edification. God said, "Let there be light"; you can also say to your spouse: "Let's start over." A single word of edification is a beam of light shining into the marriage.

**Workplace professionals.** Projects are a mess, the boss is unreasonable, colleagues shirk responsibility—this is workplace *Tohu va-Bohu*. Remember Colossians 1:17: "in Him all things consist"—including your projects. In prayer, surrender what cannot be finished and what cannot be unraveled to the Lord who sustains all things; then proceed step by step according to wisdom (the "master craftsman" of Prov. 8:30).

**Entrepreneurs and businesspeople.** Business fluctuates, cash flow is broken, no way forward is visible—Isaiah 45:18 says that God "did not create it in vain, Who formed it to be inhabited": chaos is not the end point. Like God, create through "speaking": speak out God's promises, speak out thanksgiving, speak out hope. The words of your mouth must align with God's heart.

**The elderly, the sick, and caregivers.** Physical decline and the exhaustion of long-term caregiving represent one of the deepest darknesses. But please remember: light existed on the first day, while the sun did not appear until the fourth day—your hope does not need to wait for the "sun" (circumstances improving) to appear. God Himself is your light. Caregivers, God sees every daily sacrifice you make; "God saw the light, that it was good," and God sees your sacrifice as good as well.

## V. Practical Applications for This Week

To put today's message into practice, please take away two concrete exercises:

**First**, "Let there be light" in the dark night. Pick three nights this week, turn off the lights before sleeping, and be still for 3 minutes. Recall the single most chaotic matter in your life that day, and then verbally declare a sentence of God's word, such as: "God said, 'Let there be light'" (Gen. 1:3), or "For it is the God who commanded light to shine out of darkness, who has shone in our hearts" (2 Cor. 4:6). Let God's word speak first before your heart sleeps.

**Second**, look up at creation. Pick one day this week to watch a sunrise or gaze at the starry sky, and read aloud Psalm 8:3-4: "When I consider Your heavens, the work of Your fingers, the moon and the stars, which You have ordained, what is man that You are mindful of him, and the son of man that You visit him?" Then thank God for one specific thing—let creation lead you back to thanksgiving.

## Conclusion

Let us return to the beginning.

In the beginning, ***Bereshit***—before time began, God was already there; He is the starting point of your life, and He will surely be the finish line of your life.

In the beginning, ***Elohim***—this triune God created you out of nothing through ***Bara***; He is also able to create anew out of your "nothingness."

Now, ***Ruach***—the Spirit of God, like a mother eagle, is hovering over the chaotic waters of your life, brooding over you, incubating you.

Presently, ***'Or***—God says: "Let there be light"!

## Responsive Prayer

Heavenly Father, we thank You! You are the God of the beginning, the Lord who created the heavens and the earth. Lord, we confess that our lives are often *tohu va-bohu*—empty, chaotic, and dark. But we thank You that Your Spirit has not departed; You are hovering over the face of our waters, brooding over us just as a mother eagle broods over her young.

Lord, we declare in Your name: God said, "Let there be light!" May Your light shine into our depression, our families, our marriages, our work, our businesses, and our sicknesses. Let the miracle of Ezekiel's valley of dry bones happen in our midst today—dry bones shall come to life and become an exceedingly great army!

Lord, we commit this week into Your hands. May Your *Ruach* move, may Your word be accomplished, and may You look upon us and say, "Good!" In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ we pray, Amen!
